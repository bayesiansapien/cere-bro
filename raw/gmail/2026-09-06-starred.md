1. Structure-driven molecular-ALD resists for EUV lithography
Publication: Peer-reviewed research article, ACS Nano, 3 September 2026
Authors: Thi Thu Huong Chu et al.
Process area: EUV resist deposition and reaction mechanisms
Key finding: The authors fabricated molecular-atomic-layer-deposited inorganic–organic resists using three metal species and different aromatic or aliphatic linkers. Organic-linker chemistry, not the metal centre, dominated sensitivity. Aliphatic-linker formulations delivered approximately 130× greater EUV sensitivity than aromatic equivalents. In-situ FTIR linked this difference to rapid formation of dense metal–S and metal–O networks in the aliphatic materials, versus mainly inter-ring cross-linking in aromatic systems.
Why it matters: This shifts the design focus for vapor-deposited EUV resists from metal selection toward organic-linker reaction pathways. MALD also offers thickness and composition control unavailable from conventional spin coating.
Engineering implications: The result could support dry-resist optimization for sub-1-nm-node patterning, but sensitivity must still be assessed together with resolution, line-edge roughness, outgassing, defectivity, etch resistance, and wafer-scale thickness uniformity. The full article may require subscription access; supporting information is publicly indexed.

2. Selective resist smoothing using SO₂-based ALD-like and PECVD processes
Publication: Peer-reviewed research article, Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology A, 3 September 2026
Authors: Nicolas Maldonado, Van-Long Nguyen, Christophe Vallée and Gregory Denbeaux
Process area: EUV resist smoothing and selective deposition
Key finding: SO₂ was selectively deposited on Sn-based metal-organic resist while deposition on SiO₂ was suppressed. An ALD-like physisorption process reduced blanket-film surface roughness by approximately 10%, while selective PECVD achieved reductions of up to 40% without degrading the measured resist etch rate. In-situ ellipsometry and angle-resolved XPS were used to verify deposition behavior.
Why it matters: Roughness generated during EUV exposure is transferred and sometimes amplified during pattern transfer. A selective smoothing step could improve line-edge and line-width roughness without applying a conformal film everywhere.
Engineering implications: PECVD provides the larger correction, but the experiments used blanket films as proxies. Patterned-wafer validation is still needed for CD bias, pitch dependence, sidewall versus top-surface deposition, stochastic failures and defect creation.
DOI and source [ https://substack.com/redirect/def3aa74-ed3b-4eeb-81d1-f9415c03fadd?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]
3. Lithography-free self-alignment through catalytic PMMA etching and selective HfO₂ ALD
Publication: Peer-reviewed research article, Journal of Vacuum Science & Technology A, 1 September 2026
Authors: Enzo Novoselic, Natalya Tokranova and Christophe Vallée
Process area: Area-selective etch/deposition integration
Key finding: CoOₓ catalyzed oxygen-driven PMMA removal at 300°C, while PMMA on SiO₂ remained intact under the same conditions. Raising temperature from 200°C to 300°C extended the HfO₂ nucleation delay on PMMA from fewer than 60 cycles to more than 190 cycles. A continuous single-chamber sequence subsequently produced 21 nm of HfO₂ on CoOₓ, with no measurable deposition on PMMA-covered SiO₂.
Why it matters: The study integrates area-selective etching and area-selective deposition into a self-aligned sequence rather than treating them as separate unit processes.
Engineering implications: The concept is relevant to self-aligned pattern multiplication and BEOL-compatible processing. Key integration questions remain around PMMA thermal stability, catalyst contamination, selectivity loss with feature density, defectivity, precursor memory and selectivity over production-scale cycle counts.
DOI and source [ https://substack.com/redirect/280f6fc3-ee8c-40a2-9142-16ee7f40cd9d?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]
4. Open synthetic overlay dataset based on EUV scanner physics
Publication: Peer-reviewed research article and open benchmark dataset, APL Machine Learning, 1 September 2026
Authors: Aris Magklaras, George Tsirogiannis, Christos Gogos, Panayiotis Alefragis and Alexios Birbas
Process area: Overlay control, APC and yield
Key finding: EUV-OVL-SYN contains 284,000 overlay measurements from four simulated lots of 50 wafers, with 420 targets per wafer. It reproduces a scanner-level van den Brink polynomial fingerprint with cross-wafer R2≥0.93R^2 \geq 0.93, four temporal-drift scenarios and nonlinear intra-field residuals of approximately 1.1–1.4 nm per axis, against a 0.15 nm measurement-noise floor. Ground-truth decomposition and a reproducible Python generator are supplied.
Why it matters: Public production-grade overlay datasets are scarce. This benchmark permits controlled comparison of correction algorithms because every injected drift and residual component is known.
Engineering implications: It is useful for testing run-to-run control, drift detection and nonlinear residual correction before access to sensitive fab data. Its main limitation is synthetic validity: performance on this benchmark cannot substitute for evaluation against scanner, reticle, process-stack and metrology interactions in actual production.
DOI and source [ https://substack.com/redirect/530aa0ce-61bf-4fa6-857b-1330eaddef26?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]
5. Physics-informed inversion of buried EUV mask defects using TSOM
Publication: Peer-reviewed research article, Optics Express, 31 August 2026
Authors: Wentao Gong et al.
Process area: EUV mask metrology and defect inspection
Key finding: The method separates through-focus scanning optical microscopy images into differential and symmetric channels. The axial location of maximum intensity identifies bump-versus-pit polarity, while a CNN estimates defect geometry. Using only 401 simulated samples per defect type, mean relative inversion error reached 2.55% for bumps and 3.28% for pits; reported inference time was 0.823 ms per sample.
Why it matters: Buried multilayer defects create phase errors but are difficult to reconstruct quantitatively. The physics-informed representation reduces training-data requirements and improves interpretability relative to a purely image-driven network.
Engineering implications: The speed is compatible with high-throughput analysis, but the present evidence is simulation-led. Experimental calibration must address optical-model mismatch, multilayer variability, roughness, noise, tool-to-tool transfer and uncertainty estimates before deployment in mask-blank disposition.
