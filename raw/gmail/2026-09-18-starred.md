But I am here today to call attention to a growing trend: people on the technolibertarian right calling for liability in AI (which is totally reasonable), but then using that as if it were an argument against regulation.

## 3. How a single tweet transformed the AI safety debate

**From:** Understanding AI <understandingai@substack.com>  
**Date:** Thu, 17 Sep 2026 20:21:40 +0000  
**Link:** [https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a0b1089d52f16d2](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a0b1089d52f16d2)

View this post on the web at https://www.understandingai.org/p/how-a-single-tweet-transformed-the

It was the tweet heard ‘round the world.
Last week, a little-known Anthropic employee named Jacob Coxon announced [ https://substack.com/redirect/4382edf3-be30-44c1-a3b0-763dd3d9a659?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] that he was resigning from the company. “I spent the last three years doing pretraining research at both OpenAI and Anthropic. Neither company is acting responsibly,” he wrote. “The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade.”
Coxon wasn’t the first employee to quit over similar fears. In 2024, William Saunders told a US Senate committee that he “resigned from OpenAI because I lost faith that by themselves they will make responsible decisions about AGI.” And in February, Mrinank Sharma wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/8fbda77e-8b86-41f2-800f-abdf25d00b79?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] that he was leaving Anthropic because “the world is in peril” from AI and other risks, and he felt he couldn’t act with sufficient integrity at the company.
Yet for whatever reason, it was Coxon’s message that broke through. His tweet has been viewed over 170 million times. He was interviewed on CNN [ https://substack.com/redirect/99fd7c91-cf9e-4975-9d1e-b9aadd3119d5?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], CBS [ https://substack.com/redirect/4d6010dc-639d-4c27-a48e-e20fcd8725d2?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], and Fox News [ https://substack.com/redirect/161bd0e1-4cff-47e7-bf8e-e5397cabf070?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. Lawmakers and celebrities alike referenced his resignation. AI risk was suddenly a mainstream topic.
It’s not entirely surprising that more people are paying attention now. Since the start of summer, there’s been a steady [ https://substack.com/redirect/8f7ae420-e3ba-40ec-83d5-5ecb458eb296?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] drumbeat [ https://substack.com/redirect/67252208-ece4-4e3f-9494-7146f05cb338?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] of news about AI companies struggling to keep their agents under control. And Americans are already critical of other aspects of AI development — such as the mass construction of data centers [ https://substack.com/redirect/7f7601d2-9409-4117-a8dc-36836c2b9fbf?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ].
Still, it was a bit shocking to suddenly see discussions of AI risk well outside the tech bubble. Musician Sheryl Crow mentioned [ https://substack.com/redirect/bab184d3-61b7-4a43-ad33-c5eb0eed54da?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] Coxon’s departure in an Instagram post. TMZ asked [ https://substack.com/redirect/8e04e79e-e24e-45c7-bada-5c00f700b3af?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] Matt Damon about human extinction because of AI. Joe Rogan interviewed [ https://substack.com/redirect/657864ff-c77a-44f9-a9d6-0a45741d8f96?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] prominent AI safety advocate Daniel Kokotajlo.
For some AI safety advocates, this was the crucial moment they’ve been waiting for. In a Monday LessWrong post [ https://substack.com/redirect/8813409c-93b4-42a8-8efb-87697cabb0fb?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] titled “Crunch Time is Now. Drop Everything,” the head of PauseAI, Maxime Fournes, wrote that “this is the kind of chance we have dreamed of, and we have a responsibility not to waste it.”
But it’s unclear how increased popular interest in AI safety will be channeled. Fournes wants a global treaty pausing development of frontier AI systems. That seems unlikely, at least in the short term. Donald Trump is scheduled to meet with Chinese officials next week, and AI safety is on the agenda [ https://substack.com/redirect/2909b146-5fd2-4038-9641-c2ab6e34b014?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. But recent comments from both sides suggest that they are not close to reaching a deal.
What about US regulation? AI risk is suddenly a live political issue at the national level, and more politicians than ever are discussing it. However, the House just started a seven-week recess; it looks unlikely that any AI legislation will pass before the new year.
In short, no one is going to force AI labs to change how they do business in the next few months. Despite that, some company leaders have signaled a willingness to slow down voluntarily — especially if their rivals do the same.
Congress isn’t passing a bill soon...

## 4. Semiconductor Materials Roadmap 2027-2033

**From:** The Semiconductor Newsletter <thesemiconductornewsletter@substack.com>  
**Date:** Thu, 17 Sep 2026 17:06:28 +0000  
**Link:** [https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a0b056363a8081b](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a0b056363a8081b)

View this post on the web at https://thesemiconductornewsletter.substack.com/p/semiconductor-materials-roadmap-2027

Executive Summary
The materials market has entered a new mix cycle, not simply a new upcycle. Global semiconductor materials revenue reached a record $73.2 billion in 2025, up 6.8% year over year. Wafer-fab materials accounted for $45.8 billion and packaging materials $27.4 billion; packaging grew 9.3%, versus 5.4% for wafer-fab materials. The difference reflects AI accelerators, HBM and larger multi-die packages as much as unit semiconductor demand. (SEMI, 2026 [ https://substack.com/redirect/48e138ef-de75-495e-874d-ab3f5f19c993?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
Silicon remains the base material through 2033. The important change is higher specification, not replacement: tighter defect, flatness and nanotopography requirements; more epitaxial layers; backside processing; and differentiated SOI, photonics-SOI and engineered wafers. Worldwide silicon-wafer shipments rose 5.8% to 12,973 million square inches in 2025, while revenue slipped 1.2% to $11.4 billion, illustrating that area demand and pricing can diverge. (SEMI, 2026 [ https://substack.com/redirect/710c8dbc-3526-456d-89cd-67706e7daa9c?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
Sub-2 nm logic raises materials intensity faster than wafer starts. Gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheets, backside power delivery, tighter interconnect pitches and eventual CFET architectures require more conformal ALD, selective deposition and etch, work-function metals, sacrificial layers, advanced cleans and defect-sensitive CMP. The result is more process steps and more qualified chemistries per wafer even if leading-edge wafer volumes remain concentrated in a small number of fabs.
Copper is not disappearing, but its monopoly at the smallest dimensions is ending. Copper should remain dominant in global interconnect volume. Cobalt and ruthenium gain at contacts, liners, caps and selected local-interconnect levels; molybdenum gains in memory wordlines and difficult metal-fill applications. Imec has demonstrated ruthenium semi-damascene lines at 16 nm pitch, but broad production adoption still depends on resistance, reliability, integration cost, etch control and constrained ruthenium supply. (imec, 2025 [ https://substack.com/redirect/2cf62b16-fa06-462d-babd-5001aea63989?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
High-NA EUV is a materials transition as much as an equipment transition. The 0.55-NA platform can reduce multipatterning at the tightest pitches, but it raises the burden on resist sensitivity, stochastic-defect control, line-edge roughness, underlayers, developers, rinses, masks, pellicles and contamination control. Intel plans early use; TSMC has publicly taken a more selective posture. That makes 2027–2030 an ecosystem ramp rather than an industry-wide conversion date. (ASML, 2024 [ https://substack.com/redirect/645039a6-d159-4bcc-b5d8-19f7883e34b1?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], Reuters, 2025 [ https://substack.com/redirect/a31f54fb-58b6-451d-9d5a-77382f8930e5?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
HBM is the clearest near-term materials multiplier. More DRAM die per stack, larger logic base dies, denser TSV arrays, thinner wafers, more demanding temporary bonding, finer interconnect, larger interposers and stricter warpage control increase consumption of deposition precursors, etchants, CMP, bonding films, underfills, mold compounds and thermal materials. SK hynix expects the HBM market to grow at roughly 30% annually through 2030; this is a company forecast, not an industry consensus. (Reuters, 2025 [ https://substack.com/redirect/c0f8edbc-9266-4314-8d36-2bb47915ad2d?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
Hybrid bonding moves from specialized image-sensor and 3D integration into mainstream high-end compute, but microbumps persist. Copper-to-copper/oxide hybrid bonding enables sub-10 µm pitch and lower interconnect energy. The controlling variables are surface planarity, copper recess/protrusion, oxide activation, particles, alignment, wafer bow and known-good-die economics. TSMC describes sub-10 µm pitch for SoIC and 3 nm stacking in volume production, while external roadmaps point toward 4–6 µm pitches later in the decade. (TSMC 3DFabric [ https://substack.com/redirect/58d6a466-471d-4e2c-a682-65a869e0ef22?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], TrendForce, 2025 [ https://substack.com/redirect/0598d2e2-4e66-47d1-8900-25c4057df723?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
SiC and GaN expand the power-materials stack without displacing silicon everywhere. SiC is strongest in higher-voltage traction, charging, industrial and grid applications; GaN is strongest in high-frequency, lower-to-mid-voltage conversion, including server power. The main 2027–2033 transitions are SiC from 150 mm toward 200 mm and GaN-on-Si from 200 mm toward selected 300 mm production. Infineon has demonstrated 300 mm power GaN, but yield, buffer stress, epitaxy uniformity and cost determine commercial pace. (Infineon, 2024 [ https://substack.com/redirect/94d91783-0f24-4b23-ac0d-7f9e121dd952?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ])
Thermal materials become a first-order scaling constraint. AI packages combine larger heat sources, HBM stacks, silicon interposers, high-current substrates and localized hotspots. Higher-conductivity thermal-interface materials, low-void die attach, heat spreaders, cold plates and eventually embedded or microfluidic cooling gain strategic value. Reliability, pump power, corrosion, leak risk and serviceability will keep advanced cooling selective through 2030.
Supply risk is concentrated in a small set of upstream nodes. Japan remains central in silicon wafers, photoresists, CMP and high-purity chemicals; Taiwan and South Korea dominate consumption associated with advanced foundry and memory; China controls most primary low-purity gallium; southern Africa and Russia dominate primary ruthenium; and helium supply is concentrated in the United States, Qatar and Russia. Qualification cycles measured in quarters or years make a nominally available substitute different from a production-qualified substitute.
Forecast discipline and definitions
This report separates three evidence classes:
Established: qualified and used in volume production.
Scaling rapidly: in production with expanding capacity, customers or use cases.
Emerging: demonstrated in pilot lines or limited production, with unresolved cost, yield or ecosystem constraints.
Experimental: research-stage; commercial insertion by 2033 is not assumed.
Market estimates are not always comparable. SEMI reports a $73.2 billion 2025 materials market using its wafer-fab and packaging taxonomy. Commercial databases can quote higher totals by including a wider set of substrates, ancillary consumables or electronic materials. This report uses SEMI as the market baseline and does not average incompatible estimates. All dollar values are nominal U.S. dollars. Supplier forecasts are identified as such.
