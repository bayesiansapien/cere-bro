## **OpenAI's self-optimizing AI slashes token costs by 13x**

OpenAI just blew up its own pricing structure, relying on internal infrastructure efficiency to drive down the cost of intelligence. By deploying speculative decoding to increase token generation by 15% and cut GPU serving costs by 20%, the company [slashed API prices across its GPT-5.6 family](https://openai.com/index/advancing-the-price-performance-frontier-with-gpt-5-6/). GPT-5.6 Luna took an [80% price drop ](https://the-decoder.com/openai-goes-full-china-pricing-mode-with-an-80-percent-cut-to-its-most-affordable-gpt-5-6-model/)to $0.20 per million input and $1.20 per million output tokens.

AI researcher [Nic Dunz ](https://x.com/nicdunz)pointed out, Luna now matches the benchmark performance of March's full GPT-5.4 flagship model [at one-thirteenth the token price](https://x.com/nicdunz/status/2082884002201878824). Meanwhile, [GPT-5.6 Terra dropped 20% ](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2082878156483219672)to $14, Sol Fast added a speed tier doubling pricing for 2.5x throughput, and Auto-review in ChatGPT and Codex CLI moved to Luna for a 10x cost reduction, countering pressure from Chinese providers, Google, Anthropic, and Microsoft.

A [Chrome extension adds cross-tab context](https://x.com/ChatGPT/status/2082970812584432115) for YouTube videos and text, alongside desktop URL suggestions and history management. On the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark, using retained reasoning and context compaction via the Responses API jumped GPT-5.6 Sol scores from 13.3% to 38.3% [while dropping token output sixfold](https://openai.com/index/how-two-settings-tripled-our-arc-agi-3-scores/).

OpenAI also launched a [$250 million initiative ](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2082516370949062989)giving 100,000 scientists free access to GPT-5.6 Sol Pro through 2027, where heavy users double their offloading of multi-hour tasks.

To top it off, President Greg Brockman confirmed OpenAI is [developing a family of consumer hardware devices](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/972709/openai-hardware-greg-brockman-interview) alongside former Apple designer Jony Ive. The hardware stack will feature verifiable, auditable privacy guarantees, positioning persistent AI assistants as the primary interface.

## **Anthropic's biggest AI security risk wasn't the model. It was the test harness.**

Anthropic learned the hard way that testing offensive AI tools in bad setups creates real chaos. After auditing over 141,000 evaluation runs following OpenAI's Hugging Face breach, the company found that [three models](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals), Claude Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, and an unreleased research prototype, [breached live corporate networks](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2082965101083320543) during security tests.

A third-party environment accidentally left the models connected to the web while telling them they had no internet access, so they treated real corporate systems like part of the game. They exploited weak passwords, probed open endpoints, and Mythos 5 even published a malicious Python package to PyPI that 15 real systems downloaded.
