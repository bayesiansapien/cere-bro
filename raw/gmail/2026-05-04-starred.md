Practically every day I see new signs that the backlash against AI is growing.  The above example is just one example of many.  Others like this aren’t hard to find, either:
There’s also the QuitGPT movement:
Fortune was one of the first major media outlets to catch on, last summer [ https://substack.com/redirect/f81c7758-3f9b-4ee6-82f3-bcdba1dc48b2?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]:
But it’s been a long time to coming.
§
Indeed, about year ago I predicted that the backlash against AI would grow so much that it would be a major factor in the 2028 Presidential elections.
That prediction very seems to be on track. Frustration with AI may even factor into the midterms. It’s already splitting both parties.
§
Here’s my theory about what’s going on, slightly edited from a post which has gone viral on X [ https://substack.com/redirect/ae975880-2a66-4adb-8525-6dda35dbd3e4?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ].
Outside of coding (where there is clear value), and a handful of other domains (e.g. brainstorming), Generative AI has been a net negative for society.
GenAI has been undermining secondary and college education, opening up mass surveillance, increasing disinformation, delusions, impersonation, phishing, and other forms of cybercrime, nonconsensual deep fake porn, bias in employment and other domains, and economic disparity, drowning the world in slop and unwanted, over-leveraged environment-damaging data centers that risk causing a recession.
Simultaneously it has empowered a bunch of people who want to privatize almost all the gains while leave all the downsides to society, taking almost zero responsibility. 
I don’t think we are better off than we were four years ago.
Some of this is technical (LLMs aren’t reliable), some of it is political/economic (such as the utter lack of responsible regulation). 
Most of these downsides were predictable.  (I warned of most of it in my 2023 Senate testimony [ https://substack.com/redirect/5621a808-2af4-4c07-a02d-cd86f86b2d0d?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], in fact, and so did many other folks working in AI ethics over the years. But even more striking is that OpenAI did too, even earlier, in 2019 [ https://substack.com/redirect/6c12048e-e8a8-4bc2-b6d4-e2ffdcec4fe3?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ].)
On net, things don’t look good.
§
All that said, I honestly believe some future form of AI might be great. But Generative AI has hurt more than it has helped, and been managed irresponsibly.
It’s no wonder many people have had enough.

## **Anthropic is getting pulled directly into Wall Street**

Anthropic is reportedly close to a [$1.5 billion joint venture](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/anthropic-nears-15-billion-ai-joint-venture-with-wall-street-firms-wsj-reports-2026-05-04/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) with Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, Hellman & Friedman, and other Wall Street firms to sell AI tools to private-equity-backed companies. [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/deals/anthropic-nears-1-5-billion-joint-venture-with-wall-street-firms-8f5448ee) report says the venture would effectively become a consulting and implementation arm for Claude inside portfolio companies.

This is the enterprise AI playbook getting more blunt. Don’t wait for every company to figure out AI internally. Go through the owners. Private equity firms already control thousands of businesses and obsess over margin improvement, headcount leverage, and operational efficiency. If Claude can make portfolio companies cheaper to run, the buyer is obvious.

It also fits the larger Anthropic financial story. The company has reportedly weighed a new funding round at a valuation above [$900 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/anthropic-potential-900b-valuation-round-could-happen-within-two-weeks/), while also exploring new hardware supply through AI chip startups like [Fractile](https://www.techmeme.com/260502/p6). Anthropic is no longer just selling a chatbot. It is becoming infrastructure for high-margin corporate work. [Read more.](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/anthropic-nears-15-billion-ai-joint-venture-with-wall-street-firms-wsj-reports-2026-05-04/)

## **xAI turns Grok into a cheaper, louder, more everywhere model**

xAI launched [Grok 4.3](https://docs.x.ai/developers/models/grok-4.3), a new reasoning model aimed at coding, logic, math, scientific work, and agentic tool use. [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/xai-launches-grok-4-3-with-improved-agentic-performance-and-lower-pricing) says the model improved xAI’s Intelligence Index score while cutting input and output pricing versus Grok 4.20. VentureBeat framed the launch as both a model release and a pricing move, with xAI also pushing a new voice-cloning suite.

The voice side is where the product direction gets more interesting. xAI’s homepage is now promoting [Custom Voices and the Voice Library](https://x.ai/news/grok-custom-voices), while the company’s [Imagine API](https://x.ai/api/imagine) is built around native audio generation for video. The pitch is not just better chat. It is AI that talks, clones voices, generates media, and starts to bleed into consumer interfaces.

That consumer surface is expanding too. 9to5Mac found that [Grok Voice mode is coming to Apple CarPlay](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/02/xai-is-bringing-grok-voice-mode-to-apple-carplay/), turning the car into another AI assistant endpoint. Meanwhile, The Verge reported that Elon Musk acknowledged xAI has used [OpenAI’s models](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/921546/elon-musk-xai-openai-trial-model-distillation) in training Grok, reigniting the model-distillation fight that keeps circling the frontier labs. [Read more.](https://venturebeat.com/technology/xai-launches-grok-4-3-at-an-aggressively-low-price-and-a-new-fast-powerful-voice-cloning-suite/)

## **The Pentagon signs AI deals with the biggest labs**

The U.S. military has signed agreements with seven major AI and tech companies: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/), Google, Microsoft, AWS, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection. [The AP reports](https://apnews.com/article/060cecf836c4cebcf012a3ceb5333f2c) that the deals are designed to bring AI into classified military systems for decision support, logistics, analysis, and other government uses.

This is the national-security AI story becoming explicit. The Guardian reported that the deals are part of a broader push to integrate AI into classified systems, while [Google’s own Pentagon agreement](https://www.reuters.com/technology/google-signs-classified-ai-deal-with-pentagon-information-reports-2026-04-28/) has already triggered internal backlash. The old Google Project Maven fight is back, but the industry is different now. AI labs are bigger. The contracts are larger. The government demand is much more obvious.

Anthropic remains the most visible holdout in this fight. According to AP, Anthropic refused to participate because of concerns around use cases involving surveillance and autonomous systems. That contrast matters. The frontier labs are no longer just competing on benchmarks. They are choosing where their models can operate, who can use them, and what counts as an acceptable customer. [Read more.](https://apnews.com/article/060cecf836c4cebcf012a3ceb5333f2c)

## 6. World Models, Architectures, and the Next Phase of AI

**From:** Ken Huang from Agentic AI  <kenhuangus@substack.com>  
**Date:** Sun, 3 May 2026 15:03:47 +0000  
**Link:** [https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/19dee61fceb26472](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/19dee61fceb26472)

View this post on the web at https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/world-models-architectures-and-the

A debate, not a duel
At the Spring School AI For Impact in March 2026, two of the most consequential thinkers in machine learning sat for what was billed as a debate but functioned more like a forensic disagreement about what intelligence is for (YouTube recording [ https://substack.com/redirect/4121785a-6c0f-4511-9bf3-f78ed538ef38?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). On one side, Yann LeCun, Meta’s former chief AI scientist, argued that any system that models the world by trying to reconstruct pixels is doomed because most of what happens in a video - leaves trembling, light flickering, individual molecules of air - is intrinsically unpredictable, and forcing a network to predict it wastes capacity and corrupts representations (Meta AI on V-JEPA [ https://substack.com/redirect/78a7a616-5358-4f28-b5e6-4905c17d1256?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). On the other side, Eric Xing, president of MBZUAI and longtime CMU professor, argued that abstract latent prediction without a generative validator is essentially meditation in a closed room: elegant, internally consistent, and prone to losing contact with reality (MBZUAI on PAN [ https://substack.com/redirect/cdc7f96b-2042-4311-90b5-b28359dcc2db?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]).
Their architectures crystallize the dispute. LeCun’s Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) trains an encoder to map observations into representations, then trains a predictor to map a representation of the past into a representation of the future, and stops there. There is no decoder, no reconstruction, no insistence that the system explain how the world looks (LeCun’s “A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence”, 2022 [ https://substack.com/redirect/fcda82b5-9e5e-430b-ab83-6c6397124c9c?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). Xing’s Generative Latent Prediction (GLP), which underlies the PAN world model his Institute of Foundation Models built, keeps the latent prediction but reattaches a decoder so the model has to render the next observation as a check on what the latents claim is happening (Xing et al., “Critiques of World Models”, arXiv 2507.05169 [ https://substack.com/redirect/c008ff44-0635-494c-a425-68eeb7c5d27b?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). One is faith in abstraction. The other is insistence that abstraction be validated against the messy world that produced it.
Stripped of its technical detail, this is the oldest fight in the philosophy of science: rationalism versus empiricism, played out in tensors. Should the model represent the world more compactly than the world itself, throwing away anything it cannot use? Or must it be perpetually re-grounded in observable reconstructions, even at vast computational cost? Almost everything else interesting in AI right now - diffusion versus autoregression, transformers versus state-space models, video generators versus simulators, agents versus oracles, the whole question of whether and when AI starts improving itself - connects, sometimes loosely and sometimes directly, to which side of that argument turns out to be right.
This essay walks through that argument. It begins with what a world model actually is, traces the architectural lineage from Sutton and Schmidhuber to Dreamer and Sora, sets the JEPA and GLP positions against the broader landscape of transformers, diffusion, and state-space models, then connects all of it to the rapidly maturing world of agentic AI and recursive self-improvement. The thesis throughout is that neither LeCun nor Xing is fully right, and that the next decade will be defined by hybrid architectures that learn when to trust abstraction and when to demand reconstruction. The harder question is whether we will figure that out before the systems we build are powerful enough to start figuring it out for themselves.
What a world model is, and why it matters
A world model is a learned function that takes a state of the world and an action, and predicts what comes next. That is the technical definition. The cognitive intuition is older: a system with a world model can rehearse possible futures inside its own head, evaluate them, and choose actions without paying the cost of trying everything in reality. Richard Sutton named this idea in his 1990 Dyna architecture, where an agent maintained a learned model of the environment and used it to generate “simulated experience” that supplemented real interaction (Sutton, “Integrated Architectures for Learning, Planning, and Reacting”, 1990 [ https://substack.com/redirect/6dc5ae4a-c5e7-478c-84f9-0c1ad05e2222?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). Jürgen Schmidhuber’s parallel work in 1990 and 1991 made the same proposal for recurrent networks, framing curiosity and planning around models that compress and predict (Schmidhuber’s historical overview [ https://substack.com/redirect/6849d0fe-0f5d-4f50-a011-c6e4ea7b3974?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]).
The 2018 Ha and Schmidhuber paper “World Models” is where the modern era starts. They built an agent for the CarRacing and VizDoom environments using three pieces: a convolutional variational autoencoder that compressed each frame into a 32- or 64-dimensional vector, a mixture density recurrent network that predicted the next latent given an action, and a tiny linear controller with under a thousand parameters (Ha and Schmidhuber, “World Models”, arXiv 1803.10122 [ https://substack.com/redirect/1a453953-4ea7-47dc-b2bd-b98a09440c14?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]; interactive paper [ https://substack.com/redirect/a5e9105b-4e1b-4ab6-992c-02704abb5e0a?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]). The controller was trained with evolutionary search, but the striking result was that the agent could be trained entirely inside its own dream - the recurrent network running as a virtual environment - and then deployed in the real one. The world model achieved 906 ± 21 on CarRacing-v0, clearing the threshold considered to solve the task, and 1092 ± 556 on VizDoom when trained purely in dream and tested in reality.
The reason this matters is that learning by interaction with the real world is dangerous, slow, and expensive. A robot that learns to grasp by trying every possible grasp on a real arm will break either itself or whatever it is grasping. A self-driving car that learns by collision is unacceptable. The promise of world models is that an agent can practice in imagination, and that imagination can be cheaper, faster, and safer than reality. The Moerland et al. survey on model-based reinforcement learning lays out the canonical version of this argument and the canonical risk: a model-based agent can also learn to exploit inaccuracies in its model rather than the true environment, finding policies that look perfect in dream and fail catastrophically in deployment (Moerland et al., 2020 [ https://substack.com/redirect/ccc57f05-9603-448e-9ce6-e70a58618465?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]).
The Dreamer line, developed by Danijar Hafner and collaborators across three papers, is the cleanest demonstration that this works at scale. PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model, which split the latent state into a deterministic GRU branch and a stochastic Gaussian branch, and used it to plan via cross-entropy method search; it achieved fifty times the data efficiency of the model-free D4PG baseline on the DeepMind Control Suite (Hafner et al., “Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels”, ICML 2019 [ https://substack.com/redirect/b32f9d94-95bb-4a0f-8b28-b0405cd838df?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ

This paper introduces Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE), a framework that makes harness evolution observable and falsifiable. AHE separates the system into three layers: components stored as revertible files, experience condensed from millions of trajectory tokens into structured evidence, and decisions written as predictions that get checked against task outcomes. Every edit becomes a contract you can verify or revert. Three-layer evolution model: Components, experience, and decisions are each first-class artifacts. Components are versioned files, experience is compressed evidence pulled from full trajectory logs, and decisions are explicit hypotheses with expected outcomes. Cross-model transfer: The evolved harness transfers across model families with +5.1 to +10.1 point gains, suggesting the optimizations are structural rather than overfit to a specific backbone. That is the property you actually want from harness engineering. Why it matters: Harness work is the largest hidden cost in most agent systems. AHE is the first credible recipe for letting the harness improve itself without drifting into noise, which makes it the most important agent-systems paper of the week. Reasoning flywheel from self-failure: The first loop mines the model's own errors and converts them into harder reasoning problems each round. Agentic World Modeling A massive 40-author survey lands the cleanest taxonomy of world models in agent research released so far. The paper proposes a "levels by laws" framework spanning three capability levels and four law regimes, then synthesizes 400+ works and 100+ representative systems across model-based RL, video generation, web and GUI agents, multi-agent simulation, and scientific discovery. As agents shift from chatbots to goal-accomplishers, the bottleneck moves from language to environment, and this is the first paper that gives builders a shared vocabulary across communities that have been working in isolation. Three capability levels: L1 Predictors handle one-step transitions, L2 Simulators do multi-step action-conditioned rollouts, and L3 Evolvers self-revise as the world changes. The hierarchy makes it easy to place existing systems and identify where capability gaps actually live. Four law regimes: Physical, digital, social, and scientific laws each impose different constraints on what a world model needs to capture. The framework treats them as orthogonal axes, which clarifies why a strong physics simulator can still fail at social or digital tasks. Failure-mode catalog: The survey extracts recurring failure patterns across 100+ systems, including misaligned reward shaping, drift under non-stationarity, and brittle transfer across regimes. Each failure mode is mapped to a level and law combination, so the diagnosis is grounded. RecursiveMAS Multi-agent systems usually pass full text messages between agents at every step, which causes token bloat, latency, and context dilution that all grow with team size. RecursiveMAS asks a different question: what if agents collaborated through recursive computation in a shared latent space instead of through text? The system treats a multi-agent team as a recursive computation where each agent acts like an RLM layer, iteratively passing latent representations to the next and forming a looped interaction process. Less talking, more thinking. RecursiveLink for latent communication: A RecursiveLink module generates latent thoughts and transfers state directly between heterogeneous agents, replacing natural-language messages with internal representations. The change removes the cost of re-encoding and re-parsing text on every coordination step. Inner-outer loop learning: The training algorithm uses an inner loop for per-step latent updates and an outer loop for team-level credit assignment, with shared gradient-based updates across agents. This makes joint optimization tractable instead of relying on hand-tuned communication protocols.
