## **Apple shipped the new Siri. Google built a chunk of the brains.**

Apple [released Siri AI](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/) on Monday, in beta, English only, with French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish due next month. It does personal context across your messages, mail and photos, on-screen awareness, and in-app actions, plus [Visual Intelligence, Writing Tools, Image Playground, Siri Recap and Live Rewind](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/). Server-side operations carry daily usage limits while it is in beta.

The features run on "the next generation of [Apple Foundation Models](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates), custom-built in collaboration with Google and its [Gemini](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/) models." Apple spent two years insisting the personal-context Siri was coming; it arrived leaning on a competitor's model. Supported hardware is iPhone 16 or later plus iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, M1-or-later iPads and Macs, MacBook Neo, Vision Pro, and Apple Watch Series 9 or later. It [will not ship initially in the EU](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/) on iOS, iPadOS and watchOS, and will not ship in China while Apple works through regulatory requirements.

The same week Siri landed, X Corp and xAI [dropped Apple from the antitrust suit](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/14/elon-musks-x-corp-and-spacexai-drop-antitrust-lawsuit-against-apple) they filed in August 2025 over ChatGPT's exclusive iPhone integration. They are still suing OpenAI. No reason given, no settlement disclosed. Make of that what you will now that Siri's default brain is not exclusively OpenAI's.

## **Jensen Huang breaks the “slowdown” consensus**

At Salesforce's [Dreamforce](https://www.salesforce.com/dreamforce/) on Tuesday, [Jensen Huang](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/jensen-huang-dreamforce/) said the quiet part into a microphone: ["Safety is an engineering problem, not a legal one. We don't need any new laws. We don't need new regulations."](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/we-dont-need-ai-regulation-leave-safety-to-us-nvidias-jensen-huang-says/) His proposed mechanism is product liability plus self-restraint: "if you build a product or a service, and you're not confident in its safety, then don't release it." He called the tradeoff between speed and safety "a false choice."

That is three days after [Amodei's essay](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) and two days after Altman, Musk and Nadella all signed on. It is also the opposite direction from where OpenAI spent Tuesday: policy chief [Chris Lehane told reporters in Washington](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/openai-anthropic-google-have-been-in-talks-on-ai-safety-for-weeks/) that OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind have been coordinating on safety for several weeks, that they do not need a government antitrust waiver to do it, and that OpenAI backs the FRONTIER Act provision requiring frontier labs to admit independent verification organizations. Amodei asked Washington for that waiver by name. OpenAI says it is unnecessary.

Huang was on that stage to sell something, for the record. Salesforce used the slot to launch [Koa](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/09/15/koa-reasoning-model/), its first CRM reasoning model, post-trained from NVIDIA's open [Nemotron 3 Super](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/jensen-huang-dreamforce/) on synthetic scenarios across 14-plus industries with no customer data. Salesforce claims Koa "matches or exceeds leading model performance on CRM actions with three times fewer errors" on its own internal benchmark, keeps the weights, and runs inference inside its own trust boundary. Pilots are running now with Formula 1, UChicago Medicine, Xero and 1-800Accountant, and general availability is slated for winter 2026 in US regions.
