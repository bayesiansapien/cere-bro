## 1. The new Sanders-Casar Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act – and why I oppose it

**From:** Gary Marcus from Marcus on AI <garymarcus@substack.com>  
**Date:** Thu, 3 Sep 2026 19:09:44 +0000  
**Link:** [https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a068ae4235d591e](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/1a068ae4235d591e)

View this post on the web at https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/the-new-sanders-casar-ban-artificial

Breaking news, and why I oppose it.
Sanders’ own tweet focuses on a defensible subset of the bill:
I am OK with the tweet, and I can see arguments (both pro and con) for a pause.
But the proposal itself goes too far. As I just wrote on X, 
We may need a temporary pause, maybe even one that lasts for a number of years, until such time as we have much better ideas about regulation and alignment.
And we certainly need an AI agency—as I myself told the U.S. Senate in 2023—but a permanent, unilateral ban on all research into superhuman AI is too broad, a guarantee of leaving the US behind, and not the right approach. 
It’s also naive about the complexities in benchmarking, and would unfortunately leave room for authoritarians to play games with the rules.
Furthermore, it focuses too much on hypothetical future risks (such as AI that “plan[s] ... to disempower humanity) to the exclusion of current risks, as Dr. Angela Rasmussen  writes:
§
We should all want to get to a good AI future, not a backward AI future. But this is not the way.
I salute the Sanders-Casar effort to get something down on paper for discussion, but let’s figure out a better approach. 
(In my postscript below I give one hint; my book Taming Silicon Valley gives many more. An upcoming article with Cal Newport will give still more.)
P.S. On the pausing point—separate from regulating other aspects of the more immediate risks of AI—I like the language here (boldface added), from Anthony Aguirre [ https://substack.com/redirect/2d892c26-c3be-4a7b-ab1d-7226cd495c9f?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. Perhaps Sanders and Casar might borrow it in a revised proposal:
“Development of narrowly-scoped AI tools, and those that can be reasonably expected to be significantly below the current frontier in software development, cyber, and autonomous capabilities, may continue. Systems of greater combined intelligence, generality, and autonomy may be developed only if and when their developers can demonstrate with high assurance to a capable independent authority that sufficient alignment, control, and oversight exist.”
Rather than calling for permanent ban that could be undone only by an act of Congress, this framing sets an important high bar – and hence an incentive — for the companies to meet. That’s a good model for a better, less overbearing approach that stills leaves room for future discovery, while encouraging critical research.

Today's Paper Review - LeWorldModel

Yann LeCun has long argued that intelligent AI systems need a world model: an internal representation of how the world behaves that allows an agent to predict the consequences of its actions.

His proposed architecture for learning such representations is JEPA, or Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. Instead of predicting future pixels, JEPA predicts abstract representations of the future.

The challenge? Training JEPA world models can lead to model collapse, where the model learns constant representations instead of meaningful ones.

LeCun's new LeWorldModel tackles this challenge with a simple end-to-end training approach. The work is particularly interesting given that LeCun's AMI Labs is focused on developing world models based on this research direction.

Why It Matters

World models let an agent imagine the consequences of its actions before taking them, a foundational building block for planning and robotics. A stable JEPA world model could offer an alternative to approaches that rely on large pretrained visual encoders.

LeWorldModel High-Level Architecture

( https://6da61843.click.kit-mail3.com/68umpo566dt8h53mo42tohpz5n4x4a9hp0k2m/9qhzhnhd54392gh9hn/aHR0cHM6Ly9haXBhcGVyc2FjYWRlbXkuY29tL2xld29ybGRtb2RlbC8= )LeWorldModel High-Level Architecture & Training
LeWorldModel learns to predict future observations in latent space:

Encoder: A Vision Transformer processes each image and uses its class token as a compact semantic representation of the observation.

Predictor: A Transformer that takes the last few observation representations plus the actions taken between them and predicts the representation of what comes next.

Prediction Loss: The predicted representation is compared with the encoded representation of the actual next observation using an MSE loss.

SIGReg: The key to stability. This regularizer projects embeddings onto many random directions and pushes each resulting distribution toward a Gaussian, keeping the representation space well-spread instead of letting it collapse.

Latent Planning

( https://6da61843.click.kit-mail3.com/68umpo566dt8h53mo42tohpz5n4x4a9hp0k2m/9qhzhnhd54392gh9hn/aHR0cHM6Ly9haXBhcGVyc2FjYWRlbXkuY29tL2xld29ybGRtb2RlbC8= )Latent Planning with LeWorldModel
Once trained, the model can plan directly in latent space, given an initial and a goal observation.

It samples possible action sequences, uses the predictor to imagine where each sequence would lead, , and repeatedly updates the actions distribution toward sequences whose imagined outcome lands closest to the goal.

Results Highlights

( https://6da61843.click.kit-mail3.com/68umpo566dt8h53mo42tohpz5n4x4a9hp0k2m/9qhzhnhd54392gh9hn/aHR0cHM6Ly9haXBhcGVyc2FjYWRlbXkuY29tL2xld29ybGRtb2RlbC8= )Planning Performance Comparison
LeWorldModel outperforms the baselines on Push-T and Reacher, including, on Push-T, a version of DINO-WM with access to the robot's position, which LeWorldModel doesn't have.

On OGBench-Cube, DINO-WM performs better, which the authors attribute to the environment's higher visual complexity, where DINO-WM's large pretrained encoder may have an edge.

On Two-Room, LeWorldModel also falls behind, with the authors hypothesizing that the low-dimensional nature of the environment may make the SIGReg regularization less beneficial.

Despite this, LeWorldModel is much smaller than the baselines and achieves a roughly 48× planning speedup.
