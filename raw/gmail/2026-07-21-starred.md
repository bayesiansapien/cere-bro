## 9. Kimi K3: The open-weights escalation

**From:** Interconnects by Nathan Lambert <robotic@substack.com>  
**Date:** Mon, 20 Jul 2026 15:48:28 +0000  
**Link:** [https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/19f803847ecf9839](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/19f803847ecf9839)

View this post on the web at https://www.interconnects.ai/p/kimi-k3-the-open-weights-escalation

On Thursday July 16th, Moonshot AI released their latest flagship model Kimi K3 [ https://substack.com/redirect/dca45a01-1a85-4fa6-b691-0c5df634be0c?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. K3 is a 2.8T parameter MoE model which will have its weights released on July 27th. Much of this article follows as a reflection on the state of the ecosystem, under the assumption that Moonshot keeps their promise of the weights release date. This is a more extreme view of the equilibrium, and many of the results end up in a middle ground if the state of affairs is that China has similarly powerful, but closed models (i.e. K3 is never released). 
The key fact is that either the open-to-closed or American-to-Chinese model performance gap has been reduced from the debated 6-9 months to something shorter, say 3-5 months.
From the release materials, it is clear that K3 is a true frontier model. It will be the closest open models have been to the frontier since DeepSeek R1 [ https://substack.com/redirect/130216d8-5275-49b8-8fa3-35d5c12e9f70?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. DeepSeek R1 was a different story. This was a Chinese lab being extremely quick to pivot to reasoning models and release one faster than many American companies. Kimi K3 an example of a Chinese lab executing on scaling the known areas: data, algorithms, architecture, tools, environments, etc.  
Kimi K3 comes in at #2 overall on the Vals AI index [ https://substack.com/redirect/a5498300-7973-4558-945d-8db0bc05d0b5?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], #3 overall on Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index [ https://substack.com/redirect/2ab4d379-9769-4fb3-b5c0-a2ff46f27da7?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] (only beaten by Claude Fable and GPT-5.6 Sol Max while being cheaper [ https://substack.com/redirect/23d6280f-378b-4f1d-94fb-0eb803f7f765?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]), #1 overall in Frontend Code Arena [ https://substack.com/redirect/2f4e5ee6-7c50-4cda-b405-190b541f2917?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], and more impressive results [ https://substack.com/redirect/e179307e-3740-45d9-9b93-3a04b97c6458?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ].  Moonshot AI is going toe to toe with Anthropic and OpenAI with far, far fewer resources.
It is clearly the strongest open model ever released. It should be clear looking at this model that if adversarial distillation from the closed frontier models in the U.S. contributed, it is at most to a relatively small degree. AI observers who followed the distillation panic [ https://substack.com/redirect/410015ee-82a7-411f-9385-8a1fe7532d0c?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] and came away with the wrong conclusion that Chinese AI labs are only producing good models due to IP theft are in for an awakening – that Chinese companies are extremely good at building models in the same way the leading American companies are. Moonshot AI is solving many of the same problems that folks at OpenAI or Anthropic are solving. I’m confident there will be more distillation discussion, and pressure [ https://substack.com/redirect/410015ee-82a7-411f-9385-8a1fe7532d0c?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ], but the evidence is now out that Chinese companies can do more than just fast following [ https://substack.com/redirect/a9040d1e-34eb-4227-a258-8f76515a5240?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ].

1. China’s recommits to open-source AI – showing a different read on near-term risks
Many people started following China’s AI scene relatively recently, so they can reach the conclusion that releasing models openly is their core strategy. In fact, I think most labs have a core strategy far closer to Anthropic or OpenAI – build the best intelligence possible. Having followed and engaged with the Chinese labs for years now, the best explanation for their original turn to releasing their models openly is practicality. They needed to release the models openly to get adoption, attention, and feedback (especially in the high-value, Bay Area market).
For a long time, there had been very limited policy in China [ https://substack.com/redirect/f889ff07-0575-4a5a-8d7d-99f61ba24901?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ] explaining the role of open-source AI, and what could be the “country-level strategy.” To my knowledge, no senior leaders had commented on open-source AI publicly. This changed this week too, as Xi Jinping gave a keynote address at the World AI Conference (WAIC), and very directly committed the future of China’s AI ecosystem to open-source and global diffusion [ https://substack.com/redirect/8191a48c-506b-4876-a4a0-d2722ea05a2d?j=eyJ1IjoiOGs4ZDAifQ.VI2uzD6zEHQ-HoC2J2iX6FwxM11lZ3HP_Zidnio1pMQ ]. This commitment to the status quo, the same week as the announcement of the strongest open-weight model to date, is a clear mark in the early history of modern AI.
This comes during a time period where many potential paths forward have been discussed for the Chinese AI industry – Will they stay open? Can they keep up with the American labs in scaling? Is there a growing revenue market in China? With these, the focus has been on China’s risk tolerance, the companies’ ability to monetize, and any closely related reason for a company to stop releasing their best models openly.
