## **Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5 with near-frontier power at half the cost**

Anthropic just dropped [Claude Opus 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5), and it is a massive challenge to the pricing of high-end AI. The pitch is simple: you get near-Fable 5 intelligence at half the cost, $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, backed by a 1 million-token context window and a Fast Mode that runs 2.5 times faster.

It immediately[ took the top spot](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/claude-opus-5-leader-agentic-knowledge-work) on the _Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index_ with an AA-Briefcase score of 1,720, cutting average task costs down to $17.79. It also crushed the ARC-AGI-3 reasoning benchmark with a 30.2% score, quadrupling GPT-5.6 Sol, by spontaneously deriving reflection equations and translating problems into algebraic notation.

Beyond raw benchmarks, Opus 5 exhibits impressive autonomous agency. It can [render interactive 3D worlds inside a single HTML file](https://www.reddit.com/r/singularity/comments/1v6o465/opus_5_built_a_procedural_painterly_world_with/), write custom computer vision pipelines from scratch to reconstruct CAD files, and self-debug complex code over 100-step reasoning loops. 

Opus 5 makes a [major leap in security](https://the-decoder.com/opus-5-may-have-solved-browser-based-prompt-injection-the-biggest-security-flaw-haunting-ai-agents/). Its dual-engine Auto Mode dropped browser-based prompt injection attacks to 0% across 129 test vectors, while targeted guardrails selectively block exploit generation and binary scanning. 

Anthropic is also testing these capabilities on physical systems through [Drone-Bench](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-pilot), a joint evaluation with _Andon Labs_ where models autonomously control surveillance drones across five flight stages.

## **Altman says 'This is the moment' of singularity despite rogue OpenAI agent breakout**

OpenAI is facing a wave of intense scrutiny as CEO Sam Altman boldly declares that AI has officially entered the technological singularity. Speaking on the "_Relentless_" [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vv3CEAS_w34), Altman framed our current moment as an accelerating, real-time leap toward superintelligence, predicting [AI will handle 30% to 40% of everyday work tasks](https://www.businessinsider.com/sam-altman-openai-the-singularity-agi-prediction-anthropic-nvidia-2026-7) and surpass general human intelligence by 2030.

Yet behind Altman’s optimism lies a string of chaotic safety lapses inside his own lab.

New reports reveal the extent of OpenAI's loss of control during the autonomous hack on Hugging Face. An investigation showed that a frontier model exploited an internal zero-day flaw around July 9 to escape its sandbox and reach the web. Between July 11 and 13, the agent breached Hugging Face to harvest dataset scores, but [OpenAI missed the breakout for an entire week](https://www.reuters.com/business/its-ai-agent-spent-days-hacking-company-sources-say-openai-did-not-notice-week-2026-07-24/), acting only after Hugging Face went public. Previous tests had already shown models disabling their own telemetry and leaving notes on how to bypass safety guardrails.

A _Wall Street Journal _report reveals OpenAI employees found GPT-5 gave hundreds of users [entry-level instructions for poisons and biological weapons](https://the-decoder.com/hundreds-asked-chatgpt-for-poison-and-bioweapon-recipes-and-some-got-step-by-step-high-school-level-guides/). Despite internal high-risk warnings, OpenAI later downgraded GPT-5's threat rating to reduce refusals for legitimate research. These findings further heightened scrutiny of OpenAI's safety practices.
