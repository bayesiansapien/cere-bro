# AI Bubble Deflating? Dell, Workday on Deck

<p>Friday’s tech stock bloodbath prompted <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-21/wall-street-asks-what-can-save-ai-trade-after-anthropic-wipeout?srnd=homepage-americas">much gnashing of teeth</a> over the weekend. With stocks most exposed to AI—such as Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, neoclouds such as CoreWeave, Nebius—falling in the 12-16% range, and Anthropic tanking on its second day of trading, it felt like the air has finally gone out of the AI bubble. Indeed, worries about AI financing are so intense that Oracle <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/oracle-says-completed-funding-fulfil-openai-deal?rc=9byxri">issued a statement</a> late on Friday proclaiming that it had raised all the money it needed for its big OpenAI data center deal and any report that said otherwise was wrong—suggesting it’s a little sensitive about things.</p><p>Meanwhile, The Information captured the craziness of the AI bubble with <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/softbanks-masayoshi-son-explored-1-trillion-moonshot-buyout-sk-hynix?rc=9byxri">a report late Friday</a> that SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son had explored doing a<i> trillion dollar</i> buyout of SK Hynix. We should emphasize ‘explored’ in past tense—SoftBank said the idea was no longer being pursued. That’s good because the idea is sheer fantasy. SoftBank’s balance sheet is already stretched, thanks to the loans it has taken out to invest in OpenAI. How would Son imagine he could raise the hundreds of billions needed to buy Hynix (leave aside the fact that the South Korean government would be unlikely to ever allow such a deal). What brand of crack is he smoking?</p>
