# Stop Scoring Agents Zero at 90% Done

<h2>1. The agent that did almost everything&#8212;and scored zero</h2><p>Picture a coding agent that spends fifteen hours on a multi-day engineering task. It lands the core feature, keeps the regression suite green, and matches the requirements checklist. On the last visual polish pass it misses a layout edge case. Under a binary pass/fail rubric, that run is a zero. Your leaderboard says the agent failed. Your engineers know it was ninety percent done.</p><p>That gap is the evaluation problem for serious agent work. Pass/fail is still the right primary metric when you need a hard gate. It is a terrible hill-climbing signal when the work is long-horizon, multi-system, or multi-agent. You cannot improve what your score collapses to a coin flip.</p><p>This piece is an evergreen practice note, not a breaking-paper digest. The last ~20 hours were thin on brand-new primary papers. What <em>was</em> active&#8212;fresh discussion of Android Bench 2.0 after an October 6 agent-result refresh, an October 8 update to Era&#8217;s simulation blog, and October 10 discourse around typed decision models for RAG and eval&#8212;illustrates a triad worth locking into your eval stack. Near-window, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04672">MASBench</a> (arXiv, published October 3) shows the same philosophy for multi-agent collaboration under partial observability.</p><p>The idea is simple: <strong>score progress continuously &#183; grade against exact keys in simulated estates &#183; gate and judge with typed decisions.</strong> Treat Android Bench, Era, and Jev-style decision models as <em>design patterns</em>, not product endorsements. This is about making evals climbable&#8212;not yesterday&#8217;s integrity-and-attack story.</p><h2>2. Continuous rubrics for long-horizon work</h2><p><a href="https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/android-bench-2-0-pushing-the-frontier-with-challenging-long-horizon-tasks">Android Bench 2.0</a> (blog dated September 17, 2026) is the clearest public illustration of the continuous-rubric pattern. It adds thirty long-horizon tasks&#8212;multi-day engineering work&#8212;evaluated across agent harnesses such as Claude Code, Codex, and Antigravity, with five runs per task. Alongside binary <strong>pass rate</strong>, it reports a continuous <strong>completion rate</strong> in [0.0, 1.0].</p><p>The <a href="https://developer.android.com/bench/methodology/2">methodology</a> (last updated 2026-09-17) is worth stealing even if you never touch Android. Completion is a weighted base across functional, regression, requirements, and visual dimensions, then multiplied by penalty terms. Pass/fail remains the headline; completion is the hill-climbing supplement. That division of labor matters. Binary is not obsolete. Binary <em>alone</em> is what erases ninety-percent progress.</p><p>The numbers make the design choice concrete. At launch, the blog framed the highest long-horizon pass rate as around <strong>28%</strong>, against <strong>~91%</strong> on the original (saturated) task set. As of an October 10 check of the <a href="https://developer.android.com/bench">live leaderboard</a>, the long-horizon top entry is Claude Opus 5.5 on claude-code at <strong>32.7% pass</strong> (CI 18.7&#8211;47.3) and <strong>84.7% completion</strong>, with average latency 15.2 hours and average cost $215.4. GPT 6 Astra on Codex sits at <strong>28.0% pass</strong> and <strong>82.2% completion</strong>. On the 1.0 tab, Claude Opus 5 still scores <strong>91.8%</strong>. The <a href="https://developer.android.com/bench/changelog">changelog</a> lists an October 6, 2026 agent-result refresh&#8212;including Opus 5.5 on claude-code and GPT 6 Luna / Sol on Codex&#8212;so treat &#8220;who leads today&#8221; as perishable and the methodology as the durable takeaway.</p><p>Optional depth that transfers well: pixel-diff visual checks were noisy (methodology notes differences up to 47.5% in calibration). Android Bench replaces that with a multimodal visual judge (Gemini 3.5 Flash, scoring 0.0&#8211;1.0), which calibrated to Diff = 0.00 across 360 runs. If your agent produces UI, screenshots of dashboards, or clinical form layouts, brittle pixel equality will lie to you the same way.</p><p><strong>Transfer.</strong> Finance ops agents that almost close a multi-system reconciliation, and clinical workflow agents that complete most of a charting sequence under constraint, need the same shape: multi-dimensional partial credit plus explicit penalty multipliers for broken invariants. Do not wait for a public Android-style leaderboard in your vertical. Build the rubric first.</p><h2>3. Exact-key simulations as golden datasets</h2><p>Continuous scores answer <em>how much</em> progress you got. They do not answer <em>whether the answer is right</em> when the world is a mess of CRMs, tickets, and chat threads you cannot put in production evals.</p>
