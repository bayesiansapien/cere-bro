# Springer Publishes Humanoid Robots and Physical AI Book

<p>Springer has published <em>Humanoid Robots and Physical AI: Reshaping Work, Society, and Human Purpose</em>. The live book is at <a href="https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-032-33669-9">DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-33669-9</a>. The volume runs XXVII + 381 pages across ten chapters, with 90 color illustrations, in the series <em>Advances in Data Analytics, AI, and Smart Systems</em>.</p><p>I announced the project earlier in my previous Substack article, <a href="/p/announcing-humanoid-robots-and-physical">Announcing Humanoid Robots and Physical AI Book by Springer</a>. That post covered the whole-system thesis and the preorder path. The published volume answers that question across hardware, perception, agentic architectures, control, safety and security, economics, governance, psychology, and long-horizon technology convergence. It does not treat humanoid robotics as a string of demos. It treats physical AI as an end-to-end system that must produce useful work under real constraints.</p><h2>Get the book</h2><p>Readers can open the official Springer page now:</p><ol><li><p>Go to <a href="https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-032-33669-9">Humanoid Robots and Physical AI on Springer</a>.</p></li><li><p>Choose the format your library or team needs (eBook or hardcover).</p></li><li><p>If you buy for a university or enterprise, ask your librarian about an institutional eBook license through the same Springer page.</p></li></ol><p>My earlier Springer volume, <a href="https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-90026-6">Agentic AI: Theories and Practices</a>, documented autonomous intelligence in digital systems. This new volume follows that intelligence into embodiment, contact dynamics, safety, unit economics, and human coexistence.</p><h2>Editors</h2><p>I served as Editor-in-Chief. I did not build this book alone.</p><p>Professor <strong>Chunxiao Xing</strong> of Tsinghua University joined as co-editor. He strengthened scholarly rigor across the manuscript and connected the volume to long-running research in AI, data systems, and interdisciplinary engineering.</p><p><strong>Yuyan "Lynn" Duan</strong> joined as co-editor. She brought product judgment, investor discipline, and deployment realism from AI and robotics practice, including community building through AI+.</p><p>The three of us share credit for the published structure: ten chapters that move from foundations and hardware through agentic architectures, control, safety, economics, governance, psychology, and future convergence.</p><h2>Foreword writers</h2><p>Two leaders framed the volume before Chapter 1.</p><p><strong>Vijay Bolina</strong>, former CISO of Google DeepMind and former interim CISO of Isomorphic Labs, wrote a foreword that places security at the point where models meet matter. Physical AI fails differently from software agents because a wrong decision can move mass, apply force, and harm people or facilities.</p><p><strong>Matthew R. Versaggi</strong>, White House Presidential Innovation Fellow, Distinguished Engineer, and senior leader in AI healthcare, wrote a foreword that connects this volume to <em>Agentic AI: Theories and Practices</em>. He argues that readers must judge humanoid systems through evidence, not spectacle.</p><h2>Chapter authors</h2><p>The chapter authors supplied expertise no editor can invent. Springer lists them on the live table of contents at the DOI page above. I thank each of them by name:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Grace Huang</strong> wrote across foundations, hardware, perception, agentic architectures, and control. She connected finance, product strategy, enterprise risk, and adoption criteria to the engineering chapters.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bo Tian</strong> contributed hardware and perception material with board-level architecture and AI inferencing experience from Tesla Optimus work.</p></li><li><p><strong>Manish Bhatt</strong> grounded perception, agentic architecture, and control in AI security practice, including CyberSecEval, OWASP AISVS, and vulnerability-scoring work.</p></li><li><p><strong>Tongzhou Mu</strong> contributed research depth on world models, robot simulation, and ManiSkill in the agentic architectures chapter.</p></li><li><p><strong>Chuer Pan</strong> brought Stanford research on contact-rich manipulation learned from human demonstrations into the integration and control chapter.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bhavya Gupta</strong> strengthened safety, security, reliability, and governance analysis through information security, risk, and OWASP Agentic AI community work.</p></li><li><p><strong>V&#237;ctor Mayoral-Vilches</strong> contributed specialized robot cybersecurity and teardown expertise to the safety and security chapter.</p></li><li><p><strong>Jerry Huang</strong> wrote across safety, economics, governance, psychology, and long-horizon convergence. He connected machine learning, security, scalable systems, investment, and human-system questions into one through-line.</p></li><li><p><strong>Modar Alaoui</strong>, founder of ALM Ventures, the Humanoids Summit, and the Bay Area Robotics Association, contributed the commercial ecosystem view in the psychology and coexistence chapter.</p></li></ul><p>Together these authors turned a fast-moving field into a coherent Springer volume with named platforms, deployment evidence, cost logic, and governance stakes.</p><h2>Reviewers and production</h2><p>Reviewers forced precision. Anonymous reviewers from leading humanoid and robotics companies corrected technical details with frontline experience. <strong>Jasmine Lombardi</strong> contributed more than twenty-five years of leadership across customer success, professional services, software development, global implementation, and robotics operations. <strong>Nikhil Abraham</strong>, co-founder and CEO of CloudChef and former co-founder of Rephrase AI, tested arguments against autonomous-robot deployment in commercial kitchens. Vijay Bolina and Matthew R. Versaggi also reviewed the manuscript.</p><p>The Springer team turned manuscripts into a published book. I thank <strong>Jialin Yan, Sneha Arunagiri, Brian Halm, and Ruth Milewski</strong> for editorial guidance, production coordination, and attention to detail across a subject that changes every quarter.</p><p>I also thank the families, partners, and friends who gave every contributor the uninterrupted time that serious writing requires. A multi-author technical volume does not finish without that support.</p><h2>What the published book covers</h2><p>The live Springer table of contents lists ten chapters:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Foundations and Vision</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Grace Huang, Yuyan Duan, Chunxiao Xing</p></li><li><p><strong>Hardware and Embodiment Technologies</strong> &#8212; Grace Huang, Ken Huang, Bo Tian, Yuyan Duan</p></li><li><p><strong>Perception and Interaction Systems</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Grace Huang, Bo Tian, Manish Bhatt</p></li><li><p><strong>Agentic AI Architectures and Models for Humanoid Robots</strong> &#8212; Grace Huang, Ken Huang, Tongzhou Mu, Manish Bhatt</p></li><li><p><strong>Integration and Control Systems</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Grace Huang, Chuer Pan, Manish Bhatt</p></li><li><p><strong>Safety, Security and Reliability</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Jerry Huang, Bhavya Gupta, V&#237;ctor Mayoral-Vilches</p></li><li><p><strong>Economic Transformation and Labor Markets</strong> &#8212; Jerry Huang, Ken Huang</p></li><li><p><strong>Governance, Ethics, and Philosophy</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Jerry Huang, Bhavya Gupta</p></li><li><p><strong>Psychology and Human-Robot Coexistence</strong> &#8212; Jerry Huang, Ken Huang, Modar Alaoui</p></li><li><p><strong>Technology Convergence and the Future of Human Purpose</strong> &#8212; Ken Huang, Jerry Huang</p></li></ol><p>That sequence is deliberate. Hardware and perception come before agency. Control and safety come before economics. Governance and psychology come before long-horizon forecasts. Readers who skip to market size without the adoption gates will misprice the work.</p><h2>References</h2><ol><li><p><strong>Huang, K., Xing, C., &amp; Duan, Y. (Eds.).
