(Image: Nils Huenerfuerst/Unsplash)</em></figcaption></figure></div><p><strong>A &#8220;US-China AI Dialogue&#8221; and &#8220;communication channel for AI incidents&#8221; are established. </strong>In announcements following the Trump-Xi Summit, <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-advances-a-fair-and-reciprocal-relationship-with-china-while-hosting-historic-state-visit/">both</a> <a href="https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/xw/zyxw/202609/t20260926_12031663.html">countries</a> said they had established a &#8220;dialogue to exchange views on risks and benefits&#8221; of AI. The next exchange is expected to take place around November. The two countries also said they had established a &#8220;bilateral communication channel&#8221; for incidents related to the technology. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/trump-china-xi-jinping-state-visit-dinner-tariffs-ai/">likened</a> this dialogue to &#8220;the red phone between the Kremlin and the White House during the Cold War.&#8221;</p><p>Neither country&#8217;s announcement mentioned export controls on AI chips or Chinese companies&#8217; use of a technique called distillation to replicate American AI models&#8217; capabilities. These are considered two of the most contentious AI-related issues between China and the US.</p><p><strong>There were no US-China agreements about slowing down AI development. </strong>In a speech at the UN General Assembly, shortly before the Trump-Xi Summit, Trump <a href="https://gadebate.un.org/en/81/united-states-america">said</a> that the US &#8220;totally rejects any attempt to construct a globalist scheme to control&#8221; AI. Then, on the first day of his talks with Xi, Trump again rejected any interference with AI, <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117325763597478758">posting</a> on Truth Social that &#8220;I want to leave it exactly where it is. That is China&#8217;s position also.&#8221; This may have been a rebuke of AI CEOs who have recently advocated for &#8220;pacing&#8221; development. Sam Altman and Dario Amodei <a href="https://openai.com/index/sam-altman-un-security-council-remarks/">both</a> <a href="https://transcripts.un.org/en/sc/10228">addressed</a> the UN Security Council during the General Assembly, repeating their calls for international cooperation on AI risks.</p><p><strong>China signaled openness to cooperation and criticized an &#8220;us-versus-them&#8221; narrative. </strong>Xi Jinping <a href="https://thediplomat.com/2026/09/xi-jinping-is-coming-to-the-us-why-is-he-skipping-the-unga/">did not attend</a> the UN General Assembly. However, Fu Cong, Permanent Representative of China to the UN, <a href="https://press.un.org/en/sc/16462.doc.htm">advocated</a> for enhanced cooperation and the promotion of open-source technologies. He also criticized what he described as a &#8220;banding together into a petty us-versus-them clique in the tech sector&#8221; and &#8220;certain major Powers&#8217; attempt to maintain their technological edge through monopoly in their quest for digital hegemony.&#8221; This remark may have been targeting the US government, but it could also have been aimed at US AI companies, such as Anthropic, which has said that any slowdown must be implemented in a way that maintains America&#8217;s lead over China.</p><p><strong>Many countries are increasingly concerned about loss of control of AI. </strong>During the UN General Assembly, an <a href="https://www.presidentti.fi/en/a-call-for-control-of-frontier-ai-models/">open letter</a> signed by more than 20 countries stated that AI must remain under human control, and called on UN member states to &#8220;explore creating an international institution&#8221; to govern the technology. Also during the meeting, the Prime Minister of Australia, Anthony Albanese, <a href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-24/ai-agent-accessed-australian-government-site-pm-says/107189078">announced</a> that a rogue OpenAI agent had hacked into an Australian government agency in June this year. This is the first known case of a rogue AI attacking a government agency. Yet, while international alarm is growing, meaningful cooperation on AI will hinge on agreements between the US and China.</p><h1>Anthropic and OpenAI Were Considering Mutual Auditing Earlier This Year</h1><p>The Information has <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/openai-anthropic-neared-deal-stress-test-others-ai">reported</a> that Anthropic and OpenAI nearly reached an agreement earlier this year to stress-test each other&#8217;s models for potential risks. However, the deal was apparently never implemented. Elon Musk recently <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/elon-musk-ai-safety-testing.html">suggested</a> a similar arrangement for leading AI developers, including both Chinese and US companies.</p><p><strong>Recent AI incidents show that internal models, not just public ones, pose risks. </strong>Last year, Anthropic and OpenAI ran a <a href="https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/openai-findings/">mutual</a> <a href="https://openai.com/index/openai-anthropic-safety-evaluation/">testing</a> exercise, in which each company allowed the other to evaluate its public models for alignment. The deal that they discussed earlier this year would also have focused on public models. However, this summer&#8217;s rogue AI incidents primarily involved internal, non-public models that were supposed to have been isolated from the real world but managed to access the internet. Safety testing models prior to their public release is therefore an inadequate strategy; companies must improve safety practices throughout the development and pre-release evaluation process.</p><p><strong>Amodei and Altman have said they will embed third-party evaluators in their companies. </strong>Amodei has <a href="https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier">committed</a> to embedding external evaluators with &#8220;employee-like access&#8221; within Anthropic, to provide a second opinion on safety without conflicting commercial incentives. Sam Altman <a href="https://x.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910">said</a> OpenAI would do the same. Anthropic has since <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation">announced</a> it will embed evaluators from consulting firm Accenture. However, this has raised eyebrows, with questions around whether Accenture has the <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/anthropics-first-embedded-evaluator-is-accenture/">necessary expertise</a> in frontier AI research and whether it is truly independent of Anthropic, given the companies&#8217; previous <a href="https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-and-anthropic-launch-multi-year-partnership-to-drive-enterprise-ai-innovation-and-value-across-industries">commercial partnership</a>.</p><h1>New Benchmark Tests AIs&#8217; Cheating Tendencies</h1><p>CAIS has developed a new benchmark, called <a href="https://www.cheatbench.ai/">CheatBench</a>, which measures how likely AI models are to cheat on various tasks.</p><p><strong>AI models sometimes cheat by taking unintended routes to completing tasks. </strong>AI developers train models by rewarding them with higher scores for completing various tasks. But models sometimes take unintended actions to improve their scores, such as stealing the answers or manipulating the system that&#8217;s grading them, instead of solving challenges legitimately. CheatBench measures how frequently models take these kinds of shortcuts when given difficult challenges and opportunities to cheat. Spark 1.3 perform best on CheatBench, with cheating rates of 11.2% and 39.0% respectively. Meanwhile, Google&#8217;s Gemini 3.8 Flash and xAI&#8217;s Grok 4.7 perform worst, with cheating rates of 75.2% and 78.0% respectively. However, cheating rates still vary considerably across domains, even for the better-performing models. For example, GPT-6 Astra cheated 100% of the time on tasks related to menial computation, but 0% on software engineering.</p><p><strong>Cheating behavior is relevant to alignment and safety.</strong> The concern with cheating is not just that AI models will appear to be better at certain tasks than they actually are. Cheating presents a serious risk, because agents might cheat in unexpected ways that have real-world consequences. The July attack on Hugging Face by OpenAI agents <a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-80-ai-is-assisting-cyberattacks">demonstrates</a> this. The agents were undergoing internal testing on difficult cyber challenges and were being assessed on their performance by an automated evaluation system. They hacked into Hugging Face because they believed the company&#8217;s databases contained information that would help them to trick the evaluation system and ensure that they passed the cyber
