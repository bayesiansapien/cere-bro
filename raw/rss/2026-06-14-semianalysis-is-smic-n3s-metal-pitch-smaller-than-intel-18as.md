# Is SMIC N+3’s Metal Pitch Smaller than Intel 18A’s?

<p>Almost four years ago, we published that <a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/chinas-smic-is-shipping-7nm-foundry">SMIC had started shipping 7 nm (N+1) chips</a>. Now, SMIC is shipping its third-generation 7 nm (N+3) process in Huawei&#8217;s Kirin 9030, with a <strong>minimum metal pitch of 32.5 nm</strong>, about 10% tighter than the 36 nm minimum metal pitch shipping in Intel&#8217;s latest Panther Lake CPUs on 18A.</p><p>The headline is true, but incomplete cherry picked metric. N+3 reaches the density of TSMC N6 through aggressive DUV multi-patterning and design-technology co-optimization (DTCO), but it pays for that in complexity, efficiency and process control. </p><p>We found this and more in our reverse engineering and teardown where we cover SMIC&#8217;s N+3 process technology, Huawei&#8217;s packaging, memory, architecture, and more. SemiAnalysis has been building a state-of-the-art teardown lab in Oregon capable of analyzing the world&#8217;s most advanced and important chips over the last year and half. We have already generated revenue on advanced datacenter chip teardowns including our recent reverse engineering of a major TSMC customer&#8217;s COUPE CPO optical engine + EIC 3D stack.</p><p>This is the first public report from the SemiAnalysis Teardown Engineering &amp; Evaluation Lab, or STEEL for short. The lab is aggressively scaling up and out and we&#8217;re excited to announce it publicly. This is a bit of inconvenient timing for TechInsights as they are private equity owned and currently being sold while having enjoyed virtually no credible competition for decades. This has led to TechInsights underinvesting in CAPEX.</p><p>SemiAnalysis exceeds TechInsights in revenue despite no venture or private equity ownership and being founded only 6 years ago. Source: SemiAnalysis</figcaption></figure></div><p>This report will detail our teardown of the Kirin 9030 and our findings on SMIC&#8217;s N+3 process, the most advanced in China. For comparison, we&#8217;ll show our teardown of the MediaTek Helio G99, made on TSMC N6. Through this comparison, we can look at the effect of export controls &#8211; SMIC N+3 and TSMC N6 are comparable nodes, but one is heavily export-controlled, the other free to use the West&#8217;s most advanced equipment.</p><p>Here we see both China&#8217;s progress and constraints. SMIC N+3 reaches TSMC N6-class logic density, but it requires far more aggressive DUV multi-patterning, so it does not match N6 on process maturity or cost. The Kirin 9030 Pro performs similarly to three-year-old Android flagships, and trails far behind the current flagship SoCs from Apple, Qualcomm, MediaTek, and Samsung. The efficiency gap is even wider.</p><p>Export controls have not stopped Huawei and SMIC from shipping advanced silicon, but they have forced a different path. Without EUV, SMIC is leaning harder on DUV multi-patterning, DTCO, and increasingly complex integration. The roadmap continues forward through tighter design rules and backside power, but each step adds cost and process risk. That ended in late 2020. Huawei switched to Qualcomm SoCs in its flagship smartphones, though export controls limited them to 4G-only variants.</p><p>In late 2023, Huawei returned to in-house silicon with the Kirin 9000s, a successor to the Kirin 9000, fabricated on SMIC N+2 instead of TSMC N5. The key dimensions are contacted gate pitch (CGP), cell height (CH), fin count, and the lower-metal routing grid.</p><p>To measure density, we use the Bohr metric: a weighted average of NAND2 gate area (60%) and scan flip-flop area (40%). This represents a realistic mix of combinational and sequential logic. This metric has its limitations, especially for complex cell layouts like TSMC&#8217;s FinFLEX, which alternates cells with different fin counts. Even so, it is the best metric for a pure process-level comparison.</p><p>Another important measurement is the fin pitch; it refers to the distance between two fins of the same transistor. In a FinFET process, multiple fins are used in each transistor to increase the drive current and thus performance.</p><p>TSMC N6 ships both a high-density (HD) library with 2 PMOS and 2 NMOS fins per cell, and a high-performance (HP) library with 3 of each. That is where Huawei&#8217;s roadmap stops looking like a normal foundry roadmap and starts looking like a packaging roadmap.</p><h1>Huawei&#8217;s &#964; Scaling Law</h1><p>At ISCAS 2026, Huawei unveiled its tau (&#964;) scaling law, reframing process scaling in the time domain. &#964; is the time cost of data movement and processing: switching delays in transistors, RC signal propagation delays in circuits, compute, memory, and networking latency. Outside Huawei&#8217;s terminology, this is called <a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/i/190867437/stco-co-optimizing-the-entire-system">system-technology co-optimization</a>.</p><p>This is Huawei&#8217;s answer to its lack of EUV lithography. Without EUV, planar density cannot keep pace with TSMC, Intel, or Samsung. If transistor density cannot shrink further, Huawei&#8217;s alternative is to shorten wires, reduce buffering, and stack logic vertically.</p><p>&#8220;LogicFolding&#8221;, Huawei&#8217;s implementation of this new scaling idea, is, in practice, an aggressive 3D stacking approach. AMD V-Cache places SRAM above or below a CPU die. AMD&#8217;s MI350X places active interposer dies (AIDs) underneath accelerator and compute dies (XCDs), with the AIDs handling cache, IO interfaces, the network-on-chip (NoC) and embedded metal-insulator-metal (MIM) capacitors. With LogicFolding, parts of the same logic block are split across multiple active dies bonded face-to-face at ultra-fine pitch. This allows Huawei to shorten some critical paths and reduce buffer overhead, not merely add cache capacity or offload the IO and interconnect.</p><p>Shortening wires is where the higher clocks come from. A large share of a modern core&#8217;s delay and energy budget goes into driving long interconnects and the repeater buffers along them. LogicFolding distributes a block&#8217;s critical-path gates across multiple stacked tiers bonded at a very fine pitch, so the bond interface behaves like an additional metal layer and the longest paths get shorter. Future teardowns of Kirin and Ascend chips will show both planar logic density and Huawei&#8217;s hybrid bonding solutions.</p><h1>Export Controls &amp; Future Scaling</h1><p>Export controls changed China&#8217;s optimization problem rather than ending it. EUV restrictions raised the cost and complexity of leading-edge manufacturing without freezing it. SMIC reaches N6-class logic density through DUV immersion, SAQP and DTCO, while Huawei shifts more of the burden onto architecture, packaging and system-level integration.</p><p>Future nodes will be tougher. N+3 still had room to tighten local metals and reduce cell height and CGP. Further scaling without EUV leaves fewer levers. More aggressive multi-patterning adds masks and overlay error. SMIC can keep pushing DUV, but each step will get more expensive and less forgiving.</p><p>The design side is just as critical. Huawei had domestic EDA tools and flows before the Kirin 9030, with the Kirin 9000s, 9010 and 9020 making that clear. Huawei was able to ship multiple consumer SoCs on SMIC N+2 and N+3 while cut off from the Western EDA stack.</p><p>US export controls restricted EDA tools for advanced chips in 2022 but did not target tools for more mature chips. In 2025, the US government briefly placed much broader restrictions on EDA software from Synopsys, Cadence, and others, before lifting them less than two months later as part of a trade deal tied to rare earths. Huawei has been unable to access those tools because it remains on a US trade blacklist.</p><p>That forced Huawei, SMIC and Chinese academic institutions to build their own tools and flows. Researchers at Peking University recently announced a prototype EDA tool for Huawei&#8217;s LogicFolding architecture, which requires a new flow to handle the multilayer layout and floorplan. This is not the same as replacing the full Synopsys or Cadence stack, but it shows where domestic EDA is headed: toward tighter co-optimization between architecture, process and packaging.</p><p>These advances are also diffusing into the Chinese ecosystem. SMIC is licensing its N+2 and N+3 processes to HLMC/Hua Hong at the government&#8217;s direction rather than by choice. If the same process learning feeds into Ascend accelerators for AI training and inference, the choke point shifts from one named fab to an ecosystem. Alibaba&#8217;s T-Head silicon arm and Cambricon, a Chinese AI chip designer that is expected to supply ByteDance, could also be major beneficiaries. Sanctions aimed at SMIC alone become less effective once the manufacturing knowledge has spread to other fabs and design houses.</p><p>China is not closing the gap with Intel, Samsung and TSMC. The teardown shows the opposite in several places: no EUV, no backside power, higher process complexity, and visible trade-offs.</p><p>But China is still advancing.
