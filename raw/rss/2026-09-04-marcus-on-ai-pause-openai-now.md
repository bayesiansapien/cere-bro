# Pause OpenAI, now

<p>I have often counseled calm where others might counsel panic. </p><p>I told you that the Hugging Face incident <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/5-lessons-from-the-openai-hugging?r=8tdk6">could likely have been prevented had best cybersecurity practices been followed</a>. (And I stand by that.)</p><p>I told you (and most people still seem unaware) that that the OpenAI Hugging Face incident was part of a training exercise, with some internal guardrails shut down, so it was not quite as bad as it seemed<a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-anchor-1" target="_self">1</a></p><p>I told you that Astra probably wasn&#8217;t AGI.<a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-2" id="footnote-anchor-2" target="_self">2</a></p><p>And I stand by all of that.</p><p>But I am freaked out.</p><p>What I am freaked about is not imminent AGI.</p><p>It&#8217;s OpenAI. </p><p>I simply don&#8217;t believe that they are trustworthy enough or responsible enough to be good stewards of the technology that they are developing.<a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-3" id="footnote-anchor-3" target="_self">3</a> As a company, they simply don&#8217;t have good judgment. </p><p style="text-align: center;">&#167;</p><p>Here are four considerations.</p><ol><li><p>Sam Altman cannot be trusted.  <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/article/2024/aug/03/open-ai-sam-altman-chatgpt-gary-marcus-taming-silicon-valley">I have been writing about that for a long time</a>. Ronan Farrow&#8217;s reporting <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/04/13/sam-altman-may-control-our-future-can-he-be-trusted">backs that up</a>. So does the just-dropped bombshell below that I am about to get to.</p></li><li><p>The just-released Astra reduces Chain fof Thought (CoT) monitorability,  one of the few (not especially reliable, but better than nothing) tools we have for keeping generative AI from running wild.  The AI safety community is up in arms about this&#8212;with good reason. There are tons of posts like this now, all quite right:</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>The decision to release Astra is a clear example of the willingness of OpenAI management to trade off safety in exchange for relatively modest gains in performance. The <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/red-alert-openai-is-poised-to-cross?r=8tdk6">red alert that I sounded a couple days ago </a>was on target. They really <em>are</em> playing around with new techniques that reduce monitorability. And <a href="https://x.com/tomekkorbak/status/2095596839886274689?s=61">their own data shows that monitorability is in fact compromised to some degree</a> in the newly released Astra, particularly on &#8220;destructive actions.&#8221; They released it anyway. That speaks volumes.</p></li><li><p>Something I read last night, and that only fully clicked into place this morning (see fact 4 below) terrifies me. A prominent recently departed employee (who presumably still owns significant stock, and who has repeatedly struck me as an advocate of OpenAI since he left) wrote <a href="https://x.com/jachiam0/status/2094660737155358865?s=61">an essay on X</a> basically asking people to simply accept that rogue AI is here to stay. </p><div How about if instead we pause now &#8212; before we get to &#8220;there is going to be&#8221; rather than heading full steam towards danger?</p></li><li><p>What has really chilled me this morning&#8212;and what moved to write this call to pause OpenAI&#8212;is not just Achiam&#8217;s chilling note but the revelation that there has been <em>yet anothe</em>r incident.  </p><p></p><p>Which OpenAI apparently <em>tried to keep quiet. </em>For <em>weeks</em>. </p></li></ol><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>If OpenAI is going to keep this stuff under wraps, Congress and/or the White House  needs to shut them down, at least for a while. </p><p>This company simply cannot be trusted. Their software is becoming ever more dangerous; their internal security practices leave a lot to be desired; they aren&#8217;t being straight with the public; and their proxies are preparing us to swallow the damage that they are now anticipating.</p><p> If there was ever a case for pausing a company for the public good, it would be now.</p><p>A good model might be <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receivership">receivership</a>, in which a company, typically close to bankruptcy, is put under the control of an outsider until such time as its core problems are remedied. I personally would not trust OpenAI unless and until Altman and his sidekick, Greg Brockman (whose questionable character was on display in the Musk trial) were replaced.</p><p style="text-align: center;">&#167;</p><p>The problem here, though, isn&#8217;t just OpenAI. It&#8217;s systemic. </p><p>For starters, the government has not been doing its bit. Whatever the White House screening policy is, it let the new model&#8212;arguably much more dangerous than Mythos given the decrease in monitorability&#8211; fly. (Brockman reported that the model was vetted by the White House, and they were given a green light). </p><p>That suggests to me that the White House isn&#8217;t even looking at monitorability. Of course, I have no idea what the White House is actually looking at &#8212; and nor does anyone else. And that lack of transparency is in itself is a <em>major </em>problem (about which I will write more very soon). </p><p>As Dave Troy put it while I was drafting this, there is something deeply wrong
