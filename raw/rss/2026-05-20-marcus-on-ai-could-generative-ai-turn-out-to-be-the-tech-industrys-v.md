# Could generative AI turn out to be the tech industry’s Vietnam? And could public backlash lead AI to a better place?

<div class="youtube-wrap" id="youtube2-tNH43a1EI7s"><div class="youtube-inner"></div></div><p>As I am sure you have heard by now, the <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/the-possibly-coming-ai-backlash-and?utm_source=publication-search">backlash against AI </a>i keep nattering on about is growing fast. At least three commencement speakers, include Google&#8217;s one time CEO Eric Schmidt were booed over the last few days simply for mentioning AI. </p><p>This lead Jason Calacanis (of All In) to draw a parallel </p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>But I think the analogy might run deeper.  And Jason may be underrating the student&#8217;s chances here (see later part of this essay).</p><p>In the Vietnam war, the US invested massively in a huge, disastrous campaign that drained resources and achieved nothing, fueled by arrogance. The war moved forward for years despite evidence that it wasn&#8217;t working as planned. It was also ridiculously expensive, about <a href="https://www.thebalancemoney.com/vietnam-war-facts-definition-costs-and-timeline-4154921#:~:text=More%20than%2058%2C000%20Americans%20died%20in%20Vietnam%2C%20and%20more%20than%20150%2C000%20were%20wounded.%20Based%20on%20the%20current%20dollar%20value%2C%20the%20Vietnam%20War%20cost%20the%20equivalent%20of%20about%20$1%20trillion.%20The%20U.S.%20has%20at%20one%20point%20paid%20$22%20billion%20per%20year%20in%20war%20compensation%20to%20Vietnam%20veterans%20and%20their%20families.">a trillion in today&#8217;s dollars</a>.</p><p>Could the multitrillion dollar investment in AI, burning money at unprecedented rates, and still struggling with hallucinations, unreliability and misalignment &#8211; even after truly massive investments, turn out to be another epic arrogance-fueled mistake?</p><p>Every time I think about the numbers and lack of RoI for most pilot studies and the reliability problems I think so. (Discussion in the comments below would be welcome.)</p><p></p><p>But here&#8217;s something else interesting. In <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/magazine/2026/01/02/black-swan-events-2026-00708074">my Politico &#8220;Black Swan&#8221; prediction for 2026</a> year, from January I made the seemingly wild prediction that,&#8220;<em>By the end of 2026, President Trump will have begun to distance himself from the aggressively pro-AI industry policies that characterized his AI strategy in 2025&#8221;</em></p><p><em>S</em>uddenly that notion looks like it has legs, far sooner than I expected. </p><p>As you may have seen, in a total turnaround, and perhaps prompted by fears over Mythos, Trump is now  actively considering <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/is-it-time-to-hit-the-pause-button?utm_source=publication-search">the kind of AI preflight check that </a><span class="mention-wrap"></span> <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/is-it-time-to-hit-the-pause-button?utm_source=publication-search">and I recommended here three years ago. (We urged that  &#8220;applications of AI could be governed similarly to [FDA], with authorities set up to evaluate and regulate the release of new major applications based on carefully-delineated evidence of safety.&#8221;, which is pretty much what is under consideration now)</a>.</p><p>The great Trump AI repositioning that I forecast may have begun!</p><p>Things are changing fast.</p><p>And if the backlash has enough force behind it, Trump may move further away from last year&#8217;s anti-regulatory policies.</p><p>As I argued at the end of <em>Taming Silicon Valley</em>, which I hope you will read or reread now that is so directly relevant, if the people unite, we might actually be able to get AI to a better place. </p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/could-generative-ai-could-turn-out/comments"><span>Leave a comment</span></a></p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share Marcus on AI</span></a></p><p></p>
