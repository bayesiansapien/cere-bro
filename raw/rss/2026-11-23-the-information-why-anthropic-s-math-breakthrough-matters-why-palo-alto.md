# Why Anthropic’s Math Breakthrough Matters; Why Palo Alto Networks Bought Applied Compute

<p>Silicon Valley loves a good oust-and-reinstate moment. The latest example: <b>Nvidia</b> CEO <b>Jensen Huang</b>. The chipmaker announced Monday morning that Huang, who had resigned earlier this month after reports emerged about inappropriate relationships with a supplier and employees, would return as its president, CEO and board member.</p><p>It’s not clear yet what led to his reinstatement. It seems like the U.S. government was involved or at least knew ahead of the announcement that Huang would return: President <b>Donald Trump</b> posted on Truth Social after the announcement that the “Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO” and that “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”</p><p><i>Onto today’s column…</i></p><p><b>Anthropic</b> did well last week. It went public, turning many of its employees into multimillionaires and billionaires (<a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/will-anthropics-ipo-lead-exodus-anthropic-employees">whether they stick around, though, is another question</a>).</p><p>Then on Friday, Anthropic mathematician <b>Levent Alpöge</b> <a href="https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf">claimed to have solved</a> the most famous open problem in math, the <b>Riemann Hypothesis</b>, using <b>Claude</b>. (On Saturday, two independent groups—the <b>Lean Focused Research Organization</b> and the <b>Mathlib Initiative</b>—verified that Alpöge’s solution was correct.) The discovery sent other AI researchers and mathematicians into a tizzy, adding to an already disorienting year of AI invasions to their turf.</p>
