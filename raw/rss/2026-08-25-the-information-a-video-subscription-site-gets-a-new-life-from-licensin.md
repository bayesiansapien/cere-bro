# A Video Subscription Site Gets a New Life From Licensing Videos to AI

<p>A streaming service called <b>CuriosityStream</b>, which offers documentaries on ancient Egypt, the Persian Gulf and other topics, was nearly delisted by the Nasdaq Stock Market three years ago. Squeezed by a post-pandemic glut of online streaming services, its revenue dove 27% in 2023 to a four-year-low. The service’s decision last fall to license thousands of hours of films—for which it held the rights—to companies training AI models has brought it back from the brink, though.&nbsp;</p><p>Earlier this month, the company, which was started by <b>Discovery Channel</b> founder <b>John Hendricks</b> in 2015, reported record profits, driven by revenue from licensing documentaries to AI companies. <b>Meta</b>, <b>Google</b>, <b>xAI</b>, <b>OpenAI</b> and <b>Anthropic</b> have all licensed its data, according to a person close to the company.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>The stock, which closed Monday at $2.84, has recovered from a February 2024 low of 45 cents, though that is still far from its 2021 high of $24.</p><p>“We keep doing streaming but the growth has come from AI data licensing for the past two years and it’s accelerating,” said <b>John Higgins</b>, CuriosityStream’s vice president in charge of data partnerships.</p>
