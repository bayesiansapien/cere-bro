# Huawei tells customers to find their own AI chip memory, effectively outsourcing procurement to the black market

<p><img alt="An investigation by the New York Times shows that despite US export restrictions, there is a lively trade in advanced AI chips in China. Dozens of suppliers in Shenzhen are doing business worth over 100 million dollars in some cases." class="attachment-full size-full wp-post-image" height="816" src="https://the-decoder.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/china_ai_chip.png" style="height: auto; margin-bottom: 10px;" width="1456" /></p>
<p>        Huawei is telling customers to source the high-performance memory for its AI chips on their own, pushing them onto the black market. Smugglers are exploiting a loophole in U.S. export rules by bundling HBM with low-end processors, shipping them through Hong Kong, and separating the chips on site. Prices for smuggled HBM3E memory have surged to around $4,000 per unit in just one year.</p>
