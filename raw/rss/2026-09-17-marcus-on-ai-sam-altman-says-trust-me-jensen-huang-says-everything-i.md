By standard estimates, if we had nuclear war, <a href="https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/nuclear-vault/2022-07-14/long-classified-us-estimates-nuclear-war-casualties-during">hundreds of millions of people would be killed</a>.  The blasts in Hiroshima and Nagasaki, as well as various other weapons tests, knowledge of how many warheads exist, and so on, give serious grounding to those numbers.</p><p>In contrast, the AI stuff is mere speculation. I am sure bad actors can and will use it to attack poorly protected infrastructure and to scam a lot out of people out of a lot of money. But I am not at all sure that either (a) AI will ever attack humans on its own or (b) get very far. Doomers are missing a sense of scale here. If you factor in unexplained deaths, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_deaths">Covid-19 killed between 19 and 36 million people</a> (about 8 million confirmed deaths) over a span of multiple years. Even if  for the sake of argument you accept that the misuse of AI might lead to another Covid-19 scenario (perhaps a legitimate worry), that would still be an order of magnitude less than a nuclear war. </p><p>And I am pretty confident that at that point at least 85% of the planet would support closing down or even bombing data centers. I am really having hard time getting to Sander&#8217;s estimate of &#8220;probably greater&#8221; than nuclear war.
