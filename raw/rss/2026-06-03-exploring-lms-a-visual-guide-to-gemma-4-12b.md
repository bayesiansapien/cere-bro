# A Visual Guide to Gemma 4 12B

<p><a href="https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it">A new Gemma 4 model</a> has been released and it&#8217;s an interesting one. There was an empty spot between the E4B and 26B A4B that needed to be filled and a 12B model fits nicely there. However, it wouldn&#8217;t be Google DeepMind without trying to do something special with that model and sharing it with the public.</p><p>So what is different with Gemma 4 12B?</p><p>It is encoder-free!</p><p>I&#8217;m going to be honest with you. The first time I heard &#8220;encoder-free&#8221; I was confused. Aren&#8217;t generative LLMs these days decoder-only anyway? It took me a minute to realize that the encoders for audio and video understanding were actually removed in this model but still had those multimodal capabilities.</p><div Although the model is encoder-free, all modalities are now unified within the LLM. Instead of the model having to wait for the encoders to finish processing the audio and image inputs, the LLM can get started earlier processing the input and generating output!</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://newsletter.maartengrootendorst.com/subscribe"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><p>In this guide, I want to showcase what it took to remove the vision and audio encoders and replace them with something much faster. The result, a 12B model that can handle audio and image inputs but without the need for encoders.</p><h2>Encoding and Connecting Modalities</h2><p>Before we explore Gemma 4 and how it was made encoder-free, let&#8217;s take a step back and discover what it generally means to connect modalities other than text to an LLM.</p><p>When a piece of text is given to an LLM, the LLM itself (through its token embedding layer) is in charge of splitting it up into tokens and converting them to embeddings. They are then passed to the decoder layers of the LLM which houses the attention layers used to create meaningful and contextual representations of those token embeddings.</p><p>To give an LLM the capability to understand modalities other than text, like audio and vision, much of the processing is generally not handled by the LLM. Instead, a separate encoder driven by attention is used to first process the input into embeddings. It works quite well and is used by many open-access multimodal LLMs, like Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.5.</p><p>It is not a free lunch though. The non-text encoders will need to process their inputs first before the LLM can start working. This adds some latency to the inference of these models. Moreover, it also adds quite a few parameters since the encoders are, in absolute terms, quite large.</p><p>So&#8230; What if we do not need the encoder? What if the connector is all you need?</p><p>To explore how DeepMind unified multimodal inputs, let&#8217;s first explore how the encoders in Gemma 4 work and what it took to replace them.</p><h2>The Modalities of Gemma 4</h2><p>Gemma 4 supports three modalities:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Text input</strong> &#8211; All models</p></li><li><p><strong>Image input</strong> &#8211; All models</p></li><li><p><strong>Audio input</strong> &#8211; The E2B and E4B models only</p></li></ul><p>All of these modalities require a transformer-like architecture with attention to process those inputs. For the image and audio inputs, two separate transformer encoders are used to create visual and audio tokens that the LLM can use.</p><h3><strong>The Vision Encoder</strong></h3><p>Gemma 4 E2B, E4B, 26B A4B, and 31B all have a vision encoder that is used to process input images.
