# ServiceNow Builds an AI Weapon to Target Salesforce Customers

<p>After a late summer rally, the Saaspocalypse slump in software stocks such as ServiceNow and Salesforce has returned. One reason may be signs that Anthropic’s expansion into tools outside of coding—such as tools to automate legal, finance, human resources, and sales functions, via Claude Cowork—is gaining traction with businesses, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-pushing-new-products-big-discounts-justify-huge-valuation">Kevin and I reported today</a>.</p><p>Another factor: established software-as-a-service firms are attacking each other with their own AI tools, which make it easier for them to steal customers from each other.</p><p>ServiceNow last week released a new tool that can reduce the time it takes companies to switch to its relatively new customer relationship management software from six months to six weeks. Previously ServiceNow would have needed to manually consult with a customer to figure out where their data lived in disparate systems so it could be extracted and moved onto ServiceNow’s customer relationship management software. But now the process can be automated, Paul Fipps, ServiceNow’s president of global customer operations, told me.</p><p>ServiceNow has historically made software for IT teams but it launched the CRM software less than two years ago, in an effort to woo businesses away from CRM giant Salesforce. Salesforce, meanwhile, has made a show of launching its own IT service management software and says it has poached ServiceNow customers.</p><p>Other big software-as-a-service companies are unveiling similar tools. Oracle has new AI tools to help automate switching to its enterprise resource planning software, as does rival SAP. And Workday has also released AI agents that help onboard customers to its human resources and finance software much faster.</p><p>Such tools have had limited impact so far as customers aren’t using them at a large scale, said Julie Teigland, a global vice chair of consulting giant EY. That’s because customers must be unhappy with their big software apps and await the end of their contracts to make a change to another provider, she said.</p><p>“AI will and has lowered the cost of transition in terms of data migration,” she said, adding, “You're not switching overnight your provider just because the tools are there to make it easier to switch.”</p><p>Then there’s the threat posed by Anthropic. One reason for Claude Code‘s success is that Anthropic designed it to be simple for customers to incorporate into their existing software development processes, said Mal Vivek, CEO at Zeb, a consulting firm that works with Anthropic customers. Now, a significant percentage of Zeb’s customers are in the process of adopting Claude Managed Agents and Claude Cowork, she said.&nbsp;</p><p>Those tools include products through Claude Cowork that help businesses automate legal, finance, human resources, and sales functions. Anthropic has also launched tools that help customers build their own legal and finance agents.&nbsp;</p><p>“There was no need for a company to build a user interface specifically around Claude Code,” said Vivek. “With Claude Managed Agents, I think Anthropic saw adoption would scale quickly once they put templates for these other business functional areas directly in the hands of users, making them easier to adopt.”</p>
