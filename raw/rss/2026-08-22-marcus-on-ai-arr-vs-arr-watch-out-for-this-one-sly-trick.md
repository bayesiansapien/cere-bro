fact that both terms have the same acronym.</figcaption></figure></div><p>Anthropic&#8217;s boosters are rushing to celebrate their ARR. </p><p>As they do, be mindful that the acronym ARR can actually mean two rather different things, Annual Recurring Revenue or Annualized Run Rate.  (And <a href="https://www.allacronyms.com/ARR">65 other</a> things, less immediately relevant.) You might think they are the same, but they are not.</p><p>What the boosters want you to think of is Annual Recurring Revenue. Annual Recurring Revenue is great; that&#8217;s things like subscriptions, which means next year likely to be same as (or better than) this year. Annual Recurring Revenue <em>recurs.  </em></p><p>Annualized Run Rate, alas, means no such thing. It can mean as little as you did something like took your best month, multiplied by 12 and projected that your best month this year would be your average month next year. That <em>could</em> happen. Or maybe your best month is NOT your typical month, and it will be better than your average month next year. You might in fact never have a full year that is twelve times your best month.</p><p>When people tell you Anthropic had this or that ARR, you really need to know which meaning they are talking about. They rarely tell you.  But they really mean the latter. </p><p>Indeed, THE big issue for Anthropic is that the kind of revenue they were making in the second quarter of this year may well not <em>recur</em>, at the same scale, next year, now that, in the aftermath of the collapse of tokenmaxxing, companies like ATT are <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/applied-ai/t-using-open-source-models-curb-anthropic-bills">increasingly turning to open source models from other vendors</a>, in order to save costs.
