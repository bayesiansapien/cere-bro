# Encryption Under Siege? OpenAI, Anthropic Disprove Key Cryptography Theorem

<p>Cryptographers who develop encryption technologies may not be sleeping very well these days.</p><p><b>OpenAI</b> and <b>Anthropic</b> both said Monday that their internal AI models had disproven the <b>Exponential Time Hypothesis</b>, a longstanding computer science theorem that predicts the minimum difficulty of solving a certain category of problems. Cryptographers had long assumed the hypothesis was true, and built other findings on the basis of that assumption.</p><p>Disproving the Exponential Time Hypothesis won’t endanger any current encryption methods, said <b>Matthew Green</b>, professor of computer science at <b>Johns Hopkins University</b> and founder of encryption startup <b>Zeutro</b>. But the finding is a big theoretical step, showing that some problems are easier to solve than cryptographers had thought.</p><p>That means cryptographers could also be wrong about other assumptions underlying core encryption methods such as <b>elliptic curve encryption</b>, a method underlying the majority of internet traffic, which would create a scramble to find new ways to keep information on the internet secure.</p>
