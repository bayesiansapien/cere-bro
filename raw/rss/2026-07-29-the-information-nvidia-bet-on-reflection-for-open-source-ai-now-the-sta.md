# Nvidia Bet on Reflection for Open-Source AI. Now the Startup Is Playing Catch-Up.

<p>Last August, a co-founder&nbsp;of Reflection AI flew to California to meet <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/org-charts/nvidia">Nvidia</a> CEO Jensen Huang. The fledgling startup had been making an AI coding tool, but the tech magnate encouraged him to go with a new plan, said someone familiar with the meeting, for something that at the time seemed more novel: developing open-source AI models that developers can download and modify.</p>

<p>With Huang’s backing and $800 million in initial investment from Nvidia, Reflection CEO Misha Laskin and his co-founder and technology chief, Ioannis Antonoglou, positioned their company as America’s champion in a contest with Chinese firms developing open-source AI. That vision has drawn billions of dollars of capital from other investors, including JPMorgan Chase, Sequoia Capital and 1789 Capital&mdash;a venture firm at which Donald Trump Jr. is a partner&mdash;and led to its inclusion by the Pentagon in a group of AI suppliers along with seven of the biggest tech firms in America.</p>

<p>But as interest in open-source AI crescendos nearly a year after the meeting with Huang, Reflection has yet to actually release its AI model&mdash;and it is falling further behind a growing list of rivals.</p>
