# AMD’s Valuation is Getting Ahead of Its Fundamentals

<p>AMD has been the darling of large-cap semiconductor investors this year, with its stock up 98% so far even after Friday’s big selloff. While some on Wall Street think the recent turmoil at Nvidia <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/amd-google-chip-away-nvidia-huang-exit-shakes-customers?rc=9byxri">could be an opportunity for AMD</a> to sell more of its AI chips to customers and steal market share, its valuation is already predicated on sky-high growth expectations and doesn’t reflect the geopolitical, financing and execution risks the company faces.</p>

<p>AMD, whose chips many customers regard as cheaper, second-rate alternatives to Nvidia’s, began trading at a valuation premium to Nvidia and rival chip designer Broadcom in the second half of last year. That’s when AMD announced that it had signed a deal to give OpenAI the right to buy a substantial chunk of its shares in exchange for purchasing 6 gigawatts worth of its AI chips. AMD signed a similar deal with Meta Platforms in February this year, which has helped boost its growth projections.</p>
