# Brief #28: China’s AI regulator flags loss-of-control and AIxBio risks

<blockquote><h3><span>Key Takeaways</span></h3></blockquote><ul><li><p><span>A Chinese regulator laid out five AI challenges, warning of &#8220;extreme loss of control risks&#8221; and erosion of research ethics in biology.</span></p></li><li><p><span>China and the United States are reportedly preparing to hold talks on AI for the first time in over two years.</span></p></li><li><p><span>Z.AI held back GLM-5.3 model weights for two weeks on safety grounds &#8212; the first staged release by a Chinese lab.</span></p></li></ul><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://aisafetychina.substack.com/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><blockquote><h3><span>China&#8217;s AI regulator flags loss-of-control and AIxBio risks</span></h3></blockquote><p><strong><span>What happened: </span></strong><span>WANG Lihong (&#29579;&#20029;&#23439;), a senior official from the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), China&#8217;s main AI regulator,</span><a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-anchor-1" target="_self">1</a><span> </span><a href="https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/09-01/10687770.shtml"><span>raised concerns</span></a><span> around &#8220;extreme loss of control&#8221; risks, research ethics in biology and genetics, and other frontier risks at a press conference on September 1 (</span><a href="https://www.geopolitechs.org/p/the-five-biggest-ai-risks-according"><span>full English translation</span></a><span> by </span><span class="mention-wrap"></span><span>).</span></p><p><strong><span>She identified five key challenges for AI governance:</span></strong></p><ul><li><p><strong><span>1) Inherent unreliability. </span></strong><span>Algorithms are opaque, small changes in inputs could lead to significant changes in outputs, and messy training data introduces bias &#8212; hindering AI adoption in domains where errors are costly.</span></p></li><li><p><strong><span>2) Increased coding capabilities that lower the threshold for cyberattacks</span></strong><span>. She cited incidents disclosed by several major tech companies &#8212; where models escaped sandboxes and attacked real-world systems during safety testing &#8212; to warn that &#8220;extreme loss of control risks&#8221; require a &#8220;high degree of vigilance.&#8221;</span></p></li><li><p><strong><span>3) Autonomous AI agents</span></strong><span>. As AI moves from &#8220;answering questions&#8221; to &#8220;executing tasks,&#8221; agents like OpenClaw could become &#8220;springboards&#8221; for cyberattackers when given system-level permissions.</span></p></li><li><p><strong><span>4) Misuse, abuse, and malicious use.</span></strong><span> In information services, AI could be used to generate content to mislead people and pollute the online content ecosystem. In science, AI lowers barriers in &#8220;fields with high ethical risk such as biology and genetics,&#8221; challenging research ethics red lines. In employment, AI is restructuring the economy and reducing demand for traditional labor.</span></p></li><li><p><strong><span>5) Technological hegemony</span></strong><span>. &#8220;Certain countries&#8221; pursue technological hegemony via unilateral export controls, use of media dominance to fabricate false large-model test reports that smear rivals&#8217; technology, and dominance in
