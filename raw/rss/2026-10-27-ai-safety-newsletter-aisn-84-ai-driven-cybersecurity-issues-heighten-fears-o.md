contributed to growing worries about a potential &#8220;hackpocalypse&#8221; in which AI-assisted hackers could wreak havoc and even take out large parts of the internet.</p><div vulnerabilities surged into the millions.</em></figcaption></figure></div><p><strong>AI revealed a way of attacking a system used by tens of millions of websites. </strong>On October 20, maintainers of a programming language called PHP <a href="https://www.php.net/archive/2026.php#2026-10-20-1">announced</a> that they would be releasing an emergency update to fix two vulnerabilities. PHP is <a href="https://w3techs.com/technologies/details/pl-php">estimated</a> to be used by about 70% of websites whose server-side programming language can be identified. Over the last few months, several different security researchers&#8212;all using AI&#8212;<a href="https://thephp.foundation/blog/2026/10/21/todays-php-security-release-and-how-were-strengthening-php-security/">developed</a> an exploit that attackers could use against the system. A successful attack could enable hackers to steal people&#8217;s sensitive data or place malicious links on trusted websites.</p><p>OpenAI&#8217;s Patch the Planet initiative led a team that <a href="https://openai.com/index/coordinated-disclosure-of-critical-vulnerabilities/">developed fixes</a>, which were <a href="https://www.php.net/archive/2026.php#2026-10-21-1">issued</a> on October 21. The National Security Agency <a href="https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4630238/nsa-advisory-critical-patches-recommended-for-php-vulnerabilities/">urged</a> website owners to apply them immediately or to take sites offline until they could be patched. Many websites, including ones belonging to governments, were temporarily <a href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/websites-taken-offline-over-php-risks-2026-10-21/">taken down</a> as a precaution. In the period following the announcement, the cloud computing company Cloudflare detected millions of attempts to exploit the PHP flaws across its network, <a href="https://x.com/eastdakota/status/2113079352682570051">according</a> to CEO Matthew Prince.</p><p><strong>Recent weeks have also seen a barrage of news about cyber incidents unrelated to PHP. </strong>On October 19, Reuters <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/how-one-hacker-used-ai-agents-few-thousand-dollars-break-into-scores-sites-2026-10-19/">reported</a> that a cybercriminal had deployed AI agents to autonomously hack into more than 100 companies. Two days later, Microsoft <a href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/21/storm-0940-uses-artex-agentic-ai-connectwise-supply-chain-compromise/">announced</a> that a hacker had been using an AI tool called ARTEX to &#8220;automate attacks targeting hundreds of organizations globally.&#8221; Additionally, Microsoft <a href="https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366652119/Microsoft-CVE-volumes-on-relentless-upward-trajectory">issued</a> its largest-ever software update, addressing more than 1,400 flaws&#8212;many of which were apparently discovered with the help of Anthropic&#8217;s Claude. These are a handful of <a href="https://x.com/AISafetyMemes/status/2113113711644737835">many more</a> recently reported incidents, and the striking increase in frequency has closely tracked the development of AI cyber skills that match and surpass those of human experts.</p><p><strong>AI is raising cyber risks in multiple ways. </strong>According to <a href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era">research</a> by the Google Threat Intelligence Group, the monthly rate of publicly disclosed vulnerabilities has doubled this year as a result of AI cyber capabilities. Additionally, AI lowers the skills barrier for would-be hackers and can automate parts of the process, enabling much larger-scale parallel attacks against numerous targets. These effects are behind a recent wave of <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/south-korea-japan-buffeted-by-hacks-ai-lowers-bar-cybercriminals-2026-10-09/">cyberattacks on South Korean and Japanese companies</a>. Nobuo Miwa, president of the cybersecurity company S&amp;J Corp., <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/south-korea-japan-buffeted-by-hacks-ai-lowers-bar-cybercriminals-2026-10-09/">said</a> that &#8220;AI doesn&#8217;t get tired... My view is that Japan is essentially being subjected to carpet bombing,&#8221; while Masatoshi Kumagai, CEO of GMO Internet Group, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/japan-companies-compensate-users-cyberattacks-buffet-economy-2026-10-26/">commented</a>: &#8220;The internet itself is now starting to break down.&#8221; There has also been significant speculation&#8212;though no public confirmation&#8212;that AI was used in this month&#8217;s <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/french-supermarket-chain-intermarche-hit-by-cyberattack-150-stories-targeted-2026-10-12/">attack on refrigerator systems</a> in French supermarkets. Researchers at the cybersecurity company Claroty <a href="https://claroty.com/team82/research/firmware-to-root-in-30-minutes-how-ai-is-changing-threat-intelligence">found that</a>, with AI assistance, they could develop working exploits against the same refrigerator systems in just 28 minutes for about $6 in AI usage costs.</p><p><strong>If AI shifts the offense-defense balance, some fear it could cause a &#8220;hackpocalypse.&#8221; </strong>AI tools are also helping defenders to find and patch vulnerabilities, but there are concerns that AI may create novel challenges for cyberdefense. For example, Sam Altman suggested in a recent <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=anAmTGcewX8">interview</a> that AI models might be able to find &#8220;bugs that are outside of the normal threat model of what we think a hacker can do,&#8221; such as hardware-level bugs. He added: &#8220;I no longer think we&#8217;re going to be able to just patch all the bugs. I think we&#8217;re going to have to completely switch the defense model.&#8221; If defenses do not adapt quickly enough, we might experience a period of high risk during which there are widely known vulnerabilities that cannot be patched efficiently. This could enable malicious actors to disrupt all kinds of services that rely on computer systems, potentially causing chaos.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><h1>In Other News</h1><h3><strong>Government</strong></h3><ul><li><p>The White House Super Intelligence Task Force <a href="https://www.semafor.com/article/10/15/2026/white-houses-ai-task-force-gets-to-work">met</a> for the first time and agreed to commission a report on cyber, bio, and loss-of-control risks posed by AI.</p></li><li><p>The US Department of Homeland Security <a href="https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjkgeq0k1wevo">banned</a> most government systems from using AI models made by DeepSeek.</p></li><li><p>Politico <a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/16/anthropic-ai-safety-01124654">reported</a> that Anthropic has proposed a draft AI bill to US lawmakers.</p></li><li><p>Eight US states <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/eight-us-states-sue-openai-claiming-chatgpt-harms-children-2026-10-14/">sued</a> OpenAI, claiming that the company has violated state consumer protection laws and that ChatGPT harms children.
