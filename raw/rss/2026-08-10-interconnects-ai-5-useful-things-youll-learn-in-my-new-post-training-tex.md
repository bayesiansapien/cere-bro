# 5 useful things you'll learn in my new post-training textbook (shipping now!)

<h5>Housekeeping: No voiceover on another quick &#8220;launch&#8221; post. More essays soon!</h5><p>After a few long years of finding time to document my lessons from training open models, my post-training book is done! It&#8217;s published by Manning, under the title <em>Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback: Aligning and Post-training LLMs</em>. </p><p>Telling the story of the book is a useful way to explain why you may want a copy. </p><p>The book started as a website where I wanted to document key methods of post-training that had potentially no online material explaining them. If there was something, I couldn&#8217;t find it. This existed for more topics than you would expect, given post-training was already popular in 2024 (when I bought the domain), and continues to this day. Topics like <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/09-rejection-sampling">rejection sampling</a>, <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/05-reward-models#outcome-reward-models">outcome reward models</a>, and <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/17-product#character-training">character training</a> are prime examples. This has helped make the <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/">website</a> fairly popular, as it&#8217;s still one of the few places discussing these topics at a foundational, intuitive way.</p><p>Otherwise, most of the book is about communicating intuitions and history. Much of the LLM industry is defined by core techniques that haven&#8217;t changed much in the last few years. This book was my attempt to explain in simple terms why post-training works, what trade-offs people need to make to get it right, and what misconceptions people often get stuck on. To do this, some of the older, foundational blog posts on Interconnects were reworked to stitch together the story behind key mathematical topics. For this reason, a lot of the explanatory text is likely higher voice than your average textbook. </p><p>This is the book I wanted to read when I was getting started a few years ago! With how many people still ask me basic post-training questions, in fact a population that&#8217;s accelerating in size, I suspect this book will be very well received. I still use the book regularly and hear from established researchers all over the industry that they do too. So, it&#8217;s not a beginner book &#8212; it&#8217;s more tailored to someone who has already finished a bachelor&#8217;s degree in CS &#8212; but if you master it you will be far ahead in your post-training worldview.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://www.interconnects.ai/p/5-useful-things-youll-learn-in-my?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share</span></a></p><h4>A discount to readers!</h4><p>The book is also freely available <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/">online</a> and comes with a full 12 hour course (<a href="https://rlhfbook.com/course">slides</a> + <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jQPiH-KB4B0&amp;list=PLL1tdVxB1CpVpEtMHxwuR4uI4Lxjw00_y&amp;pp=sAgC">video on YouTube</a>), a simple <a href="https://github.com/natolambert/rlhf-book/tree/main/code">code-base</a> with suggested exercises, and model completion comparisons. The histories that lead to modern post-training</h2><p>Knowing how a field came to be has always been fascinating to me. As you become an expert, knowing the history of your field better than anyone is what lets you make the best predictions (Bill Gurley gives similar advice in his recent <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Runnin-Down-Dream-Thrive-Actually/dp/0593799666">book</a>). In a time when the foundations of deep learning were being built, like the transformer, the core of modern post-training was also born in the alignment field. The book will walk you through 3 eras, when <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/02-related-works#origins-to-2018-rl-on-preferences">researchers learned to do RL on preferences</a> generally until ~2018, spent a few years learning <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/02-related-works#to-2022-rl-from-human-preferences-on-language-models">how to apply it to language models</a> from 2019 to 2022, and from 2023 on exploited the examples set by ChatGPT. Much like those early to scaling LLMs, the people who created this field a decade ago deserve incredible credit for how modern progress has unfolded.</p><p>Chapter 2 of the book is a crash course on this, but the book is littered with this type of thinking.</p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2" href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wzyr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa96b87d7-181d-4dc0-9a5b-6b1e5fc31675_2401x552.png" target="_blank"><div class="image2-inset"><source type="image/webp" /><img alt="" class="sizing-normal" height="335" src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wzyr!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa96b87d7-181d-4dc0-9a5b-6b1e5fc31675_2401x552.png" width="1456" /><div></div></div></a></figure></div><h2>4. Dispelling the magic of &#8220;distillation&#8221; </h2><p>It&#8217;s really nice to have a boring textbook <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/12-synthetic-data">chapter</a> on distillation given the broader AI policy discussions ongoing. This one, chapter 12, explains the various industry-standard ways that outputs from an LLM are used to train downstream models. When the technique is often described in nefarious ways, and as a tool of geopolitical competition, it&#8217;s a deescalatory action to break out a 300 page textbook to explain to someone how broad the term they&#8217;re attacking is.</p><p>With this, chapters 10 through 12 are all about making some opaque practices of the data industries clearer to readers. </p><p>The distillation chapter continues the theme I outlined above, explaining the key changes that needed to be made to transition the early knowledge distillation literature of 2015 to 2-3 key insights that got us to the multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) of models like Xiaomi MiMo-V2-Flash and DeepSeek V4.</p><h2>5. A survey of all the other little headaches you encounter when trying to do post-training right</h2><p>The second half of this book goes into a tour of over-optimization, regularization, evaluation, and character training, which is all about the many ways post-training can go wrong and what you need to stay on top of. This is what differentiates the book most from those that are just a list of code exercises and equations, but it explains things like <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/15-regularization#implicit-regularization">why RL generalizes when SFT forgets</a> (at the math level) or which techniques frontier labs use to <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/17-product#crafting-model-character-and-products">shape the personalities of the models</a> and <a href="https://rlhfbook.com/c/14-over-optimization#qualitative-over-optimization">why those often go too far</a>. The book presents the tools you will use and then opens up the floodgates of all the challenges you&#8217;re going to face when you actually try to put them to use. </p><div><hr /></div><p>As I wrote the takeaways for the book, I was reminded of an old piece of advice I gave for the AI era of building companies, and how <strong>people </strong><em><strong>do</strong></em><strong> need to care about research right now</strong>. With the pace of progress in AI, the time it takes for a research paper to land in a frontier model is 3-9months. Previously in big tech, that would be years, so it was fine to take a hands off approach to new research. For people with companies relying on being at the frontier in specific niches of LLMs, the dynamic today can make or break the company. This book is useful because it trains you at understanding which research matters &#8212; it helps you develop research taste.</p><p>If none of this resonates with you, you should buy my book because
