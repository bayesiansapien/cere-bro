# Nadella’s AI moat is the learning loop your company owns, not the model you rent

<p>In Microsoft&#8217;s Stanford CS153 session with Michael Abbott, Satya Nadella frames the firm&#8217;s problem in one line: if models learn from data, what remains of a company whose value has lived in tacit knowledge from operations and people. His answer is not a bigger rented model. It is a company-owned learning loop&#8212;what he calls a &#8220;hill-climbing machine&#8221;&#8212;built from private evaluations, workflow traces, rewards, and outcomes that stay inside the tenant. </p><p>Today we cover: </p><ol><li><p><strong>The firm question Nadella asks:</strong> what survives when general intelligence is available to every competitor. </p></li><li><p><strong>What the &#8220;hill-climbing machine&#8221; is, in operational terms:</strong> private evals, RL environments, traces, and retained IP. </p></li><li><p><strong>Why consume-only AI adoption can buy productivity and still lose retention:</strong> traces and rewards leave the firm. </p></li><li><p><strong>How to measure a loop-owning company:</strong> eval coverage, trace capture, model-swap survival, cost per successful private outcome. </p></li><li><p><strong>Harness mapping:</strong> eval, memory, and loop engineering as the control layer around rented models. </p></li><li><p><strong>Limits and Microsoft&#8217;s commercial stake:</strong> what the talk asserts, what remains unproven, and how to read Build product scaffolding. </p></li></ol>
