# The Iron(wood) Throne: Google's TPU Stakes Its Claim on Inference

<p>In November 2025, <a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpuv7-google-takes-a-swing-at-the">we published a newsletter saying that Google is selling TPUs</a> to external customers. Since then, we have been proven right over and over again with Oracle, <a href="https://openai.com/careers/software-engineer-tpu-compilers-san-francisco/">OpenAI</a>, Anthropic, and Meta all becoming customers of TPUs just as our <a href="https://semianalysis.com/accelerator-hbm-model/">Accelerator Model</a> reported.</p><p>Hardware demand is only half the story, as NVIDIA&#8217;s moat has historically been its software and large developer ecosystem. This is where TPU is also making headway,  partnering with upstream maintainers across the entire ecosystem, including the Meta PyTorch team, vLLM (Inferact, RedHat), SGLang (RadixArk), llm-d, Ray, etc. <a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpu-inferencex-full-steam">This is not a surprise to folks that have read our September 2026 article laying out Google TPU&#8217;s externalization strategy</a>.</p><p>Back in our September article, we published the first third-party TPUv7 results on InferenceX on single-turn workloads, showing that in an apples-to-apples FP8 comparison on Qwen 3.5 397B, TPUv7 Ironwood) delivered up to 50% better performance per dollar than B200 and B300.</p><p>We also reported that many Google TPU customers were asking for AgentX results, since long-context, multi-turn agentic serving is where inference spend is heading.</p><p>Previously, Jensen went on the <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hrbq66XqtCo">Dwarkesh Podcast</a> and model/scenarios</a>, but TPUs delivering better performance per dollar or competitive performance per dollar is a new experience that NVIDIA senior leadership is unwilling to accept. As soon as TPU posted its first AgentX result on Qwen3.5 397B FP8 showing that even TPUv7 FP8 could beat GB300 NVL72 FP4, NVIDIA tasked a strike force of human engineers and agent swarms to work 997, late into the night until 2am local time and on weekends, across USA and mainland China. <a href="https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2112258243372146742">Unfortunately for NVIDIA, their first GB300 FP4 counter submission is still 1.64x worse per dollar than TPUv7 FP8 for external customers across most of the curve.</a> We do think that with enough agent swarms, NVIDIA could regain a slight performance lead on GB300 NVL72 FP4 vs TPUv7 FP8, but on NVIDIA FP8 versus TPUv7 FP8, we believe that it is not possible for NVIDIA to regain SOTA for certain models.</p><p>One thing we hear a lot from open-source maintainers in PyTorch and NVIDIA customers is that there is a lot of bureaucracy; simple things, like adding a new feature to the NVIDIA driver to support a certain feature for PyTorch CudaGraph capture, have been stuck in &#8220;internal NVIDIA legal review&#8221; for three months. It makes little sense for lawyers to be the blocker for technical driver features. We have seen similar challenges when trying to get NVIDIA to add features like improving ConnectX, NVSwitch, HGX Board (Vulcan, Umbriel) DCGM power telemetry, where it takes a while just to get the timeline for the timeline. In comparison, <a href="https://github.com/ROCm/rocm-systems/pull/12724">startup mindset companies like AMD (under the leadership of Anush, Ramine, Vamsi, Lisa)</a> can rapidly develop these features.</p><p>There needs to be an cultural shift in massively reducing bureaucracy at NVIDIA. This is one of the biggest risks to NVIDIA Moat that Jensen has to tackle. In our experience and many customers experience, Nvidia is now slower moving than AMD, Google, and Amazon to external suggestion, git commits, and feedback. </p><p>That being said, certain teams at NVIDIA are cutting through the bureaucracy, <a href="https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2111627094878790023">such as the NVIDIA ex-CentML vLLM team</a>. AMD and <a href="https://github.com/Inferact/tpu-megakernels">TPUs</a> <a href="https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2109841575547211955">added innovative Megakernel decode support to open source popular production inference engine</a>. Within a week, they were able to defend the NVIDIA by <a href="https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61867">rapidly adding and merging Megakernel support too (PR approved by AMD vLLM maintainer).</a></p><p>TPU is making big headway into the ecosystem, and multiple models, like Day 0 Meta&#8217;s Muse Spark Open support and <a href="https://llm-d.ai/blog/deploy-kimi-k3-1-pro-with-llm-d">Kimi K3.1 Pro already has day 0 TPU support</a>,  as it applies the NVIDIA playbook of giving open-source maintainers lots of chips. <strong>In contrast, NVIDIA&#8217;s contribution of clusters to community maintainers has increasingly been wrapped in DMV style bureaucracy and stingy at best. For a $5 trillion company, one would expect NVIDIA to be more liberal in contributing resources to OSS developers who work for free on improving the NVIDIA stack. We hope Jensen figures out how to eliminate the bureaucracy slowing progress.</strong></p><p>In comparison, AMD has been increasingly less stingy and providing the upstream vLLM, TileRT, SGLang,
