# Autonomous Penetration Testing in 2027: Ridge Security and Six AI Pentesting Platforms Compared

<p><em>Disclosure: This article is sponsored by Ridge Security. I am an advisor to Ridge Security and thank its executive team for the opportunity to collaborate. I also wrote the <a href="https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/the-harness-advantage-in-autonomous">analysis of a model benchmark</a> that Ridge Security published in September 2026, which is cited below. Descriptions of every vendor, including Ridge Security, are based on public information (company websites, press releases, marketplace listings and trade press) as of the date above. Capabilities in this market change monthly, so confirm details with each vendor.</em></p><p>Penetration testing has a cadence problem. Most organizations still test on an annual or semiannual cycle, while their applications, APIs, and infrastructure change every day. A new class of platforms is closing that gap by using AI agents to do the work of a pentester: map the target, form attack hypotheses, execute exploits within an approved scope, prove what is actually exploitable, and hand back evidence and fixes.</p><p>2026 is the year this category moved from experiments into procurement. XBOW raised a $120 million Series C at a valuation above $1 billion in March, Novee emerged from stealth in January with $51.5 million, and in July IDC named Ridge Security an IDC Innovator in <em>IDC Innovators: Autonomous Penetration Testing for DevSecOps, 2026</em>.</p><p>The platforms below share a goal but differ sharply in how they get there: what they can test, where their context comes from, which AI models they rely on, and where they run. This guide profiles seven of them, starting with Ridge Security, and closes with a buyer checklist and FAQ.</p><h2><strong>Quick picks</strong></h2><ul><li><p><strong>Ridge Security (RidgeGen, RidgeBot, RidgeOne):</strong> for enterprises and MSSPs that need governed autonomous testing across the whole estate, from web apps and APIs to networks, hosts, Active Directory and IoT/OT, with on-premises deployment and a choice of AI model.</p></li><li><p><strong>XBOW:</strong> for teams that want a cloud service focused on continuous testing of web applications and APIs, with Microsoft Security Copilot and Sentinel integration.</p></li><li><p><strong>Novee:</strong> for application-heavy enterprises that want a purpose-trained offensive model across web, API, mobile, desktop and LLM applications.</p></li><li><p><strong>Equixly:</strong> for API-first organizations and teams shipping MCP servers and LLM applications.</p></li><li><p><strong>Xint.io:</strong> for teams that want black-box web application testing and code analysis from the offensive research team at Theori.</p></li><li><p><strong>Prime Security:</strong> for product security teams that want white-box pentesting tied to design reviews and code reviews.</p></li><li><p><strong>Corgea:</strong> for startups and developer-led teams that want AI pentesting bundled into an AI-native AppSec platform.</p></li></ul><h2><strong>What autonomous pentesting is, and what it is not</strong></h2><p>Autonomous penetration testing uses AI agents to carry out scoped offensive testing with minimal human direction. A capable platform discovers or ingests the attack surface, decides which attack paths to pursue, executes them under policy, verifies that an exploit actually works, and reports the result with evidence, remediation guidance and a way to retest the fix. It is easiest to understand by contrast with tools buyers already own:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Dynamic application security testing (DAST)</strong> sends predefined payloads to a running application. Agentic platforms add reasoning: they follow application logic, chain weaknesses across steps and roles, and test business rules that signature-based scanners cannot see.</p></li><li><p><strong>Breach and attack simulation (BAS)</strong> replays known attack scenarios to check whether security controls detect or block them. Autonomous pentesting looks for exploitable paths in your specific environment.</p></li><li><p><strong>Pentesting as a service (PTaaS) and bug bounty</strong> are delivery models built around human testers. Several of these providers now add agents of their own, which blurs the line.</p></li><li><p><strong>Human-led pentesting and red teaming</strong> remain the benchmark for novel research, high-risk systems and formal attestations. Autonomous platforms are best understood as continuous coverage between, and in support of, those engagements.</p></li></ul><p>Gartner groups related tools under adversarial exposure validation (AEV), and IDC frames the application-focused segment as autonomous penetration testing for DevSecOps. Whatever the label, the buyer&#8217;s question is the same: can the platform find real, exploitable risk safely and repeatedly, and prove it?</p><p>Figure 1 shows the agentic loop that separates Ask them in this order, and treat vague answers on proof, guardrails or data residency as a reason to pause.</p><h2><strong>Seven questions to ask any vendor</strong></h2><ol><li><p><strong>What can it actually test?</strong> Some platforms cover only web applications and APIs. Others extend to mobile, LLM applications and MCP servers. A few also test networks, hosts, Active Directory, cloud and IoT/OT. Real intrusions rarely stay at the web tier, so match the scope to where your risk lives.</p></li><li><p><strong>How does it prove a finding?</strong> Look for a validation step that is separate from discovery, reproducible evidence (requests, responses, payloads, scripts), and a clear policy for unconfirmed results.</p></li><li><p><strong>How is it kept safe?</strong> Ask whether guardrails are enforced outside the model or depend on the model following instructions; whether there are graded testing modes for production and non-production; how scope is confined; whether credentials ever appear in model prompts or logs; and what is recorded for audit.</p></li><li><p><strong>Where does its context come from?</strong> Black-box platforms start from a URL. Others use credentials, API specifications, source code, design documents or cloud configuration. Ask whether what the platform learns in one run carries into the next.</p></li><li><p><strong>Which AI model does it use, and who controls that choice?</strong> Approaches range from proprietary offensive models, to orchestration of several frontier models, to bring-your-own-model designs that include self-hosted open-weight models. This affects cost, data exposure, and what happens when a model refuses an offensive step mid-test.</p></li><li><p><strong>Where does it run, and where does your data go?</strong> Most platforms are SaaS. If you are in a regulated sector, test segmented or air-gapped networks, or cannot send findings and credentials to a third party, on-premises deployment is decisive.</p></li><li><p><strong>How does it fit your workflow and budget?</strong> Check CI/CD triggers, ticketing such as Jira, SIEM and SOAR feeds, APIs, and automatic retesting. Ridge Security: governed, model-agnostic testing across the whole estate</strong></h2><p><strong>What it is.</strong> Ridge Security is a Silicon Valley company co-founded by Lydia Zhang (President) and Nick Mo (CEO). It built its business on RidgeBot, an automated penetration testing and continuous security validation platform, and has since moved into agentic AI with RidgeGen. Ridge serves enterprises in financial services, government, telecommunications and other critical industries. In July 2026 IDC named it an IDC Innovator in <em>IDC Innovators: Autonomous Penetration Testing for DevSecOps, 2026</em> (doc #US54175326).</p><p><strong>The product family.</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>RidgeGen</strong> is Ridge&#8217;s agentic AI platform for continuous offensive security testing, generally available to enterprises and managed security service providers since August 2026.</p></li><li><p><strong>RidgeBot</strong> is the continuous security validation platform for IP-based environments, including network infrastructure, operating systems, databases, applications, websites, OT and IoT. RidgeBot 7.0 (June 2026) added fully automated Active Directory penetration testing, from enumeration and credential extraction to lateral movement and Domain Admin path validation, mapped to MITRE ATT&amp;CK.</p></li><li><p><strong>RidgeOne</strong> (October 2026) is a self-contained appliance that pairs RidgeGen with an open-weight model running on NVIDIA DGX Spark, so testing runs inside the customer&#8217;s environment with no external AI calls and no ongoing token costs.</p></li></ul><p><strong>How RidgeGen works.</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Finding is separated from proving.</strong> Test Agents probe the attack surface, Validation Agents independently reproduce what they find, and an Evidence Gate promotes only proven findings to the report.</p></li><li><p><strong>Stateful by design.</strong> RidgeGen preserves what it learns during an assessment, and a shared Ridge Knowledge store builds enterprise memory across runs. Operators can also teach it about their environment through an open prompt interface. This matters for multi-step attack chains, where one proven weakness, such as an authentication bypass, should change what gets tested next.</p></li><li><p><strong>Reasoning is separated from authority.</strong> Safety-critical policy is enforced outside the model&#8217;s discretion. Testing runs in one of three zones: <strong>Safe</strong> (the production default, with write, modify and delete actions blocked), Guarded (temporary, cleaned-up writes allowed only to prove a finding) and <strong>Authorized-Aggressive</strong> (non-production only, with signed authorization). Higher-impact actions need explicit approval and are logged for audit.</p></li><li><p><strong>Credentials stay out of the model.</strong> SafeBox applies credentials at the tool boundary, so they never appear in a prompt, in agent memory or in stored evidence.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bring your own model, and your own agents.</strong> RidgeGen supports Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini and Vertex AI, OpenAI, Grok and self-hosted open-source models, and customers can switch between them. Custom agents can be added, but they inherit the platform&#8217;s authority model, tool boundaries and verification gates.</p></li><li><p><strong>Fix and verify.</strong> Each validated finding comes with a remediation plan, and one click re-runs the original attack path to confirm the fix. A REST API and integrations with enterprise ticketing and ITSM platforms including Jira and ServiceNow, DevSecOps pipelines, SIEM, SOAR and vulnerability management tools, including CrowdStrike Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, move results into existing workflows.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Where Ridge stands out.</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Scope beyond the web tier.</strong> Most platforms in this guide test applications. Ridge&#8217;s portfolio covers applications and APIs and also the infrastructure an attacker moves into next: hosts, networks, Active Directory and IoT/OT. For buyers who want one governed program instead of separate tools for application and internal testing, this is the main differentiator.</p></li><li><p><strong>Your data stays in your environment.</strong> RidgeGen deploys on-premises as a self-contained package, and RidgeOne goes further by running the model locally too. That is a practical requirement in many regulated, government and critical-infrastructure environments, and it is uncommon in a category dominated by SaaS.</p></li><li><p><strong>Model choice, backed by evidence that the harness matters.</strong> In a September 2026 benchmark that Ridge published (I wrote the analysis), eight AI models ran inside RidgeGen across 96 model-target runs against VAmPI, Metasploitable3, OWASP Juice Shop and OWASP WebGoat. Grok 4.5 led cumulative coverage at 77%; Claude Opus 4.6 reached 63% at roughly $217 per run; Gemini 3 Flash reached 52% at about $5.42 per run; and the open-weight GPT-OSS-120B had the best efficiency, at 16.9 findings per million tokens for about $2.32 per run. Frontier models also sometimes refused legitimate steps mid-workflow, which Ridge handles with architecture and tool-layer controls rather than relying on the model. The takeaway: the best general-purpose model is not automatically the best pentester, and a platform that lets you choose can tune for coverage, cost or data locality.</p></li><li><p><strong>Depth on a common target.</strong> In a March 2026 head-to-head on OWASP Juice Shop with every tool using the same model (Gemini 3 Flash), RidgeGen reported 55 findings, all evidence-backed and nine of them critical, compared with 27 findings for Shannon (10 of them validated) and six for Strix. It received Frost &amp; Sullivan&#8217;s 2026 Global New Product Innovation Recognition for RidgeBot, a 2026 Intellyx Digital Innovator Award and a 2026 GOVIES award. RidgeBot holds a 4.5 out of 5 rating on G2 from 101 reviews.</p></li></ul><p><strong>What to consider.</strong></p><ul><li><p>Ridge offers several products for different buyers, so map your use case to the right one: RidgeGen for in-depth testing with an agentic approach, RidgeBot for broad and fast validation, and RidgeOne for a turnkey private appliance.</p></li><li><p>When you bring your own frontier model, its token spend is an operating cost you manage, and model choice changes coverage, as the benchmark shows. XBOW: cloud-native testing of web applications and APIs at scale</strong></h2><p><strong>What it is.</strong> XBOW was founded in 2024 by Oege de Moor, who created GitHub Copilot and GitHub Advanced Security, and is based in Seattle. questions</strong></h2><p><strong>What is autonomous penetration testing?</strong></p><p>Autonomous penetration testing uses AI agents to plan and execute scoped attacks against your systems, verify which weaknesses are actually exploitable, and report them with evidence and remediation guidance, continuously rather than once or twice a year.</p><p><strong>How is it different from DAST or breach and attack simulation?</strong></p><p>DAST runs predefined checks against a running application, and BAS replays known attack scenarios to test security controls. Autonomous pentesting reasons about your specific environment, chains weaknesses into attack paths, and proves exploitability.</p><p><strong>Can autonomous pentesting replace human pentesters?</strong></p><p>Not entirely. It provides continuous, repeatable coverage at a scale people cannot match, but expert testers remain important for novel research, complex high-risk systems and compliance attestations. Most mature programs combine both.</p><p><strong>Is it safe to run against production?</strong></p><p>It can be, if guardrails are enforced outside the AI model. Look for graded testing modes (for example, Ridge Security&#8217;s Safe, Guarded and Authorized-Aggressive zones), strict scope confinement, approval gates for intrusive actions, credential isolation and complete audit logs.</p><p><strong>Which autonomous pentesting platforms can run on-premises?</strong></p><p>Among the platforms in this guide, Ridge Security deploys RidgeGen on-premises as a self-contained package and offers RidgeOne as a turnkey appliance with a local open-weight model. Novee reportedly offers bastion-node and on-premises options. XBOW states that it runs only as SaaS. For the others, check with the vendor.</p><p><strong>Does the choice of AI model matter?</strong></p><p>Yes, but less than many buyers assume. Ridge Security&#8217;s eight-model benchmark found that the most capable general-purpose model was not automatically the best autonomous pentester, and that coverage and cost varied widely by model. The surrounding harness (planning, execution control, verification and guardrails) matters as much as the model.</p><p><strong>What does it mean that Ridge Security is an IDC Innovator?</strong></p><p>IDC named Ridge Security an IDC Innovator in <em>IDC Innovators: Autonomous Penetration Testing for DevSecOps, 2026</em> (doc #US54175326, July 2026).
