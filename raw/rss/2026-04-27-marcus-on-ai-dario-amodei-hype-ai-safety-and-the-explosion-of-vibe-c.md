totally predictable (as I noted on X <a href="https://x.com/garymarcus/status/2048552931923013916?s=61">in a tweet with around 2 million views</a>), and unsurprising in a system that mimics data without truly understanding what was asked of it.</p><p>And I am sure we will see many more catastrophic failures soon enough, involving things like privacy, security and data loss. Already there have been many.</p><p>But what does this have to do with Anthropic&#8217;s CEO Dario Amodei? And who is to blame? And what does it all mean? </p><p>&#167;</p><p>I&#8217;ll get to the latter two questions in a moment. Perhaps the most important point, one that goes well beyond coding, is at the end.</p><p>But, first, the reason that I bring up Amodei is his steady hype for AI coding, and especially this, from a couple day ago:</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>I don&#8217;t know if this is a case of pump and dump prior to the upcoming IPO or he if really believes what he said. </p><p>But what he is saying is that we can  eliminate not just line coders but the people who architect and maintain systems. That seems to me to be vastly overhyping what autonomous AI is currently in a position to do. </p><p>And I am far from alone.  For example, software architecture legend Grady Booch called out Amodei harshly on X, writing</p><blockquote><p> &#8220;<a href="https://x.com/grady_booch/status/2048410378309451980?s=61">I think that @DarioAmodei does not understand software engineering and  that he is working feverishly to pump up the valuation of his company in anticipation of its forthcoming IPO.</a>&#8221;</p></blockquote><p>The influential software engineer Gergely Orosz was equally critical, writing that &#8220;<a href="https://x.com/gergelyorosz/status/2048454331645481034?s=61">The only people who believe any of [what Amodei said] are non-coders</a>&#8221; and clarifying that the tools only work in a fully trustworthy way when they are supervised in domains in which the user already has experience.</p><p><a href="https://x.com/garymarcus/status/2048402129631764811?s=61">Absurd</a> remarks like Amodei&#8217;s <em>hype</em> the idea that coding agents are all we need. They are not.</p><p>&#167;</p><p>When disaster strikes, a lot of people want to blame the user.<a class="footnote-anchor" href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/feed#footnote-1" id="footnote-anchor-1" target="_self">1</a>  And there is some merit to that. The user <em>should</em> have had separate offsite backups (and do many many other things. For sure, every experienced programmer should know this.</p><p><em>But we just created a whole generation of vibe coders who don&#8217;t.</em></p><p>And we now have a legion of synthetic coding agents (like Cursor) that also apparently don&#8217;t always follow these kinds of basic principles (e.g., always keep independent backups), either. </p><p>&#120298;&#120309;&#120310;&#120304;&#120309; &#120310;&#120320; *&#120306;&#120325;&#120302;&#120304;&#120321;&#120313;&#120326;* &#120324;&#120309;&#120326; &#120324;&#120306; &#120320;&#120309;&#120316;&#120322;&#120313;&#120305; &#120315;&#120316;&#120321; &#120321;&#120319;&#120322;&#120320;&#120321; &#120304;&#120316;&#120305;&#120310;&#120315;&#120308; &#120302;&#120308;&#120306;&#120315;&#120321;&#120320;.</p><p>&#167;</p><p>People keep blaming the users for the growing number of vibe-coded fiascos. Doing so is *half* right. </p><p>Users <em>are</em> screwing up - by letting vibe coded stuff access their files, without proper backups, without proper monitoring, without adequate sysadmin, etc.</p><p>But that&#8217;s <strong>exactly why</strong> we still need software engineers &#8212; and why what Amodei recently said about software engineers disappearing is so absurd.</p><p>Skilled coders can use vibe-coded tools, with careful oversight.</p><p>But amateurs using vibe-coding tools are asking for trouble. </p><p>Using them without a strong prior working knowledge in coding and software maintenance and sysadmin *is* the mistake.</p><p>&#167;</p><p>Another thread on X is about why some coders are starting to write code by hand again, after their initial infatuation with coding agents.</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>What&#8217;s the answer? Part of it is (as the above author notes in his thread), because with AI it&#8217;s &#8220;too easy to slopify a codebase&#8221; &#8211; which means after you write the code, it&#8217;s hard to maintain it. (For example, people sometimes wind up with multiple copies of data, which can wind up causing hard-to-diagnose downstream problems). Maintainability is yet another serious issue beyond the recurring issues of data loss, privacy leaks, and security breaches. In the long run, it may be the most serious.</p><p>&#167;</p><p>Now here&#8217;s the interesting thing. Tools like Claude Code <em>can actually</em> be very useful. (As noted in a previous essay, they are neurosymbolic, not pure LLMs, but that&#8217;s a story for a different day.)</p><p>But you have to know what tweets like these mean, and how to act on them, to use them safely:</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>And most people don&#8217;t. </p><p>In the hands of very skilled practitioners <em>who pay a lot of attention, and treat the outputs with considerable scrutiny, </em>coding agents can be astonishing. But that kind of expert knowledge, which coding agents can&#8217;t be counted on for, is exactly why we need to keep software engineers in the loop. And it is why most of us who are in the know have such a hard time taking Amodei&#8217;s hype about getting rid of software engineers (who look at the whole problem, and not just isolated bits of code) seriously.</p><p>&#167;</p><p>But I have one more lesson, it&#8217;s the most important one, relevant even if you aren&#8217;t a coder and never plan on using these tools. And it is this:</p><p>AI agents are wildly premature technology that is being rolled out way too fast. The deepest lesson about the vibe coded AI agent disaster story that is running around is NOT about losing your data. </p><p>&#120284;&#120321;&#8217;&#120320; &#120302;&#120303;&#120316;&#120322;&#120321; &#120276;&#120284; &#120320;&#120302;&#120307;&#120306;&#120321;&#120326;. </p><p>The user in the lost data situation wasn&#8217;t (totally) naive. He thought (and makes explicit in his essay linked above) <em>system prompts</em> (hidden prompts that are built in by the AI companies) and guardrails would save him. </p><p>They didn&#8217;t.</p><p>What the user known as @lifeof_jer discovered was, in his own words, that system prompts&#8212;the basis for much of the mediocre work that passes as technical work in &#8220;AI safety&#8221; these days &#8211; were merely &#8220;advisory, not enforcing&#8221;, things the system often follows, but not always.  </p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a
