# Anthropic tells investors quarterly revenue up nearly 50% to $16.8bn ahead of IPO

<p><strong><a href="https://www.ft.com/content/11632d03-358c-454b-af95-d06d4072932b?syn-25a6b1a6=1">Anthropic tells investors quarterly revenue up nearly 50% to $16.8bn ahead of IPO</a></strong></p>
The phrase "told investors" appears four times in this FT article, which reports on Anthropic's day of hosting investors on Wednesday. Lots of details in here from "<a href="https://simonwillison.net/2023/Nov/22/deciphering-clues/#hints-to-look-out-for">people with knowledge of the matter</a>".</p>
<p>Key things I learned from the article:</p>
<ul>
<li>Q1 revenue (Jan/Feb/Mar) was "about $4.8bn"</li>
<li>Q2 (Apr/May/Jun) was $11.5bn</li>
<li>Q3 (Jul/Aug/Sep) was $16.8bn</li>
<li>In Q3: $1.70 in revenue for every $1 spent on "computing power"</li>
<li>Adjusted operating margins (excluding stock-based compensation) were 11%</li>
</ul>
<p>This point about distillation is worth quoting in full (highlight mine):</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The start-up has also clamped down on “distillation” — a controversial practice whereby rival AI labs train their models on the outputs of more advanced ones.</p>
<p>When rival companies “distil” their models from Anthropic’s models, that involves heavy usage and spending.</p>
<p>Anthropic told investors on Wednesday <strong>distillation accounted for 5 per cent to 7 per cent of revenue</strong> and that they had cut it back “materially” in the third quarter of the year, said four people with direct knowledge of the matter.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Napkin maths says that if distillation was 5% of their Q2 revenue (before they cut it back in Q3) then unnamed "other labs" spent over half a billion dollars distilling Anthropic's models in that quarter!</p>
<p>Also notable: Anthropic appear to no longer be leaning on "annualized run-rate revenue" (<a href="https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/29/anthropic/">previously</a>) in these communications to their investors.
