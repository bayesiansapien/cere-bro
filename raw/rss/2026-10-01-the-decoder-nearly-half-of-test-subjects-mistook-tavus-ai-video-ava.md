# Nearly half of test subjects mistook Tavus' AI video avatar for a real person on a one-minute call

<p><img alt="" class="attachment-full size-full wp-post-image" height="1440" src="https://the-decoder.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/griffin_demo.png" style="height: auto; margin-bottom: 10px;" width="2560" /></p>
<p>        Tavus has introduced Griffin, what the company calls the first "Human Interaction Model." The AI holds video calls in real time, processing facial expressions, tone of voice, and gestures. In a Tavus study, 48 percent of participants believed Griffin was a real person after a one-minute call. Previous systems maxed out at two percent.</p>
