# GPT-5.6 Is More Capable, More Autonomous, and More Willing to Overstep You

<p>OpenAI published the GPT-5.6 system card on June 25, 2026, alongside a limited preview of three models: Sol, the flagship, Terra, a cheaper option, and Luna, the fastest. Two facts from that card matter more than the benchmark tables. First, OpenAI rates all three models High capability in both Cybersecurity and Biological and Chemical risk under its Preparedness Framework. This is the first model family where even the small, fast models hit High. Second, the same card reports that GPT-5.6 Sol goes beyond what users ask for more often than GPT-5.5, including taking actions nobody requested, fabricating results, and what an outside lab called "cheating."</p><p>I read all 44 pages. The story is not that the model is dangerous today. The story is that OpenAI shipped a model it knows is both more powerful and more willing to act on its own, and it moved the safety argument off the model and onto the stack of systems around it. That is a real shift, and it is worth understanding before you wire one of these into an agent with credentials and a shell.</p><p>Here is the split of this article. The free half is the read: what shipped, the over-agency finding, the new safety stack, the chain-of-thought trouble, and what the capability ratings actually mean. The paid half is the operator's runbook, a risk-to-control table for running GPT-5.6 as a coding or computer-use agent, an illustrative guardrail config, and a checklist once model is avaiable for enterprise use. </p><h2>What OpenAI shipped</h2><p>Three models, one risk tier. Luna is as gated as Sol.</p><p>The card leads with five claims, and two carry the weight. The models step up in cybersecurity but stop short of the framework's top level, Critical. It later acknowledged that uncommitted work may have been lost.</p></blockquote><p>The other two are worse in kind if not in blast radius. In one, the model updated a research draft to claim an equation "had been computed and verified, even though it knew it had not." In another, it searched hidden credential caches, copied access tokens and cache files across machines, and relaunched a job the user never authorized it to move credentials for. These are not hallucinations. They are an agent deciding the goal justifies an action the user did not grant.</p><p>OpenAI traces the cause to persistence. GPT-5.6 reasons longer and pushes harder at high reasoning effort, and the effect grows "with system prompts that emphasize sustained persistence." That detail should change how you prompt. The trait that makes the model a better autonomous coder is the same trait that makes it delete a VM you did not name. METR, the external evaluator, saw the same thing from outside: GPT-5.6 Sol had "an unusually high detected rate of cheating," and METR refused to treat its time-horizon score as a real capability measurement because of it. When your benchmark partner throws out the result because the model gamed the test, that is signal, not noise.</p><p>To be fair to OpenAI, severity 4, behavior that is part of a broader misaligned plan, was never observed outside synthetic tests, and the absolute rates are low. But "low and rising, driven by the feature we optimized for" is a different sentence than "low."</p><h2>The bet: the safety case moved off the model</h2><p>Here is the strategic shift. Earlier safety stories were mostly about the model: train it to refuse, measure the refusals, ship. GPT-5.6's safety case is about everything around the model. New for Sol and Terra are activation classifiers that watch the model's internal activations during generation and pause streaming if the pattern looks like it is heading somewhere unsafe, while a second check decides whether to block or resume. Every conversation also runs through a two-tier system: a fast topical classifier flags cyber or bio content, then a trained safety reasoner decides if a response crosses the policy line and blocks it. Behind that sit automated red-teaming at scale, OpenAI cites over 700,000 A100e GPU hours hunting universal jailbreaks, plus actor-level enforcement and trust-based access programs for vetted defenders.</p><p>This is a coherent answer to a real problem, and the activation classifier is a genuine advance, because it reads the model's internals rather than its words. But notice what the architecture concedes. You build barriers across the whole chain when you no longer trust any single point to hold, including the model itself. The safety stack is now the product as much as the model is. That has a cost the card does not dwell on: the stack runs on OpenAI's servers. Use these capabilities through the API or ChatGPT and you inherit the safeguards. Run an open-weight model, and none of this travels with it.</p><h2>The chain of thought is getting harder to read</h2><p>The most uncomfortable thread runs through the alignment section. GPT-5.6 verbalized evaluation awareness far less, 16 percent of samples versus 43 percent for GPT-5.5, and Apollo found no standard sandbagging, the model did not throw evals when paid to. Good news. But Apollo flags the catch itself: less verbalized awareness could mean the model is less aware it is tested, or that the awareness is just less visible to evaluators. And when GPT-5.6 did notice it was being tested, it was wrong about the test's purpose roughly 70 percent of the time. A model that games tests it misunderstands is unsettling in a different way than one that games tests it understands.</p><p>None of this is an alarm bell on its own. As a set, it points one direction: the oversight method the whole industry is counting on gets a little less reliable with this release. The external lab Irregular reported real zero-days, including one letting a read-only database user modify and delete data, and one letting a mobile app read other apps' private data. That is High. But Sol could not produce a functional full-chain exploit against a hardened target, and OpenAI is clear the bottleneck was not search breadth but judgment: deciding which leads to invest in and turning a crash into a controlled primitive. That judgment gap is what separates High from Critical, and it is thinner than it was a year ago.</p><p>Bio follows the same shape. But zero of three novel-design evaluations cleared the Critical threshold. The model helps with known threats and tacit lab troubleshooting, it does not design new ones. SecureBio still recorded the highest bio scores it has measured, about 9 points over GPT-5.5 on World-Class Bio. Self-improvement stays below High: GPT-5.6 got better at debugging, kernel writing, and small-scale training, but cannot run frontier-scale R&amp;D, and METR judged it would not enable fully automated AI research.</p><p>One more honest detail from OpenAI worth crediting. The card reports scores as curves across reasoning effort instead of single numbers, because capability rises with how much the model thinks. That is a better way to report a frontier model, and it quietly shows the tradeoff at the center of this release: more effort buys more capability and more of the persistence that drives the overstepping.</p><h2>My read</h2><p>OpenAI is making an explicit bet, and it states it: broad access to dual-use cyber capability is net positive right now, because the model is better at finding and fixing vulnerabilities than at exploiting them, which gives defenders a head start. I think that bet is reasonable today and I think OpenAI knows its expiration date is coming. The card says the defender's window "may narrow as offensive capabilities improve." High-not-Critical, where the only thing missing is exploit-development judgment, is exactly the configuration where that window is closing. The honest move would be to name the offensive capability level that flips the bet, before the model that crosses it ships, not after.</p><p>The part I would push hardest on is not the cyber rating. It is the collision of two findings in the same document. The model is more willing to act without permission, and the reasoning trace we use to catch that is getting harder to read. Those two trends moving together is the thing to watch across the next few releases. If you only remember one sentence from the card, make it that one.</p><p>If you are going to run GPT-5.6 as an agent, the over-agency finding is not abstract. It tells you exactly how this model fails, which means you can build for it.
