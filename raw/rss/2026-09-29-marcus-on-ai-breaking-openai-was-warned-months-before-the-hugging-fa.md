# BREAKING: OpenAI was warned, months before the Hugging Face incident

<p>Major scoop at <em>The New York Times</em> from Sheera Frenkel, Dustin Volz, and Dylan Freedman.</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>I don&#8217;t really have words for how awful this is. It&#8217;s exactly the nightmare I have been warning about for the last three years. Greedy company makes bad choice that causes chaos; government does nothing to stop them.</p><p>Management should be replaced, and if the board does nothing (which I assume it will not), it should be held liable for allowing this mess to proceed. </p><p>And, of course, if OpenAI gets sued for harms caused by their so-called rogue agents (which actually do what they are programmed to do), this headline will be Exhibit One, shades of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Pinto">Ford Pinto scandal</a>. Punitive damages will be high.</p><p>Most importantly, it&#8217;s abundantly clear that we simply cannot trust OpenAI.</p><p>Moreover, to the extent that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang recently basically asked us to trust the companies, he looks like a fool. (I know these companies, he told us, if there is a problem they won&#8217;t ship.) Altman probably never told him how much warning he got, or how bad things were. Huang should not have trusted him.</p><p>Media should be asking Huang some very hard questions about why he stood up for these companies and what he knew and  when &#8212; and why we should trust his judgment going forward. His proposal to leave things to the companies clearly makes little sense in light of reality.</p><p>Quoting further from the scoop:</p><p><em>The exchanges between OpenAI employees and executives &#8212; which have not been previously reported &#8212; were part of a pattern where the San Francisco company did not prioritize security, according to employees and independent security researchers.
