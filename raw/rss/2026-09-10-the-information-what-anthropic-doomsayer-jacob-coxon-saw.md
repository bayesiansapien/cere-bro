# What Anthropic Doomsayer Jacob Coxon Saw

<p>Yesterday, Amir and I <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-researchers-terminator-style-warning-caught-fire">examined the shock waves</a> that have rippled through the AI industry and far beyond since former <b>Anthropic</b> researcher <b>Jacob Coxon</b> very publicly quit his job over concerns that AI “could kill us all”—and another Anthropic AI safety specialist put the probability of such a catastrophe at greater than 10%.</p><p>Coxon told me that is hardly the most pessimistic view inside Anthropic. “People have varying probabilities that—barring some substantial coordinated slowdown—the whole thing ends in doom,” he said in an interview later Wednesday. Those estimates range higher than 50% among some Anthropic employees, he said.</p><p>Coxon was able to gauge the fears of his fellow employees because “Anthropic has a substantially more transparent internal culture than OpenAI,” where Coxon worked for years before joining Anthropic in May.</p><p>Coxon, who focused on the earliest stages of training AI models at Anthropic, said he decided to leave the company because he was gradually “starting to viscerally feel the fear of where the tech's going to be like the next two years or even the next one year.”</p>
