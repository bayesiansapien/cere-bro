# Plaintiff hid invisible AI instructions in court filings to secretly influence automated review

<p><img alt="A Trojan horse made from court documents feeds data into an AI chip, surrounded by symbols of justice such as the scales and the gavel." class="attachment-full size-full wp-post-image" height="768" src="https://the-decoder.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/trojanisches-pferd-prompt-injection.png" style="height: auto; margin-bottom: 10px;" width="1376" /></p>
<p>        A plaintiff in Connecticut embedded invisible prompt injections in court filings, formatted in 3-point white text on a white background, to manipulate a potential AI review system. Judge Spader compared the attempt to secretly tampering with a jury and revoked the plaintiff's electronic filing privileges. The court stressed that Connecticut doesn't use AI to review filings, but the intent alone was enough to warrant sanctions.</p>
