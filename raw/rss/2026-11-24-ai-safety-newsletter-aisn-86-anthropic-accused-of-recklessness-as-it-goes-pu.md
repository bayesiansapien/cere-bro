It set a record as the <a href="https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ipo-by-numbers-fdca56f1cda70c9908899057e7e591e6">largest-ever IPO</a>, raising more than $81 billion at an initial valuation of $1.5 trillion.</p><p><strong>The day before the IPO, a public statement highlighted Anthropic&#8217;s risk-taking. </strong>The <a href="https://misanthropicstatement.com/">MisAnthropic Statement</a> was led by CAIS and now has more than 1,000 signatories, including Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners, and Fields Medal recipients. It points out that Anthropic, more than any other AI developer, has carefully cultivated its image as a responsible actor. However, Anthropic is also racing toward superintelligence, has <a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-69-department">watered down</a> its safety commitments, and has &#8220;<a href="https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/16/anthropic-ai-safety-01124654">lobbied</a> [lawmakers] for self-serving regulation,&#8221; according to the statement.</p><div y2="14"></line></svg></button></div></div></div></a></figure></div><p><strong>Anthropic has accelerated recursive self-improvement, a major risk to humanity. </strong>Today, AI companies including Anthropic increasingly use AI for specific tasks within the AI development process. They are aiming to remove humans from the loop altogether, allowing AI models to build the next generation of AI models fully autonomously&#8212;a process called recursive self-improvement (RSI). This could trigger runaway progress too quick for humans to follow, potentially producing an AI that vastly exceeds human intelligence with no assurances that it would act in our best interests.</p><p>Anthropic has <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement">warned</a> that full RSI &#8220;might increase the risks of humans losing control over AI systems.&#8221; Yet by focusing on improving AI coding capabilities, Anthropic has <a href="https://x.com/tegmark/status/2123079474639962552">advanced RSI</a> more aggressively than any other company. It also appears to have used its own progress toward RSI to attract investors, <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm">claiming</a> that its accelerated development is &#8220;strengthening our leadership position.&#8221;</p><p><strong>Anthropic still has a &#8220;halo effect,&#8221; even inside safety.</strong> Despite racing toward superintelligence, Anthropic has so far received relatively little criticism from AI safety organizations. This may be due to the <a href="https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/effective-altruism-is-too-tied-to-the-ai-industry-to-stop-it">deep</a> <a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-85-us-midterms-favor-candidates">ties</a> between Anthropic and the AI safety ecosystem and the dominant assumption that Anthropic is the most responsible AI developer. Now, even among those protesting against Anthropic&#8217;s pursuit of RSI, there is a <a href="https://x.com/hendrycks/status/2124561174436139176">reluctance</a> to view it straightforwardly as a negative force.</p><p><strong>Anthropic has accrued significant power within AI safety.</strong> The support between Anthropic and the AI safety ecosystem goes both ways, as Anthropic&#8217;s leadership built most of the AI safety ecosystem and is roughly its sole funder. This discourages <a href="https://x.com/hendrycks/status/2123070133522350176">AI safety organizations</a> and <a href="https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2123086773844672689">researchers</a> from criticizing Anthropic, for fear of losing future funding or career opportunities. Anthropic&#8217;s co-founders have <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund">pledged</a> to donate 80% of their equity, meaning that the IPO will significantly increase the company&#8217;s philanthropic giving&#8212;and potentially further strengthen its influence over the AI safety ecosystem.</p><h1>AI Companies Sued Over Loss-of-Control Risks</h1><p>On November 12, four public advocacy groups filed a <a href="https://www.platkinllp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Irreplaceable-et-al.-v.-Anthropic-et-al.-FINAL-AS-FILED.pdf">lawsuit</a> against Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceX. The plaintiffs&#8212;<a href="https://irreplaceable.org/">Irreplaceable</a>, <a href="https://www.citizen.org/">Public Citizen</a>, <a href="https://heatinitiative.org/">Heat Initiative</a>, and the <a href="https://secureainow.org/">Alliance for Secure AI</a>&#8212;say the companies&#8217; behavior is &#8220;blatantly illegal&#8221; and the lawsuit seeks to prevent &#8220;their unlawful path to disaster.&#8221;</p><p><strong>The plaintiffs say companies do not understand AI systems or how to control them. </strong>Leading figures from all five companies are quoted acknowledging the severe threats posed by advanced AI development&#8212;and RSI and superintelligence in particular. Yet all five companies are racing to build superintelligence first. The lawsuit states that AI systems are &#8220;black boxes&#8221; that companies do not fully understand. It cites the <a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-78-internal-models-escape-openai">Hugging Face attack</a> by rogue agents as an example of AIs&#8217; unintended behavior and argues that AI companies &#8220;have no realistic plan to prevent the continued harm caused by their technology.&#8221;</p><p><strong>The lawsuit points out that other hazardous technologies are carefully regulated. </strong>The plaintiffs quote David Robinson, who previously led OpenAI&#8217;s safety reports but <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/10/openai-safety-team-resignation/688881/">resigned</a> from the company last month. Robinson has said that &#8220;[W]e&#8217;re talking about a harm that&#8217;s much larger, for example, than a single nuclear power station melting down. And the internal controls and safety and redundancies are just nowhere near what the world expects for a nuclear power facility.&#8221;</p><p><strong>The state of California also recently sued OpenAI over rogue-agent incidents. </strong>Last week, California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, <a href="https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/it%E2%80%99s-golden-state-not-wild-west-attorney-general-bonta-sues-openai-over">saying</a> that &#8220;the Hugging Face attack was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability.&#8221;</p><p>Previous legal action against AI companies has mostly focused on their use of pirated work to train AI models, data centers&#8217; environmental impacts, and AI models&#8217; alleged role in encouraging users to harm themselves and others. The new lawsuits are among the first to challenge broader loss-of-control risks.</p><h1>Jensen Huang&#8217;s Affair Raises Questions About Nvidia Chip Exports</h1><p>On November 12, Jensen Huang <a href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition">resigned</a> as CEO of AI chip company Nvidia, amid news reports <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html">about</a> <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html">alleged</a> <a href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/former-nvidia-ceo-huang-had-relationship-with-second-employee-sources-say-2026-11-12/">affairs</a>, including one with a suspected Chinese spy. The news raised concerns that Huang&#8217;s personal life may have influenced Nvidia&#8217;s sales to foreign companies. On November 23, Huang was <a href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director">reinstated</a> as CEO by the company&#8217;s board, which said it was &#8220;confident that Mr. Huang&#8217;s reinstatement poses no national security risk.&#8221;</p><p><strong>Nvidia&#8217;s AI chips are crucial to AI development and national security. </strong>Nvidia, an American company, creates the most advanced AI chips in the world, giving the US a major technological advantage. Since 2022, the US government has used <a href="https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-implements-new-export-controls-advanced-computing-semiconductor-manufacturing-items-peoples">export controls</a> to limit sales of advanced chips to China and help prevent Chinese AI companies from catching up to their US counterparts. However, China has been trying to circumvent the restrictions, both by smuggling in chips and trying to rent access to chips located in third countries.</p><p><strong>Huang had an affair with an executive at a company that may have sent chips to China. </strong>The FBI is <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-justice-department-has-not-concluded-alice-huang-was-chinese-spy-blanche-says-2026-11-12/">investigating</a> Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and former director of a cloud-computing provider called Megaspeed. The Wall Street Journal has <a href="https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d">reported</a> that she used her relationship with Jensen Huang to help Megaspeed become an Nvidia Cloud Partner, giving it better access to Nvidia chips. Megaspeed is based in Singapore, but according to the Wall Street Journal the company may have <a href="https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d">passed on supplies</a> of Nvidia chips worth almost $12 billion to China. Megaspeed is also <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/jensen-huangs-yearslong-ties-alice-huang-enabled-nvidia-chip-sales-china">suspected</a> of renting chips remotely to Chinese companies.</p><p><strong>Huang&#8217;s personal life has raised questions about his pro-export lobbying.</strong> Nvidia <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/19/business/economy/nvidia-chips-commerce-china-huang.html">applied</a> for permission to sell 40,000 chips to Megaspeed in April this year. More generally, Huang has often used his <a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2026/11/13/timeline-president-trump-jensen-huang-close-relationship/">closeness to President Trump</a> to advocate for <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/nvidia-will-resume-h20-ai-chip-sales-china-soon-rcna218808">weaker</a> <a href="https://edition.cnn.com/2025/12/08/tech/nvidia-h200-chips-china-trump-export">export controls</a>. While Nvidia has clear financial incentives to sell its chips abroad, the reports about Huang&#8217;s affair have drawn greater scrutiny to his lobbying efforts.</p><p><strong>The US has introduced new restrictions on Chinese companies&#8217; chip access. </strong>In the wake of the revelations, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has <a href="https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/nvidia-jensen-huang-us-treasury-ai-chips-china-ckwd9037f">demanded</a> that Nvidia explain whether it knowingly allowed its chips to reach China via third parties. The US government has also <a href="https://www.npr.org/2026/11/16/nx-s1-6042883/nvidia-jensen-huang-commerce-department">announced</a> a new rule intended to prevent Chinese companies from remotely accessing export-controlled chips located in third countries. Nvidia has <a href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-of-directors-establishes-national-security-and-export-control-oversight-committee">established</a> a new committee to oversee national security policies and export controls compliance.</p><p><strong>Market dynamics and the White House may have swayed the board. </strong>In recent years, Huang has become the face of the AI transformation, developing a public image as a <a href="https://time.com/collections/time100-ai-2024/7012761/jensen-huang-2/">rockstar CEO</a>. Nvidia&#8217;s shares fell sharply on the news of his <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/fa3b0a35-1aa9-4f31-97b4-e4c599e3e654?syn-25a6b1a6=1">affairs</a> and <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/a93307a7-e2cd-4abd-bd89-c38832d8c40e?syn-25a6b1a6=1">resignation</a>, and there were <a href="https://standwithjensen.com/">calls</a> for him to be brought back, including <a href="https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117663497310955803">from Trump</a>. The company&#8217;s board said reinstating Huang was &#8220;in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers&#8221; and also said it had &#8220;received input from senior levels of the United States government.&#8221; In an interview on the day Huang was reinstated, Trump <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/cnbc-transcript-president-donald-trump-speaks-with-cnbcs-eamon-javers-and-squawk-on-the-street-today.html">claimed</a> to have &#8220;made the deal.&#8221; Nvidia shares <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo.html">soared</a> after the board&#8217;s announcement.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><h1>In Other News</h1><h3><strong>Government</strong></h3><ul><li><p>President Trump <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=amnszmQrLFU">announced</a> that he and Chinese President Xi Jinping had agreed to &#8220;do some joint guardrails&#8221; on AI, following their <a href="https://www.reuters.com/live/trump-xi-putin-live-leaders-hold-unprecedented-summit-shenzhen-2026-11-18/">meeting</a> in Shenzhen.
