# The great Generative AI unraveling

<p>Since August of 2023, I have been warning that, financially, generative AI might <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/what-if-generative-ai-turned-out?r=8tdk6&amp;utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&amp;utm_medium=web">turn out to be a dud</a>. </p><div class="digest-post-embed"></div><p>As I outlined in the very next article after that was that the problem with generative AI was that everyone was following the same path, using very similar techniques, training on very similar datasets. I warned that as a consequence OpenAI (which was then flying high) would not be able to keep its lead; that nobody would have much of a technical moat, and that there would be price wars, and the profits would be hard to eke out. Three years later, prices are lower, competition is higher, and profits remain elusive.</p><p>Training is ridiculously expensive and getting more expensive.  The Claude 5.5 model family alone <a href="https://x.com/jukan05/status/2120493524974276752?a=OIhdbf-tKBIOVeBKgEA&amp;s=61">reportedly cost $10 billion to develop</a>. And inference is expensive too. That leaves very little room for profits, especially in such a competitive market.</p><p>I also warned that LLMs lacked reliability; a raft of studies showing a lack of ROI seems to bear that out. There is an argument that things are improving, but even if they do they may not improve fast enough to justify the trillions being invested.</p><p>Meanwhile, nobody will be surprised if the massive Microsoft outage stems from code that was written by generative AI. And the systems remain poor at following instructions. The Klarna effect, in which companies rehire humans after making extravagant claims around AI, is yet another  sign that GenAI is still not as good as it was cracked up to be. (Some future of AI may be better, of course, but we are not there yet).</p><p>All that said, I didn&#8217;t get <em>everything</em> right. In particular hypey valuations persisted a <em>lot</em> longer than I expected. I am really surprised that the dance lasted as long as it did.</p><p>But it does look now like we may finally be near the end. </p><p>The Huang Affair and the Blue Wave have led to some pretty dramatic changes in fortunes. Rockets like Nvidia and Coreweave and Oracle which used to gain every month, are all losing steam; Anthropic was hoping for a $2T valuation at IPO and will now count themselves lucky if they get $1.5T.  </p><p>Nvidia is down 29% over the last six months, Oracle 35%, CoreWeave 54%, and Anthropic&#8217;s perceived valuation is down 25% in just a matter of weeks. </p><p>People may not want to admit it, but those numbers sure look like a bubble popping to me.</p><p>And that downward slide is continuing today. </p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p style="text-align: center;">&#167;</p><p>In a wild moment last night, I was live on CNBC Fast Money, warning that the whole thing might fall apart, in light of Nvidia&#8217;s troubles, because so many companies were reliant on Nvidia&#8217;s financing. Suddenly my conversation with the host, Melissa Lee,  was interrupted by a breaking news bulletin: a neocloud company called N-Scale was in deep doo-doo, having overcommitted.</p><div
