The model is what you expect a 0.1 version improvement over the previous one&#8212;Opus 4.6&#8212;to be. Better generally across benchmarks, clearly focused on agentic and coding skills.</p><p>The most notable aspect of this release is not reflected in benchmarks: it&#8217;s been two and a half months since Anthropic released <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-6">Opus 4.6</a>. The pace of launches keeps increasing, and the reason is, to an unknown degree, that the frontier labs&#8212;Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind (to a lesser degree)&#8212;are deep in (RSI) recursive self-improvement territory. the street speaks. (I&#8217;m hearing that the model is worse than expected, but again, firsthand experience is the only valid experience with these things; for tips, you can read <a href="https://x.com/i/status/2044847848035156457">this thread</a> by Claude Code creator, Boris Cherny.)</p><p>But, notably, Opus 4.7 is not the best.</p><p>If you look at the right side of the chart, you will find, in a darker cream-colored background, the imposing presence of <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/8b8380204f74670be75e81c820ca8dda846ab289.pdf">Mythos Preview</a>, a model that Anthropic announced with much fuss a couple of weeks ago, but that it won&#8217;t release due to risks to cybersecurity. <a href="https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/what-happens-when-ai-gets-too-good">I wrote about Mythos</a> last week.
