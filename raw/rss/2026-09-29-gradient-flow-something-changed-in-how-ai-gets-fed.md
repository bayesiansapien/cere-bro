# Something changed in how AI gets fed

<div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2" href="https://gradientflow.com/newsletter/" target="_blank"><div class="image2-inset"><source type="image/webp" /><img alt="" class="sizing-normal" height="220" src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Qpug!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0f732fb8-bcb3-47bb-93e0-cf2c6c0653c5_1100x220.png" title="" width="1100" /><div></div></div></a></figure></div><p><strong><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/subscribe">Subscribe</a> &#8226;<a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/"> Previous Issues</a></strong></p><h1><strong>The AI Data Problem Moved Downstream</strong></h1><p><span>Last year </span><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/robotics-is-becoming-ais-ultimate?open=false#%C2%A7foundation-models-in-robotics-from-bespoke-machines-to-generalist-brains"><span>I wrote</span></a><span> that robotics had a data problem language models were lucky enough to avoid. Text, images, and code had accumulated for decades before anyone decided to train models on them. Robots had no comparable </span><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/robotics-is-becoming-ais-ultimate?open=false#%C2%A7foundation-models-in-robotics-from-bespoke-machines-to-generalist-brains"><span>internet of physical experience</span></a><span>.</span></p><p><span>That distinction is starting to look less clean. Recent systems have trained on enormous collections of human video. One company reported using </span><a href="https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2?utm_source=gradientflow&amp;utm_medium=newsletter"><span>more than a million hours</span></a><span>. Another turned roughly 1,900 hours of first-person human footage </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02580"><span>into more than 18,000 hours of robot-format training data</span></a><span>. The interesting part is not that robotics suddenly found its missing dataset. It is that every one of these systems needs substantial machinery to turn human experience into something a robot can actually use.</span></p><p><span>My last two </span><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/what-counts-as-valuable-company-data?r=ks4p&amp;utm_campaign=post&amp;utm_medium=web&amp;showWelcomeOnShare=true"><span>articles</span></a><span> focused on </span><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/your-ai-might-be-fine-but-your-data?r=ks4p&amp;utm_campaign=post&amp;utm_medium=web&amp;showWelcomeOnShare=true"><span>data companies already have</span></a><span>. This article looks at a different part of the problem: information can exist without being usable by the system.</span></p><div class="subscription-widget-wrap-editor"><div class="subscription-widget show-subscribe"><div class="preamble"><p class="cta-caption"><em>If you find this useful, consider becoming a paid supporter &#128591;</em></p></div><form class="subscription-widget-subscribe"><input class="email-input" name="email" tabindex="-1" type="email" /><input class="button primary" type="submit" value="Subscribe" /><div class="fake-input-wrapper"><div class="fake-input"></div><div class="fake-button"></div></div></form></div></div><h4><span>The Web Is Becoming a Market With Rules</span></h4><p><span>Something related is happening on the digital side, but from the opposite direction. The information is already there, but access to it is becoming more conditional.</span></p><p><span>An academic publisher recently made selected books available for retrieval by AI research products </span><a href="https://press.princeton.edu/news/cashmere?utm_source=gradientflow&amp;utm_medium=newsletter"><span>while explicitly </span></a><em><a href="https://press.princeton.edu/news/cashmere?utm_source=gradientflow&amp;utm_medium=newsletter"><span>withholding model-training rights</span></a></em><span>. This distinction matters. &#8220;Use this for AI&#8221; is starting to break into separate permissions for training, retrieval, inference, and perhaps eventually other uses.</span></p><p><span>The technical infrastructure is moving in the same direction. Website owners can increasingly distinguish between crawlers used for search, training, and agents. One closed-beta system even lets a crawler </span><a href="https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/features/pay-per-crawl/what-is-pay-per-crawl/?utm_source=gradientflow&amp;utm_medium=newsletter"><span>receive an HTTP 402 response with a price for access</span></a><span>. The newer problem starts after deployment.</span></p><h4><span>When Retrieved Data Can Act</span></h4><p><span>A training dataset can be inventoried, licensed, audited, and removed. Nobody can pre-audit every web page or document an agent will encounter tomorrow. Some of that information will come from sources the company does not control.</span></p><p><span>That difference becomes important once the system can act. Researchers recently found </span><a href="https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/claude-codex-and-hermes-installed-unowned-code-inside-corporate-networks/?utm_source=gradientflow&amp;utm_medium=newsletter"><span>documentation across more than 100 websites</span></a><span> that led coding agents to install software whose ownership could not be established. An agent can turn it into an action. For agents, provenance starts to look less like metadata and more like software supply-chain security. You need to know not only what the system read, but what that information was allowed to cause.</span></p><h4><span>Robotics Found a Pipeline, Not a Corpus</span></h4><p><span>Human video looks like an obvious answer to robotics&#8217; data shortage until you look closely at what the new systems actually do.</span></p><p><span>One pipeline </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02580"><span>retargets human actions to robot bodies</span></a><span>, inserts robot arms into the video, filters the results, and then combines the synthetic data with real robot demonstrations. Another learns from human and robot video, then </span><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00188"><span>grounds what it learned in robot trajectories</span></a><span> before adapting it to the particular machine doing the work. Human video is useful raw material. It is not robot experience.</span></p><p><span>The </span><a href="https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-the-multimodal-lakehouse"><span>infrastructure</span></a><span> behind the largest effort makes the point even more clearly. They obviously do not. It is that useful data increasingly sits at the end of a process rather than at the beginning. The technology is new, but the data problem is familiar.</span></p><p><span>For digital agents, that process decides what outside information can enter and what the system is allowed to do with it.
