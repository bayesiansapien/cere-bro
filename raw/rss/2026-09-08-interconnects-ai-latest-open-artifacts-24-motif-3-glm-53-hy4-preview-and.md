# Latest open artifacts (#24): Motif-3, GLM-5.3, Hy4-preview and open model licenses

<p>Avid Artifacts readers know that we have been covering not only models but also their licenses for quite some time. There was a period when custom licenses were all the rage, for example <a href="https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct/blob/main/LICENSE">the custom Qwen2.5 72B-Instruct license</a> or <a href="https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct/blob/main/LICENSE">the Llama licenses</a>. DeepSeek had a <a href="https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/LICENSE-MODEL">custom license for DeepSeek V3</a> before <a href="https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1/blob/main/LICENSE">R1 changed it to MIT</a>, which has resulted in many (Chinese) model makers adopting MIT or Apache 2.0 licenses in 2025.</p><p>In 2026, open models are more competitive than ever, which has led to two interesting developments: Western model makers adopt open licenses, with both <a href="https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it">Google</a> and <a href="https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B">Meta</a> switching to Apache 2.0. Chinese model makers at the frontier, however, are becoming more restrictive: <a href="https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3">Kimi K3</a> comes with a license which requires commercial agreements for those who run inference or fine-tuning services, and <a href="https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3/blob/main/LICENSE">MiniMax M3</a> requires agreements above a revenue threshold and has prohibited use cases.</p><p>The newest addition is Zhipu&#8217;s <a href="https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3">GLM-5.3</a>, which switched from MIT (GLM-5.2 and earlier) to a custom license with the following clause for inference and fine-tuning providers:</p><blockquote><p><em>If the Licensee or any of its affiliates operates a Model as a Service business, and the aggregate revenue of the Licensee and its affiliates exceeds 10 billion US dollars (or the equivalent in other currencies) in total over any consecutive 12 months, the Licensee must pass Z.AI&#8217;s security review before using the Software or its derivative works for any commercial purpose. The scope and method of the security review shall be reasonably determined by Z.AI.</em></p></blockquote><p>While the 10 billion US dollar threshold is very high compared to other licenses of this kind, &#8220;affiliates&#8221; is not defined in the license, which adds uncertainty and creates barriers to adoption. Furthermore, the license is provided in both English and Chinese, with the Chinese text using &#8220;&#20851;&#32852;&#26041;&#8221; for affiliated parties, which <a href="https://kjs.mof.gov.cn/zt/kjzzss/kuaijizhunzeshishi/200806/t20080618_46245.htm">does have a definition in Chinese law</a>.</p><p>We are by no means legal experts and there are obvious reasons why those licenses are created. However, we want to highlight the issues that come with creating such licenses, especially in a world with a lot of valid open and closed alternatives.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-24-motif-3?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share</span></a></p><h3><span>Our Picks</span></h3><ul><li><p><strong><a href="https://huggingface.co/Motif-Technologies/Motif-3"><span>Motif-3</span></a></strong><span> by </span><a href="https://huggingface.co/Motif-Technologies"><span>Motif-Technologies</span></a><span>: Motif is one of the few hidden gems out there, showcasing innovation in their model training with very limited resources compared to others.
