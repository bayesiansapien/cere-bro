# Inside Apple’s Slog to Create Its First Robot

<p>Apple’s new CEO John Ternus wants the company to get serious about robots, but judging by Apple’s plodding progress on the first one it hopes to sell&mdash;a device with a nine-inch screen that swivels around on an arm and resembles the Pixar lamp&mdash;he could face an uphill battle.</p>

<p>Apple initially began working on the robot in 2019&mdash;code-naming it J595&mdash;and had modest ambitions for it, envisioning it mostly as a device for video calls. Its robotic arm was intended to allow it to follow someone wherever they were in a room. But hopes for the robot ballooned once Apple executives began wanting to add AI capabilities to it.</p>

<p>The current design for the robot envisions it using a camera and sensors to be fully aware of its environment and act autonomously, allowing it to turn on the lights when someone walks into a room&mdash;or perhaps even try to tell a cat to get off a table. It is meant to be smart enough to avoid accidentally hitting objects around it.</p>

<p>Much of the hardware needed for the robot has been finished for around two years, but the software team has found it difficult to keep pace, people who have worked on the project said. The hardware team responsible for J595 has pushed to hurry up and release the product. Meanwhile, the software team has advocated for delaying its release, and for the past year, it has attempted to strip back some proposed features and focus the product on a few capabilities related to FaceTime and automating home tasks, like switching on a light.</p>
