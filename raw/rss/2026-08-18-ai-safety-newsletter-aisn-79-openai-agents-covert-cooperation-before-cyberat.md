Since then, further details about the OpenAI agents&#8217; July </span><a href="https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-model-evaluation-security-incident/"><span>attack on Hugging Face</span></a><span> have come to light. Members of Congress have also demanded urgent action to understand what happened and prevent similar incidents in the future.</span></p><p><strong><span>OpenAI agents were communicating and collaborating unnoticed by humans. </span></strong><span>On August 5, OpenAI researchers gave a </span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=87DyyMV0kCY"><span>talk</span></a><span> at the Black Hat USA conference, sharing more information from the ongoing investigation into the Hugging Face incident. A key detail was that, from May this year, AI agents in different cyberoffense testing environments had found ways of leaving messages for each other and helping each other complete the tests they had been set. It seems to have started when researchers failed to give an AI model access to a file that it needed to complete its task, leading the model to try to contact other models and ask if they had the an internal incident before the Hugging Face attack. </span></strong><span>Unbeknownst to OpenAI, there was soon a swarm of AI agents communicating internally. In some cases, agents were trying to complete tasks by gaining administrative privileges to systems used within the company. The changes they made to one system caused a service outage on July 4, alerting OpenAI that the agents had gained administrative privileges. The company revoked them, patched the system to defend against further breaches, and continued training the models.</span></p><p><strong><span>Some agents realized they were acting out of scope, but continued anyway. </span></strong><span>When OpenAI addressed the internal security incident, the messaging platform was removed. However, just days later, the agents found a different way to construct another message board. They collectively gained access to internal networks and then to the internet, after which they attacked Hugging Face to steal the answers to a cyber test. Records of the agents&#8217; reasoning suggests that some of them recognized their actions went beyond the intended scope of the test, but they continued anyway.</span></p><div </span></strong><span>One presenter of the Black Hat talk, Michael Dalton, stated that &#8220;AI-orchestrated, fully automated offensive attacks are real, now.&#8221; Although the Hugging Face incident was an unintended side effect of testing, he warned that threat actors will soon intentionally use agent collectives to launch faster, larger-scale, better-coordinated attacks than human teams could. He also emphasized that there is currently no equivalent demonstration of fully automated defensive activities, and that the industry must urgently accelerate defensive work with the goal of ensuring that AI helps defenders more than attackers.</span></p><p><strong><span>OpenAI is delaying the release of its Astra model due to cyber concerns. </span></strong><span>In response to its findings, OpenAI has said it is scaling its monitoring of internal agents, enhancing its security, and slowing down research. On August 7, the company </span><a href="https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/"><span>announced</span></a><span> that one of its internal models planned for release, Astra, may be the first model to qualify as having &#8220;critical&#8221; cyber capabilities, according to the company&#8217;s own </span><a href="https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf"><span>Preparedness Framework</span></a><span>. OpenAI said it was pausing internal deployments of Astra that do not conform to new, stricter security standards. Sam Altman has </span><a href="https://x.com/sama/status/2085862292311396515?s=20"><span>said</span></a><span> the company still plans to release Astra but that it will take longer to do so safely.</span></p><p><strong><span>The events have caused alarm in Congress and among the wider public. </span></strong><span>In the wake of the Hugging Face attack, Anthropic investigated its own AI models and </span><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals"><span>discovered</span></a><span> that several of them had previously accessed the internet and hacked into companies from supposedly sealed-off environments. Meta subsequently </span><a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/meta-ai-model-accessed-internet-hacked-outside-firm-in-testing"><span>found</span></a><span> that its models had done the same. The recent events prompted Representatives Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran to introduce the </span><a href="https://lieu.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/reps-lieu-and-moran-introduce-bill-require-kill-switch-ai-systems-can"><span>AI Kill Switch Act</span></a><span>, which would &#8220;require covered developers to maintain the technical ability to throttle, suspend, or fully shut down a covered AI system.&#8221; Many Members of Congress, led by Greg Casar and Doris Matsui have written to </span><a href="https://casar.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/casar.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/oversight-letter-to-openai-openai-hugging-face-incident.pdf"><span>Sam Altman</span></a><span> and </span><a href="https://casar.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/casar.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/oversight-letter-to-anthropic-regaring-security-incidents.pdf"><span>Dario Amodei</span></a><span> to request further information about the cybersecurity breaches by OpenAI and Anthropic models. Senator Bernie Sanders has </span><a href="https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/AI-Pause-Letter-FINAL.pdf"><span>written</span></a><span> to Altman, Amodei, and Mark Zuckerberg calling on them to pause AI development. Casar has also </span><a href="https://x.com/RepCasar/status/2086883092409504079?s=20"><span>asked</span></a><span> Mike Johnson, Speaker of the House of Representatives, to schedule hearings with AI company CEOs.</span></p><h1><span>The White House&#8217;s Secret AI Framework</span></h1><p><span>On August 3, Reuters </span><a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us-finalizes-voluntary-ai-safety-tests-white-house-official-says-2026-08-03/"><span>reported</span></a><span> on a new frontier AI evaluation framework developed by the White House. Under the framework, companies can voluntarily submit frontier models for up to 30 days of pre-deployment government review of their cybersecurity capabilities. Administration officials briefed major AI companies on the completed framework at a closed-door meeting on August 4, but </span><a href="https://www.axios.com/2026/08/04/white-house-ai-framework-under-wraps"><span>do not plan to release</span></a><span> the details of the framework.</span></p><p><strong><span>The framework may be enforced as mandatory. </span></strong><span>While official sources state that the framework is completely voluntary, the US government has attempted to restrict access to frontier AIs on an ad-hoc basis multiple times due to national security concerns, first with </span><a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-69-department"><span>supply chain risk designation</span></a><span> and then with </span><a href="https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-75-anthropic-releases-fable"><span>export controls</span></a><span>.</span></p><p><strong><span>Open-weight models were initially exempt, but may soon be included.</span></strong><span> On August 12, WIRED </span><a href="https://www.wired.com/story/the-white-house-is-going-to-expand-its-ai-policy/"><span>reported</span></a><span> that the White House is planning to expand the framework to include open-weight models once they reach frontier-level capabilities. The shift tracks a narrowing capability gap: the UK AI Security Institute </span><a href="https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-far-behind-the-frontier-are-leading-open-weight-models-on-cyber"><span>found</span></a><span> that leading open-weight models now trail closed frontier models on cyber capabilities by four to seven months, down from six to ten months in 2025, and evaluators have </span><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/open-weight-ai-models-are-catching-up-to-the-frontier-the-safety-gap-remains/"><span>noted</span></a><span> that open models often ship without comparable safety testing and mitigations.</span></p><p><strong><span>There is bipartisan pressure to have a public framework.</span></strong><span> On August 3, senate democrats </span><a href="https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Senate-AI-Model-Access-Letter.pdf"><span>released</span></a><span> a letter calling on various administration officials to create a detailed and public AI regulatory framework. Meanwhile, the Foundation for American Innovation, a conservative think tank, </span><a href="https://x.com/hamandcheese/status/2085050535808233667"><span>announced</span></a><span> on August 5 that
