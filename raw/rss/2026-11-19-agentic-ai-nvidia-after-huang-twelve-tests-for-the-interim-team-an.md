# NVIDIA After Huang: Twelve Tests for the Interim Team and the Board

NVIDIA announced on 12 November 2026, in a <a href="https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-CEO-Transition/default.aspx">NVIDIA release</a>, that Huang resigned as president, chief executive, and board member due to widely reported scandle. keeps export controls a compliance task and not a revenue shock.</p></li><li><p>The new committee &#8212; whether independent directors gain real authority and a public charter for export control oversight.</p></li></ol><p>We start from a common assumption: a company built around one person breaks when that person leaves. 188,000 people signed a petition for his return, though some people named on it later denied signing. People gave their loyalty to the person, not to the org chart.</p><p>We trace the reason to the structure Huang built. Developers write code in one place with CUDA, and they run it across generations of hardware. Customers buy one system in the rack: the chip, the processor, the link, and the network, joined together. Customers get one new architecture each year on the cadence. No single person carries those three facts. The interim team inherits them.</p><p>We count the rack as the second fact. A customer does not buy a chip. A customer buys a rack with the graphics processor, the central processor, the link between them, and the network joined in one system. NVIDIA sells the rack as one product with one software stack. A rival that sells only a chip competes against a part, not a product. That is why developers and customers stay through a leadership change.</p><p>We still weigh the shock. Huang concentrated three risks in himself: the plan, the relationships, and the judgment. Huang carried all three. The interim team now has to write down the plan, spread the relationships, and share the judgment. The interim team writes down the plan in a published succession plan and a published timetable. The interim team spreads the relationships through named sponsors for each hyperscaler and each AI lab. The interim team shares the judgment through a council, not a single calendar. We measure that work with twelve tests.</p><p>We draw two scenarios from the same record. In one, the interim team continues: a permanent chief executive, a leadership council, and the new committee carry the structures Huang built. In the other, Huang returns in a governed role beside a permanent chief executive, and he uses his strengths inside board control. We base both scenarios on the same foundation: developers write CUDA, customers buy the rack-scale system as one product, and we track a published annual roadmap.</p><p>We mean three things by institutional here. First, developers write for the software base, and they write the libraries and papers for it too, so they keep using it after any one leader leaves. Second, NVIDIA sells a system, not a chip, and rivals copy systems slowly. Third, customers read a public, dated roadmap, so they can plan around it without a keynote. We saw all three hold through the resignation week. Developers and customers change slowly, and they change on evidence, not on headlines.</p><p>We saw one break. Investors sold hard on the announcement, and investigators and reporters scrutinized the circumstances of the resignation. The interim team cannot inherit trust. It has to earn it again, in public, with numbers and with names. NVIDIA reported the first numbers in the quarter. We name the permanent search as the next test. The board moved the same way twice: it named a finance chief as interim chief executive and created a new committee for export control.</p><p>Kress and Buck form the bench, the other half of the story. Kress ran the finances through the boom years. Buck built the software behind the boom. We define the interim period by those two names. We expect the permanent search to name a third.</p><p>We do not weigh the twelve tests equally. We judge whether the company governs itself by succession and the committee. We judge whether the product keeps its lead by CUDA and supply. We judge whether the money and the law stay on the company's side by financing and geopolitics. We judge how fast the answer arrives by the rest.</p><p>The board searches for a permanent chief executive with no disclosed deadline. Investors read the silence as risk. The interim team gained time from the quarter, not certainty. We watch two markers for whether the interim team holds the line: the permanent appointment and the first GTC without Huang.</p><p>We turn to the return question next. Huang keeps his strengths after a resignation. We ask which roles keep those strengths inside board control. We name five roles that do that, and we make them governable through the new committee.</p><p>We count the committee as the newest fact in the record. The board created it the same day as the third-quarter report. Its members give the compliance teams a direct line to independent directors. Compliance teams had no such line before. We read it as the single clearest sign that the board intends to govern differently. We ask whether the line carries real authority. We name that question the twelfth test.</p><p>Practitioners ask different questions than investors do. Investors ask whether the stock recovers. Practitioners ask whether they can keep building on a stable platform, whether they can read a public roadmap, and whether independent directors keep the compliance line clear. We answer all three from the record. Developers kept building on a stable platform through the quarter. NVIDIA kept the roadmap public. The board added a committee to the compliance line. We read the answers as favoring the optimists on stability and the skeptics on governance. We take that split from the record.</p><p>We map the twelve tests in Figure 1:  four with leadership, three with product, three with money, and two with law.</p><div leadership, product, money, and law.</em></p><p>We test whether the interim team turns a founder-run company into a founder-built institution. The interim team needs a permanent chief executive, a published plan, and a committee with real authority for that turn.</p><h2>1. Succession: no heir, no written plan</h2><p>Huang co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 and served as its only chief executive. He ran an unusually flat organization, with no heir apparent and no formal succession plan. NVIDIA warns in its own annual report that it depends heavily on its executive team and that weak succession planning could hinder strategic planning and execution.</p><p>When Huang resigned, the board had published no Plan B. The board named Colette Kress interim chief executive while she remains chief financial officer, a post she has held since 2013. Harvey C. Jones became the board's first-ever chair. The board accepted the resignation on 11 November, per a <a href="https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-resigns-as-nvidia-chief-executive/5309411">Register summary</a> of the 8-K filing.</p><p>NVIDIA set the roadmap for the next 12 to 18 months before the resignation. NVIDIA ships Blackwell Ultra in volume. NVIDIA ramps Rubin. A new chief executive does not need to invent either. Ian Buck, a CUDA co-creator, leads hyperscale and high-performance computing.</p><p>The interim team can formalize what Huang never formalized. The board chooses a permanent chief executive first, through an open internal and external search with a published timetable. A small leadership council &#8212; chief executive, chief financial officer, technology chief, operations chief &#8212; replaces the single-person structure, with named deputies and a written emergency-succession plan.</p><p>Huang keeps his product intuition in a founder-chairman role beside a permanent chief executive, without restoring the single-point structure. We name the risk on the other side: if Huang returns and re-centralizes power, he recreates the succession problem the resignation solved.</p><h2>2. CUDA: developers hold the lead in the installed base</h2><p>Developers write portable kernels once with Triton and run them across hardware. Developers now take it as the default kernel-generation path inside the PyTorch compiler, so they write a growing share of new PyTorch code without touching CUDA directly. AMD shipped ROCm 7 in September 2025, and AMD shipped ROCm 10 on 27 August 2026. By some estimates, buyers now spend about two-thirds of AI compute spending on inference, and they find alternatives suit inference better than training.</p><p>We trace the lead to accumulation, not to a single interface. NVIDIA launched CUDA in 2006&#8211;07. Twenty years later, in April 2026, over 6 million developers worldwide used it. We count three layers in the stack: pre-optimized libraries, framework defaults, and developer habit. Independent assessors put ROCm at 90 to 95 percent of H100-level performance on some inference workloads.</p><p>NVIDIA also absorbs the threat. With a Triton-to-Tile-IR backend from January 2026, developers compile Triton directly to CUDA Tile IR. If NVIDIA completes its September 2026 agreement to acquire Hugging Face for $12.93 billion, NVIDIA reaches the distribution point for open models: 18 million developers and about 3 million models.</p><p>New leadership can compete on openness where it matters. We test openness by Hugging Face neutrality, with no NVIDIA-compute requirement. We shift the center of the lead from CUDA the language to the full AI-factory stack &#8212; Dynamo, NIM microservices, NVLink Fusion &#8212; where customers care less about portability because they find the value in the system.</p><p>Huang as chief architect could lead the open-platform turn without it looking like defensive lock-in. He backed CUDA in 2006 with about $10 billion. He told Lex Fridman in March 2026 that developers hold the lead in the installed base, not in the acronym.</p><h2></h2>
