# There Are Likely More Spies at AI Companies, Says Security Executive Lisa Thiergart

<p>Other countries likely haven’t yet stolen AI models from leading U.S. companies&mdash;but only for a lack of trying, said Lisa Thiergart, co-founder and CEO of Security Level 5, a startup that works with AI companies and the intelligence community to secure AI data centers. “If a nation state really wanted to steal the weights right now, they could. However, I think that they have cheaper ways to get what they need,” she said on the latest episode of AI Deep Dive. (The full episode <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=upszW2K6iiw">is available here</a>.)</p>

<p>The episode was recorded shortly after OpenAI accused one of its employees, a Saudi national, of hiding a recording device in an office conference room. In the interview, Thiergart said it is “pretty likely” that there are other spies in U.S. AI companies&mdash;a suspicion buttressed by recent reports from the New York Times and the Financial Times that some U.S. officials believed Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/nvidia-board-launching-investigation-reporting-ceo-targeted-chinese-informant">had a romantic relationship</a> with a Chinese businesswoman recruited as a spy by China’s top intelligence agency.</p>
