# Fed Hike Changes AI Funding Story

<p>Put aside, for just a moment, all the arguments about AI safety. Today’s decision by the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates may be a bigger issue for the AI sector in the short term. Long-term bond yields had already risen, of course. But the Fed’s action—pushing up short- and medium-term borrowing costs—seems sure to crimp the debt-fueled AI boom, particularly given the prospects that <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/16/business/fed-interest-rates-dot-plot.html">another rate hike</a> is possible later in the year.&nbsp;</p><p>The most vulnerable to higher rates are surely small companies that have weak or no credit ratings but need to raise money, such as would-be neoclouds hoping to finance data center projects. Think Rum Group, which <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-strikes-13-7-billion-compute-deal-trump-linked-rum-group?rc=9byxri">plans a big data center in Georgia</a> for a deal with Anthropic but doesn’t have financing for the project yet. (For more on startups’ financing challenges, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/the-information-finance/compute-needs-big-payment?rc=9byxri">see here</a>). But the ripple effects of higher interest rates will eventually affect everyone, including big tech firms spending a fortune to expand their data centers for AI.</p>
