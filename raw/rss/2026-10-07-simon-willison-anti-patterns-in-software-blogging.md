# Anti-Patterns in Software Blogging

<p><strong><a href="https://refactoringenglish.com/blog/anti-patterns-software-blogging/#summary">Anti-Patterns in Software Blogging</a></strong></p>
Some excellent writing advice from Michael Lynch. Michael warns against "meandering intros", misjudging your reader's existing knowledge, assuming they'll read your previous posts, and excessive formality.</p>
<p>He also warns against <a href="https://refactoringenglish.com/blog/anti-patterns-software-blogging/#overreliance-on-links">overreliance on links</a> as an excuse not to explain terminology. This one hurt! I do this all the time, but I have a nagging suspicion that almost nobody ever clicks on them.</p>
<p>(In <a href="https://lobste.rs/s/rwdufq/anti_patterns_software_blogging#c_qdhhsd">a Lobste.rs comment</a> Michael clarifies that "My rule of thumb is that my article should still make sense to the reader even if they don't click any links". That works for me.)</p>
<p>This point about using your own voice is crucial:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Beginner software bloggers suffer from a mass delusion that you have to write in a stiff, overly formal way for people to take you seriously [...]</p>
<p>Just write the way you talk.</p>
<p>With so many developers delegating their writing to AI, software blogging is becoming bland and homogenous. Readers are hungry for writing with personality.</p>
</blockquote>
