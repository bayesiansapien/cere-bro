# Why OpenAI’s Jalapeño Might Sicken Nvidia

<p>Exactly who at OpenAI decided to name the company’s first internally designed chip Jalapeño? That might have seemed like a good idea a few months ago—perhaps OpenAI imagined that name would serve as a subtle indicator that the chip is a hot property. But it’s not ideal right now, when the <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-salmonella-jalapeno-august-2026">Food and Drug Administration is warning</a> that products containing jalapeños shouldn’t be eaten, served or sold thanks to a salmonella outbreak tied to the peppers.&nbsp;</p><p>OpenAI must be hoping that’s not an omen, and so far it doesn’t seem to be. The AI firm on Tuesday <a href="https://openai.com/index/jalapeno-first-results/">unveiled the first test results</a> for Jalapeño, claiming the chip showed “a significant performance advance: Jalapeño can serve more AI work per unit of power while also returning responses more quickly.” OpenAI CEO Sam Altman <a href="https://x.com/sama/status/2092339694210040187?s=20">tweeted</a> that “we made a chip and it is fast.” AI research firm SemiAnalysis <a href="https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/openai-jalapeno-better-than-nvidia?_gl=1*1v04gwg*_ga*MTcwNjUwMTYwMC4xNzg3Njk0NTM1*_ga_FKWNM9FBZ3*czE3ODc2OTQ1MzQkbzEkZzAkdDE3ODc2OTQ1MzUkajYwJGwwJGgyMTA1Njc4MzU2*_gcl_au*MTM1MDI4ODIwMC4xNzg2MTE5MDY2">put out a review</a> claiming that Jalapeño is “better than Nvidia Blackwell.” Ouch.</p>
