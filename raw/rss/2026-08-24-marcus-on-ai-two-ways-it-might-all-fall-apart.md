# Two ways it might all fall apart

<div class="pullquote"><p>&#8220;How did you go bankrupt?&#8221; Bill asked.</p><p>&#8220;Two ways,&#8221; Mike said. &#8220;Gradually and then suddenly.&#8221;</p><p>&#8220;What brought it on?&#8221;</p><p>&#8220;Friends,&#8221; said Mike. &#8220;I had a lot of friends. False friends. Then I had creditors, too. Probably had more creditors than anybody in England.&#8221;</p><p>&#8212; <em>Ernest Hemingway, The Sun Also Rises, 1926, three years before the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression">Great Depression</a> began.</em></p></div><p>The  market is jittery today, with most the big tech stocks down in early trading. I still expect the whole thing to collapse, sooner or later.  Maybe not today, but eventually. Maybe suddenly, or maybe as a slow <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/the-generative-ai-fizzle?r=8tdk6&amp;utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&amp;utm_medium=web">fizzle</a>. Or, as Hemingway put it, first gradually and then suddenly.</p><p>One route to collapse is psychological; the other has to do with unpaid bills. The two are by no means mutually exclusive, and certainly overlap. I have tended to focus on the former; this morning I read something fascinating spelling out how the latter might go.</p>
