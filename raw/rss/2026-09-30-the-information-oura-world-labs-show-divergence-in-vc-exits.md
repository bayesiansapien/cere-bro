# Oura, World Labs Show Divergence in VC Exits

<p>It’s turning out to be a great year for venture exits—as long as you’re talking about M&amp;A.&nbsp;</p><p>Take Monday’s news that Advanced Micro Devices would purchase Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs. The $8.2 billion all-stock purchase of the two-year-old AI startup adds to the growing list of such deals this year, which include SpaceX’s purchase of Cursor, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/nvidia-agrees-buy-open-source-model-repository-hugging-face-12-9-billion">Nvidia’s purchase of Hugging Face</a> and <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/exclusive-stripe-exclusive-talks-buy-startup-openrouter-around-10-billion">Stripe’s acquisition of OpenRouter</a>. The sale is also <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/andreessen-horowitz-turned-ai-bets-8-billion-bonanza">another win for Martin Casado’s</a> infrastructure team at Andreessen Horowitz, which was World Labs’ largest shareholder and owns nearly 14% of the company, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/amd-buy-fei-fei-lis-world-labs-8-2-billion">I reported</a>.</p><p>An expected IPO gusher, on the other hand, is at serious risk of turning into a drought. On Tuesday, health-tracking wearable company Oura said it was <a href="http://theinformation.com/briefings/oura-postpones-ipo-citing-uncertainty-ipo-market">delaying its IPO</a>, previously expected to take place later this week, blaming “market uncertainty.”</p>
