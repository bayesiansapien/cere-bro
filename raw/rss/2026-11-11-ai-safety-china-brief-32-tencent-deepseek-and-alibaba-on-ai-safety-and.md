# Brief #32: Tencent, DeepSeek and Alibaba on AI safety and open-source

<h3>Key Takeaways</h3><ul><li><p>Tencent says it filtered offensive cyber content from Hy4&#8217;s training data and that the model scored well below its high-risk threshold for biological misuse.</p></li><li><p>Tencent&#8217;s chief AI scientist urged pre-release safety testing. Alibaba and DeepSeek researchers also discussed open-weight risks.</p></li><li><p>Z.ai (Zhipu) co-founder Tang Jie urged researchers to be &#8220;humble&#8221; about recursive self-improvement (RSI) and called for international cooperation on safety.</p></li></ul><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://aisafetychina.substack.com/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><h3>Chinese AI labs are ramping up their open-weight risk management</h3><p><strong>What happened:</strong> Rising cyber capabilities appear to have prompted a more public discussion of open-weight risk management in China&#8217;s AI industry.</p><p><strong>The latest example: </strong>Tencent says it <a href="https://hy.tencent.ai/research/hy4">assessed the potential for cyber-offensive and biological misuse</a> in its latest flagship model, Hunyuan Hy4, released on November 4, and took steps to reduce those risks:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Cyber offense:</strong> Tencent limited cybersecurity training data to defensive material related to proof-of-concept (PoC) generation and vulnerability patching, while filtering out offensive content such as exploit development, reverse engineering, and penetration testing. Tencent&#8217;s internal cybersecurity teams found no elevated risk in their red-team tests of Hy4 on offensive tasks.</p></li><li><p><strong>Biosecurity:</strong> Hy4 scored &#8220;well below&#8221; the high-risk threshold on Tencent&#8217;s in-house benchmark of open-ended experimental design.<a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-anchor-1" target="_self">1</a> The company concluded that the model does not meaningfully lower the barrier to causing biological harm.</p></li></ul><p>Tencent has promised more detail on its safety evaluations in an upcoming technical report accompanying the Hy4 weight release.</p><p><strong>Four days after the release, YAO Shunyu (&#23002;&#39034;&#38632;), Tencent&#8217;s Chief AI Scientist, <a href="https://www.yicai.com/news/103431271.html">further emphasized the need for frontier safety</a> at a conference in Hong Kong. </strong>Per <a href="https://www.yicai.com/news/103431271.html">Yicai</a>:</p><blockquote><p>&#8220;Yao Shunyu noted that before a model is released, teams must carry out extensive cybersecurity testing to ensure it can be made available safely. <strong>He believes the cyberattack and biology-related capabilities of future models may reach a level sufficient to cause actual harm.</strong>&#8221;</p></blockquote><p>Yao argued that an ideal model should be strong in cyber <em>defense</em> and weak in <em>offense</em>. However, since the two capabilities cannot be fully separated, he said, safeguards are needed at every stage. During training, developers should curate their data and strengthen defensive capabilities. Before releasing a model or opening its weights, they should evaluate its safety. After launch, they should continue to monitor how it is used.</p><p><strong>In case you forgot:</strong> Yao is a <a href="https://news.southcn.com/node_179d29f1ce/9af84753d9.shtml">young superstar</a> in China&#8217;s AI industry. target="_self">2</a> earned a PhD at Princeton, and worked at OpenAI before joining Tencent in December 2025 to lead its LLM development.<a class="footnote-anchor" href="#footnote-3" As their models&#8217; cyber capabilities grow, industry leaders such as Yao Shunyu and Liu Dayiheng are speaking more publicly about the risks.</p><p><strong>But this does not mean that Chinese companies will abandon open-weight models.</strong> The companies discussed here continue to pursue open-weight releases, even as they filter training data, stage releases, or restrict access to particular versions. As we unpacked in our recent <a href="https://aisafetychina.substack.com/p/china-is-open-sourcing-its-best-ai">explainer</a>, the overarching assessment appears to be that the misuse risks from open models are manageable, and that their benefits &#8212; such as empowering cyber defenders &#8212; outweigh the risks. </p><div class="digest-post-embed"></div><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://aisafetychina.substack.com/p/brief-32-tencent-deepseek-and-alibaba?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share</span></a></p><h3>Also on our radar</h3><ul><li><p><strong>TANG Jie (&#21776;&#26480;), co-founder of Z.ai (Zhipu), <a href="https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3372834/self-improving-ai-key-step-towards-agi-safety-key-zai-founder">said</a> that researchers should be &#8220;humble&#8221; in the face of recursive self-improvement (RSI). </strong>He suggested &#8220;the world should organise something together so that we can guarantee the safety for not just one country but for all the world&#8221;. Tang&#8217;s remarks suggest that Z.ai&#8217;s leadership is considering the safety implications of <a href="https://z.ai/blog/glm-built-its-inference-infrastructure">their RSI strategy</a>.</p></li><li><p><strong>Foreign Minister WANG Yi (&#29579;&#27589;) <a href="https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbzhd/202610/t20261023_12046093.shtml">discussed</a> AI risks with Asia Society Co-Chair John Thornton and New York Times columnist Thomas Friedman. </strong>Per the MFA readout, the Americans called malicious use of AI the greatest risk to China-US stability. Wang said both countries have a responsibility to manage AI well and called for joint discussion of globally applicable rules and standards.</p></li><li><p><strong>China&#8217;s legislature <a href="https://www1.xinhuanet.com/politics/20261106/c4af584570ec48bd889cd079cae2e034/c.html">voted to ratify</a> the agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO)</strong>. China is WAICO&#8217;s initiator and host, and the vote brings the organization a step closer to being operational.</p></li><li><p><strong>The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) <a href="https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/xwdt/wszb/swwfbhpyzdxdn/">said</a> China would accelerate work on comprehensive AI legislation and develop safety standards covering the full AI life cycle. </strong>The government will regularly review how the rules work in practice and revise them as needed, while also aiming to lower companies&#8217; compliance costs. This reflects China&#8217;s longstanding view that AI development and safety can advance together.</p></li><li><p><strong>The World Internet Conference (WIC), China&#8217;s flagship internet conference, took place in Wuzhen November 2-5 with a heavy emphasis on AI, including safety.</strong></p><ul><li><p>In his <a href="https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202611/04/content_30189225.html">opening keynote</a>, <strong>Premier LI Qiang (&#26446;&#24378;)</strong> called for international cooperation on open-source development and argued that as AI becomes more humanlike, people must remain in control and machines must remain tools they can trust.</p></li><li><p>The conference&#8217;s <em><strong><a href="https://cn.wicinternet.org/pdf/%E3%80%8A%E6%90%BA%E6%89%8B%E6%9E%84%E5%BB%BA%E5%85%A8%E7%90%83%E5%BC%80%E6%94%BE%E4%BA%BA%E5%B7%A5%E6%99%BA%E8%83%BD%E8%A1%8C%E5%8A%A8%E5%80%A1%E8%AE%AE%E3%80%8B.pdf">Joint Initiative on Global Open AI Action</a></strong></em><strong> </strong>calls for shared computing resources and datasets, an open-source seed fund for teams in the Global South, and support for sovereign AI projects. It also proposes classifying open-source models by capability, applying safeguards by tier, and strengthening international cooperation on emergency response.</p></li><li><p>The <strong>AI Safety and Governance Program</strong> of the WIC Specialized Committee on AI released a <a href="https://www.wicinternet.org/pdf/FrontierAISafetyandGovernance2026RiskUnderstandingandCoordinatedGovernance.pdf">report</a> co-led by ZENG Yi (&#26366;&#27589;) and Se&#225;n &#211; h&#201;igeartaigh.
