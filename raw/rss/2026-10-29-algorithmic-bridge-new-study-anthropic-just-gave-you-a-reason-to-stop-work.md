At this point you will surely try to avoid the obvious choice&#8212;blue, which wins in most countries&#8212;but AI doesn&#8217;t bother with humans&#8217; universal need for uniqueness. I actually wanted to ask you a follow-up question: </p><p>If you happened to pick a different thing than AI in either of those cases, did you feel, if only for an instant, a pang of having chosen wrong? Let me ask it differently: Had this been a more serious matter rather than a kid&#8217;s game&#8212;like a work decision&#8212;would you change your answer if AI contradicted you?</p><p>This one, you can take your time. But odds are, you will lie to yourself.</p><p>A clever reader will think something along the lines of &#8220;preferences are often arbitrary, and when informed by analysis, it&#8217;s expertise that sets them in stone, so why would I change what I decide just because a machine disagrees?&#8221;</p><p>Well, brace yourself.</p><p>A <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/519c17a5d23a794e8b6d50a854048d0b61e0d73a.pdf">new Anthropic study</a> suggests most people would indeed change answers. It doesn&#8217;t matter much if you have stated a preference, you&#8217;d likely switch toward the AI&#8217;s choice anyway. Even worse, having done so, you will convince yourself it was all your unswayed decision. What Anthropic has found should radically change your perception of how much AI is actually influencing you. And it should change, hopefully, how you interact with AI moving forward.</p>
