# S&P Discovers Nvidia’s Governance Lapses

<p>Credit agencies aren’t known for being ahead of the pack when it comes to spotting weaknesses at companies. (If you don’t believe me, check out their role in <a href="https://www.tavakolistructuredfinance.com/credit-rating-agencies/">the 2008 financial crisis</a>.) But S&amp;P’s <a href="https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3667832">note on Wednesday</a>, alerting the market to the possibility it could downgrade Nvidia’s credit rating, sets new standards in stating the obvious.</p><p>S&amp;P declared that the recent drama surrounding CEO Jensen Huang “exposed vulnerabilities in Nvidia’s management and governance framework.” That’s true, but some of the vulnerabilities it cited, around “board structure, composition and effectiveness,” were hiding in plain sight. For instance, Nvidia’s board didn’t have a chairperson until two weeks ago, when Huang resigned and Harvey Jones was appointed Nvidia’s “first-ever chair.” (Huang was reinstated on Monday.) Nvidia explained in past securities filings that its board “ensures that each member has an equal voice in the affairs and management of NVIDIA by having an independent Lead Director, rather than a chairperson.” Some might say going without a chair empowered Huang—it’s a governance weakness that S&amp;P could have flagged in the past.</p>
