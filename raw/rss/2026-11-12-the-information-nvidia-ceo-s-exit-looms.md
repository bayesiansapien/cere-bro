# Nvidia CEO’s Exit Looms

<p>Well, that didn’t take long. Jensen Huang’s time as CEO of Nvidia appears to be coming to an end, <a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-is-preparing-to-oust-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-30f421ad?mod=hp_onevizplusstories_pos_1">according to multiple reports</a> that the board was meeting Wednesday night to consider his future. Just three days ago, it announced an inquiry into whether he was the “target of a foreign intelligence effort,” but at the time the board said it had “confidence in Jensen.” It seems likely that confidence was undermined by a <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html?searchResultPosition=1">New York Times story</a> on Wednesday that said Huang had had an affair with a company executive in addition to an industry executive who may also be a Chinese spy.&nbsp;</p><p>If the board dumps Huang, it will demonstrate that no CEO is indispensable, no matter how vital their role is. Many observers had insisted the opposite in recent days, perhaps understandably. After all, Huang co-founded the company and still owned 3.6% of the stock, making him one of the biggest shareholders. As we wrote <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/ceo-jensen-huang-runs-nvidia-with-a-strong-hand?rc=9byxri">in this piece</a>, he “has a hand in nearly every aspect of the company’s day-to-day operations.” Along with Sam Altman, he’s been probably the most articulate advocate for AI. Most importantly, his vision of the future has positioned Nvidia to benefit more than any other company from the AI boom, driving its market capitalization to a high of $5.8 trillion in early October, according to Koyfin.</p>
