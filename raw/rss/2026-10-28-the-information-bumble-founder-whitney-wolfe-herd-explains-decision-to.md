# Bumble Founder Whitney Wolfe Herd Explains Decision to Take Company Private

<p>Bumble cofounder and CEO Whitney Wolfe Herd said on Tuesday a sale of her 12-year-old dating app was important for the “for the longevity of Bumble’s mission” given the way the public markets had treated the company, whose stock had dropped to near $2.</p>

<p>Herd said she would “disassociate” watching the stock price but was newly optimistic about Bumble’s&nbsp;future after new tech investment firm Ithaca Holdings agreed to take the company private in a $1.3 billion deal announced earlier this month.</p>

<p>“I felt like I was stuck under a wave for like five years,” she said at <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/features/wtf-women-in-tech-media-and-finance?rc=zjctrx">The Information’s <u>WTF Summit</u></a><u>,</u> in her first interview since Bumble agreed to a sale. “But now I’m on the beach.”</p>
