# AI models often give the right answers but point to the wrong sources

<p><img alt="Graphic: AI tool highlights text passages in a PDF to identify errors and incorrect information." class="attachment-full size-full wp-post-image" height="1047" src="https://the-decoder.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CiteVQA-Spot-Error-PDF-Hallucination-Nano-Banana-Pro.jpg" style="height: auto; margin-bottom: 10px;" width="1920" /></p>
<p>        Leading AI models like GPT and Gemini routinely cite text passages in document analyses that don't actually support their answers. Even when the answer is right, the cited evidence is often wrong. Researchers at Peking University call this "attribution hallucination," a risk for regulated fields like law and medicine. Their new CiteVQA benchmark is the first to test for it systematically.</p>
