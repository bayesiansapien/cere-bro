# Meta Reaches AI’s Crowded Frontier—Will Users Care?

<p>If it’s Monday, it must be time for another AI model release. This time, it was Meta Platforms’ turn, <a href="https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder">unveiling Muse Wonder,</a> which it claims can “deliver state-of-the-art performance” on certain tasks. It was a triumphant moment for the company, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/meta-plans-fourth-restructuring-ai-efforts-six-months?rc=9byxri">whose AI efforts</a> previously went through a slump that prompted a massive hiring binge over the past 14 months or so. It now claims it is “pushing the frontier” of AI.</p><p>That would be notable if only everyone wasn’t claiming to be doing the same thing. Two weeks ago, <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/">Google unveiled</a> its latest model, Gemini 4 Argon, which it says “delivers frontier performance in complex workflows” across various subjects (you wouldn’t want it to deliver frontier performance in simple workflows, would you?). In early September, <a href="https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/">OpenAI announced GPT-6 Astra</a>, which it said was “state of the art on computer use, browsing, software engineering, cybersecurity, science and professional work.” Also in September, Anthropic <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1">introduced Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1</a>, which it claimed to be “the world’s most advanced models for coding and knowledge work.”</p>
