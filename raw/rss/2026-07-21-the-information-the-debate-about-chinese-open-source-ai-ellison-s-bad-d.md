# The Debate About Chinese Open-Source AI; Ellison’s Bad Day

<p>How protectionist is the Trump administration? Sure, President Donald Trump loves tariffs. But does he really want to drive up AI costs for U.S. business by banning Chinese open-source AI? Chatter about that is growing in the wake of a story <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/trumps-ai-agenda-collides-reality?rc=9byxri">we published on Saturday</a>, which revealed that the administration had discussed the idea of banning open source last year.&nbsp;</p><p>Axios, meanwhile, <a href="https://www.axios.com/2026/07/20/ai-us-china-open-source-kimi">reported on Monday</a> that last week’s release of Kimi 3 from Chinese firm Moonshot had stoked conversations about banning foreign open source AI (more on <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/new-kimi-k3-model-means-u-s-china-ai-race?rc=9byxri">the new Kimi model</a>). A ban would set off an uproar. More and more <a href="https://x.com/brian_armstrong/status/2070670644577280109?s=20">companies are finding</a> that ever-advancing Chinese models—such <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/new-kimi-k3-model-means-u-s-china-ai-race?rc=9byxri">as the one from Moonshot released last week</a>—are a good way to control their AI spending.&nbsp;</p>
