A large number of polls from Gallup, Pew Research Center, NBC News, the Washington Post, Change Research, Marquette Law School, and others have measured what Americans feel and think about AI, datacenters, AI companies, and the people who run them.</p><p>Everyone has a vague sense that Americans are turning against AI&#8212;more so than in any other country&#8212;but no one has put together the full map. It&#8217;s lost in fruitless discussions on X, localized anecdotes and repetitive news stories.</p><p>This is the full map.</p><p>I&#8217;ve gathered every major piece of survey data I could find.</p><p>The global conclusion that emerges from this compilation is simple and, as far as I can tell, unprecedented: every available measure&#8212;partisan, demographic, regional, longitudinal&#8212;points in the same direction.</p><p>Here&#8217;s the outline:</p><ol><li><p><strong>AMERICANS DON&#8217;T WANT DATACENTERS NEAR THEM</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>AMERICANS DON&#8217;T TRUST AI IN GENERAL</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>AMERICANS DON&#8217;T LIKE THE PEOPLE BUILDING AI</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>AI HAS BECOME A BIPARTISAN PROBLEM</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>WHERE THE AI BACKLASH HAS MATERIALIZED</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>WHAT WE DON&#8217;T KNOW</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>GLOBAL CONCLUSIONS</strong></p></li></ol><p>When someone asks what we know about the general sentiment against AI, you show them this compilation. When someone argues that anecdotal evidence is not enough, you show them this. AMERICANS DON&#8217;T WANT DATACENTERS NEAR THEM</h3><p>Multiple pollsters have asked versions of this question between 2025 and 2026, with remarkably consistent results. WHAT WE DON&#8217;T KNOW</h3><p>Four critical gaps remain in the data:</p><ul><li><p><strong>No baseline to compare.</strong> Gallup&#8217;s data-center question was asked for the first time in March 2026, so there&#8217;s no trend line. The Change Research and Washington Post&#8211;Schar comparisons offer before/after snapshots, but there is no systematic annual tracking of data-center sentiment across the same population over time. We don&#8217;t know what the starting point used to look like nationally.</p></li><li><p><strong>Causation is unmapped.</strong> Americans oppose data centers, dislike AI CEOs, and distrust AI broadly, but which is driving which? Did the CEO favorability collapse poison attitudes toward datacenters by repeatedly claiming AI will take everyone&#8217;s jobs? Or did local datacenter fights poison attitudes toward CEOs, which were already pretty dismal? Did the broader AI anxiety create the conditions for local opposition, or did local opposition generate the broader anxiety? The correlations are clear but not so much the causal arrows.</p></li><li><p><strong>An (un)informed opposition.</strong> What do opponents actually know about datacenters? I&#8217;ve intentionally left this question out: for instance, Gallup&#8217;s open-ended responses show people citing water, energy, pollution, and utility bills, but no poll has tested whether respondents can distinguish accurate from inaccurate claims about datacenter impacts. This matters for predicting whether the backlash is durable and immutable or responsive to new evidence, but the opposition is real anyway: it shapes votes and kills projects whether or not it is factually grounded.</p></li><li><p><strong>The election hypothesis.</strong> No American election has yet been won or lost on AI. The 2026 midterms will be the first natural experiment on that. Until the votes are counted, the claim by multiple analysts that AI backlash translates into electoral outcomes is just a prediction. Cost of living still dominates voter priorities, and AI&#8217;s role may be as an issue <em>underneath</em> economic anxiety rather than above it.</p></li></ul><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2" href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P9WP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4638f229-39eb-42c2-9200-e7e5c0cfad03_963x89.png" target="_blank"><div class="image2-inset"><source type="image/webp" /><img alt="" class="sizing-normal" height="89" src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!P9WP!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4638f229-39eb-42c2-9200-e7e5c0cfad03_963x89.png" width="963" /><div></div></div></a></figure></div><h3>VII. GLOBAL CONCLUSIONS</h3><h4>Core finding: every available measure converges on a collapse of public opinion</h4><p>Across every pollster&#8212;Gallup, Pew, Change Research, the Washington Post, Marquette, Morning Consult, Heatmap, NBC News, Politico, the University of Maryland&#8212;the direction is the same: Americans have soured on AI datacenters, on AI itself, and on the people building it.</p><p>It happened faster than the backlash against nuclear power, than the backlash against wind farms, or any comparable infrastructure opposition in the modern polling era. Change Research measured a 40-point net swing in a single year. In three years, the Washington Post&#8211;Schar poll captured a 34-point collapse in Virginia, the one state that should be most favorable to data centers due to associated benefits. Morning Consult found support for an outright nationwide ban rising 4 points in one month. This is a collapse of public opinion, not a gradual shift.</p><p>The speed makes this story urgent; the convergence makes it important.</p><p>When every pollster, using different samples, different question wordings, different modes (telephone, online, in-person), and different time points, finds the same thing, you have a consensus. The opposition to AI infrastructure is bipartisan in composition and only varies slightly in intensity by party, gender, and region. It is not limited to abstract attitudes&#8212;it has materialized in $156 billion of disrupted projects, 188+ opposition groups, 300+ state bills, and federal legislation&#8212;and even the industry&#8217;s own political spending strategy treats the AI brand as too toxic to defend on-message.</p><p>When they dare defend it, though, the reaction is as homogeneous as the polls suggest. Below is a video of what happens when a commencement speaker talks to university students about AI (featured: Eric Schmidt, former Google CEO; Scott Borchetta, Big Machine Records CEO; Gloria Caulfield, Tavistock Development VP):</p><div class="native-video-embed"></div><p>Two things worth noting from this.</p><ol><li><p>This <a href="https://youtu.be/FZh_0uRgrg4">doesn&#8217;t</a> <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:ugcPost:7462259441602764801/?dashCommentUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afsd_comment%3A%287462339402971705345%2Curn%3Ali%3AugcPost%3A7462259441602764801%29">happen</a> in STEM schools (might be the only exception). This was quite shocking to me: <a href="https://news.gallup.com/poll/708224/gen-adoption-steady-skepticism-climbs.aspx">Gallup</a> found that Gen Z high usage has not decreased despite anger having increased. They know what they&#8217;re protesting against <em>because</em> they know what AI is doing to them.</p></li></ol><p>It&#8217;s booing today, what will be tomorrow? I don&#8217;t want to sound alarmist but there&#8217;s only one good moment to warn about violence: too soon. As I wrote in &#8220;<a href="https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/ai-will-be-met-with-violence-and">AI Will Be Met With Violence, and Nothing Good Will Come of It</a>&#8221;:</p><blockquote><p>There&#8217;s a lot of friction to escalating violence, but that friction dissolves the moment this sentiment starts to be common. Normally, it just fades away anyway, but there&#8217;s one scenario where I see it inevitably escalating:</p><p>If people feel that they have no place in the future.</p></blockquote><p>This is America screaming they are being left behind and they don&#8217;t want to be left behind.</p><p>But why has the sentiment changed so much so rapidly? Why have the poll results on datacenters and AI degraded so intensely over the past year? Why now?</p><p>The underlying dynamic is obscured by the intensity of the backlash but my general reading is this: the sentiment <em>was always there</em> but it only became legible after it reached a certain critical mass. The snowball needs to get big before it starts rolling down the mountain&#8217;s side. Once it does, though, it rapidly turns into an unstoppable avalanche.</p><p>We&#8217;re living through the first moments of that avalanche.</p><p>But let me give you a more specific take. I think it can be of value to the industry, if anyone there is hearing the rest of the world anymore or their unprecedented success has fully deafened them.</p><p>Americans never got to form an opinion about AI gradually. They didn&#8217;t get to see both good and bad over time, balancing them out. There are various reasons for this.</p><ol><li><p>The technology arrived as a cultural event&#8212;ChatGPT in November 2022 &#8220;took the world by storm,&#8221; as they say&#8212;and within three or four years, it was everywhere.</p></li><li><p>The industry has force-fed AI to everyone; companies are cramming it into every existing digital service to retroactively rationalize the massive spending.</p></li><li><p>They keep warning that we&#8217;ll lose our jobs as they keep enthusiastically rolling out the very products responsible for the presumed mass unemployment (Claude Code and Codex and etc.).</p></li><li><p>They claim to be worried if the full-blown commercial race goes wrong (e.g., recursive self-improvement) but then allude to competition pressures to keep going.</p></li><li><p>They wreaked havoc on the digital landscape with <a href="https://substack.com/@thealgorithmicbridge/note/c-246008752">insane amounts of slop</a>.</p></li><li><p>And after all of that, they&#8217;re asking for permission to reshape the physical world: the landmass, the electrical grid, the water supply, the view from people&#8217;s backyards.</p></li></ol><p>The speed of the buildout outpaced the speed at which people could form a considered view and the natural response to sudden change with dangerous undertones is resistance and, to whatever degree possible, organized counterattack. Factuality is an afterthought, if at all.</p><p>Social media allows unprecedented speeds for sentiment to spread&#8212;as well as information and misinformation&#8212;but I believe the AI industry&#8217;s unfortunate messaging was the primary cause.</p><p>None of this tells us whether the backlash is permanent, though.</p><p>The nuclear analogy is instructive but cuts both ways: nuclear power lost public trust in the 1970s and never fully recovered, which suggests infrastructure opposition can be durable for decades. On the other hand, nuclear opposition was reinforced by Three Mile Island and Chernobyl, specific, catastrophic events that validated the fear.</p><p>The AI backlash has no equivalent singular disaster (yet); it&#8217;s driven by diffuse anxieties about energy, water, jobs, utility bills, corporate power, and the speed of change. Whether the industry course-corrects before a crystallizing event, or whether the diffuse anxiety <em>becomes</em> the crystallizing event through accumulated experience is an open question&#8212;one they shouldn&#8217;t take long to answer.</p><p>Otherwise, these polls will soon be regarded as the beginning of the end.</p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2" href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LFth!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd470dde0-72df-4985-8b2c-3ffe9b77eef6_963x89.png" target="_blank"><div class="image2-inset"><source type="image/webp" /><img alt="" class="sizing-normal" height="89" src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LFth!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd470dde0-72df-4985-8b2c-3ffe9b77eef6_963x89.png" width="963" /><div></div></div></a></figure></div><p><em>This compilation took a lot of work.
