Hypothesis</a></figcaption></figure></div><p>The stunning news Friday, just after the market closed, was that Levent Alp&#246;ge <a href="https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf">appears to have proven the Riemann Hypothesis with the help of Anthropic&#8217;s Claude</a>. </p><p>This is truly, genuinely amazing.</p><p>But it has also (as is often the case) been widely misinterpreted.</p><p>To begin with,  on social media, many people seem to think that  AI *alone* solved the Riemann Hypothesis.  This is not the case. </p><p>Indeed, as far as I can tell, current AI could <em>not</em> have done it on its own. </p><p>Critically, the solution required extensive <em>human</em> guidance from Alp&#246;ge, an exceptionally talented mathematician with expertise in AI. He worked <em>together</em> with the AI to jointly solve RH, over a period of months.  </p><p>As AI math pioneer Christian Szegedy put it, &#8220;Levent made all the right (life) choices at the right time&#8221; in order to achieve the result, and we wouldn&#8217;t be here otherwise. He didn&#8217;t just type in a prompt &#8220;<em>Solve Riemann</em>&#8221;, and get the final answer. His contributions in shaping the end product were considerable.</p><p>Fundamentally, then, a brilliant mathematician with brilliant instincts drove a deeply collaborative process. </p><p>Importantly, the results do not mean that AGI has arrived, or anything like that. (If it had, you wouldn&#8217;t have needed the human mathematician to guide the whole process.)  </p><p>It doesn&#8217;t even mean the result is that useful or technically innovative; it is 250,000 pages (hardly elegant), and no general lessons about how to do math have yet been drawn, nor (so far as I have heard) have any new techniques or concepts yet been introduced.</p><p>Most importantly (from the perspective of gauging future impact), Alp&#246;ge and the Claude AI system relied heavily on a unique set of resources that  you won&#8217;t find in a field like medicine. The solution depended heavily on not only on Alp&#246;ge&#8217;s considerable talents but also on a special-purpose symbolic AI solving system, <a href="https://lean-lang.org/">Lean</a>, and a vast math-specific library, <a href="https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/mathlib/">mathlib</a>, built to work with it. The latter, mathlib, consists of &#8220;<a href="https://lean-lang.org/use-cases/mathlib/">over two million lines of formalized mathematics&#8221; built over multiple years</a> by hundreds if not thousands of people.</p><p>That library is fantastic for math, but also specialized for math. It is presumably of relatively little value for solving more open-ended problems in domains like biology, business, or robotics.  </p><p>And critically Lean and mathlib revolve around formal verifiability, which is straightforward in math but difficult to achieve elsewhere. Exactly as <a href="https://x.com/garymarcus/status/1870117249371017619?a=Q0rTFuHxJk15gzFA9dQ&amp;s=61">I predicted in 2024</a>, verifiable methods have helped more with math than with other domains. </p><p>In short, it&#8217;s not at all obvious that whatever Alp&#246;ge did has the generality many people are hoping for.</p><p>As Jeremy Kahn  put it &#8220;Solving RH doesn&#8217;t help you negotiate a contract better or correctly figure out a corporate tax return. So no real change to enterprise value prop.&#8221; Or in Google DeepMind&#8217;s Sebastien Krier&#8217;s words, &#8220;Solving an old pure math problem does not immediately widen Anthropic&#8217;s operating margins&#8221;.</p><p style="text-align: center;">&#167;</p><p>It&#8217;s feels to me kind of like that moment when DeepBlue beat Garry Kasparov in chess in 1997: an absolutely amazing use of brute force in a special-purpose way that didn&#8217;t generalize to all that much else.  A lot of people wrote about how it would change the world; it didn&#8217;t. </p><p>The market seems to get all of this&#8211; <a href="https://app.hyperliquid.xyz/trade/xyz:ANT">RH hasn&#8217;t moved Anthropic stock much after hours</a> &#8212;even if a lot of AI fans don&#8217;t.</p><p>Clinging to the Riemann results like a life raft after the stock market correction&#8211; as AI hypesters on social media are doing&#8212;doesn&#8217;t change the fact that (a) current AI still isn&#8217;t trustworthy enough to for many applications and (b) is still not profitable enough to warrant the massive investments.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/subscribe?"><span>Subscribe now</span></a></p><p></p>
