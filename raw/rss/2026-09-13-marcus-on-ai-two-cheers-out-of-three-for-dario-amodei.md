wrote</a> what may prove to be one of the most consequential essays of the year, if not the decade: 3,500 words on the current AI situation that (at least to some degree) advocates for a &#8220;pacing&#8221; of AI development, which Sam Altman and Elon Musk quickly endorsed.</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>We should all salute their tentative agreement to slow down the acceleration of this technology, given that not one of the AI companies seems to have good control over it. I hope they will sign in blood, and put actual teeth in their agreement, and that the government will hold them to it and force it to become something substantive. If Dario catalyzes all that, god bless him.</p><p>And I especially love Dario&#8217;s commitment to transparency; Sam seems on board and I hope Elon is down for that, too. </p><p>But yeah, there are some reasons to be cynical, too. </p><p style="text-align: center;">&#182;</p><p>To begin with, the essay begins with the usual hypey bullshit, which is used to advertise generative AI. As I already noted here yesterday, that includes both hyperbolic doom and hyperbolic cheer.  AI <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/could-rogue-agent-swarms-take-over?r=8tdk6">is not really going to take down the internet</a>, and (as discussed in another earlier essay) it&#8217;s <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/no-dario-amodei-we-will-not-be-curing?r=8tdk6&amp;utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&amp;utm_medium=web">not going to cure cancer either, at least not anytime soon or on its own</a>.</p><p>Then there is the self-indulgent construction of the whole thing, which David Sacks well-deconstructed in <a href="https://x.com/davidsacks/status/2098973625252708460?s=61">a long tweet yesterday</a>.
