# OpenAI Preps ‘Athena’ Data Tool to Make its Agents More Accurate

<p>In its effort to win more business customers, OpenAI this fall launched an agent designed to help firms analyze their data and get answers to internal questions about their business. But at least one customer that has used the agent so far has found it isn’t yet accurate enough for widespread use.</p><p>That’s a big problem. But OpenAI isn’t sitting still. Representatives of the ChatGPT creator have told some customers and partners that they are developing a data management platform to help the agent perform better, according to the customer and three other people who spoke to OpenAI in recent weeks.</p><p>The data management platform, code-named Athena, has similarities to what is known in the industry as a semantic layer, which companies use to create standardized definitions for business metrics such as revenue and customers, one of the people said. This type of software helps agents understand the difference between gross revenue and net revenue, or active and paying customers, the person added.</p><p>One of the biggest reasons agents fail when companies use them with large amounts of complex business data is that they have to work out on their own what the data means and how it fits together, said Jason Strimpel, a managing director at Andersen Consulting who helps large companies use AI.&nbsp;</p><p>“A semantic layer gives the agent that context from the start, often with plain-English descriptions of each item, so the agent has to guess less and [can work] faster and more accurately,” he said.&nbsp;</p>
