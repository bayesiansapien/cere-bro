# OpenAI’s Roadmap To Making Cyber Bigger Than Coding Remains Blurry

<p>Tech stocks, including <b>Nvidia</b>, were trading down early Tuesday, as the enthusiasm sparked by <b>Jensen Huang’s</b> unexpected reinstatement as CEO dissipated. On Monday, stocks had <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/tech-stocks-rally-across-board">rallied</a> strongly across the board, lifting the <b>Nasdaq 100</b> index 6.8%. This story has been full of twists and turns… What else could happen?</p><p>Meanwhile, a series of high-profile cyberincidents in recent weeks have affected banks, government institutions and other critical infrastructure—including, most recently, the <b>Federal Bureau of Investigation’s</b> national crime database, which the <b>White House</b> confirmed over the weekend was targeted in a hack.</p><p>Though it’s still unclear whether AI played a role in some of these breaches, the attacks have intensified fears that the technology is emboldening cyber criminals. For <b>OpenAI</b>, this brewing fear around cybersecurity also represents something else: the company’s next big business opportunity.</p><p>The potential of cybersecurity as a revenue driver—as OpenAI seeks to bolster its finances ahead of a potential initial public offering next year—was a key theme that emerged from my conversations with OpenAI’s leaders <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/brockman-pushes-openai-toward-cybersecurity-and-showdown-ai-safety">for a profile of the company’s president, Greg Brockman,</a> which we published last week.&nbsp;</p>
