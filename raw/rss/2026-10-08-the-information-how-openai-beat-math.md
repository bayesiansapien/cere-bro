# How OpenAI Beat Math

<p>Mathematicians are likely feeling the kind of anxiety that software engineers have experienced for months in the wake of <b>OpenAI</b> release on Tuesday of a whopping 722 research papers with AI-created solutions to a range of problems across math subjects.&nbsp;</p><p>AI’s success solving ultra-difficult math problems—even though it still makes mistakes doing simple math—is due to several factors. Solutions to math problems can often be verified, much like software code, and there’s a lot of overlap between mathematics and the work that AI researchers do (and which these researchers are trying to automate in pursuit of <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/google-deepmind-exec-says-unprecedented-capex-actually-bet-rsi?rc=c48ukx">RSI</a>).</p><p>And with enough computing resources, the AI can essentially do the heavy-duty number crunching that would take humans years to do, and it doesn’t give up. One mathematician who is part of a group advising OpenAI on how to publicly disclose its math breakthroughs told me that they refer to these abilities as brute force and stamina.</p>
