For many, the turning point seems to be the events of the last few days, when much of the industry but not Anthropic came out vigorously for &#8220;open-weight&#8221; models (which can be downloaded, given sufficient hardware, but which are not fully open source, because e.g. their training sets are generally not publicly available). Many signed a letter; Amodei instead issued a statement explaining his resistance, which many saw as tone-deaf and self-serving, in part because the statement called for locking down distillation by competitors even as it just came out that <a href="https://x.com/hedgiemarkets/status/2081534588485296565?s=61">the company has been destroying rare books in bulk in order to distill their contents</a>. </p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>I found myself (somewhat unusually) heartily agreeing with David Sacks:</p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p>All told, it would appear that most of the goodwill that Amodei accumulated in standing up to Hegseth has dissipated, much as most of the goodwill towards Altman eventually did. </p><p>Maybe one or the other, Altman or Amodei, will regain the public&#8217;s trust, but it is far from obvious that they will. In Altman&#8217;s case, I think dishonesty and lack of candor is the core issue; with good reason, people just don&#8217;t trust him anymore. For Amodei, who maybe means well, I think the issue is more one of tone deafness and self-certainty, coupled with a tendency to present arguments that frequently appear self-serving. </p><p>With apologies for the AI slop (the text at the bottom is particularly hilarious), I made a meme in their honor.
