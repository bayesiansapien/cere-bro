# Why Price Cuts May Rattle Anthropic's IPO Pitch

<p>Anthropic’s IPO paperwork is expected to become public sometime soon. But it was OpenAI finance chief Sarah Friar who drew a standing-room-only crowd Tuesday at San Francisco’s Palace Hotel, as Goldman Sachs kicked off its annual Communacopia + Technology conference with a Q&amp;A with Friar. In her talk, Friar made clear how important price has become for determining model popularity—a potential problem for Anthropic, widely seen as <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/applied-ai/anthropic-customers-bills-80-higher-need-glean-says?rc=9byxri">the high-end AI provider.</a></p><p>Friar described how a price cut on OpenAI’s GPT 5.6 Luna model led to a tenfold increase in use, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/openai-cfo-touts-user-growth-resulting-lowering-prices?rc=9byxri">my colleague Anita Ramaswamy reported</a>, giving OpenAI the highest market share on OpenRouter, a service developers use to access different models. We wrote <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/openai-makes-gains-anthropic-among-openrouter-customers?rc=9byxri">in mid-August</a> that Luna had generated more than twice the token usage of Anthropic’s Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 models on OpenRouter. Judging from Friar’s comments Tuesday, the increase in usage was enough to offset the price cut.</p>
