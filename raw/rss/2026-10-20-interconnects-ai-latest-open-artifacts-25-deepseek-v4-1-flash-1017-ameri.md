# Latest open artifacts (#25): DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, American-made models, and new regulatory controls in the US & China

<p>The geopolitics of open-weight AI are accelerating again this fall, as we would expect with the risks of all AI systems (open and closed) rising rapidly. We&#8217;ve seen many news items in the last week showing both the U.S. and Chinese governments positioning themselves for some controls on open models.</p><ol><li><p>MOFCOM, the Ministry of Commerce of the People&#8217;s Republic of China, released a policy proposal (it&#8217;s not yet a law) that would require <a href="https://x.com/zephyr_z9/status/2111071903469478291">frontier AI models</a> and some other technologies to get pre-approval from the government before they can be shared internationally. We found the reaction to this to be overblown &#8211; as the document states that open-source technologies will be <a href="https://x.com/ZilanQian/status/2111073916194013274">set up for automatic approvals</a> &#8211; but many in geopolitical circles say this is a big deal because it proposes a regulatory framework that could be used in the future to regulate China&#8217;s AI industry. <br /><br />Others we trust on this issue say this proposal could be more about preventing issues like the <a href="https://www.npr.org/2026/04/27/g-s1-118892/china-blocks-meta-from-acquiring-ai-startup-manus">sale of Chinese startup Manus</a> to Western companies, rather than being about frontier AI models. Either way, the wheels of control are moving around AI, and open-weight models are not free from any influence.</p></li><li><p>The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) released an <a href="https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-21447.pdf">order on DeepSeek</a>, which restricts DeepSeek itself, as a company, and DeepSeek&#8217;s models, as a technology platform, from being a vendor in federal civilian procurement. It is not clear how this will relate to finetuned models or models trained on the outputs of DeepSeek (e.g., Nvidia&#8217;s Nemotron models have long relied heavily on DeepSeek models in their training recipes &#8211; will they be banned?), but this is an expected action and reinforces the need for a domestic open model ecosystem. <br /><br />Other model makers in China, such as Zhipu AI, makers of GLM, are already on the Entity List under Commerce/BIS, which restricts their access to American technology.</p></li></ol><p>DeepSeek followed the above news up by releasing their internal inference stack publicly, <a href="https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer">DeepInfer</a>, which is likely far more efficient than existing open-source tools like vLLM/SGLang for their models. <a href="https://x.com/zephyr_z9/status/2112499092031185134?s=46">Estimates</a> point to DeepSeek making up to 90% gross margin when selling tokens on this inference stack. It is well-known that the top AI labs have much more inference-efficient tools internally, and this is the first time we&#8217;ve seen a leading lab share them. Inference companies will be quick to adopt these tools, which will drive prices for DeepSeek&#8217;s models down across the ecosystem (or margins up). While not an open model or dataset, we consider this release to be one of the bigger open-source AI artifacts in the last few months.</p><p class="button-wrapper"><a class="button primary" href="https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-25-deepseek?utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email&amp;utm_content=share&amp;action=share"><span>Share</span></a></p><p>As of writing this, Kimi K3.1 is rumored to be released very soon, with metadata associated with it <a href="https://x.com/mylifcc/status/2112397528272695299">starting to appear in public libraries</a>. Exciting times!</p><p>You can view the models in this email, and all previous issues, on our Artifacts Hub.
