# Breaking: Demis Hassabis endorses preflight safety testing for AI

<p>I am genuinely excited. </p><p>In my <a href="https://www.techpolicy.press/transcript-senate-judiciary-subcommittee-hearing-on-oversight-of-ai/">2023 Senate testimony</a>,  in <a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/is-it-time-to-hit-the-pause-button?r=8tdk6">a 2023 essay here</a>, and in my 2024 book <em><a href="https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/5828/Taming-Silicon-ValleyHow-We-Can-Ensure-That-AI?__cf_chl_rt_tk=GrWhD9iiX7vQLMUUogReFS4qOD96mYmeWP5gA7Ee6kM-1784038139-1.0.1.1-CW2Tds3gTBgFLXlgS0gPKwqGAvyT1keqMHSGap7hmxo">Taming Silicon Valley</a></em>, I strongly advocated for a system of preflight testing for AI models deployed at large scale, ideally mandatory, transparent and independent, modeled perhaps on the FDA&#8217;s analysis of costs and benefits for pharmaceuticals. When Senator Kennedy asked for my most urgent suggestion for AI policy, preflight testing was it.</p><p>At the time, back in 2023, the Senate seemed receptive. But nothing happened, and things changed markedly when Trump came to office. As recently as a few months ago,  getting the US to implement preflight testing seemed hopeless, given a great hostility to AI regulation that was then widespread in Washington. </p><p>But the Mythos moment got The White House to consider and in fact implement preflight testing to some degree (<a href="https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/what-washington-must-do">though not with the transparency or independence or breadth or mandatoriness that I believe to be critica</a>l). </p><p>Now, I am thrilled today to report that Google DeepMind&#8217;s CEO Sir Demis Hassabis <a href="https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2076957440109625718?s=61">has just come out strongly and publicly in favor a version of preflight testing</a>. </p><div
