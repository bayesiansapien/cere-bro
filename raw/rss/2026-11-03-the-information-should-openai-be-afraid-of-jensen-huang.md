# Should OpenAI Be Afraid of Jensen Huang?

<p>News flash: A lot of people in Silicon Valley are afraid of <b>Nvidia</b> CEO <b>Jensen Huang</b>.</p><p>They have understandable reasons to be fearful, as Leo, Phoebe and I detailed in our <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/nvidia-uses-soft-power-influence-customers-shape-policy">story</a> this morning. The chipmaker has a lot of money and influence to throw around, since Huang has the ear of several top government officials, including <b>President Trump</b>.&nbsp;</p><p>More importantly for model developers like <b>OpenAI</b>, Nvidia controls the flow of AI chips they need to develop and run today’s most advanced models. It’s been hard enough for AI developers to get access to those chips, so it makes sense that the companies would want to avoid anything that might upset Huang.</p><p>This has led to a situation where OpenAI has avoided taking a public stance on the current debate over chip sales to China for fear of alienating Nvidia and Huang, which generates plenty of revenue from Chinese companies and wants access to the Chinese market itself. (Nvidia isn’t allowed to sell its most advanced tech directly to that market, but Chinese firms like <b>ByteDance</b> and <b>DeepSeek</b> still find a way to access the chips outside China’s borders or through <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/smugglers-get-expensive-memory-chips-china-south-korea">smuggled hardware</a>.)</p>
