BUILDING CASTLES IN THE AIR</h3><p>Half of the revenue backlog reported by four of the largest companies on earth comes from two startups fully dependent on the funding and patronage of those four companies. The circularity of the AI industry strikes again.</p><p>On Tuesday, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-commits-spending-200-billion-googles-cloud-chips">The Information reported</a> that Anthropic has committed to spending $200 billion on Google Cloud over five years. Google&#8217;s stock climbed ~2% in after-hours trading. Alphabet is, at the time of writing, the most valuable company on earth. At the same time, it is investing <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/google-plans-invest-up-40-billion-anthropic-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-04-24/">$40 billion in Anthropic</a>, $30 billion of which is compute credits.</p><p>So, Google is giving Anthropic cloud credits. Anthropic commits to spending them and a lot of extra revenue on Google&#8217;s cloud. Google books that as revenue backlog. Google stock goes up. Oracle: <a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/openai-oracle-sign-300-billion-computing-deal-among-biggest-in-history-ff27c8fe">$300 billion over five years</a>. Amazon: <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001018724/000101872426000012/amzn-20260331xex991.htm">$138 billion</a>. OpenAI&#8217;s committed cloud spend exceeds $688 billion. Oracle&#8217;s cloud revenue backlog <a href="https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2025/Oracle-Announces-Fiscal-Year-2026-Second-Quarter-Financial-Results/default.aspx">surged 438% year-over-year to $523 billion</a>, largely driven by its relationship with OpenAI.</p><p>Across Microsoft, Oracle, Google, and Amazon, OpenAI and Anthropic together account for roughly half of <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-commits-spending-200-billion-googles-cloud-chips">more than $2 trillion in cloud backlog</a>. The four hyperscalers plan to spend <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-30/us-big-tech-ratchets-up-ai-spending-past-700-billion-this-year">around $725 billion</a> on AI infrastructure in 2026 alone, nearly twice as much as in 2025. Anthropic and OpenAI, but we also know that, on the one hand, power users change AI provider every couple of months (which means that, using run-rate revenue, OpenAI and Anthropic are counting some users more than once), and, on the other hand, AI revenue can increase or decrease over time depending on whether clients and consumers manage to make a return on their investment.</p><p>So far, Anthropic and OpenAI are meeting revenue targets, but here&#8217;s the truth: A product that&#8217;s highly attractive and worth its price would have the same revenue profile as a product that&#8217;s highly attractive <em>and unreliable at the same time.</em> We just don&#8217;t know which one AI will be next year.</p><p>The circularity is, in other words, the financing mechanism for a technology <em>too expensive</em> for any single company to build alone and <em>too unreliable</em> as to be applied to real-world tasks to the degree that its theoretical capabilities would suggest.</p><p>I don&#8217;t dispute the fact that the &#8220;circularity&#8221; is both legal and logical&#8212;and can be good if it works out!&#8212;in the sense Amodei claims. What I dispute is whether his confidence is justified.</p><h3>III. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT</h3><p>The AI industry needs one thing to survive: that the run-rate revenue they claim transforms into actual revenue. Which will only happen if enterprise clients and individual consumers outside the loop find it worth paying for AI products at scale in the long run. That&#8217;s the only metric that can tell us something about the near-term future of the AI industry.</p><p>Turns out that, beyond Anthropics&#8217; and OpenAI&#8217;s own usage measurements, beyond independent reliability benchmarks, beyond deals, projected revenues, spending commitments, and infrastructure CapEx&#8212;beyond all of that, the data from the real world tells two conflicting stories.</p>
