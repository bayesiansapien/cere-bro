Despite an <a href="https://9to5google.com/2024/05/14/google-io-2024-keynote-ai-competition/#:~:text=But%20even%20though%20the%20event,about%20the%20same%20topic%20%E2%80%93%20AI.">underwhelming I/O event</a> earlier this year, several <a href="https://x.com/NoamShazeer/status/2067400851438932297">heavyweight departures</a>, and a <a href="https://x.com/emollick/status/2075068836856913928">slower model release pace</a> than its competitors, Google is still alive. It is out of the race because it <em>withdrew</em>. Weird, right? Why would Google do that willingly?</p><p>To answer that, I&#8217;m going to tell you the story of why and how Google left the AI race that OpenAI and Anthropic <em>are betting everything on</em>.</p><p>Here&#8217;s my hypothesis, stated plainly: Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis&#8212;a pioneer of AI as we understand it today&#8212;doesn&#8217;t think that automating AI research with coding agents (AI systems that can program better AI systems) is the correct approach to artificial general intelligence (AGI, the kind of AI that&#8217;s as good as humans at everything). Google DeepMind&#8217;s leadership sees OpenAI and Anthropic&#8217;s bet at best as an off-ramp, and at worst as a dead end.</p><p>Hassabis is betting on something else: world models. Models that can understand and simulate the real world, not just predict the next token.</p><p>And he&#8217;s steering Google accordingly. The AI race is actually a race between two theories of intelligence <em>and also </em>between two kinds of company: startups that need AI to become a business, and an incumbent whose existing business can subsidize AI for years. As we will see, Hassabis&#8217;s decision puts Google in a life-or-death situation. He could make Google the absolute leader or an irrelevant laggard.</p><p>That said, to write Google off the story would be a serious mistake. I&#8217;m going to explain why I think Google has a unique chance here.</p><p>You won&#8217;t see official confirmation about my hypothesis; as far as the executive is concerned, Google is still competing directly with OpenAI and Anthropic. I contend that&#8217;s not quite true anymore. Follow the breadcrumbs with me. I&#8217;ll put them together into a coherent picture. The evidence is public, but that doesn&#8217;t mean I&#8217;m right. This is informed speculation, so please, take it with a grain of salt.</p><p>If I manage to change your view of <em>what the hell Google is doing</em>, then I&#8217;ll be satisfied with my work.</p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link image2" href="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iBQx!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd0387f4c-5932-44fb-94c7-2ceb37330efe_963x89.png" target="_blank"><div class="image2-inset"><source type="image/webp" /><img alt="" class="sizing-normal" height="89" src="https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iBQx!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd0387f4c-5932-44fb-94c7-2ceb37330efe_963x89.png" width="963" /><div></div></div></a></figure></div><h3>I. AS ANTHROPIC AND OPENAI RUSH FORWARD</h3><p>The first piece of the puzzle is that Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman think that AI models will soon be autonomously improving themselves&#8212;this is known as &#8220;recursive self-improvement&#8221; (RSI): model A makes a better model B that makes a better model C, etc.&#8212;and that this is the key to achieving AGI.</p><p>The best approach to pursue this goal is also the simplest: build datacenters and use brute force on algorithms that can be scaled that way, like learning and search. That&#8217;s the fundamental insight of <a href="http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html">Richard Sutton&#8217;s Bitter Lesson</a>, which both OpenAI and Anthropic hold sacred. If new architectures or algorithms are needed, those better models, equipped with a lot of compute, will find them somehow.</p><p>That&#8217;s the theory. What does this creed look like inside the lab? We only need to read their testimonies.</p><p>You have engineering staff at both companies admitting that they <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement">barely write any code</a> anymore. It is swarms of coding agents&#8212;Claude Code and Codex&#8212;that do. Employees have become <a href="https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/how-ai-is-taking-away-your-ability">managers of agents</a> rather than developers and researchers. <a href="https://x.com/bcherny/status/2004897269674639461">Claude Code writes Claude Code</a> and <a href="https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/claude-code-coded-claude-cowork">wrote Claude Cowork</a>. GPT-5.6 Sol &#8220;<a href="https://x.com/larrylv/status/2075279982847541278">autonomously post-trained</a>&#8221; GPT-5.6 Luna. They say straight away that &#8220;<a href="https://x.com/LiuZuxin/status/2075284705281445922">RSI is coming</a>.&#8221; Human supervision is still required for many things&#8212;we&#8217;re in the final moments of the &#8220;human-in-the-loop&#8221; stage&#8212;but one prediction is that by 2028, there&#8217;s a <a href="https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research">60% chance that RSI is achieved</a>. That&#8217;s what Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark wrote in a newsletter in May. <a href="https://ai-2027.com/">Others predict RSI even sooner</a>. Things are going so fast that Anthropic is in favor of a coordinated slowdown: it&#8217;d be &#8220;<a href="https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement">a good thing</a>.&#8221; OpenAI staffers, even those who advocate for cautious progress over excessive fear-mongering, are <a href="https://x.com/tszzl/status/2081122092096065771">saying things like</a>: &#8220;if we could coordinate a global capabilities slowdown today i would likely press that magic button.&#8221;</p><p>So what we have here are the two top AI startups deeply convinced on one goal and one trajectory and pushing everything to get there. If I were Google, I&#8217;d have to be 100% convinced this isn&#8217;t the way. The rate of progress displayed by Anthropic and OpenAI is vertiginous for the world and rather unsettling for their competitors.</p><div class="captioned-image-container"><figure><a class="image-link
