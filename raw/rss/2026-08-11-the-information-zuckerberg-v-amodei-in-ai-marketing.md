# Zuckerberg v. Amodei in AI Marketing

<p>If at first people don’t pay attention to what you say, say it again. That appears to be Meta Platforms CEO Mark Zuckerberg’s philosophy. About two weeks after publishing a roughly 1,000-word opinion piece in The Wall Street Journal headlined “The AI Future Is for Everyone,” Zuckerberg published <a href="https://www.meta.com/thefutureisforeveryone/">a 6,500 word version </a>of the same piece on Meta.com on Monday. (Today’s essay carried a nearly-identifical headline, while the first half-dozen paragraphs were almost word for word what ran in the WSJ opinion piece.) Hint to Zuckerberg: Shorter is better.</p><p>Zuckerberg’s opus was part of a growing number of lengthy essays, published under the bylines of AI leaders, that discuss AI and society. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is a frequent contributor to the genre, with his most recent publication “<a href="https://darioamodei.com/post/policy-on-the-ai-exponential">Policy on the AI Exponential</a>.” OpenAI CEO Sam Altman <a href="https://blog.samaltman.com/">has his own blog</a>, supplemented by weighty posts from his company like <a href="https://openai.com/index/industrial-policy-for-the-intelligence-age/">this one</a>. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella <a href="https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2066182223213293753?s=20">posts on X</a>. For the most part, these essays are long and dry. In other words, they’re perfect for an AI chatbot to summarize. It makes you wonder if they’re meant to prove to consumers the value of AI!</p>
