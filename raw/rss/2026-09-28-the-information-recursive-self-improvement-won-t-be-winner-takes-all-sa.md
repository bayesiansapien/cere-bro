# Recursive Self-Improvement Won’t Be Winner-Takes-All, Say Founders of Ricursive

<p>Recursive self-improvement, the idea that AI models could get so good at research and development that they train themselves, without a need for human researchers, has captivated Silicon Valley. Some in the industry have suggested that the first company to achieve RSI will gain all the benefits: after all, once one company manages to cross that threshold, how could competitors still at least partly reliant on slow human labor ever hope to catch up?</p>

<p>But just because the pace of RSI will be exponentially fast doesn’t mean it’s winner-takes-all, argued Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini on <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-zMl-ycfhQA">the latest episode of The Information’s AI Deep Dive</a>. They are the co-founders of Ricursive Intelligence, a year-old startup developing AI systems for designing AI chips. They previously worked at Anthropic and Google, where they led work on AlphaChip, an AI system for chip design.</p>
