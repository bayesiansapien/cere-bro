# AI’s Power Crunch Is Reviving a Long-Stalled Energy Source

<p>AI’s insatiable appetite for power is one of the most resonant signals in our <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/projects/aiindex">AI Inflection Index.</a> The theme first appeared meaningfully in our coverage in late 2021 and picked up momentum in the third quarter of 2024&mdash;well before the largest grid operators in the U.S. began reporting a surge in capacity payments.</p>

<p>Tech giants are so desperate for electricity that they’re backing technologies long considered closer to pipe dreams than pipelines. Nuclear fusion, the holy grail of energy innovation, is one example. But tech companies have put even more capital into geothermal energy projects. Last week, for example, <a href="https://www.theinformation.com/org-charts/google">Google</a> announced a $500 million <a href="https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-signs-50-mw-geothermal-power-purchase-agreement-with-google">deal</a> with Quaise Energy to secure 50 megawatts of geothermal power. Quaise will drill three miles under central Oregon to reach extremely hot rock, pump water down to be heated into steam, and use that steam to spin turbines that generate electricity.</p>
