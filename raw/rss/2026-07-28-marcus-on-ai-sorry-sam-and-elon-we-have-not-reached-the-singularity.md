A human at OpenAI launched it; with a physical firewall it wouldn&#8217;t have happened. It wasn&#8217;t spontaneous combustion; it was a drill.</p><p>Had OpenAI ordinary guardrail classifiers been in place, it wouldn&#8217;t have happened. It doesn&#8217;t count as &#8220;uncontrollable&#8221; if you have to give the system a head and shoulders head start.  (Yes, we do have <em>poorly</em> controlled AI, but that&#8217;s not new, either.)</p><p>&#167;</p><p>Another definition of the singularity, per a TIME interview with Ray Kurzweil, who is often associated with the term, is &#8220;a moment when humans merge with AI via brain-to-computer interfaces to become &#8220;much smarter&#8221;&#8221;.</p><p>We are clearly not there yet, either. </p><p>&#167;</p><p>Another notion is that a singularity is a moment in which we can no longer peer forward into the future, but I don&#8217;t think we are there yet, either, as an entrepreneur on X pointed out, rightly ripping intp all the recent Singularity talk as nothing more than PR: </p><div class="twitter-embed"></div><p></p><p>&#167;</p><p>Going back to a justly famous paper by I. F. Thus the first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make, provided that the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control.</p></blockquote><p>&#8212;<span>&#8202;</span>Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine (1965), IF Good.</p><p>In this sense, the singularity is usually supposed to mean a step <em>beyond</em> AGI that can do anything a person, even an expert, can do, and much more. </p><p>The idea that we have reached <em>that</em> point is laughable. come up with many more. Which is part of why a bunch of companies are quietly rehiring. </p><p style="text-align: center;">&#167;</p><p>None of this means we won&#8217;t someday (most likely not this decade) reach a moment of superintelligence. And if we don&#8217;t radically up our game on AI safety, it could be out of our control. Maybe it will help lower prices; maybe it won&#8217;t. Brain-computer interfaces will surely improve greatly (though it may take many decades or even centuries to get where Kurzweil dreams of).</p><p>But intelligence is multidimensional; we may have made lots of progress on some, and not so much on others. I doubt there will be a single magic moment that our descendants think of as the &#8220;Singularity&#8221;. If there is, it&#8217;s not one that&#8217;s going to happen in this decade.</p><p>CEOs can say whatever they like.
