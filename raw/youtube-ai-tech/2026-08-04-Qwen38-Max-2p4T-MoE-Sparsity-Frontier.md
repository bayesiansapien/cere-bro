# Qwen 3.8 Max IS OUT! Best Open Model? (Fully Tested)

**Channel:** WorldofAI
**Published:** 2026-08-04
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vmLwsoVRo30

## TL;DR
Alibaba shipped Qwen3.8-Max on 3 August 2026: a 2.4 trillion parameter sparse MoE with roughly 95B active per token, 1M context, multimodal, at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output. Weights for both the Max and a smaller Qwen3.8-27B were promised for the following week. The video's hands-on verdict is more useful than its benchmark commentary, which is internally contradictory and badly transcribed. The verdict: genuinely strong on frontend, Three.js and 3D scene generation, roughly six to seven out of ten on OS-clone and complex app tasks, and disqualified as a daily driver by a specific failure mode, which is that it overthinks trivial requests and is therefore slow where slowness is least tolerable. The reviewer would rather use DeepSeek V4 Flash or Kimi K3, both of which are cheaper and faster. For anyone working on inference economics, the headline is not the 2.4T number. It is that 95B active is now a heavy activation budget, and the models beating Qwen on cost-adjusted terms are running at 13B.

## Key Takeaways
- **The architecture in one line: 2.4T total, ~95B active, about 4 percent activation per token.** That is the number that determines serving cost, not the 2.4T.
- **Pricing is $2 in, $6 out per million tokens, with cached input at $0.25 per million.** The 8x cache discount is aggressive and is the most exploitable part of the pricing sheet.
- **1M token context, multimodal across text, images and video.** Visual input feeds the whole workflow rather than sitting in a separate understanding step.
- **Open weights were promised, not delivered, at the time of this video.** Qwen3.8-Max and Qwen3.8-27B were slated for Hugging Face and ModelScope the week of 10 August 2026. The video speaks as though this has already happened. It had not.
- **Benchmarks are strong and self-reported.** Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 86.6, PaperBench at 93.0, IFBench at 82.8, SWE-bench Pro at 67.7. As of launch week neither Artificial Analysis nor LMArena had independently scored it.
- **Where it genuinely wins: frontend and 3D.** Single-shot black hole simulation with correct starlight bending, a solar system with asteroid belt and per-planet detail, a Three.js living room with orbit controls, shadows and an animated SVG on a TV. The reviewer rates this above DeepSeek on the same prompts.
- **Where it does not: complex stateful app clones.** Windows 11 clone at about 6 out of 10, macOS clone at 6.9 with bugs. Reviewer judged Opus 4.8 and Kimi K3 better on these prompts.
- **The disqualifying flaw is latency on easy queries.** Overthinking simple tasks, no visible adaptive compute control. This is a routing problem, not a capability problem.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The number worth staring at is 95B active out of 2.4T total, a sparsity ratio of roughly 25 to 1. Alibaba's framing is that this delivers frontier quality while reducing inference cost and latency, and that is true relative to a dense 2.4T model, which is a thing nobody would build. The more relevant comparison is against the current open-weight field, and there Qwen looks expensive. DeepSeek V4 Flash runs at 13B active and reportedly beats GLM-5.2 at around 40B active on every agentic benchmark row where both appear. Qwen3.8-Max is carrying roughly 7x DeepSeek's activation budget to land at 56.6 on DeepSWE against DeepSeek's 54.4, while Kimi K3 takes that benchmark at 69. That is a bad trade on a per-active-parameter basis, and it is the honest read behind the reviewer's confused "ranked 12th but leads most practical benchmarks" commentary.

The sharper data point in the same family is Qwen3.6-35B-A3B scoring 73.4 on SWE-bench Verified with 3B active parameters. That is a different and easier benchmark than the SWE-bench Pro 67.7 that Qwen3.8-Max reports, so the two are not directly comparable and it would be wrong to say the small model beats the large one. What it does establish is that Qwen's own lineup contains an extremely well-tuned sparse configuration at 3B active, which makes the 95B activation budget on the flagship look like a deliberate quality-maximising choice rather than an efficiency-driven one. Two different points on the frontier from the same lab, which is useful because it isolates the activation variable.

The overthinking complaint is the most actionable engineering signal in the video and deserves more weight than the reviewer gives it. A model that burns reasoning tokens on trivial requests is not exhibiting a quality problem, it is exhibiting the absence of an inference-time compute controller. The cost consequence compounds badly at $6 per million output tokens: a request that should have cost 200 output tokens costing 3,000 is a 15x overrun that no benchmark captures, because benchmarks measure accuracy at whatever token count the model chooses. This is precisely the case for an external router. Qwen3.8-Max should not be receiving the easy queries at all, and the fact that the reviewer experienced it as "not practical for coding tasks" despite acknowledging an "incredible performance-to-cost ratio" is the signature of a model that is correctly priced per token and badly behaved about how many tokens it emits.

The $0.25 per million cached input rate is the most exploitable line in the pricing. Against $2 standard input that is an 87.5 percent discount, and paired with a 1M context window it changes the economics of long-context agentic work substantially. The pattern it rewards is a large stable prefix, a codebase, a document corpus, a fixed system context, with small varying suffixes. Anyone building on this should be structuring prompts prefix-first and measuring cache hit rate as a first-class metric, because at that discount ratio the difference between a 20 percent and an 80 percent hit rate on a 500k-token prefix dominates every other cost line.

Finally, the domain split the reviewer found is a real routing signal and it is unusually clean. Strong on frontend, design taste, Three.js and 3D scene composition. Mediocre on complex stateful application logic. Those are separable task classes, which means a routing policy could plausibly send visual and generative-UI work to Qwen3.8-Max and stateful agentic coding to Kimi K3 or DeepSeek, and beat either model used alone on cost-adjusted quality.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The transcript is severely ASR-corrupted and several claims need correcting before anyone acts on them. The model is repeatedly called "Gemini 1.5 Pro Max," and also appears as "Qwen 3.5 Max," "Qwen 3.0 Max," and "quant 3.8 max." All of these refer to Qwen3.8-Max. "GPT-4 1.6" is GPT-5.6 Sol. "Opus 4.7" appears where Opus 4.8 belongs. "Kimiko 3" is Kimi K3. None of these are the reviewer's errors, but they make the video unusable as a citation without correction.

On substance, the video's benchmark section contradicts itself and the resolution matters. It first claims the model ranks 12th, behind Opus 4.7, DeepSeek V4 Flash and Grok 4.5, calling this lackluster. It later claims the model "leads most of the practical benchmarks." Both cannot be true. The published figures show Qwen3.8-Max at 86.6 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, behind GPT-5.6 Sol (max) at 88.8 but ahead of Claude Opus 4.8 and Claude Fable 5 at 84.6, and leading PaperBench at 93.0 and IFBench at 82.8. The reviewer's own composite ranking is presumably from the WorldofAI harness, which is not a standard benchmark. The reconciliation is that Qwen posts leading numbers on self-reported evaluations and middling numbers on this reviewer's own tests, which is exactly the pattern to expect from a launch-week model.

The independence caveat is the one to hold onto. As of launch week, Artificial Analysis and LMArena had not scored Qwen3.8-Max, so every headline number is vendor-published. The one third-party signal available was a preliminary WebDev Arena snapshot putting Qwen3.8-Max at 1,668 ±18 against Kimi K3 at 1,676 ±12. Those confidence intervals overlap substantially, so that is a statistical tie, not a win for either. Treat the frontend superiority claim as plausible and unproven.

Two more corrections. The video presents the open-weight release as a fact and a headline feature. It was an announced commitment for the week of 10 August 2026, with nothing shipped at the time of recording, so the "true state-of-the-art model we can run locally" framing was premature. And the claim that Opus 5 costs "approximately eight times more" than Qwen3.8-Max is not verifiable from the video and should be treated as an estimate. Separately, running a 2.4T parameter model locally is not realistic for individual hardware regardless of licence, which is why the 27B checkpoint, not the Max, is the one that matters for local deployment. The reviewer does eventually make this point himself.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
The immediate experiment is a cost-adjusted activation benchmark across the open-weight field, because the current numbers make a routing case that nobody in the video is making. Take a representative slice of internal coding and agentic tasks and score Qwen3.8-Max at 95B active, DeepSeek V4 Flash at 13B active, Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2 at roughly 40B active, then plot quality against active parameters rather than against total parameters or list price. The hypothesis worth testing is that the field's quality-per-active-parameter frontier is currently held by DeepSeek V4 Flash and that Qwen3.8-Max is off it. If that holds, the flagship's role is a fallback tier for the specific task classes where it wins, not a default.

For routing work specifically, the frontend versus stateful-logic split is a directly testable partition. Build a two-model policy that sends UI generation, design and Three.js work to Qwen3.8-Max and stateful agentic coding to Kimi K3, and compare against each model alone on blended cost and quality. This is a cleaner partition than the usual difficulty-based one because it is a capability difference rather than a complexity difference, and capability differences are stable across query distributions in a way that difficulty estimates are not.

On the overthinking failure, the concrete mitigation is a pre-router that never lets trivial queries reach this model, plus explicit reasoning-budget caps where the API supports them. Instrument output tokens per request as a distribution rather than a mean, because the pathology here is a fat tail on easy inputs, and a mean will hide it. If the p90 output length on requests you classify as simple is more than a few times the p50, the model is burning money in a way that no accuracy metric will ever surface.

When the 27B open-weight checkpoint lands, it is the artifact actually worth downloading. A 27B dense or lightly-sparse model that inherits the flagship's frontend and 3D strengths is a realistic local deployment target and a legitimate distillation and quantization subject, which the 2.4T Max never will be. Verify at that point whether the frontend advantage survived the size reduction, since design taste is exactly the kind of capability that distillation tends to preserve poorly.
