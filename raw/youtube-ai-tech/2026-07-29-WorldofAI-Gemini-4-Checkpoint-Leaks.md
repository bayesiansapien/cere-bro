# Gemini 4 LEAKS! Google's Most Powerful AI Model EVER!

**Channel:** WorldofAI
**Published:** 2026-07-29
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g192Vf0OjOk

## TL;DR
New Gemini checkpoints are appearing in A/B tests inside the Gemini app and on Arena, and it is unclear whether they are Gemini 3.5 Pro or Gemini 4. The narrative: Gemini 3.5 Pro was expected in June, plans changed when Google switched the base from Rev 24 to a newer Rev 25 base which meant far less post-training, early results were underwhelming while Anthropic shipped Fable 5 and OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5, so Google delayed rather than ship something uncompetitive. Logan (Gemini product lead) has confirmed the most ambitious pre-training run yet has started, for the Gemini 4 series. The presenter's own testing on Arena (Minecraft clone with infinite terrain and water physics, a 3js rope bridge with a rolling ball producing realistic ripples, an animated typewriter with synchronized levers and carriage) shows real capability, with the caveat that Arena and the app run lowered reasoning effort to manage compute demand so this is not API-quality output. His prediction, explicitly labelled speculation: natively multimodal, a multi-trillion-parameter mixture of experts possibly exceeding 10 trillion total parameters with only a fraction activated at inference, trained with substantially more compute than any previous Gemini. His verdict on the checkpoints: undercooked, still behind Fable 5 and Opus 5, but SVG generation and 3D world generation remain Gemini's strongest domains and he expects a Gemini release in August.

## Key Takeaways
- **The most credible fact in the video is the reasoning-effort caveat, and it is the one most reviewers get wrong.** Arena and the Gemini app lower reasoning effort to manage aggregate compute demand, so A/B test outputs systematically understate API-quality performance. Any model comparison drawn from Arena outputs is comparing serving configurations as much as models. This single observation invalidates a large fraction of published model comparisons.
- **The Rev 24 to Rev 25 base switch story is the interesting mechanism, if true.** The claim is that changing the pre-training base mid-cycle reset the post-training budget, which is why early 3.5 Pro results were weak. Post-training is where a large share of usable capability now comes from, so a base change is not a small perturbation, it invalidates the entire post-training stack built on the old base. This is a real and underappreciated cost of base-model churn.
- **The delay decision is a defensible strategic read.** Shipping a model that cannot compete against contemporaneous Fable 5 and GPT-5.5 releases damages the brand more than being late does. That Google chose to delay twice and then start a new pre-training run rather than ship suggests the 3.5 Pro line was judged unsalvageable rather than merely behind.
- **Gemini's persistent comparative advantages are correctly identified: SVG generation and 3D or 3js world generation.** These are genuine domain strengths that have held across Gemini generations, and the demos (typewriter with mechanical detail, rope bridge with ripple propagation) show them. His framing that mechanical detail and synchronized moving parts are the impressive part, rather than raw 3D generation, is the right observation.
- **The MoE prediction is unsourced speculation and he says so.** Multi-trillion total parameters, possibly above 10 trillion, with a fraction activated. He is explicit that Google has confirmed no parameter count.
- **The channel format carries a mid-roll sponsor read (Context Dev) placed inside the technical discussion**, which is worth noting when weighing the surrounding claims.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
Two mechanics here are worth real attention, and one is directly relevant to optimization practice.

**The reasoning-effort disclosure is the most useful thing in the video and it generalizes into a measurement principle.** When a provider serves the same weights at reduced reasoning effort to control compute cost, the served model and the API model are different products. This is a serving-side quality knob, not a model property, and it means published benchmark comparisons must specify the serving configuration or they are uninterpretable. For anyone building routing systems this is not a curiosity, it is the core insight: **reasoning effort is a continuous cost-quality dial that providers already operate as a load-management mechanism.** A routing policy that only chooses between models is ignoring a dimension that providers themselves find more useful than model selection. Choosing reasoning effort per query, against a per-query quality floor, is a finer-grained and often cheaper lever than swapping models, and the fact that Google uses it to absorb demand spikes is evidence of how much headroom it offers.

**The MoE prediction, whether or not the specific numbers are right, points at the actual architectural question.** A model with 10 trillion total parameters and a small activated fraction has an extreme ratio between memory footprint and compute per token. That configuration is not compute-bound at inference, it is bound by expert weight movement: the memory capacity to hold all experts and the bandwidth or interconnect to route activations to the selected ones. As total-to-active ratios grow, the binding constraint moves further from FLOPs and further toward memory hierarchy and expert placement. The practical consequences are that expert-parallel serving topology, expert caching by access frequency, and routing-locality optimizations matter more than arithmetic efficiency, and that quantization of expert weights becomes the highest-leverage compression target because the weights are the thing being moved. GLM-5.2's published configuration is a useful reference point for where the open frontier sits: 744B total with 40B active, roughly an 18x ratio. Kimi K3 is a 2.8T MoE. If Gemini 4 is in the multi-trillion range with a small active fraction, the ratio is larger still, and the serving problem becomes almost entirely a memory-systems problem.

**The base-switch cost has a compression analogue worth carrying.** If changing the pre-training base invalidates the post-training stack built on it, the same logic applies downward: a change to the base model invalidates calibration sets, distillation teachers, quantization configurations, and routing thresholds tuned against the previous base. The FactSet talk from the same week documents the equivalent failure at the prompt layer, where a model upgrade broke skill compliance because positional attention shifted. The general principle is that **artefacts tuned against a specific model are liabilities on a schedule**, and the schedule is set by someone else's release cadence.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The core facts check out. Several of the surrounding details are wrong or overstated, and the timeline is more specific than the video conveys.

**The Gemini 4 confirmation is real and dated.** Logan Kilpatrick, product lead for Google AI Studio and the Gemini API, disclosed the training run on **21 July 2026**, describing Gemini 4 as Google's "most ambitious pre-training run yet." He did not specify whether that ambition refers to size, data, compute, architecture, or multimodality. Sundar Pichai has separately called it "significantly larger" than any prior Gemini, with coding and autonomous agents as stated priorities. **Google has announced no release date, architecture, context window, benchmark, price, or access plan.**

**The 3.5 Pro delay story is corroborated with more precision than the video offers.** Reporting indicates Gemini 3.5 Pro was delayed **three times** and that Google started training Gemini 4 instead. As of late July 2026, Google says 3.5 Pro is still testing with partners. So the video's framing that Google may skip 3.5 Pro entirely is a live possibility rather than speculation.

**The competitive context is understated.** The Gemini 4 announcement arrived alongside the launch of **Gemini 3.6 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, and Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber**. Gemini 3.6 Flash reportedly cuts token costs and scores higher on every benchmark than its predecessor. This substantially changes the reading. The video's premise is that Google has been "on a bit of a downfall," but Google shipped three models and started a frontier pre-training run in the same week. The accurate framing is that Google is competitive and shipping fast at the efficient end while its flagship line has slipped, not that Google is failing.

**The August release prediction is unsupported.** He says he is not sure but believes there will be a new Gemini model in August "for sure." No source supports a Gemini 4 date, and Gemini 3.6 Flash already shipped in July, which may be what his intuition is tracking.

**The frontier comparison set he names is current.** Opus 5 and Fable 5 are the relevant Anthropic frontier references, and on the open-weight side GLM-5.2 (released 13 June 2026, MIT-licensed, 744B total with 40B active, 1M context) leads open weights on agentic and coding benchmarks, with Kimi K3 (Moonshot, 27 July 2026, 2.8T MoE) taking the top open-weight position on LiveBench coding and agentic coding. So the frontier moved on the open side two days before this video, which is context the video does not mention and which matters more for anyone choosing a deployable model than Gemini 4 speculation does.

**On the parameter-count speculation, treat it as noise.** No parameter count has been confirmed, the presenter labels it a prediction, and the specific "exceeding 10 trillion" figure has no sourcing. It is not usable for planning.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Add reasoning effort to the routing decision space, not just model identity.** This is the actionable insight buried in the video's caveat. Google operates reasoning effort as a load-management dial, which is direct evidence of how much cost-quality headroom it contains. A routing policy that selects only among models is leaving that dimension unexploited. Build the policy over the joint space of (model, reasoning effort) against a per-query quality floor, which is strictly finer-grained and frequently cheaper than model swapping alone.
2. **Discard any model comparison sourced from Arena or from consumer app A/B tests unless the serving configuration is stated.** Reduced reasoning effort in those surfaces means the comparison measures serving configuration as much as model capability. This disqualifies a large share of published model-quality commentary, including most of what this video shows, and knowing to discard it is worth more than any individual data point.
3. **Treat every model-tuned artefact as having an expiry set by someone else's release cadence.** Calibration sets, distillation teachers, quantization configurations, routing thresholds, and long system prompts are all tuned against a specific base. The Rev 24 to Rev 25 story shows the cost at the pre-training level and the FactSet incident shows it at the prompt level. Version these artefacts explicitly against the model they were fitted to, and budget for re-fitting rather than treating it as an exception.
4. **If Gemini 4 lands as a very high total-to-active-parameter MoE, prepare the memory-systems side rather than the compute side.** The binding constraint at extreme ratios is expert weight movement, not arithmetic. The preparation that pays is expert-parallel serving topology, frequency-based expert caching, routing-locality analysis, and expert-weight quantization. Arithmetic-efficiency work has low returns in that regime. Use GLM-5.2's 744B-total-to-40B-active ratio as the current open reference point when reasoning about where the curve is going.
5. **Track Gemini 3.6 Flash rather than Gemini 4 for anything decision-relevant this quarter.** A shipped model with lower token costs and better benchmarks is immediately usable. An unannounced flagship with no date, price, or access plan is not. The video's attention allocation is inverted relative to what is actionable, and the same inversion is common in AI news coverage generally.
6. **Note the open-weight frontier moved on 27 July with Kimi K3.** For a deployment where weights must be self-hosted, that is the consequential event of the week covered by this video, and the video does not mention it. GLM-5.2 and Kimi K3 are the models to benchmark against for open-weight agentic work, not Gemini checkpoints.
