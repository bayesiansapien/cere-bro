# AI in GTM at Notion — Flora Liu

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4I7WgiEquo

## TL;DR
Liu is a product growth engineer at Notion who moved onto a newly formed GTM engineering team, and her opening line is the thesis: a year ago she would have called building a GTM system a marketing ops problem, and now she calls it one of the most interesting distributed systems problems she has worked on. The substance is an architecture, not a sales pitch. Every GTM workflow reduces to four questions, what do we know about the customer, what should happen next, how do we execute that safely, and did it work, which become four layers named Know, Decide, Act, Learn. Snowflake computes truth via daily and sometimes real-time transforms into a small set of modeled versioned entities. DynamoDB serves truth as a denormalized key-addressable profile queryable in milliseconds with no joins, with agent-generated artifacts keyed by the same IDs. Signals, defined as single customer events important enough to change what happens next, trigger durable Temporal workflows that fan out research, draft three emails, score them, and pick a winner. The architectural commitment that makes it interesting is that humans and agents operate on the same substrate rather than the agents living in a layer above the business, and the build-versus-buy rule is stated crisply: own the context layer, rent everything else.

## Key Takeaways
- The four-question reduction is the reusable artifact here. Know, Decide, Act, Learn is not a diagram drawn after the fact, it is what fell out when a cross-functional team of CX, RevOps, product, engineering and sales looked for the pattern underneath every workflow they had.
- Compute truth and serve truth are separated by design and by store. Snowflake runs the transforms and produces versioned entities with clear ownership and timestamps. DynamoDB holds a denormalized key-addressable projection so that agent reads are millisecond lookups with no joins. This is CQRS applied to a context layer, and the reason is latency at agent read time.
- Agent-generated artifacts are persisted next to the source data and keyed identically. Research snippets, summarized notes and rolling summaries live in the same key space as the profile, so a downstream consumer reads structured and unstructured context in one shot rather than assembling it per call.
- Structured plus unstructured together is the hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. The facts that actually determine the next action live in prose: the champion just left, do not contact this customer again, blocked in legal. An automation that cannot see those does something catastrophically wrong rather than merely suboptimal.
- Three named failure modes from the pre-system state, and they are the generic ones for any multi-vendor context pipeline. Data quality, where conflicting systems of record produce wrong contact-to-account mappings and one bad mapping loses a rep's trust permanently. Data latency, where every vendor adds a hop and you end up automating on yesterday's world. Structured-versus-unstructured, above.
- Signals are the trigger primitive, and the interesting half is external. User-driven signals like hitting an AI limit are reactive by construction. Company raised funding, hiring signals, tech stack shift are not user-initiated at all, and Liu is explicit that those are what let the system be proactive rather than reactive.
- The no-signal path is not idle. When nothing has happened for a customer, a predictive engine recommends the most relevant product features and drives lifecycle email and in-app nudges. Absence of a signal is itself a routable state.
- Routing and eligibility were promoted to a first-class primitive precisely because they were previously scattered, one rule in the email tool, another in sales. Consolidating them into a single classifier consumed across the codebase is what prevents double-sends and incoherent cross-channel messaging.
- Agents are deliberately not allowed to talk to customers. For sales-assist workflows humans approve by default, and Liu explicitly frames this as a security boundary, not just a quality one: a contact-sales form submission is untrusted user input, and with an agent in the middle the trust boundary is exactly where it would otherwise silently break down.
- Temporal handles retries, dedupes and resume-from-failure so the team writes sequential GTM logic. The specific benefit she names is blast-radius containment: one malformed transcript cannot take down the whole batch.
- The generate-score-select loop is the standard best-of-N pattern in production clothing. A research sub-agent runs concurrent research, three email drafts are produced, scored, and a review agent picks the highest scorer and edits it. Every LLM step is traced for later evaluation.
- Learn is wired as a control loop, not a dashboard. The naive version she names and rejects is a data analyst inspecting outputs. The built version threads engagement history back into the decision layer so the system itself decides whether to continue a thread, advance, or pivot.
- The stated goal is raising the floor rather than the ceiling. Encoding the strongest reps' patterns means a ramping rep learns which signals matter and what good follow-up looks like without every lesson being passed down manually.
- Results are early and honestly hedged: 13 weeks in, enterprise reps show increased qualified opportunities, and users receiving context-aware recommendations were 63% more likely to take the next step. No absolute baselines are given for either.
- "Shadow your best human" is the process advice, with the sharp version attached: the chaos of a rep's twenty open tabs was the spec, and if you encode a mediocre process you get a mediocre agent.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The compute-truth versus serve-truth split is the piece worth stealing wholesale, and it is a latency-budget decision dressed as a data-modeling decision. Snowflake is a columnar warehouse optimised for scans and joins over large batches, which is the correct engine for the transform that produces versioned entities, and exactly the wrong engine for a lookup on the critical path of an agent turn. Warehouse query latency lands in the hundreds of milliseconds to seconds even when warm. An agent that makes five context reads inside one reasoning turn pays that five times, serially, before the model emits a token. Publishing a denormalized key-addressable projection into a key-value store converts every one of those reads into a single-digit-millisecond point lookup, and the denormalization is what removes the joins that would otherwise reintroduce the variance. The generalisation, and it applies directly to retrieval systems: the store you compute a representation in and the store you serve it from should be chosen independently, because their access patterns have nothing in common. This is the same reason an embedding index is built offline in a batch job and served from a structure tuned for approximate nearest neighbour rather than from the training pipeline's storage.

Keying agent-generated artifacts by the same identifiers as the source entities is a smaller point that pays a disproportionate return, and it is the fix for a failure mode endemic to agent memory systems. The common shape is that generated summaries and research land in a separate store with their own identifiers and their own retrieval path, so consuming them requires a second lookup, a join, or worse, a similarity search. Co-keying makes the derived context free to fetch once you have already fetched the entity, which means a rolling summary costs nothing extra at read time and can therefore be used unconditionally. If retrieving your cached summary is more expensive than regenerating context, the cache will not be used, and co-keying is what keeps that from being true.

The three-drafts-scored-and-selected pattern is best-of-N sampling, and it is worth being precise about what it buys and what it costs, because the talk states it without the arithmetic. Best-of-N with a separate scoring pass multiplies generation cost by N and adds a scoring call, so a three-draft flow costs roughly four model calls where a single-shot flow costs one. It buys variance reduction, which is exactly what you want when the output goes in front of a customer and a bad draft is expensive, and it is a poor trade when the output is intermediate and a downstream stage can recover. The routing implication is direct and Liu does not draw it: the N generations do not need the same model tier as the scorer. Drafting is a generation task where diversity is a feature, so a cheaper model sampled at higher temperature is often the better generator, while selection is a discrimination task where the stronger model earns its cost. Discriminating between candidates is reliably easier than producing the best candidate, which is the whole basis for reward-model-guided decoding, and it means the correct configuration is usually cheap-and-many generating, expensive-and-one selecting. Anyone running best-of-N with the same model for both roles is overpaying on the branch that needs it least.

Durable execution deserves the attention it gets here and it is under-adopted in ML pipelines specifically. The property Temporal provides is that workflow state survives process death, because the event history is recorded and replayed deterministically, so a crash resumes at the last completed activity rather than at the top. For a workflow that touches enrichment, web search and multiple LLM calls, each of which can fail, rate limit or time out independently, the alternative is bespoke retry and checkpoint logic written per pipeline and debugged per outage. The economics are more favourable for LLM steps than for ordinary RPCs because a completed LLM call represents real spent money, so resuming past it rather than replaying it is a direct cost saving, not just a latency one. Temporal announced Serverless Workers and Google ADK integration on top of the OpenAI Agents SDK at Replay 2026, and the competitive set now includes Inngest with step-level retries and exponential backoff, and Restate. The distinguishing question between them for an agent workload is whether long-running GPU-bound activities are supported cleanly, which Temporal handles by heartbeating so the activity fails only when the heartbeat times out rather than on a fixed wall-clock deadline.

The one architectural claim that should be pushed on is the shared-substrate argument. Liu's version is strong and mostly right: if humans and agents cannot read from the same substrate you are building two systems that drift apart, and she is candid that the substrate being Notion is convenient for a talk given by a Notion engineer. The reason it holds up beyond the convenience is the format claim, that plain markdown with databases and hierarchies is a representation both a model and a person can navigate. That is genuinely true and it is the same reason markdown beats raw HTML at an LLM API boundary. The part that is unaddressed is that a document substrate is a weak place to enforce invariants. There is no schema validation, no referential integrity, and no transactional guarantee on a markdown page, which means the correctness of the context layer rests entirely on the Snowflake and DynamoDB pipeline underneath and the Notion surface is a view. Presented as a view it is correct. Presented as the context layer, which is how the talk frames it, it overstates what the substrate itself guarantees.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The architecture as described is consistent with what Snowflake itself has published about internal context layers for AI agents, and the pattern is converging rather than idiosyncratic. Snowflake's own engineering blog on building a context layer for AI agents describes the same separation of a governed semantic layer over warehouse data from a low-latency serving path, and the vendor ecosystem has moved fast enough that "context layer" is now a product category with entrants from Atlan and ZoomInfo among others, plus ontology-in-Snowflake patterns appearing in mid-2026 practitioner writing. Snowflake Summit 2026 shipped Horizon Context, Datastream and Pipeline Builder, which is the same bet from the warehouse side: the warehouse wants to be the place agents get their grounding. Notion's choice to compute in Snowflake and serve from DynamoDB rather than serving from Snowflake directly is a mild vote against that positioning, and on latency grounds it is the right call today.

The role Liu occupies is itself the trend. GTM engineering has crystallised into a named function distinct from RevOps and SalesOps during 2026, with the differentiator stated the same way across sources: RevOps maintains systems and reports on revenue outcomes, while a GTM engineer builds the systems that automate and accelerate the motion. The adjacent Forward Deployed Engineer role has moved from a Palantir idiosyncrasy to a mainstream title, with Salesforce alone committing to hire 1,000 FDEs and compensation benchmarks landing around $175K to $260K base and $230K to $360K OTE. The forecast attached to the GTM engineer role, that it will mean overseeing fleets of interoperable agents each assigned to a segment of the motion rather than shipping point solutions, is precisely the system Liu described. Worth noting the sober counterpoint from the Alexander Group's analysis, that the FDE is a surgical instrument rather than a scaled solution and works economically only when confined to the highest-opportunity accounts with incentives tied to time-to-first-value. Read against Notion's numbers, that is a caution about where the qualified-opportunity lift came from.

On the results, the honest reading is that they are directionally encouraging and not yet evidence. Thirteen weeks with "increased qualified opportunities" and no baseline is not a measurable claim, and 63% more likely to take the next step for users receiving context-aware recommendations is the kind of figure that is very hard to separate from selection effects, since the population receiving a context-aware recommendation is by construction the population the system found something to say about. Liu hedges appropriately, calling it early and promising, which is more restraint than most conference talks show. Nothing in the search results independently confirms either number, and both should be treated as internal and unaudited.

## Architecture & Optimization Intersections
The best-of-N asymmetry is the item with the most direct carryover to routing work. The observation that discrimination is cheaper than generation, so the selector should be the expensive model and the generators should be cheap and numerous, is a routing policy that most production stacks get backwards by defaulting to one model for the whole flow. It is also directly measurable: hold total cost fixed, then sweep the split between generator tier and selector tier, and the quality-per-dollar curve will usually not peak where both are the same model.

The signal primitive maps onto routing more literally than it first appears. A signal is defined as an event important enough to change what should happen next, which is exactly the predicate a routing policy needs and rarely has. Most routing is stateless per request, classifying difficulty from the prompt alone. A signal layer is the stateful version: an accumulated fact about the session or user that changes which policy applies, independent of the current query's surface features. The cheap version to try is a per-session state record updated by explicit events, prior escalation, prior tool failure, prior user correction, consulted by the router before the per-query classifier runs.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. Split compute-truth from serve-truth in any context or retrieval system on an agent's critical path. If an agent read hits a warehouse or any join-heavy store, measure the per-read latency and multiply by reads per turn. If that product is a meaningful fraction of turn latency, publish a denormalized key-addressable projection to a KV store and read from there. The transform stays where it belongs.
2. Co-key derived artifacts with source entities. Summaries, extractions and cached research should share the identifier of the thing they describe so retrieving them is free once the entity is fetched. A cache that costs more to consult than to bypass will be bypassed.
3. Split the model tier inside best-of-N flows. Generate with a cheaper model at higher temperature for diversity, select with the stronger model, and measure quality per dollar against the single-tier baseline. Discrimination is the easier task and should not get the more expensive model by default.
4. Put durable execution under any multi-step LLM pipeline where a completed model call represents spent money. Resuming past a completed call rather than replaying it is a direct cost saving on top of the reliability win. Temporal, Inngest and Restate are the live options; for GPU-bound activities check heartbeat support specifically rather than fixed timeouts.
5. Consolidate scattered eligibility and routing rules into one classifier consumed everywhere. Liu's motivation was double-sends; the equivalent in an inference stack is model-selection logic duplicated across services that drifts until two paths route the same request differently, and you find out from a support ticket.
6. Add a stateful signal layer to routing rather than classifying each query cold. A per-session record of escalations, tool failures and user corrections, consulted before the per-query classifier, captures exactly the information a stateless router cannot see.
7. Treat the presence of an agent in a pipeline as a trust boundary and mark where untrusted input enters. Liu's contact-sales form is the canonical case. Any pipeline where user-controlled text reaches a model that can call tools needs that boundary named explicitly, because prompt injection is the failure the architecture diagram never shows.
8. Shadow the strongest practitioner before encoding a process, and be suspicious of automating anything you have not watched being done by hand. The version of this that applies to optimization work is that a benchmark encoding a mediocre workload produces a model tuned for a mediocre workload, and the tabs a person actually has open are the honest spec.
