## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
- **M3.1 Flash's active-parameter ratio is the story.** 23B active of 428B total is a ~5% activation ratio, which is what buys 200 tok/s at $0.15/M. The cached-input price ($0.015/M, 10x cheaper than fresh input) means the economics of a long-context agent on this model are dominated by prefix-cache hit rate. This is the kind of model a router should keep in the cheap tier for scaffold-then-refine workflows, which is exactly the host's own usage pattern (MiniMax for the draft, Opus for the polish).
- **Hallucination rate is the hidden routing cost.** The Decoder reports a 93% hallucination rate for M3.1 Flash on Artificial Analysis's eval because it attempts nearly every question. For a router that is the key constraint: cheap, fast, and confident-when-wrong is fine for code generation with a test harness and dangerous for RAG or factual QA without a verifier. Pair it with the grounding checks from the Pamela Fox workshop page before routing factual traffic to it.
- **Nvidia's megakernel claim, if real, is the purest inference-optimization headline of the week.** Fusing the entire decode step into a single persistent kernel removes launch overhead and intermediate HBM round-trips; the 12K-line CuTe DSL figure is plausible for a hand-fused MoE decode path. 1.68x on identical silicon is in line with other megakernel results (Cohere reported 1.58x over vLLM on a 30B MoE with a single-CUDA-file megakernel). The transferable lesson: for MoE decode at batch size 1, kernel-launch and memory-traffic overhead, not FLOPs, are the ceiling.
- **GLM 5.3's Terminal-Bench jump (4.6 to 28.3) from post-training alone** is a reminder that for agentic benchmarks, the policy gap between base and tuned checkpoints is enormous. It also means distillation targets should be the post-trained checkpoint, and that a small model with the right post-training can beat a larger one with the wrong one.
- **Kimi K3.1's four reasoning levels are a per-request compute knob** the router can set. If "max" is default, the cheap win is routing easy requests at "low" and measuring the quality delta.
