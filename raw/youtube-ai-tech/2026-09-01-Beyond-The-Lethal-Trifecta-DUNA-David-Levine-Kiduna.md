# Beyond the Lethal Trifecta: Agentic Commerce on the Open Internet (David Levine, Kiduna Club)

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-09-01
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tE2z8-hqoLY

## TL;DR
The closing session of the conference, and the most speculative talk in the batch. Levine's history is genuinely good: LambdaMOO in 1993 as an existence proof that composable, community-governed virtual economies work when governance, technology, economics, and culture align, followed by thirty years of platforms and engagement algorithms extracting that value until no open internet economy remained. His diagnosis of why agentic commerce cannot happen on the open internet is Simon Willison's lethal trifecta: private data, untrusted content, and the ability to act. His proposed solution is legal and cryptographic rather than technical: register agents under a DUNA, a decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association with real legal standing, and establish identity, authority, and boundaries between agents using JWTs resolvable to a state registry with a blockchain audit trail. He announced registering what he presents as the first such agentic organization, number 628407, hours before speaking. The accountability layer is a real contribution. The claim that it resolves the lethal trifecta does not hold, and Willison's own analysis says why.

## Key Takeaways
- **The LambdaMOO framing is the best part and is not nostalgia.** A text world running on one Sparcstation at Xerox PARC felt infinite because everything was nouns and verbs that composed: someone writes a program, everyone adopts it into their player class, a scooter becomes a motorcycle. His claim is that four things had to align, governance (an architecture review board, wizards), technology, economics (PARC funding), and culture, and that culture is the one people ignore.
- **His platform critique.** Platforms and algorithms are extractive by construction, replacing connection with engagement, so there is no open internet economy left, only silos. This lands harder read alongside [the Meta design-liability litigation](../global-news/2026-08-31-Meta-Legal-Troubles-Product-Design-Liability-ColdFusion.md), where a court reached substantially the same conclusion about mechanism.
- **The lethal trifecta, correctly attributed and correctly stated.** Private data on your machine that agents can reach, untrusted content from the open internet that an agent cannot reliably distinguish from a real job board, and the ability to take actions. Combine all three and a malicious page or inbound email can convince a naive agent that it is being instructed by its principal.
- **Why enterprises retreated inward.** Keep agents inside the perimeter, one per SaaS silo, then spend enormous effort wiring them together with APIs and MCP servers while losing cross-domain context. Sales agents, finance agents, research agents, none of which share what they know.
- **What a DUNA is, per his description.** Composable, permissionless, accountable, registered. No board, no executives, no corporate shell. Member-governed with full legal standing: can own property, enter agreements, raise capital, earn profits, open bank accounts, hire and fire. Crucially it can be profitable but cannot distribute profits to members, because doing so would make membership units securities.
- **His identity architecture.** Ordinary JWTs carrying claims and a blockchain address, resolvable up to a registered organization, functioning much like DNS: you look up an organization ID and can trace an audit trail down to an individual action. His framing of why blockchain finally has a use: verifying agentic identity and attributing responsibility, so that if an agent is sued you can trace who is accountable.
- **Decision markets for governance is the most interesting speculative idea.** Rather than voting, members trade pass and fail tokens on proposed policies, prediction-market style. His reasoning is that LLMs are reward-oriented, so a mechanism where your tokens are worth more if you side with the eventual winner elicits better judgement than agents persuading each other. Infuse agents with your purpose, values, and experience, then let them argue with skin in the game.
- **The Kiduna product.** Agents are "allies," built through inform (load a vector database), instruct (system prompt, character, stance), empower (connect accounts), enact (specific abilities and automations, long-horizon goals), and align (purpose). Organizations are then built as software rather than as software plus people plus paperwork. The first Kiduna is an umbrella organization for builders with agent templates, so you register under it rather than forming your own.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
- **The identity layer is real and useful, and it solves a different problem than the one advertised.** JWT claims resolving to a registered legal entity with an on-chain audit trail gives you *attribution and accountability*. That is genuinely missing infrastructure, and it is exactly the gap [Edge & Node identified](./2026-09-01-Agent-Spending-Without-Controls-Edge-And-Node.md) when they described being handed a bare wallet address with no counterparty information. Legal standing plus resolvable identity is what a chief legal officer needs before signing off on autonomous spend.
- **But attribution is not prevention, and this is the central flaw.** Willison's own conclusion, restated in his June 2025 post and consistently since, is that injected instructions have no reliable signature, that detection therefore cannot make a configuration safe, and that the only fix is to **remove one of the three legs**. Levine's architecture removes none of them. An agent inside a DUNA still holds private data, still reads untrusted web content, and still acts. If it is successfully injected, the JWT chain will faithfully record which registered organization exfiltrated the data and who is liable. That is a legal remedy, not a security control. Calling it "resolving the lethal trifecta" conflates deterrence with defence.
- **What would actually engage the trifecta.** Capability scoping at the tool layer, so a compromised agent cannot reach the data or the egress path in the first place. That pattern appears repeatedly in this same conference: [Cloudflare's zero-access-by-default agents](./2026-08-26-Cloudflare-GTM-Agents-Three-Pillars-Justin-Joyce.md), [Exa's caller-differentiated permissions](./2026-08-26-Exa-Knowledge-Systems-GTM-Stack-Jeffrey-Wang.md), [Ramp's config-scoped tool grants](./2026-08-26-Ramp-GTM-Orchestration-Building-Blocks-Arman-Vaziri.md), and Notion's decision that agents never talk to customers and inbound form text is untrusted by construction. Each removes a leg. Identity attestation complements those controls; it does not substitute for them.
- **Decision markets deserve to be taken more seriously than the rest of the talk.** The mechanism-design intuition is sound: a market aggregates dispersed private information better than deliberation when participants are rewarded for accuracy rather than persuasion, which is the standard argument for prediction markets. Applying it to LLM agents adds something real, because reward-oriented systems are unusually susceptible to being talked into consensus, and a token price is harder to argue with than another agent. It is also unvalidated. He asserts "all this science says you reach much better decisions this way" without citation, and prediction markets have known failure modes (thin liquidity, manipulation, correlated priors) that get worse when the participants are models sharing a base distribution and therefore sharing the same errors. Worth an experiment, not a governance guarantee.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The legal substance is real and his timeline is wrong in a way worth correcting.

DUNA legislation grants DAOs legal personhood and limited liability, letting them contract, own property, and shield members from personal liability. Wyoming enacted the first DUNA Act in March 2024. Alabama and West Virginia followed in 2026, making three states. West Virginia specifically signed its DUNA Act via Senate Bill 670 on April 3, 2026, with provisions **effective July 1, 2026**, not the day before this talk. West Virginia's definition also requires at least 100 members joined by mutual consent under an agreement for a common nonprofit purpose, which is a material constraint Levine does not mention and which sits awkwardly with a first-mover registration. The a16z crypto team did publish the model framework and the supporting advocacy, so his attribution is correct.

Two things follow. First, the "this capability did not exist until yesterday" framing, the historic-moment photograph, and the count of 21 witnesses are theatre around a statute that had been in force for two months. Second, the more interesting claim, that a DUNA is better suited to autonomous agents than to blockchains, is his own and is arguable. Legal standing plus a member-governed structure with no required executives does map unusually well onto an agent collective, and nobody appears to have tried it before. That part may genuinely be first.

On the trifecta, the literature is unambiguous and it is the reason to discount his central claim. Prompt injection has produced real exploits against Microsoft 365 Copilot, ChatGPT plugins, Google Bard, and Slack. Vendors selling guardrails advertise catching "95% of attacks," which Willison notes is precisely the problem: a 95% filter against an adversary who can retry is not a control. The recommended mitigation across the security literature is architectural boundaries that eliminate one leg, not detection and not attribution.

Where Levine is directionally right despite this: the enterprise-perimeter retreat he describes is real and costly, and something does need to make cross-organisational agent interaction safe enough to attempt. Resolvable identity backed by a legal entity is a plausible component of that, alongside the compliance screening Edge & Node demonstrated and the capability scoping the rest of the conference converged on. Presented as one layer among several it is a reasonable contribution. Presented as the resolution, it overclaims substantially.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Treat the lethal trifecta as a design constraint and remove a leg, deliberately, for every agent you deploy.** This is the actionable core and it does not require any of the talk's machinery. For each agent, write down which of the three it has: private data access, untrusted input, egress capability. If it has all three, change the architecture. Concretely: an agent that reads external documentation should not hold production credentials; an agent with write access to internal systems should not ingest arbitrary web content; an agent processing untrusted input should have no network egress beyond a fixed allowlist.
- **Keep the identity idea, discard the claim.** Resolvable counterparty identity with an audit trail is worth building for cross-organisational agent interaction and for anything that spends money. Just do not book it as a security control against injection. It is an accountability control, and those are complements.
- **Adopt the "untrusted by construction" type discipline.** The cleanest version of this in the whole conference is Notion treating inbound contact-sales form text as untrusted specifically because an agent sits downstream. Mark untrusted text at the type level and strip its tool-calling authority. Cheap, and it removes a leg rather than filtering it.
- **Try decision markets on a low-stakes internal question before believing in them.** If you run multi-agent deliberation for anything (design review, eval triage, prioritisation), the pass/fail token mechanism is a genuinely cheap experiment against the default of agents talking each other into agreement. Watch specifically for correlated errors: agents built on the same base model share priors, so a market among them can be confidently wrong in unison, which is the failure mode a human prediction market largely avoids.
- **Do not register anything under this yet.** The West Virginia statute requires at least 100 members, the legal treatment of agent-operated entities is untested in any court, and the product is pre-launch with an early-access signup. Interesting to track, premature to adopt.
- **Read Willison's original post rather than any vendor's summary of it.** It is short, it is the source of the framing everyone in this space now uses, and its conclusion, that there is no reliable detection and you must remove a leg, is the part most often dropped in retelling.
