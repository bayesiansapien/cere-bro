# LLM Knowledge Bases: a practical guide — Ben Holmes, Warp

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-08-12
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I3bpdgFJCUY

## TL;DR
This is a working demonstration of the exact architecture this wiki runs on, which makes it the most directly actionable talk of the day. Holmes builds the Karpathy LLM-wiki pattern end to end: an immutable raw directory of markdown, an agent skill that enriches each raw note with tags, source URL, and backlinks, a second pass that generates browsable wiki pages of people, concepts, organisations, and sources, and finally agent-written HTML visualisations over the whole graph. The four implementation details worth stealing are all small and all load-bearing. He writes an enrichment timestamp into each note so subsequent passes can find only what has not been processed, which is idempotency implemented in the file itself rather than in a separate state file. He keeps a controlled tag vocabulary in a reference folder and instructs the agent to be reluctant to invent new tags, because otherwise the model gets creative and the tag namespace fragments into uselessness. He puts capture ahead of organisation, arguing voice dictation at roughly 200 words per minute is the only input method fast enough to generate the raw volume the rest of the pipeline needs, and notes that local models such as Handy have made this free. And he moves the whole loop into scheduled cloud runs, syncing the markdown folder into a sandbox with the Obsidian headless CLI, running the skill, and syncing back, so he wakes up to a refreshed wiki rather than waiting on an agent. The framing he closes on is the right one: it is your own daily paper, and the graph view is useful less for navigation than for seeing where the gaps in your own thinking are.

## Key Takeaways
- The pipeline in four stages: raw folder of unstructured markdown, enrichment into an interconnected web, wiki generation on top of the enriched notes, then visualisation. Each stage is an agent skill, and each stage's output is still plain markdown on disk.
- Capture before structure, stated as the binding constraint: to get an LLM to generate wikis and visualisations you need a lot of raw material, so the goal at capture time is volume, not quality. Rambly, unformatted, badly punctuated notes are fine and he says so explicitly.
- Voice dictation as the throughput argument. Roughly 200 words per minute, faster than any typist, and awkward in an office but worth it. His own examples are podcast notes dictated immediately after listening.
- The tooling has gone free. Handy is open source and runs a local model entirely on device. Voice Ink is a similar tool with a one-time fee, hotkey activation, and a mobile app, and it formats output into sentences and paragraphs rather than a wall of text.
- The enrich-note skill does four specific things, and this is the reusable spec: stamp an enrichment timestamp, generate tags from a controlled list, research the source with web tools to recover the URL, and find related notes by key-term search and write them in as backlinks at the bottom.
- The tag-vocabulary discipline is the detail most people miss. Tags live in a dedicated reference folder so the agent has a concrete list to choose from, and the instruction explicitly tells it to be reluctant to add new ones, with permission to extend only on a genuine pattern. His reason is direct: Claude loves to get creative, so tell it not to.
- The timestamp is what makes the pipeline incremental. On a later pass the agent looks for notes without a stamp and processes only those, which means you can rerun the whole skill over the whole folder as often as you like without cost or churn.
- What enrichment alone buys you, before any wiki exists: a Wikipedia rabbit hole of your own thoughts. His example is scattered chapter notes from a novel, automatically linked, so he stops hunting for what he wrote two weeks ago and navigates by topic instead.
- The wiki layer produces entity pages, and his categorisation is people, places, and things: sources, concepts, people, organisations. He points out the unexpected entries are the valuable ones, his example being a jazz musician turning up in his AI wiki because of notes about AI's effect on the music industry.
- The provenance he cites: this is Karpathy's gist, findable by searching his name plus wiki, and Karpathy himself calls it an idea rather than a product. Holmes's own prompt was little more than describing what he cares about, pointing at the raw folder, and pasting the rest of the gist.
- The workplace application he flags: heavy meeting-note takers get a people section with an entry per person, backlinks to every related meeting, and links back to the raw notes. Useful for customer success and account work.
- Why scheduling matters: generating these on demand is slow enough to be annoying, so the loop belongs in the background on a cadence. He notes that Codex-style local automations require your laptop to be open, which is the reason to move to cloud runs.
- The cloud mechanism, described concretely: sync the markdown folder into a sandbox, run the agent skill inside it, sync back when done. He uses the Obsidian headless CLI specifically to avoid manual push and pull, and says a git clone would work equally well if you keep notes in a repo.
- The payoff framing, and it is the right one: come back in the morning to a fresh wiki, like a daily paper that is entirely yours.
- The visualisation layer needs no tooling at all. He asked an agent to read the markdown and build a graph view in HTML and Tailwind, with clickable nodes, and then trivially restyled it ("put it in space") because it is just generated code. Also demonstrated a GitHub-style contribution chart tracking his own note-taking consistency.
- What the graph is actually for, which is the insight rather than the demo: a bird's-eye view of what you are interested in and where the gaps in your thinking are. Navigation is the lesser use.
- Tools named: Handy and Voice Ink for dictation, hub.md (Hubble) as his free open-source agent-accessible notes app, oz.dev for cloud automations with schedule and Slack and iMessage triggers, and Warp as a harness that can drive any model including open weights. He mentions using GLM 5.2 for the enrichment task specifically.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The right heading for this one is not architecture in the model sense, so read it as pipeline architecture, and note first that this talk describes the system you are already running. This repo is precisely the Karpathy pattern: an immutable `raw/` of transcripts, a `wiki/` of domain folders the agent owns, `CLAUDE.md` as the schema, and `pipeline.sh` plus a launchd plist as the scheduler. So the value here is not the concept but the four implementation details this pipeline handles differently, and three of them are improvements.

The first is where idempotency lives. This pipeline tracks state in `connectors/state/processed.txt`, a separate append-only file listing processed filenames. Holmes stamps the artifact itself. The difference matters under failure: an external state file can disagree with reality in both directions, claiming a file was processed when the write failed halfway, or losing the record and reprocessing everything. A stamp inside the output is self-describing, so recovery is a directory scan rather than a reconciliation. The stronger version, which neither of us does, is to stamp with a schema or prompt version rather than only a timestamp, so that when the synthesis instructions change materially you can find every artifact produced under the old schema and reprocess exactly those. That is the cheap version of what a migration would otherwise require, and it is the single highest-value change available to this pipeline.

The second is the controlled vocabulary, and his diagnosis of why it is necessary is exactly right. An agent asked to tag freely will produce a tag namespace whose cardinality grows roughly linearly with the corpus, because near-synonyms never collide: model-compression, compression, quantization, quantisation, model-quantization all coexist. At that point tags carry no retrieval value, which is the same failure Hylak describes when he says [clusters are not issues](2026-08-12-Raindrop-Hylak-Floor-Is-The-Frontier.md) because you do not control the boundaries. A fixed vocabulary with a reluctance instruction and an explicit extension path is the correct design, and the extension path is what keeps it from ossifying. This wiki currently has an implicit vocabulary in the form of six domain folders, which is coarse but genuinely controlled. What it lacks is a cross-cutting tag layer, and that is the missing retrieval axis: there is no way to ask for everything about MoE across `ai-tech`, `deep-science`, and `finance`, even though those notes exist and reference each other.

The third is the backlink pass, and it is worth separating from tagging because it does different work. Tags are classification and backlinks are association. The existing wiki files already do this well by hand, with dense inline links to related pages, and that cross-linking is where most of the compounding value of the corpus sits, because a synthesis that positions a new talk against three prior ones is worth more than the same talk summarised alone. Holmes's contribution is that the backlink pass can run as a separate scheduled sweep over the whole corpus rather than only at write time. That matters because a note written in June cannot link forward to a talk from August, so the graph is systematically sparse in the older direction unless something goes back and repairs it. A periodic backlink sweep is how you fix a corpus that only ever links backwards.

The fourth is capture, and it is the one place his pipeline is weaker in kind but stronger in coverage. This pipeline's capture is automated ingestion of YouTube transcripts, which is higher volume and zero effort but entirely externally sourced. Holmes's is voice dictation, which is lower volume but captures original thought. The gap that leaves in a transcript-only wiki is real: there is no record of your own reaction to a talk beyond what the synthesis engine inferred, no notes from experiments you ran, no reasoning from work conversations. A `raw/` folder containing only other people's ideas produces a wiki about the discourse rather than about your thinking, and the graph view he uses to find gaps in his own thinking cannot work if none of the nodes are yours. Adding a dictation path into the same `raw/` folder is a small change with a large effect on what the corpus can tell you.

On the model-selection point, his use of GLM 5.2 for enrichment is a small routing decision with a clear structure worth noticing. Enrichment is a mechanical extraction task: pull a URL, choose from a fixed tag list, run key-term search, format frontmatter. Synthesis is the hard reasoning step. Those are different capability requirements and they do not need the same model, which is the [waterline-then-push-down](2026-08-12-LangChain-Trivedy-Improving-Agents-Data-Mining.md) discipline applied to a personal pipeline. Splitting a single expensive synthesis call into a cheap structured-extraction pass plus an expensive reasoning pass is the routing pattern in miniature, and the mechanical pass is exactly the kind of work where a cheap open model matches a frontier model.

The visualisation section is a throwaway in the talk and a real point about generated tooling. He did not install a graph tool; he asked for HTML and Tailwind and then restyled it on a whim. The property that makes this work is that the artifact is disposable: nothing depends on it, so a regenerated version is free and correctness barely matters. That is a useful test for when to generate tooling versus adopt it. Read-only views over data you own, regenerated on demand, are the ideal case. Anything that becomes a dependency is not.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The Karpathy gist is real, dates from April 2026, and the pattern has been documented well enough by others that the canonical three-layer description is worth adopting as vocabulary. Layer 1 is the raw sources directory: curated articles, papers, notes, and images, immutable, read by the LLM and never modified, functioning as ground truth. Layer 2 is the wiki directory of markdown the LLM owns completely, containing entity pages, concept pages, summaries, an index, and a log, written by the model and read by you. Layer 3 is the schema, a CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md that converts a generic agent into a disciplined wiki maintainer by defining page structure, ingestion rules, and answer formatting. The three core operations are ingest, query, and lint.

Two of those are missing here and both are worth adding. This pipeline has ingest and a schema, but no index or log page in Layer 2 and no lint operation at all. Karpathy's own framing treats lint as a first-class health check, and for a corpus of nearly 600 raw files and several hundred wiki pages that is not a nicety. The concrete lint checks that would fire today: wiki pages with no inbound links, wiki links pointing at files that do not exist, raw files present in `processed.txt` with no corresponding wiki page, near-duplicate pages (there is already a visible instance, with two separate files on the Snorkel task-fidelity talk from 2026-06-02), and files violating the CLAUDE.md structure. Several community implementations exist, including an Agent-Skills-compatible version that adds citation handling and linting, which is the closest thing to a reference for what a lint step should check.

The tooling Holmes uses checks out. Warp is now positioned as an open platform for automating development, and Oz is its orchestration layer for cloud agents providing environments, triggers, integrations, orchestration, and observability with a CLI, API, and SDK, so the schedule-plus-Slack-trigger setup he shows is a documented product feature rather than a demo hack. Hubble at hub.md is free and open source and he is candid that a lot is missing. Handy is genuinely local and genuinely free, which is the part that has changed recently: dictation used to carry a subscription and no longer needs to.

One caveat on his enthusiasm. Enrichment quality degrades quietly in a way this pattern does not surface, because an agent that cannot find a source URL will sometimes produce a plausible one, and a backlink chosen by key-term search can connect two notes that share vocabulary and nothing else. Both failures look identical to success in the rendered output. The mitigation is boring and necessary: require the agent to leave the field empty rather than guess, and spot-check a sample of generated backlinks periodically. This is the same [floor-versus-ceiling](2026-08-12-Raindrop-Hylak-Floor-Is-The-Frontier.md) point in a personal-tooling context, where the floor is a wiki that confidently tells you something you never thought.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. Add a schema version to the enrichment stamp in this pipeline, not just a date. Every wiki file should record which CLAUDE.md revision produced it. When the synthesis instructions change materially, you can then select exactly the affected files and reprocess them, which is currently impossible without reprocessing all 594 raw files.
2. Move state tracking into the artifacts and treat `processed.txt` as a cache rather than as truth. A scan for raw files with no corresponding wiki page is self-correcting; an external list is not, and it silently diverges whenever a run dies mid-write.
3. Add a lint skill and schedule it weekly. Concrete checks worth running on this corpus today: orphan wiki pages with no inbound links, broken internal links, raw files marked processed with no output page, near-duplicate pages by title similarity (start with the two Snorkel files from 2026-06-02), and structural violations of the CLAUDE.md template. Lint is one of Karpathy's three core operations and it is the one missing here.
4. Run a periodic backlink repair sweep over the whole wiki, not just at write time. Notes can only link backwards at creation, so the graph is systematically sparse toward older pages. A monthly sweep that revisits pages and adds forward links to newer related material is what converts a chronological pile into an actual graph.
5. Add a cross-cutting tag vocabulary on top of the six domain folders, kept in a reference file, with the reluctance instruction copied verbatim from his skill. The domain folders cannot express that an MoE memory discussion in `deep-science` connects to a sparsity note in `ai-tech` and a capex note in `finance`. Without a controlled list the agent will fragment the namespace within a month.
6. Open a dictation path into `raw/`, because this is the real gap. Install Handy since it is local and free, and dictate your reactions to talks, notes from experiments, and reasoning from work conversations into the same folder the transcripts land in. A wiki built only from other people's transcripts describes the discourse; the graph-gap analysis he demonstrates only works if some of the nodes are your own thinking. This is the highest-value change on this list.
7. Split the pipeline into a cheap extraction pass and an expensive synthesis pass, and route them to different models. Frontmatter, source URL recovery, tag assignment, and key-term backlink candidates are mechanical and a cheap open model will match a frontier model on them. Reserve the expensive call for the synthesis and the persona sections. This is your own routing thesis applied to your own infrastructure, and it is measurable: compare cost per file and spot-check output quality before and after.
8. Generate the graph view over this wiki and use it for gap analysis rather than navigation. With several hundred pages across six domains, ask an agent to build a clickable HTML graph from the existing inline links and look for what is missing: which domains never connect to each other, which concepts appear once and never recur, where the `deep-science` to `ai-tech` bridges the CLAUDE.md persona section asks for are actually absent.
9. Require empty fields over guessed ones in the enrichment instructions. An unfindable source URL should be blank and a weak backlink should be omitted, because a hallucinated citation in your own knowledge base is worse than a gap in it and looks exactly the same in the rendered page.
