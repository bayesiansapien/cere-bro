# Stop Fine-Tuning to Fix Retrieval Problems (Anant Srivastava)

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-10-04
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qflLT3SoVbw

## TL;DR
A short conference talk arguing that where knowledge lives (prompt, memory/retrieval, or weights) is an architecture decision that most enterprise teams make by accident, through months of small prompt edits, index additions and opportunistic fine-tunes. Srivastava's lens: prompt holds behavior (small, stable, editable), memory holds facts (large, fast-changing, citable, access-scoped), weights hold what has stopped changing (reflexes, formats, converged human judgment). The single axis that decides weights is rate of change. Fine-tune only for cost or a real capability gap, never to patch a retrieval miss. Sound and well grounded in research, but the talk is a framework with no numbers, and one of its examples (ICD-10 codes) is weaker than presented.

## Key Takeaways
- **The accident pattern:** PM tunes tone in the prompt, support adds refund docs to RAG, an engineer pastes the product catalog into the prompt, ML fine-tunes on six months of tickets. When the catalog updates, the agent still invents product names because the old catalog leaked into the weights via the tickets. Nobody owned the boundary.
- **Prompt diagnostic:** is it small, stable, and about how to behave rather than what to know? Tone, escalation-after-three-failures, "give concrete next steps." Facts in the prompt cost tokens and trigger lost-in-the-middle.
- **Memory diagnostic:** too large to fit, changing faster than you can retrain, or scoped per user/repo? Access control forces it into memory, since weights cannot enforce who sees what.
- **RAG hygiene:** code-aware chunking plus denormalized metadata (repo, permitted users) for pre-filtering. Unfiltered top-k "RAG mush" confuses the model more than it helps.
- **Memory is not a reasoning fix.** A model that cannot reason over 3 to 5 documents will not reason over 50. Fix the model, not the context.
- **Weights diagnostic:** has the information stopped changing, and have humans converged? Content moderation and claims processing: frontier model plus inconsistent human overrides until override rates flatline, then fine-tune the stable center and keep humans on the contested edge, with drift monitoring.
- **Why fine-tune at all:** almost always cost (small model, high volume, stable pattern), rarely capability, because frontier models are already capable.
- **Circulation loop:** context generates durable memory; memory is pulled into context per session; stable retrieval patterns and formats graduate from memory into weights; once in weights, you stop retrieving few-shot examples for them. The agent improves by doing its job.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The useful reframe for an optimization researcher is that this is a three-tier placement problem with a cost model. Prompt tokens are paid on every call (mitigated by prefix caching only if the prefix is stable, which argues for keeping volatile facts out of it). Retrieval adds latency and retrieved tokens per call but has near-zero update cost. Weights have zero per-call token cost and the highest update cost, plus a hidden correctness cost: fine-tuning on unknown facts measurably raises hallucination. The "memory to weights" arrow is effectively distillation of retrieval: once a format or mapping is in weights, you delete the few-shot exemplars from context, shrinking the prompt and the KV cache per request. That is a real inference saving and is the strongest argument for the cost-driven fine-tune he describes.

Where the talk is loose: his summary table says weights are about "how to reason," while his own examples are about reflexes and formats. Fine-tuning a small model rarely adds reasoning; it compresses a narrow, stable input-output mapping. That is exactly the regime where distillation and quantization of a small specialist pay off, and also where a router can send the stable center to the cheap model and the contested edge to a frontier model.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The core claim holds up well. Gekhman et al. (EMNLP 2024) showed that LLMs learn fine-tuning examples containing new facts more slowly than consistent ones, and once learned, those examples increase hallucination roughly linearly with their share of the data, supporting the view that pretraining stores facts and fine-tuning teaches their use. Ovadia et al. (EMNLP 2024) found RAG consistently beat unsupervised fine-tuning for knowledge injection, both for previously seen and entirely new facts. The leaked-catalog anecdote is exactly the Gekhman failure mode. The hybrid he does not name is RAFT (Berkeley), which fine-tunes the model to use retrieved documents and ignore distractors: that is the principled version of his "memory to weights" arrow, training the retrieval reflex rather than the facts.

The ICD-10 example is weaker than he presents. The Mount Sinai NEJM AI study did find off-the-shelf GPT-4 reaching only 34% exact match on ICD-10, which supports "do not rely on the model to know codes." But a 2025 npj Health Systems paper fine-tuned on about 74,000 ICD-10 code-description pairs and lifted exact match from under 1% to 97%, so "never fine-tune on the codes" is not settled. The defensible version is: the codebook is a slowly changing, versioned fact table (annual updates), so retrieval of candidate codes plus a fine-tuned selector is the robust design, and pure weights-only coding needs retraining per code-set release. Speaker identity checks out: Anant Srivastava presents as a principal technologist for data.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Audit before you distill.** Before fine-tuning or distilling any routing or specialist model, scan the training set for volatile facts (prices, catalogs, policy text, model names). Strip or template them so the student learns the mapping, not the facts. This is the cheapest guard against Gekhman-style hallucination.
- **Use override-rate flatlining as a fine-tune trigger.** For any routing or classification task with human review, log override rates per slice; fine-tune a small model only on slices where overrides have plateaued, and route the contested edge to the frontier model. This turns his "stable center vs contested edge" into a concrete router policy.
- **Measure the context savings.** When a format moves into weights, quantify the prompt and KV-cache reduction from dropping few-shot exemplars. That number, not accuracy, is usually what justifies the fine-tune.
- **Default RAG fix list before any training:** metadata pre-filtering, code- or structure-aware chunking, reranking, and RAFT-style distractor training if the model misuses good chunks.
