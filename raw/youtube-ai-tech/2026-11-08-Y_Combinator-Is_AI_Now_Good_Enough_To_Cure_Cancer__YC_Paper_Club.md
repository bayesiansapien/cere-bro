# Is AI Now Good Enough To Cure Cancer? | YC Paper Club

**Channel:** Y Combinator
**Published:** 2026-11-08
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-XL2gClQ2gg

## TL;DR
The tenth and final YC Paper Club is an AI-for-bio session whose real payload is not oncology, it is the claim that the recipe frontier labs use for self-improving AI research transfers directly to biology. Four speakers converge on one loop: a foundation model proposes candidates, a cheap in-silico verifier scores them, expensive physical experiments validate a top slice, and the results feed back into the model. Alfredo (Omanta) applies it to per-patient tumor genomics and reports real remissions. Yusuf Roohani (Intology, ex-Arc ML lead) shows that an agent harness tuned for autonomous LLM post-training beats off-the-shelf coding agents at molecular property prediction under matched GPU budgets. Garrick (Evo 2 lead) shows that a single data-mixture decision moved Evo 2 from state of the art to bottom of the pack. Divya shows Evo 2 does few-shot in-context learning over DNA, a capability nobody trained in, improvable with GRPO against a sequence-to-function reward model. The "cure cancer" title is marketing. The transferable content is data curation, harness engineering, and RL loop design.

## Key Takeaways
- The unifying thesis from the host: anything with a verifier is RLVR-able. In silico verification is fast and cheap, wet-lab verification is slow and expensive, but the loop structure is identical. Biology is just the same algorithm with a worse cost constant on the evaluator.
- Yusuf's result is the most directly relevant to optimization work. Intology's Locus system, originally built to top a post-training benchmark on autonomously post-training Qwen models, was pointed at molecular binding prediction and beat Anthropic and OpenAI off-the-shelf agents in the same sandbox at matched GPU hours. The claim is that generic coding agents are not optimized for search under noisy, expensive evaluation.
- Yusuf also said he favors harness engineering over weight updates. Low-hanging fruit lives in the harness first, with post-training reserved for baking in fast retrieval. He explicitly wants general-purpose solutions to avoid benchmaxing.
- Garrick's data lesson is the single densest technical fact in the session. Evo 2 (7B and 40B dense, hybrid attention plus convolution, 9 trillion base pairs, context extended to 1M base pairs) deliberately throws away trillions of tokens, keeping one genome per species and upweighting annotated genic and conserved regions. Leaving in 50 percent random whole-genome sampling costs 0.16 Spearman on the BRCA1 variant-effect benchmark. That delta is the entire gap between leading and last.
- Evo 2 has a known weakness on distal enhancer elements, and another group fixed it purely by changing data composition. Manurva, the follow-up, is bidirectional specifically to capture co-evolution and base-pairing interactions that the causal model missed, and it surfaces hundreds of non-coding RNAs that statistical methods miss in even well-studied bacterial genomes.
- Divya's finding: Evo 2 does genuine few-shot ICL from 4 to 64 examples, emergent from pure nucleotide next-token prediction. Mechanistic analysis points at induction-head-like circuits, and the proposed cause is the burstiness and long-tail power-law structure of genomic data, the same hypothesis used for natural language.
- Post-training with GRPO against a sequence-to-function reward model improved Evo 2's ability to use its own in-context examples. Reward hacking was controlled with auxiliary terms on sequence entropy and GC content. A designed enhancer validated as cell-type specific in mouse brain.
- Yusuf's negative result deserves equal billing. Agentically scraping all public transcriptomic data took 36 million cells to over 500 million, and the haul was overwhelmingly immune cells with T cells over-represented by an order of magnitude. Scale bought almost no diversity. His conclusion is to scale AI-guided experimentation rather than data collection.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
Three mechanics here map onto compression, routing, and attention work.

First, data mixture as the dominant lever. Evo 2's 0.16 Spearman swing from a mixture decision, with trillions of tokens discarded on purpose, is a cleaner natural experiment than most pruning ablations. The principle generalizes: with a fixed token budget, deduplicating toward diverse conserved signal beats volume. Garrick made this explicit when asked about intra-species variance. Training on a thousand humans would mostly buy redundancy, so one genome per species plus annotation-weighted augmentation is the better allocation. Note the two-phase curriculum as well: short context focused on genic regions first, then context extension to 1M base pairs. That is exactly the cheap-then-expensive schedule used for long-context LLMs, and it is a template worth copying when extending any context window under a compute cap.

Second, tokenization. Evo 2 tokenizes at the nucleotide level, with a vocabulary of four. Garrick said byte-level helped specifically on mutation prediction, where single-base resolution is the task. That is a concrete data point for the end-to-end byte-level argument: resolution matters where the task resolution is fine, and coarser tokenization silently destroys the signal you are trying to predict.

Third, and most useful, the cost model of the loop. Yusuf frames pure-AI research and biological discovery as the same optimization problem with different evaluator cost and noise. When a sample costs real money you shift spend from sampling to extracting information per sample, which is why he reports better results from harness and context engineering than from weight updates. Note the asymmetry he draws against Divya's result: at the biological design layer, where a wet-lab eval costs weeks, GRPO post-training against a cheap in-silico reward model was worth it, because the reward model itself is the cheap proxy. The decision rule is not "harness over weights," it is "post-train only where you have a cheap, non-hackable proxy; otherwise spend on the harness."

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The technical claims hold up better than the framing. The ICL result is real and published independently as "Genomic Next-Token Predictors are In-Context Learners" (arXiv 2511.12797, v3 March 2026), which reports log-linear gains in pattern induction as in-context demonstrations increase, on a model at mid-sized LLM scale. The Evo 2 phage genome design work reached Nature in March 2026, so "AI-designed sequences going into patients" is grounded rather than speculative. Arc's own virtual cell line is further along than the talk implies in one sense and less in another: State and the 2026 Stack preprint add in-context learning and produced the Perturb Sapiens simulated atlas, while Arc's stated goal of replacing needle-in-a-haystack experiments is a 2029 target, not a demonstrated result. The 2026 Virtual Cell Challenge is now fully zero-shot with no training set released, which is the right response to the diversity problem Yusuf described.

Two claims should be marked down. The host's assertion that AlphaFold is "now used at some stage in every drug brought to market" is unverified, and he admitted he only vetted it by asking roughly ten people. The traceable source is Hassabis saying almost every pharma company he has spoken to uses AlphaFold, and an April 2026 restatement that almost every drug developed from now on will probably have used it. Those are adoption anecdotes and forecasts, not a count. No AI-designed drug has FDA approval yet. Alfredo's "we're there, we're right at the cusp" on solving cancer is sales talk on top of a genuinely strong infrastructure story. The honest version of his contribution is narrower and still valuable: hospitals do not mine the full genomic data they already generate, insurance already covers sequencing for advanced and recurrent tumors, and agents can close that gap. His own clonal-evolution case study, where three prior chemotherapy lines were retrospectively wrong for the tumor's biology, is an argument about avoiding harm from bad therapy selection, which is a different and more defensible claim than curing cancer. Credit where due, he was appropriately skeptical of the Korean "malignancy reversal" organoid claims and asked for outcome data.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- Steal the Locus framing for your routing work. The session's strongest evidence is that an agent harness tuned for search under noisy, expensive evaluation transfers across domains at matched compute. Your routing problem has the same shape: noisy quality evals, real per-query cost, large hypothesis space. Build your router-policy search as an explicit AI R&D compute curve (performance versus GPU hours, not tokens) and compare against an off-the-shelf coding agent baseline. That is a publishable ablation and nobody in the routing literature reports it this way.
- Run the Evo 2 data-mixture experiment on your own pretraining or calibration corpus. Replace volume with diversity-weighted deduplication plus upweighting of high-signal regions, and measure the delta on your hardest benchmark. A 0.16 correlation swing from mixture alone sets the expectation that your calibration set for quantization or your distillation corpus is probably a bigger lever than your method.
- Apply the reward-hacking guardrail pattern. Divya's auxiliary entropy and GC-content terms are a domain-specific version of a general trick: when RL-ing against a proxy reward, add cheap distributional sanity constraints on the output. For router or compression policies optimized against an LLM judge, add analogous constraints (output length distribution, refusal rate, entropy) before you trust the reward curve.
- Adopt the decision rule explicitly in your next experiment plan. Spend on harness and in-context conditioning when the evaluator is expensive and the proxy is weak. Spend on post-training only when you have a cheap reward model you can stress-test for hacking.
