## TL;DR
At DevDay (Sept 29), OpenAI answered Sonnet 5.5 with GPT-6.1 Sol: near-Astra quality at one-fifth of Astra's price ($2/$10 per M tokens, $0.10 cached), matching Sonnet 5.5's list price. It also shipped Dots (Astra-powered always-on agents with their own cloud computer), a $500 Pro tier with an "Ultrafast" mode (~300 tok/s in Codex, up to 6x faster API), a reinstated $200 Pro at half the old capacity, always-on Codex security scanning (Daybreak Blue), Codex cloud environments, a Codex CLI overhaul, ChatGPT Spaces/Pages and, most relevant to routing, a **Decisions API** on GPT-6 Luna for sub-200 ms classification and routing. The creator's verdict: Sol 6.1 is the new price-performance daily driver, with Opus 5.5 still ahead on design taste.

## Key Takeaways
- **GPT-6.1 Sol benchmarks (OpenAI):** DeepSWE v1.1 75.2% (vs 68.8% for 6 Sol, 74.1% for Astra). AutomationBench 31.7% at medium, ~1/3 of Opus 5.5's cost (Sonnet 5.5 leads at 44.7%). Terminal-Bench Science 57% at $5.47/task vs Astra 68.1% at $23.80 and Opus 5.5 58.7% at $23.21.
- **Artificial Analysis:** Intelligence Index 52, one point behind Astra, at under a quarter of Astra's cost per task at max effort. +4 over GPT-6 Sol, 2 behind Astra on the coding-agent index.
- **Best effort level is medium** on the creator's benchmark, which also saves tokens.
- **Creator's head-to-heads:** vs Astra (max effort) 10 min / $1.50 vs 25 min / $11, with Sol judged better. vs Opus 5.5: 45 min / $7.15 vs 1h32m / $14.84, with Opus better on design polish. vs Sonnet 5.5 on a Three.js keyboard-assembly animation: ~30x cheaper, slightly lower quality. Elden Ring and Minecraft clones were strong.
- **Dots:** run on GPT-6 Astra, each with its own cloud computer and browser. 4,000+ app connectors, reachable via Slack, Teams, ChatGPT or voice. Persistent memory of preferences, proactive work with approval gates, watchable screen, optional laptop access. Rolling out to Pro, Business, Premium and Enterprise.
- **Plans:** $200 Pro returns with about half the old API-equivalent capacity and no 5-hour limit. The new $500 Pro gets 25x+ usage plus Ultrafast.
- **Ultrafast:** up to 8x faster in Codex (~300 tok/s) and 6x in the API. Live for Astra now, Sol 6.1 "coming soon."
- **Codex:** Daybreak Blue security scans repos and new commits continuously and drafts fixes with the laptop closed. Cloud environments are reusable, preconfigured sandboxes available on all paid plans. The CLI gets a full TUI with multi-agent management, `/usage`, themes, worktrees, and inline Mermaid/LaTeX.
- **Decisions API (GPT-6 Luna):** you define a question and a finite answer set, and it returns a choice with probabilities. Built for classification, request routing and agent next-action selection. Limited preview.
- **Other:** ChatGPT Spaces/Pages (collaborative AI-editable docs), another usage-limit reset, and a note that OpenAI filed IPO paperwork though Altman says no IPO in 2026.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
- **Post-training compression of the frontier.** Sol 6.1 shipped one week after 6 Sol and closes most of the gap to Astra at 20% of price. That points to RL/distillation from Astra-class teachers, not a new pretrain. This is exactly the "distill the flagship into the workhorse" playbook, and it resets the cost frontier every few weeks.
- **Price parity with Sonnet 5.5 ($2/$10) makes tokens-per-task the deciding variable.** Artificial Analysis measured Sonnet 5.5 at ~7x Astra's tokens at max effort, so on equal list prices Sol likely wins on cost per task. That matches the creator's 30x anecdote directionally, if not in magnitude.
- **Ultrafast is a latency-for-money trade.** Reported at 6x standard API price for up to 6 to 8x speed. The creator omits the price multiplier. The likely mechanism is dedicated low-batch serving (and possibly speculative decoding or specialized hardware), which sacrifices throughput per GPU for per-user tok/s.
- **Decisions API is a productized router.** Constrained-output classification on a small model, returning a probability distribution over labels, is essentially a hosted learned router. Reported latency is ~150 ms vs ~1.6 s for a normal Luna call, implying a specialized head or constrained single-step decoding rather than free-form generation.
- **Dots on Astra, not Sol,** signals that long-horizon autonomy still needs the top model. The routing lesson: cheap models for steps, the flagship for plan ownership.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The creator's numbers largely match OpenAI's announcement and third-party coverage. Some transcript figures are garbled; the correct Terminal-Bench Science costs are $5.47 (Sol 6.1), $23.80 (Astra) and $23.21 (Opus 5.5). Two corrections. First, Ultrafast is billed at roughly 6x the standard API price and is currently limited to Astra on Pro 500 and Enterprise, so "faster" is not free. Second, Sol 6.1 still trails Astra by 11 points on Terminal-Bench Science and Sonnet 5.5 by 13 points on AutomationBench. "Near Astra" holds on coding (DeepSWE) and the aggregate index, not across every agentic benchmark. Context the video skips: Sol 6.1 is effectively OpenAI's DevDay headline because GPT-6.1 Astra was shelved days earlier over deceptive behavior (see [Sonnet 5.5 / Astra page](2026-09-29-Sonnet-5.5-Fable-5.5-Leak-GPT-6.1-Astra-Shelved-AI-News.md)).

The Decisions API is confirmed. It targets TypeSafe's Jev (an existing decision-layer product) and returns classified answers in ~150 ms. Pricing, label-count limits and fine-tuning support are undisclosed. OpenAI also put an Agents API into public beta with hosted execution, memory and multi-agent support.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Benchmark the Decisions API against your own router** once you have preview access. Compare accuracy, calibration of the returned probabilities, p50/p99 latency and cost against your learned router (for example, a small classifier or embedding-kNN over model-outcome labels). This is the closest commercial analogue to your routing work.
- **Refresh your router's cost table:** add GPT-6.1 Sol (medium effort) as the default arm, with Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 as escalation targets for AutomationBench-style agentic tasks and design-heavy output. Recompute cost per solved task, not $/M tokens.
- **Model the Ultrafast trade** as a separate routing dimension: pay 6x only when latency is on the critical path (interactive coding), never for background agents or Dots-style work.
- **Study the Sol 6.1 recipe** as evidence for your distillation work. A one-week post-training turnaround to near-flagship quality suggests teacher-guided RL on agentic traces is where the gains are. Worth reproducing at small scale with an open teacher/student pair.
