# You Don't Retrieve Memories. You Generate Them.

**Channel:** Curt Jaimungal  
**Published:** 2026-06-01  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVa14WZYjZY  

## TL;DR
This video presents a radical theory of cognition: the human brain is an auto-regressive next-token predictor, just like a Large Language Model. Memory is not a retrieval of stored files but a "just-in-time" generative process. What we call "remembering" is actually the brain predicting the next cognitive token based on the "pregnant present."

## Key Takeaways
- **The Brain as LLM:** Cognition is the continuous computation of the "next cognitive token" over a loop of past and likely future trajectories.
- **Generative Memory:** We don't store facts; we store the *capacity* to generate them. Asking "what did you do last summer?" is a prompt that triggers an auto-regressive generation.
- **The Pregnant Present:** Every "next token" takes into account the entire past and the projected trajectory of the future, leading to long-range narratives and complex motor control (like walking or dancing).
- **Infinite Potentiality:** Much like an LLM can answer an infinite number of questions without storing every answer, the human mind possesses a generative capacity that far exceeds static storage.

## Core Architecture & Research Claims
The speaker advocates for a shift from "storage and retrieval" models to "Autoregressive Frameworks for Cognition." This theory posits that the brain is an optimized generator rather than a feedback-correction loop (challenging older "Predictive Coding" theories). The "tokens" are not just words but neural states, allowing for a unified theory of language, vision, and motor control.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
In 2024 and 2025, researchers like Prof. Elan Barenholtz popularized the idea that "Memory is Hallucination." Studies using representational alignment (Binz et al., 2025) have shown that the internal activations of frontier LLMs like GPT-5.5 align significantly with human fMRI data, suggesting the "next token" objective might be the fundamental algorithm of intelligence.

The 2025 launch of "BrainOmni" (Xiao et al.) allowed researchers to predict "next brain tokens" directly from MEG data, enabling the generation of synthetic thoughts. While LLMs still rely more on surface-level statistical patterns ("Embers of Autoregression"), the phase transition to "Geometric Memory" seen in 2026 models suggests that generative systems are finally moving toward the "Platonic representations" found in the human mind.
