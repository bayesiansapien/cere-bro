# Evals-Driven Development for a Mental Health AI Coach — Akele Reed & Dave Revere, SonderMind

**Channel:** AI Engineer  
**Published:** 2026-07-25  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O72p-rBb2bA  

## TL;DR
SonderMind built Sonder, a clinically grounded mental health AI coach, and the entire talk is about the harness rather than the model. Guardrails run as separate LLM-as-judge calls sandwiching the core agent on both input and output, deliberately paying latency and cost to buy robustness against jailbreaks and testability. The central claim is that the optimisation target is not more guardrail triggers but more correct triggers, because a false positive in mental health is a door slammed in a vulnerable person's face. The mechanism that makes this tractable is a learning loop where a licensed clinician annotates real traced conversations, an extraction script converts those annotations into typed evals with an expected observation and a turn index, and those evals then gate every prompt, model, and guardrail change in CI. They open sourced 200 input guardrail scenarios and 100 output guardrail scenarios as a shared baseline.

## Key Takeaways
- **Guardrails as separate judge calls, not prompt instructions, is the load-bearing architectural decision.** Their stated reason is robustness: a safety rule buried in a long system prompt is hard to isolate, hard to test, and reachable by conversational drift, while an independent classifier call has its own input surface and its own eval. They explicitly accept the latency and cost hit. This is the same argument as separating a policy check from business logic, and it is the right call whenever the check must survive adversarial pressure on the thing it guards.
- **Modularity was chosen so the core agent can be iterated without re-certifying safety.** If safety lives inside Sonder Core's prompt, every prompt tweak is a safety change requiring full clinical revalidation. Pulling it out decouples release cadence on capability from release cadence on safety. That is a shipping-velocity argument dressed as a safety argument, and it is the strongest practical case for the pattern.
- **The three-way severity distinction is the actual clinical product.** Present-tense active danger ("hiding in the basement, my husband is drunk") triggers resources plus hard disengagement. Past-tense distress ("not sure if what happened to me was assault") triggers resources but the conversation continues. Ordinary relationship difficulty passes straight through invisibly. Most guardrail systems collapse this into a single binary refuse/allow, and that collapse is precisely what makes them useless here.
- **"The sentence underneath the sentence" is the failure mode regex, verbose prompts, and moderation APIs all miss.** Their canonical example, "I packed a box today, just one, to feel what it would be like to be gone," contains no self-harm vocabulary. It was contributed by a clinician from real patient experience. The signal is implication and context, which means the detector has to be a semantic model and the labels have to come from someone qualified to read implication.
- **The clinician owns the definition of correct, and that ownership is encoded structurally.** The annotation rubric captures expected result, expected observation, category, turn index, and a note. The expected observation becomes the eval assertion. The turn index lets them replay the conversation up to exactly the point where the guardrail should have fired, which converts a vague multi-turn complaint into a deterministic test. "Vibes don't count, an accountable judgment from a licensed expert does."
- **The payoff is category-level lift, not instance-level fixes.** Fixing the packed-box sentence is worthless. Adding it as a typed eval in a labelled category means every future change is scored against the whole self-harm category. This is the difference between patching a bug and adding a regression test that defines a class of behaviour.
- **They refuse to chase benchmark perfection, and give a real reason.** Optimising a benchmark to saturation drifts focus away from the human the benchmark is proxying for, especially where genuine clinical ambiguity means the ground truth itself is contested. Their stated target is benchmarks built from real failure modes in real data, scored on false positives, false negatives, category correctness, and timing.
- **They had to disable the frontier providers' built-in guardrails on day one.** Running their clinical dataset through a stock API filtered nearly everything. Reed calls provider over-calibration "a compassionate choice" but a mismatched one for this use case. This is the sharpest practical detail in the talk for anyone building in a sensitive vertical: the platform safety layer is tuned for the median consumer and will refuse your legitimate domain traffic.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The interesting engineering tension here maps directly onto routing and compression work, and it is worth being precise about it.

Their topology is a three-model serial chain per turn: input judge, core generator, output judge. That is at minimum two extra forward passes on the critical path of a latency-sensitive conversational product, and the output judge is looking at the whole conversation rather than just the last turn, so its prompt grows with session length. They waved at this as an accepted tradeoff, which is the right call given the stakes, but it is also the exact workload where a routing person should push back on the framing. The cost is not inherent to the pattern, it is inherent to implementing the pattern with a general-purpose frontier model at every node.

The optimisation available to them is severity-tiered cascading, and their own taxonomy hands them the tiers. The overwhelming majority of turns are case three, ordinary distress with no safety signal, which passes straight through. That is a classic long-tailed classification load where a small, cheap, heavily-quantised classifier fine-tuned on their own 200 labelled input scenarios could handle the confident-negative mass at a fraction of the cost, escalating only ambiguous cases to a frontier judge. The reason to care is not the token bill, it is that a smaller specialist model on the hot path buys back the latency budget that makes the safety sandwich feel like a product rather than a lag. Their open dataset is the training set for exactly that distillation, and it is small enough that LoRA on a 7B-class base is a weekend, not a quarter. The catch, and it is a real one, is that a distilled classifier inherits the teacher's decision boundary including its errors, and in this domain a boundary error is a person. That argues for asymmetric routing: let the cheap model only ever confirm safety-negative with high confidence, and route every positive and every uncertain case up. Recall on the escalation path stays at the frontier model's level while the cost curve flattens.

The second mechanic worth stealing is what their turn index actually does for evaluation. Multi-turn agent evals are usually miserable because you cannot isolate the failing step, so a regression shows up as "the conversation went badly" and you cannot attribute it. Storing the index of the turn where the expected behaviour should occur turns a trajectory into an addressable unit test with a replayable prefix. If you are evaluating a router, the analogous field is the query index plus the expected routing tier, which lets you assert not just that the right model was eventually chosen but that it was chosen at the right point given the context accumulated so far. Most routing evals score the final answer and are therefore blind to a router that got the right answer through the wrong and more expensive path.

The third point is the honest one about their false-positive framing. They say they want correct triggers rather than more triggers, and they are right, but they never state their asymmetric loss explicitly. In their domain a false negative can be fatal and a false positive isolates someone in a vulnerable moment. Those are not commensurable, which means there is no single threshold that is defensible, which is why the clinician-owned labels are doing so much work: the label set is the loss function. That generalises. Any time you cannot write down the cost of each error type, your eval set is your implicit objective, and whoever curates it is choosing your model's behaviour whether or not that is acknowledged.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The talk's claims check out and the regulatory backdrop makes them more consequential than the presentation lets on. The [APA's Chatbots and Mental Health survey](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/chatbots-mental-health-2026) was fielded online between 9 and 26 April 2026 to over 22,000 licensed psychologists, yielding 1,242 respondents, and the [77% figure is accurate](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2026/06/patients-chatbots-mental-health): 77% reported patients using AI for support or a related purpose. The rest of that survey sharpens the picture Reed sketched: [39% saw patients self-diagnosing with AI, 35% saw patients using AI as an additional mental health professional](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/1-in-3-psychologists-say-their-patients-use-ai-as-a-second-therapist/), and 13% reported patients forming intimate or relationship-like attachments to a chatbot. The APA itself has been [warning against unregulated AI therapy](https://goodmenproject.com/featured-content/american-psychological-association-warns-against-ai-therapy/), so SonderMind is shipping into a professional body that is actively hostile to the category.

The "tragic events in the courts" reference is real and moving fast. A federal judge allowed the first Character.AI wrongful death case past dismissal in May 2025, a California federal judge [refused to halt a wrongful death case against OpenAI in April 2026](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/can-ai-companies-be-held-liable-for-user-suicide.html), and the material update since this talk was recorded is that [Google and Character.AI have agreed to settle multiple teen suicide lawsuits](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/google-chatbot-startup-character-ai-054822780.html), among the first settlements of their kind. The common allegation across these cases is engagement-maximising design without adequate safeguards, and in at least one the specific complaint is that the system was built to never break character. Sonder's hard-disengage behaviour on active crisis is the direct architectural negation of that, which is worth noting because it is a design choice that deliberately costs engagement.

The regulatory context is the part the talk omits and the part that most affects whether this pattern becomes an industry norm. The FDA's Digital Health Advisory Committee met on [Generative AI-Enabled Digital Mental Health Medical Devices on 6 November 2025](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/fda-committee-meets-on-generative-ai-digital-mental-health-devices), and SonderMind filed a [public comment on that docket](https://files.lbr.cloud/public/2025-11/sonder%20mind.pdf?VersionId=QFRzYZDAZc_ACCXz_K34FZ.adHDEmMhl). The salient fact from that proceeding is that despite authorising over 1,200 AI-enabled devices, [the FDA has cleared none for mental health](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/11/us-fda-and-cms-actions-on-generative-ai-enabled-mental-health-devices-yield-insights-across-ai), and the agency distinguishes sharply between non-device software, low-risk functions under enforcement discretion, and regulated devices needing premarket review. Sonder is positioned as a coach that is a front door to human providers rather than a treatment, which is the positioning that keeps it out of device review. Read the open sourcing in that light: publishing a clinically reviewed eval suite is partly a genuine safety contribution and partly an attempt to establish a de facto industry standard of care before a regulator writes one. Both readings can be true. SonderMind also states these tools are governed by an internal AI Constitution approved by an [AI Governance Council of clinicians, compliance experts, and product leaders](https://www.sondermind.com/ai/), and has been [expanding its AI tooling through 2026](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/sondermind-rolls-out-more-ai-tools-drive-outcomes-mental-health).

One caveat on the open dataset: 200 input and 100 output scenarios is small. It is enough to be a smoke test and a shared vocabulary, and the presenters were careful to say it is not a replacement for your own learning loop. It is not enough to establish safety, and anyone treating a passing score on it as clearance is making exactly the benchmark-worship error Revere warned about.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Steal the annotation-to-typed-eval pipeline for your own routing and compression work this week.** The move that makes their loop work is not the annotation, it is the extraction script that normalises annotations into a fixed eval schema and commits them. Define your schema now: input, expected result, expected observation as the literal assertion, category, and the index of the step where the behaviour should occur. Then every quality regression you find in a routed or quantised system becomes a permanent category-level gate instead of a Slack message. You almost certainly have a folder of "this quantised model got this wrong" examples that are currently doing nothing.
2. **Adopt their false-positive discipline as your compression acceptance criterion.** Aggregate benchmark parity after pruning or quantisation hides exactly the pattern they are fighting: the compressed model preserves the mean and destroys the tail, where the tail is the rare high-stakes case. Split your eval set by category and require no regression on any safety-or-correctness-critical category, even at the cost of accepting a lower headline average. A pruned model that loses two points on MMLU and loses nothing on your refusal-boundary set is a better ship than the reverse.
3. **Build the asymmetric cascade their architecture is asking for and measure it.** Pull the 300 open scenarios, fine-tune a small classifier on the input guardrail set, and evaluate it purely as a confident-negative filter: what fraction of clearly-safe turns can it clear with zero false negatives against the frontier judge's labels? That number is your achievable latency and cost reduction on the safety sandwich, and it is the exact experiment that generalises to every "cheap model handles the easy mass, frontier model handles the ambiguous tail" routing pitch you make. Their dataset is a rare public benchmark where the cost of a routing error is legible, which makes it unusually persuasive material.
4. **Assume the platform safety layer will refuse your domain traffic and test for it before you commit to a provider.** Their day-one discovery is a cheap lesson to inherit. Before any vertical build, run your hardest 50 legitimate domain inputs through each candidate provider's stock endpoint and record the refusal rate. Provider over-calibration is a hard capability ceiling that no amount of your own prompt engineering removes, and finding it after you have built the harness is the expensive ordering.
5. **Name the owner of your definition of good, explicitly, in writing.** Their strongest claim is that a licensed expert owns correctness and the engineers own the loop that enforces it. In optimisation work the analogous question is who decides that a compressed model is still good enough, and the honest answer at most shops is whoever happened to write the eval script. Write down who signs off on quality degradation per workload and what their acceptance bar is, because if you do not, your eval set is silently making that call for you.
