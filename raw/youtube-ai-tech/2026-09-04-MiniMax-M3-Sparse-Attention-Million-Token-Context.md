# Why AI Agents Need Million-Token Context | Thomas Wolf & Olive Song, MiniMax

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-09-04
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Cxe5dv2Xlw

## TL;DR
Thomas Wolf of Hugging Face interviews Olive Song of MiniMax about M3, a 428 billion parameter MoE with roughly 23 billion active, a genuinely functional 1 million token context, and native multimodality. The technical core is MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA), which has a two-branch design: an index branch that selects at a high level which blocks of context matter, and a sparse attention branch that computes only over the selected blocks. Song's argument for why long context became urgent is specifically agentic: their earlier models handled 10 million tokens for passive tasks like reviewing a dumped-in book, but an agent interacting with an environment across many rounds and accumulating tool responses exhausts short context on complex tasks. The second technical claim is native multimodality trained from step zero rather than bolted on, because post-hoc adapter training degrades text performance, vision convergence is pulled toward the text solution, and mid-training continuation is highly recipe-sensitive and does not transfer across model scales. The organisational detail is the most surprising: MSA was designed by an intern.

## Key Takeaways
- **The MSA two-branch design is the architecture to understand.** An index branch operates at a coarse level to select which context blocks matter, then a sparse attention branch performs the actual computation only over those selected blocks. Song emphasises that the design goal was simplicity and scalability, so that both context length and model size can be scaled with the same architecture.
- **The agentic justification for long context is the correct one and it is under-argued elsewhere.** A 10 million token passive summarisation task is not what forces long context. What forces it is an agent doing multiple rounds, receiving full tool responses, and interacting with an environment, where the transcript grows unavoidably and truncation loses state the agent needs.
- **Native multimodality from step zero, with three specific reasons why the alternatives fail.** Post-hoc adapter training harms text performance and vision does not converge well because the model has already settled into a text-optimal solution. Mid-pretraining continuation is highly recipe-sensitive, varying with architecture, data mixture, and learning rate, which means experimental conclusions do not transfer to larger models. Both approaches are also less scalable in data terms.
- **How they made step-zero training work,** which is the part most labs fail at because the model collapses after a few steps: substantial work on the vision transformer, interleaved data (natural data keeping images and videos in place rather than masking them out), careful cleaning and masking, and strong reward modelling.
- **The intern detail is a genuine organisational finding, not a throwaway.** MSA was designed by an intern. Song contrasts this with labs where interns lack access to the data and the work, and notes MiniMax's structure explicitly enables it.
- **Their research organisation is bottom-up and worth describing precisely.** Solid foundations and infrastructure so anyone can work with the model. After a release, when people are free, they experiment, write their own evaluations, find weaknesses themselves, and propose what to improve. Others interested join the project. Work runs for weeks or months, and successful results ship into the final training of the next model. Architecture work in particular can require long investigation including redoing pre-training evaluations.
- **Distribution scale is larger than the model discussion suggests:** MiniMax apps reach over 300 million people across roughly 200 countries and over a million companies. Song's framing is that models came first (multimodal in and out was the CEO's plan from day one, before ChatGPT) and apps followed because most people cannot experience a model through an API.
- **Research automation is already substantial.** They build their own research harnesses that automate much of their workflow, including kernel optimisation, letting models post-train other models, and automated data generation. Song says M3 is particularly good at long-horizon work and kernel optimisation, and that they harness that internally to speed iteration. Asked whether M3 is building M4, her answer is that it is building M3.1.
- **Wolf's historical framing is a good one.** A great deal of work on attention efficiency, including many linear attention variants, largely disappeared when FlashAttention arrived, because it turned out the need was more efficient kernels rather than a different mathematical formulation. He notes with approval that the field is now returning to first-principles questions about what attention is and how to make it more efficient.
- **On the future, Song is asked about trillion-token attention (Jeff Dean's pitch) and says it is worth exploring, requiring joint architecture and hardware research.** She confirms models above a trillion parameters are planned, and names multi-agent systems and model routing as what she currently finds most exciting, partly because they reveal what models can and cannot do.
- **The ask to the community:** report problems, especially with multimodality since this is the first time they have combined it, and request features, with thinking-effort control given as an example.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
This is the most directly relevant talk in the corpus for Amit, and the important observation is convergence: MSA and Tencent's Gated DSA with IndexCache, released within weeks of each other, are the same architectural idea.

Both split attention into a cheap selection stage and an expensive computation stage. MiniMax calls it an index branch plus a sparse attention branch. Tencent caches selected sparse-attention indices and reuses them across layers. In both cases the insight is that the selection function is far cheaper than attention itself, so you spend a little compute deciding what to attend to and then attend to a small fraction. That is the architecture that makes million-token context economically viable, and two independent frontier labs arriving at it in the same window is strong evidence it is the right answer rather than a design preference.

The design question that separates implementations is where the selection cost is amortised. Tencent amortises across layers by caching indices, on the assumption that which tokens matter is stable through network depth. MiniMax amortises across blocks by selecting at a coarse granularity. Those are different bets with different failure modes, and the empirical question is which assumption holds better at long context. That is a well-posed comparison and nobody appears to have run it.

The efficiency numbers are what make the case, and they are large. Per-token compute at 1 million context reduced to roughly one twentieth of the previous generation, with 9x prefill and 15x decode speedup against M2 at 1M context. Note the asymmetry: decode improves more than prefill. That makes sense, because prefill is compute-bound and already parallel across the sequence, while decode is memory-bandwidth-bound and every step must attend over the entire accumulated context. Sparse selection cuts the per-step attention read, which is exactly the decode bottleneck. So for agentic workloads, which are decode-dominated across many turns, the effective speedup is closer to the 15x figure than to the 9x one, and that is the number that matters for Song's agentic argument.

The native-multimodality reasoning is a clean statement of a general training principle worth internalising. Her argument against post-hoc adapters is that the model has already converged to a text-optimal solution, so the vision pathway is fitted into a landscape shaped entirely by text, and adding it degrades the text performance it was fitted around. Her argument against mid-training continuation is subtler and more important for research practice: it is recipe-sensitive across architecture, data mixture, and learning rate, which means conclusions from small-scale experiments do not transfer to larger models. That is a statement about experimental validity, not just about performance, and it is the stronger objection. Any training intervention whose optimal recipe depends on scale cannot be validated cheaply, which makes it enormously expensive to develop.

The intern-designed-architecture fact deserves attention as an organisational finding rather than a curiosity. The stated enabling conditions are specific: infrastructure good enough that anyone can experiment with the model, access to real data and real work, and a bottom-up proposal process where you find your own weakness, write your own evaluation, and propose your own project. That is a research organisation optimised for the fact that architectural insight is unpredictable in origin, and it is the opposite of a roadmap-driven structure. For Amit, the transferable element is the middle piece: the reason people can propose good projects is that they first write their own evaluations and find their own weaknesses, so the proposal comes with a measurement already attached.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
Every technical claim checks out, and the published figures are more specific than the interview.

**MiniMax M3 was released June 1, 2026**, with 428 billion total parameters and roughly 23 billion active per token, a 1 million token context, and native image and video input. So the conversation was recorded around June and published in September, which explains Song saying "earlier this month."

**The attention stack is grouped-query attention combined with MiniMax Sparse Attention**, and MSA cuts per-token compute at 1M context to roughly one twentieth of the previous generation. Measured against M2 at 1M context, M3 delivers **9x prefill and 15x decode speedups**. There is a dedicated MSA paper (arXiv 2606.13392), which is worth reading directly given how central the mechanism is.

**On capability, M3 scores 80.5% on SWE-bench Verified** and is reported to match Claude Sonnet 4.6 on real-world agentic benchmarks. It is described as the first open-weight model to pair frontier agentic coding with native multimodality, which supports Wolf's observation that it is the only top-five open model that is genuinely multimodal.

**One correction on licensing, and it matters.** Wolf repeatedly calls this an open-source model. The weights are published on Hugging Face under a **custom minimax-community license**, not under a standard open-source licence. That is a meaningful difference from Tencent's HY4, which shipped under Apache 2.0, and it affects what a company can actually do with it. Open weights and open source are not the same thing, and the distinction should be checked before building on it.

For comparison against the adjacent release: Tencent's Hunyuan HY4, released August 28, 2026, is 770B total with 49B active, 1M context, Apache 2.0, using Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention with IndexCache. So M3 is roughly half the total parameters with half the active parameters, and is multimodal where HY4 is not. Two labs, two months apart, converging on sparse attention with a cheap selection stage as the route to million-token context.

**Caveats on the source.** This is a friendly on-stage interview at an event where the interviewer is a co-founder of the platform hosting the model, and the benchmark and capability claims are the lab's own. The 300 million users, 200 countries, and one million companies figures are self-reported and unaudited. Song is candid about current multimodal limitations and explicitly asks for failure reports, which is a good sign. The "an intern designed it" story is charming and unverifiable.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Read the MSA paper (arXiv 2606.13392) alongside HY4's Gated DSA, because the comparison is the interesting artefact.** Both split attention into cheap selection plus sparse computation, and they amortise the selection cost differently, MiniMax across coarse blocks and Tencent across layers via index caching. Which amortisation assumption holds better at long context is an open, well-posed empirical question with immediate practical stakes.
- **Note that decode speedup exceeds prefill speedup, and that this is the number that matters for agents.** 15x decode against 9x prefill reflects that decode is memory-bandwidth-bound over the full context while prefill is compute-bound and parallel. Agentic workloads are decode-dominated, so sparse attention's real payoff is larger than the headline prefill figure suggests. Amit should quote the decode number when arguing for sparse attention in agentic serving.
- **Adopt the recipe-sensitivity argument as a criterion for research investment.** Song's strongest objection to mid-training multimodal continuation is not that it performs worse, it is that the optimal recipe varies with architecture, data mixture, and learning rate, so small-scale conclusions do not transfer. Any technique with that property is expensive to develop regardless of its ceiling, and scale-transferability of experimental conclusions is a criterion worth applying explicitly to Amit's own project selection.
- **Check the licence before building on an "open source" model.** M3 ships under a custom minimax-community licence, not a standard open-source licence, while HY4 ships under Apache 2.0. Wolf uses the terms interchangeably on stage and they are not interchangeable in a commercial context.
- **Steal the evaluation-first proposal process.** The reason MiniMax gets useful bottom-up proposals is that contributors first experiment with the released model, write their own evaluations, and find their own weaknesses, so a proposal arrives with a measurement attached. That single requirement, bring your own eval, would improve most research proposal processes and it costs nothing to impose.
- **Take the agentic-context argument seriously as a capacity planning input.** The reason 1M context matters is not document summarisation, it is that multi-round agent transcripts with full tool responses grow unavoidably. If Amit is sizing serving capacity for agentic workloads, the context length to plan for is set by transcript accumulation over turns, not by input document size, and it is far larger than intuition suggests.
- **Watch the multi-agent and model-routing direction that Song names as most exciting,** because it is Amit's area and because her reason for finding it interesting is the useful one: routing across a multi-agent system reveals what models can and cannot do, which makes it a diagnostic instrument as well as a cost optimisation.
