## TL;DR
Nvidia unveils the **RTX Spark**, a unified "superchip" for laptops that combines a 20-core Grace CPU with a Blackwell GPU. Designed for offline AI, it can run 120-billion parameter models locally, effectively challenging Apple’s M-series dominance in the professional and gaming laptop markets.

## Key Takeaways
- **Specs Powerhouse:** The chip features 6,144 Blackwell CUDA cores (equivalent to a desktop RTX 5070) and up to 128GB of LPDDR5X unified memory via NVLink-C2C.
- **Offline AI Sovereignty:** Capable of running massive 120B parameter models (e.g., gpt-oss-120B) completely offline, ensuring data privacy and zero latency.
- **AAA Gaming on Battery:** Using DLSS 4.5 and frame generation, the chip pushes 100 FPS at 1440p in AAA titles, a category where Apple still lags.
- **Form Factor Shift:** The unified silicon eliminates the need for large desktop towers, enabling server-grade power in thin-and-light laptops.

## Core Architecture & Research Claims
- **NVLink-C2C:** The high-speed interconnect provides 300 GB/s bandwidth between CPU and GPU, matching the unified memory architecture advantages previously exclusive to Apple Silicon.
- **1 Million Token Local Context:** The large memory pool enables local "agentic" workflows with massive context windows, allowing AI to reference entire local databases securely.
- **Grace-Blackwell Fusion:** This is the first consumer-facing implementation of Nvidia's data center architecture, bringing FP4 precision and 1 petaflop of AI compute to the PC platform.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The RTX Spark was formally unveiled at Computex 2026. Web results confirm a Fall 2026 launch window for laptops from ASUS, Dell, and Microsoft (Surface Laptop Ultra). This platform represents NVIDIA's move to counter the "AI PC" push from Intel and Qualcomm by leveraging their dominant Blackwell architecture.

A critical correction to common misconceptions: while Apple Silicon still holds an efficiency lead in pure "web browsing" battery life, the RTX Spark's ability to maintain high performance *without* thermal throttling during heavy AI inference or gaming marks a significant shift. The inclusion of **NVIDIA OpenShell** also provides a local governance layer for AI agents, which is a major selling point for privacy-conscious enterprise users.
