# "AI Is a Massive Scam!" (Ed Zitron)

**Channel:** The Diary Of A CEO
**Published:** 2026-08-27
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJBp2WPSZR0

## TL;DR
A clip from Steven Bartlett's episode with tech critic Ed Zitron, structured as a rapid myth-teardown. Zitron's position: generative AI is a con, sold as magic while being expensive unprofitable cloud software; the industry generates no real economic growth because every major player runs at enormous losses; AI is not replacing jobs and there is no economic data showing it is; the trillion-dollar China race is manufactured urgency; and the models are already in the wrong hands, namely Zuckerberg, Altman, and Amodei. The financial claim is close to correct with an important nuance, the jobs claim is defensible in aggregate but wrong in the specifics that matter, and the framing as a deliberate con is where reasonable people should get off the bus. See also [the August 28 clip from the same episode](../ai-tech/2026-08-28-Is-AI-Just-A-Scam-Ed-Zitron-DOAC.md).

## Key Takeaways
- **"OpenAI lost $20.9 billion last year."** This is a real number derived from real documents, and it is specifically the operating loss: $34 billion in total costs and expenses against $13.07 billion in 2025 revenue. Zitron's own reporting, based on audited financials, puts the net loss attributable to the company at $38.53 billion, roughly 7.5 times the $5.09 billion lost in 2024 on $3.7 billion of revenue. So he is quoting the conservative figure, not the inflated one.
- **"None of these people can say they are on a path to profitability."** The strongest part of his case, and largely accurate at the frontier-lab level.
- **"AI will replace all human jobs, no economic data supports it."** Correct as stated, because "all jobs" is a strawman. The aggregate data does show no detectable rise in unemployment among highly exposed workers since late 2022. It also shows a roughly 14% drop in job-finding rate in exposed occupations versus 2022, and measurable concentrated damage in specific categories. Both things are true, and the clip only reports one.
- **The circularity critique, which the clip only gestures at.** Zitron's fuller argument is that Microsoft, Google, and Amazon fund OpenAI and Anthropic, who spend that money back on GPUs and cloud compute from those same three companies. This is the most serious analytical point he makes and it deserves engagement rather than the rhetoric surrounding it.
- **"They are already in the wrong hands."** A values claim rather than an analytical one, and the Shrek and Lord Farquaad comparison plus the poverty-versus-GPUs line are moral framing. Fine as opinion, not evidence.
- **Zitron's credibility positioning.** Sixteen years in the tech industry, states he loves technology, objects to being misled. Bartlett notes he is the first guest with this view, which says more about guest selection than about consensus.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The economics here are directly downstream of inference cost structure, which is Amit's professional territory, and that is the frame in which Zitron is both most right and least interesting.

- **Unit economics, not revenue, is the actual question.** $13.07 billion of revenue against $34 billion of cost is not necessarily a con, it is a company subsidising inference to buy market share while betting that cost-per-token falls faster than price. That bet is not absurd: cost per unit of capability has fallen by orders of magnitude through quantization, distillation, better kernels, speculative decoding, MoE sparsity, and KV-cache optimization. The question Zitron never engages is the derivative. If serving cost per useful task is dropping 5 to 10x annually and revenue is growing, the loss is a financing problem. If it is not, he is right.
- **The capex-to-revenue gap is the number that matters.** Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta plan roughly $725 billion of capex in 2026, up about 77% from $410 billion. Against that, direct AI services revenue is on the order of $25 billion, roughly 4% of infrastructure spend. Microsoft's AI business is at a $37 billion annual run rate growing 123%, AWS at roughly $150 billion annualised growing 28%, Google Cloud backlog above $460 billion. So the revenue is real and growing fast, and the spend is growing faster.
- **The debt signal is the genuinely worrying datapoint.** Aggregate capex for the big five now exceeds projected cash flows after buybacks and dividends, and the group raised $108 billion in new debt in 2025. Self-funded capex from a monopoly cash flow is a strategic bet. Debt-funded capex is a leveraged bet, and leverage is what turns an overbuild into a crisis. This is the strongest version of the bear case and Zitron does not need the word "con" to make it.
- **Where a bubble bursting helps rather than hurts optimization work.** If capital tightens, the value of every technique that reduces cost per token rises sharply. Pruning, quantization, distillation, and routing are counter-cyclical skills. The AI capex supercycle has made brute-force scaling the default because compute was effectively free at the margin. A funding squeeze makes efficiency the differentiator again.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
On the finances, Zitron is not fabricating. His reporting on OpenAI's 2025 audited numbers, $13.07 billion revenue, $34 billion total costs, $38.53 billion net loss attributable, is specific, sourced, and has not been credibly refuted. He is a genuine primary source here, not a commentator recycling headlines, and dismissing him because his tone is hostile would be a mistake.

On jobs, the evidence is more interesting than either side's summary. There is no detectable increase in aggregate unemployment for highly exposed workers since ChatGPT launched. But: job-finding rates in exposed occupations are down roughly 14% versus 2022, entry into the most exposed jobs has fallen by about half a percentage point, 38% of employers report shifting basic data processing away from entry-level workers onto AI, and 31% have raised experience requirements for entry-level roles as a result. PwC's 2026 barometer found AI-exposed entry-level roles are seven times more likely to demand traditionally senior skills, with those roles growing 35% since 2019 while other entry-level roles declined 10%. In narrower markets the effect is sharp: online freelance writing jobs fell 2% within four months of ChatGPT's release with writer earnings down 5.2%, while demand for machine learning programming rose 24%.

The honest synthesis is that Zitron's headline claim survives ("AI is not replacing all jobs") while the implied claim fails ("nothing is happening to labour"). The effect is not mass unemployment, it is a closing of the bottom rung, which is a distributional harm that unemployment statistics are structurally bad at capturing. Anyone using aggregate unemployment to dismiss AI labour effects is measuring the wrong variable.

Where Zitron overreaches: "con" and "scam" are claims about intent, and intent is not established by unprofitability. Amazon was unprofitable for years, as was Uber. A company can be sincerely wrong about its own path to profitability. His separate prediction that OpenAI runs out of cash by 2027 and triggers a tech depression is falsifiable and dated, which is to his credit, but it is a forecast, not a finding, and it rests on assumptions about funding availability that a single large raise would invalidate. Also worth noting the incentive symmetry nobody mentions: Zitron has built a substantial media business on this thesis, exactly as the labs have built valuations on the opposite one. Both are talking their book.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Steal the operating-loss framing for internal cost arguments.** The $34 billion against $13.07 billion comparison is the clearest possible articulation of why cost-per-token work is strategic rather than cosmetic. When arguing for headcount or compute on quantization, distillation, or routing projects, the argument is not "we can save money," it is "the industry's aggregate revenue is 4% of its infrastructure spend, and the gap closes through efficiency or it does not close."
- **Track the capex-to-revenue ratio quarterly as a career and portfolio signal.** Roughly $725 billion capex against roughly $25 billion direct AI services revenue is the single most informative number in this space. If that ratio improves through 2027, the bear case weakens. If capex keeps growing while the ratio worsens and debt issuance continues, a funding contraction is the base case, which raises the value of optimization expertise and lowers the value of scaling-dependent bets.
- **Do not use aggregate unemployment to reason about AI labour effects.** For anyone hiring or mentoring, the actionable finding is that the entry-level rung is being removed while senior-skill expectations are pushed downward. That has direct implications for how junior researchers are onboarded: the tasks that used to build competence through repetition are now automated, so competence has to be built deliberately rather than incidentally.
- **Separate the financial critique from the moral one when evaluating this content.** The financial analysis is well-sourced and worth acting on. The intent claim is unfalsifiable and worth discarding.
