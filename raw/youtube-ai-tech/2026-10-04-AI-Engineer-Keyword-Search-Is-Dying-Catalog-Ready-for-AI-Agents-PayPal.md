# Keyword Search Is Dying. Is Your Catalog Ready for AI Agents? (PayPal)

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-10-04
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXb2Wx1r_Pk

## TL;DR
PayPal's director of product for agentic commerce frames shopping as moving from a "search era" (keywords, SEO) to an "intent era" (natural-language needs resolved by agents) and eventually a "delegation era" (agents transact on your behalf). The technical core is a small internal experiment: enriching merchant product data (filled attributes, deeper descriptions, buyer context, review-based trust signals, product identity) consistently improved agent recommendations over an unenriched baseline, with the biggest gains for thin catalogs. Over-enrichment and boilerplate text diluted the signal and increased hallucination. Directionally useful, but no numbers, no named models, and several market stats on stage are misattributed or inflated. Low signal for core research work; moderate as a retrieval data-quality lesson.

## Key Takeaways
- **Enrichment beats no enrichment, every time** in head-to-head agent recommendation tests. Thin or weak catalogs gain the most.
- **Quality over volume.** Structured, specific attributes (material: mesh, fit: true to size) help; marketing boilerplate ("family-owned since...") hurts. Over-enrichment caused more hallucination and worse rankings.
- **Keyword vs semantic:** keyword is precise and predictable but misses intent; semantic captures intent but drifts. The recommended answer is hybrid retrieval.
- **No single optimization target.** Agents use different models, rankers and non-deterministic vector retrieval, so "GEO" lacks the clear playbook SEO had. The prescription is to run experiments.
- **Catalog specs were built for Google and Meta ad feeds**, not for agent readability. Merchants need agent-oriented product data.
- **Market claims cited:** Bain 15 to 25% of commerce agent-executed by 2030, 39% of US shoppers already using AI for shopping, "693%" AI referral traffic, up to 4x higher conversion when shoppers start with an agent.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
This is a retrieval data-quality result, and it generalizes. Enrichment raises recall for semantic retrieval by placing the document embedding closer to intent-phrased queries (e.g. "comfortable for marathon training" now lands near a doc that contains "cushioned, breathable, running, training"). Over-enrichment hurts for a known reason: adding loosely related tokens pulls the embedding toward a centroid, reducing discriminability between products, and gives the downstream LLM more irrelevant context to confabulate from. Hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus dense vectors, fused with reciprocal rank fusion or a learned reranker) is the standard fix because keyword match anchors precision on SKUs, sizes and colors while dense retrieval handles paraphrase. The non-determinism point is real: approximate nearest neighbor indexes (HNSW, IVF) and model-dependent rerankers mean the same catalog can rank differently across agents, which is a routing-like problem where the merchant cannot see the router.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The macro numbers are partly misquoted. Bain's December 2025 forecast is $300B to $500B of US agentic commerce by 2030, 15 to 25% of online retail, not "all commerce," and Bain counts agent-influenced purchases; Morgan Stanley's stricter definition lands at a similar midpoint of roughly 15 to 17%. The "$1.1T US" figure on stage does not match Bain's range. The 693% figure is Adobe's year-over-year growth in AI-referred traffic to US retail sites during the 2025 holidays, not a merchant-reported metric, and the 39% of consumers using AI for shopping is also an Adobe survey stat. The "4x conversion" claim is unsupported: Adobe's own data shows AI traffic flipping from converting worse to converting about 42% better than other traffic by March 2026, with revenue per visit 37% higher, which is meaningful but far from 4x. Adobe Q1 2026 data showed AI traffic up 393% year over year, still from a small base.

On PayPal itself: Agentic Commerce Services launched October 2025 with Store Sync (pushing merchant catalogs, inventory and fulfillment into AI surfaces like Perplexity via partners Wix, Cymbio, BigCommerce/Feedonomics and Shopware) and Agent Ready (accepting agent-initiated payments, slated for early 2026). PayPal has since moved to acquire Cymbio, so this talk is effectively marketing for a catalog-syndication product. The enrichment finding aligns with Adobe's separate observation that many retail sites are not machine-readable enough for AI visibility.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- RAG and routing work: treat document enrichment as a tunable knob with a sweet spot. When building retrieval corpora or tool descriptions for routers, add structured, discriminative attributes and strip boilerplate; measure recall@k and downstream hallucination rate as enrichment grows, not just recall.
- Default to hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus dense plus reranker) for any agent-facing catalog, including tool catalogs for LLM routers.
- Investment angle (secondary, finance): agentic commerce is real but early and the stage numbers overstate it. If evaluating PYPL on this theme, anchor on Bain's 15 to 25% of online retail by 2030 and watch competition from OpenAI, Google and Stripe checkout protocols before treating it as a PayPal-specific moat.
