## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
- **Looped transformers are the most relevant item here for your compression work.** Weight-tied depth recurrence gives more effective depth at the same parameter count, so memory footprint stays flat while compute per token rises. Implications:
  - **Quantization:** Errors in looped layers *compound* across iterations, so per-layer sensitivity is multiplied by the loop count. Expect looped blocks to need higher precision than equivalent unrolled layers.
  - **Pruning:** Pruning a shared block removes capacity from every iteration at once, so structured pruning is riskier, but the parameter savings are amortized.
  - **Routing and adaptive compute:** Loop count is a natural per-query knob (early exit on easy queries). That directly addresses GLM-5.4's "thunderous thinking" problem: a router that predicts the needed loop depth is the latent-reasoning analog of picking a cheaper model.
  - **"Fewer output tokens" ≠ cheaper.** Latent compute moves cost from decode tokens into per-token FLOPs. For memory-bound decode, extra loop FLOPs can be nearly free (arithmetic intensity rises), which may make looped models *better* suited to batch-1 inference than CoT-heavy ones.
- **DeepMind's result is verifier-driven multi-agent search again** (compare the Anthropic FLT run and Bun's differential testing). Cost-aware verification (proving resource bounds, not just correctness) is directly transferable to kernel autotuning.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Prototype loop-aware quantization.** Take an open looped or recurrent-depth model (for example a Huginn-style recurrent-depth model, or tie the weights of a few middle layers in a small Qwen and briefly fine-tune), then quantize to INT4 with GPTQ and measure the error growth per loop iteration vs an unrolled baseline. If the error compounds super-linearly, a per-iteration precision schedule (FP8 for early loops, INT4 for later ones, or the reverse) is a novel, publishable compression angle. Pair it with a loop-depth router for adaptive compute.
