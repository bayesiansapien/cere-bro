# ChatGPT Work for Sales: Account Planning and Meeting Prep

**Channel:** OpenAI
**Published:** 2026-07-28
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hjXi7b01s2c

## TL;DR
The problem framed for a CRO is consistent execution across a sales organization, especially when new sales plays are introduced. OpenAI's answer is a personal sales assistant meant to make every seller act like a top seller. The demo: a seller asks it to "plan my day," and because it draws on Slack, Google Calendar, Salesforce, and other business context, it identifies highest-priority actions and recognizes that a Catalyst workshop (a new sales play) is scheduled for tomorrow. It has already created a command centre site with opportunity context, stakeholder mapping, and workshop objectives, generated a PowerPoint with customer-specific mobile mockups, produced a narrated video with animation as a pre-workshop teaser, and reflected all assets back into the site. The seller then posts the package to the customer Slack channel so the whole account team is prepared.

## Key Takeaways
- **The stated business problem is play adoption, not productivity.** New sales plays fail because most sellers cannot execute them without support. The claim is that the assistant embeds execution into the operating rhythm of the business, which is a distribution-of-competence argument rather than a time-saving argument.
- **The specific gap it targets is real and well identified.** "Most sellers don't have a dedicated team of designers and creatives available on demand." A workshop-based play requires customer-specific artefacts (decks, mockups, a narrated preview video), and generating those is exactly what blocks adoption of high-effort plays.
- **Multi-modal output is the differentiator in this demo versus its companions.** PowerPoint with customer-specific mobile mockups, plus a narrated video with animation. Not a document, not a summary, an asset bundle across formats.
- **The work was done before it was asked for.** The assistant "has automatically created" the command centre site and "has generated" the assets. The seller's "plan my day" request surfaces work already completed by an ambient process, which is the same event-driven pattern as the companion account-research demo rather than an interactive one.
- **The command centre site as a persistent artefact is the underrated structural choice.** Rather than returning outputs into a chat transcript, the assistant maintains a site that is updated as assets are produced. That gives the work a stable location the account team can reference, which is a different information architecture from conversational output.
- **The final step is human-initiated distribution.** The seller packages and posts to Slack. The agent prepares, the human ships.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The mechanically distinctive feature of this demo compared to its three companions is that it is **generation-heavy across modalities**, and that has a specific cost structure worth understanding.

A narrated video with animation plus a PowerPoint with rendered mockups is not a text-generation workload. It is a pipeline of text generation, image generation, layout, speech synthesis, and video composition, each with different hardware profiles and very different cost per unit output. Text decode is memory-bandwidth-bound on small batches. Image and video generation are compute-bound and dominated by diffusion or transformer sampling steps. Speech synthesis is cheap but latency-sensitive if interactive and trivially batchable if not. The practical consequence is that **the cost of a workshop asset bundle is dominated by the video, not by the reasoning**, which inverts where most people would look for optimization in an "AI sales assistant."

The ambient-precomputation pattern is the second mechanic and it is the more transferable one. Because the assistant generated everything before being asked, this is speculative work: the system spent compute on the possibility that the seller would need workshop assets. That is a cache-versus-recompute tradeoff at the workflow level. Speculative generation trades wasted compute for zero perceived latency, and it only pays when the hit rate is high enough. For expensive multi-modal generation the break-even hit rate is much higher than it is for a cheap text summary, which means the correct design almost certainly precomputes the brief and the stakeholder map ambiently while generating the video only on confirmation. The demo does not distinguish these, and a naive implementation that speculatively renders video for every calendar event would be extremely expensive.

The connector fan-in (Slack, Calendar, Salesforce) is the context-assembly cost, and it is a prefill problem rather than a decode problem. Assembling day-planning context across three or four systems produces a large input with a small output, which is the opposite ratio to most chat workloads. That configuration is where prefill optimization, prompt caching, and KV-cache reuse across the day's repeated queries pay disproportionately, because the same account and calendar context is re-read many times.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
**ChatGPT Work is a shipped product and this is a first-party demonstration of internal use.** OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work in July 2026 alongside the global rollout of the GPT-5.6 family. It is an agentic mode powered by GPT-5.6 that turns a single goal into finished deliverables including documents, spreadsheets, presentations, and dashboards, by gathering context across connected apps and files, and it decomposes larger goals into subtasks worked over extended timeframes. The multi-format asset generation shown here is therefore a documented capability rather than a bespoke demo.

**The connector integration is supported by a separate launch.** OpenAI's Workspace Agents are described as shared, always-on Codex agents plugging into Slack, Salesforce, and 60-plus enterprise applications, which is the infrastructure underneath the Slack and Salesforce context in this demo.

**The approval behaviour matches platform design.** ChatGPT Work asks a human for approval before carrying out sensitive actions, and enterprises control its access to browser use, plugins, and connected tools through ChatGPT Enterprise controls. The seller-posts-to-Slack step is consistent with that boundary.

**What is conspicuously absent, and matters most for judging the claim.** The video makes a specific, testable business claim (new sales plays get adopted because the assistant removes the asset-creation barrier) and offers no evidence for it. No adoption rate before and after, no measure of whether AI-generated workshop assets perform comparably to human-made ones, and no cost per asset bundle. It is also worth noting the honest tension in the pitch: the value proposition is customer-specific creative work at scale, and the failure mode of generated creative work at scale is that it becomes recognizably generic, which is precisely what would kill a workshop-based play. The demo cannot address this and no first-party demo will.

**The comparison worth drawing.** Nubank's simulation talk from the following day establishes what a credible enterprise agent-quality claim looks like: TNPS trajectories across five production agents, sim-to-real correlation, and 80% domain-expert label agreement. Against that bar, this demo is a capability showcase, not evidence.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Separate ambient precomputation from on-demand generation by cost tier.** This is the concrete design lesson. Precompute the cheap, high-hit-rate artefacts (briefs, stakeholder maps, context assembly) ambiently. Generate expensive multi-modal artefacts (narrated video, rendered mockups) only on confirmation. The demo blurs these, and a literal implementation that speculatively renders video per calendar event has a cost profile that will not survive contact with a finance team. The general rule: speculative generation's break-even hit rate scales with the cost of the artefact.
2. **Recognize context-assembly workloads as prefill-dominated and optimize accordingly.** "Plan my day" across Slack, Calendar, and Salesforce is a large-input, small-output request repeated many times against largely unchanged context. That is the ideal case for prompt caching and KV-cache reuse, and it is a completely different optimization target from the decode-bound chat workload most inference tuning assumes. Identifying which requests in a product have this shape is a cheap, high-return audit.
3. **Look for the cost in the video pipeline, not the reasoning.** For any multi-modal generation workflow, profile before optimizing. The intuition that the LLM is the expensive part is wrong when the output includes animated video, and effort spent on text-side quantization while the diffusion steps dominate is misallocated.
4. **Demand adoption and quality metrics before believing the play-enablement claim.** The claim is that generated assets unblock a high-effort sales play. The testable version is whether plays supported by generated assets convert comparably to plays supported by human-made ones. Without that, the assistant may be increasing asset volume while decreasing asset effectiveness, and volume is the metric a demo can show.
5. **Steal the persistent-site pattern over conversational output for multi-artefact work.** A stable location that accumulates and updates artefacts is a better information architecture than a chat transcript for anything a team needs to reference. This applies well beyond sales: any agent producing a bundle of related outputs over time benefits from a canonical surface rather than a scrollback.
