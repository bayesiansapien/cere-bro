# Data Quality Is the Compute Multiplier (Ari Morcos, DatologyAI)

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-07-31
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PdK6x7PQNM

## TL;DR
Morcos argues that in a compute-scarce 2026, data curation is the highest-leverage optimization lever available, because better data does not just shift the loss curve down, it changes the exponent of the scaling law itself. DatologyAI's pipeline (clean, curate, create, compose) delivered a 14 absolute point accuracy gain on VLMs holding everything else constant, matched Qwen 3.5 4B quality with 145x less training compute, and let Arcee train a frontier-competitive 17T-token open model for under $20M all-in. His closing frame: data quality is the single most underleveraged compute multiplier, and it is a frontier research problem, not a data-janitor problem.

## Key Takeaways
- **Compute scarcity is real and worsening.** H100 rental prices reversed a multi-year decline and are roughly 40% off their late-2025 lows. Reasoning models burn 8x the tokens of non-reasoning models, projected to 5x again within a year. Morcos claims Google capped Meta's Gemini usage on inference constraints, and that OpenAI selling forward token capacity is a hedge against genuinely running out of frontier inference.
- **Curation bends the scaling law.** His NeurIPS best-paper "Beyond Neural Scaling Laws" showed data selection changes the power-law exponent, not just the constant. Everything DatologyAI does descends from that result.
- **The four Cs.** Clean (heuristic filters, aggressive n-gram benchmark decontamination), curate (quality classifiers, topic taxonomy balancing, semantic redundancy reduction, up/downsampling, task distribution matching), create (synthetic rephrasing), compose (mixing and sequencing across at least three training phases, plus continuous curricula).
- **VLM result.** Curating the 25B-token Mammoth fusion-adapter dataset pushed a model well past the Pareto frontier set by Qwen 3 / 3.5 / InternVL, 14 absolute points over the uncurated input, with no post-training. Same models are also markedly more concise: roughly 35x fewer FLOPs per correct answer than Qwen 3.5.
- **Multilingual result.** With only 8% multilingual tokens (max ~6B tokens per language), curated models beat Qwen 3 multilingual MMLU at ~8x less compute. Curating only the English data measurably improves non-English accuracy, and the transfer magnitude correlates with linguistic similarity to English. The effect runs in reverse too, weaker.
- **Small-run extrapolation works.** Two 1T-token dense Llama-style models drew a line that passed almost exactly through Arcee's 17T-token hypersparse Trinity Large, trained with 50x more compute. You can derisk a hero run at 1/50th the cost if you simulate token scarcity correctly.
- **Rephrasing beats generic synthetic data.** Transform a source document into one of hundreds of templates (true/false sets, Q&A, etc). No model collapse, because all information originates in the source. The student can exceed the rephrasing model, because reformatting is a strictly easier task than teaching. Critically, rephrasing randomly chosen documents fails. Only rephrasing high-quality documents pays.
- **Mid-training makes post-training 2-3x more effective.** Thomson Reuters: 100B tokens of mid-training (under 1% of the pre-training budget) lifted LegalBench ~5 points with zero catastrophic forgetting, because the majority of the mix was still pre-training-distribution data. The bigger result: applying their unchanged post-training harness to the mid-trained checkpoint nearly tripled the post-training delta.
- **Cost floor is far lower than the industry narrative.** Arcee had never trained a model before mid-2025. Under $20M total (salaries, compute, failed runs, several models) produced something competitive with GLM5 and Kimi.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The load-bearing idea is maximizing marginal information gain per data point, which is the training-time analogue of what Amit already does at inference time. Redundancy reduction is semantic, not exact-match dedup: two documents that are not the same tokens but convey the same information are treated as duplicates. That is functionally a pruning criterion applied to the data manifold instead of the weight tensor, and the same intuition (remove what contributes no new signal, then retrain) transfers directly.

Two mechanics deserve attention. First, the conciseness result: curation reduced mean tokens per response without a length penalty in the objective, which means verbosity is partly a data artifact, not an inherent property of reasoning models. If response length is a data-side variable, then inference cost is addressable during pre-training, not just via decode-time tricks. Second, the mid-training-amplifies-post-training result implies the RL starting policy quality is a multiplier on RL efficiency. A better-positioned initial policy produces better rollouts, so the same GRPO/PPO budget extracts more. That is an argument against treating pre-train, mid-train, and post-train as separately owned pipeline stages.

The catastrophic-forgetting avoidance recipe is worth stealing verbatim: when domain-adapting, keep the majority of the mix representative of the original pre-training distribution and inject only a minority of domain data. This is a data-level replay buffer and it is cheaper than any regularization-based continual learning method.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The compute-scarcity framing holds up precisely. SemiAnalysis's H100 one-year rental index shows pricing rising from a $1.70/hr/GPU low in October 2025 to $2.35/hr/GPU by March 2026, almost exactly the 40% Morcos cites, driven by Nvidia shifting TSMC N3/N4 wafer allocation from H100 lines toward Blackwell while on-demand capacity sells out across tiers. Nvidia separately announced a roughly 20% H100 rental increase for 2026. The one claim I could not independently corroborate is the specific Google-capping-Meta anecdote and the OpenAI token-futures product. Treat those as directional color rather than confirmed fact.

The Arcee results check out and are stronger than the talk implies. The Trinity Large technical report (arXiv 2602.17004) describes a 400B-total / 13B-active sparse MoE trained on 20T tokens, split roughly evenly between DatologyAI synthetic data and curated web data, with the curated portion organized as a 10T-token three-phase curriculum (7T general, 1.8T high-quality text, 1.2T STEM/math/code). DatologyAI generated over 8T tokens of synthetic web, code, and STEM data, about 6.5T of it via BeyondWeb rephrasing. Arcee released Trinity under Apache 2.0. There is also a companion paper on multilingual curation for a 20T-token dataset (arXiv 2602.15210), which is the fuller version of the cross-lingual transfer result shown here. Note the talk says 17T tokens while the technical report says 20T. The report is the citable number.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Run the small-model extrapolation trick before your next compression sweep.** Morcos derisked a 50x-larger run from two small ones. The same protocol applies to pruning and quantization: fit the recovery curve at two small scales under simulated token scarcity, then predict whether your target sparsity survives at production scale. This kills expensive dead-end sweeps early.
2. **Treat conciseness as a data-side knob in routing work.** The 35x FLOPs-per-correct-answer gap came from curation, not decoding. For an LLM router, tokens-per-correct-answer is the real cost metric, not tokens-per-second. Add mean-response-length-conditional-on-correctness to your router's cost model and re-derive the routing thresholds. Chatty-but-correct small models may currently be routing as cheap when they are not.
3. **Apply the replay-mix ratio to distillation.** When distilling a specialist, keep the majority of the distillation set drawn from the general pre-training distribution and only a minority domain-specific. This is the cheapest known fix for the specialist-loses-general-ability failure mode, and it costs nothing but mix ratios.
4. **Audit whether your post-training experiments are confounded by starting-policy quality.** If mid-training triples the post-training delta, then any A/B of RL recipes on differently-initialized checkpoints is measuring initialization, not the recipe. Fix the checkpoint before comparing algorithms.
5. **Semantic redundancy reduction on your own eval sets.** Before the next routing benchmark, dedup semantically rather than exactly. Redundant near-duplicate queries inflate confidence in whichever model happens to handle that cluster.
