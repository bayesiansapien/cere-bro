# Scaling to Long Horizons (Ross Taylor and Chengxi Taylor, General Reasoning)

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-07-31
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bvtay8wGYI

## TL;DR
Two halves. Ross Taylor (ex-Meta reasoning lead, Galactica and Llama 2/3 post-training) tells the Galactica story as a natural experiment proving a state-of-the-art base model is worthless without RL, then reveals that his team ran essentially the o1/R1 recipe in 2023 (continued pre-training on math/science, then PPO with verifiable rewards and a value model initialized from an outcome reward model) and got SOTA math results but never saw the reflective inference-time-scaling behavior. His diagnosis two years later is the purest form of the bitter lesson: better base models, more RL compute, longer context. Chengxi Taylor then argues long-horizon RL breaks GRPO's assumptions and requires reintroducing the critic, and presents KellyBench, where every frontier model lost money running a Premier League betting strategy over a full season.

## Key Takeaways
- **Galactica vs ChatGPT as a controlled experiment.** Shipped two weeks apart, both on good base models. Galactica beat PaLM, Chinchilla, and GPT-3.5 in scientific domains (68% vs 49% on math, 36% vs 19% chain-of-thought at 30B vs PaLM's 540B) and still failed as a product because it lacked RLHF. InstructGPT made the same point louder: a 1B RLHF'd model beating 175B, two orders of magnitude fewer parameters.
- **Galactica's underappreciated contributions.** First LM to seriously demonstrate data efficiency (105B curated tokens vs Chinchilla's trillions, when consensus was "more tokens"). First major empirical result for multi-epoch training, later formalized as the four-epochs-of-repeated-data rule. First framing of thinking tokens as an internal working-memory process inside explicit tags, distinct from chain-of-thought prompting and scratchpads.
- **Meta ran the o1 recipe two years early and it did not emerge.** Continued pre-training toward math/science, then PPO with verifiable rewards (not GRPO) with a strong outcome reward model initializing the value model. SOTA internally on math. No backtracking, no reflection, no inference-time scaling. Cause: Llama 2 base quality and a 4K context window.
- **The sociology point.** OpenAI having a GPT-4-level model before anyone else let them see further. Capability prerequisites determine which ideas are visible to you at all.
- **Context is the binding constraint on long horizons.** Wiles on Fermat took ten years of reading, scratchpads, and walks, plausibly tens to hundreds of billions of tokens. Current frontier is 1M. Compaction (generate to the limit, summarize, continue) is the workaround, and RL can optimize the compaction policy and the task jointly.
- **Three optimization problems specific to long horizons.** Gradient variance scales with trajectory length, rewards are sparse so credit assignment is hard, and variable trajectory lengths destabilize the objective.
- **The critic comes back.** A value model reduces variance, operates at trajectory level (which composes well with compaction), encourages batch diversity, and enables bootstrapping (a signal before episode end, described as the dopamine analogue). The cost is training a second model and reintroducing value bias, which is exactly what GRPO was designed to avoid.
- **Context tools.** File-system tools as external scratchpad, self-search over prior trajectory, archive tools to build on prior results. Explicit warning that archive tools invite cheating by retrieving a previous answer instead of reasoning.
- **KellyBench.** Agents build ML models to bet a Premier League season with $100K. Every frontier model lost money.
- **Pipeline RL and its long-horizon breaking point.** Start training while sequences are still generating, trading off-policyness for GPU utilization. Their empirical tolerance is roughly 8 steps of staleness. When a single inference takes weeks, you exceed that and GPUs idle. Bootstrapping via the value model is the escape, at the cost of bias.
- **Their diagnosis of why models are bad at long horizons.** The field is overfit to coding and procedural tasks where the solution space is one or two paths. Not enough open-ended tasks. Not enough simulation of a world containing other agents with different goals.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The GRPO-to-critic pendulum is the technically important claim and it is well argued. GRPO's win was eliminating the value model by using within-group relative advantage, which works when you can cheaply sample K rollouts per prompt and trajectories are comparable in length. Long horizons break both conditions: K rollouts of a weeks-long episode is unaffordable, and variable-length trajectories make group normalization ill-conditioned. Bootstrapping from a learned value function is the only mechanism that yields a training signal before an episode terminates, so if episodes are long enough, the critic is not optional. The bias-vs-idle-GPU tradeoff Chengxi describes is the honest framing: value bias is the price of not stalling the cluster.

The 8-step staleness tolerance is a useful concrete number and matches the broader async-RL literature, where decoupling rollout from training yields 2-3x throughput but stale trajectories cause off-policy instability, and capping policy lag trades speedup for stability.

Ross's causal claim about why reflection did not emerge in 2023 (base model quality, RL compute, context length) is plausible but underdetermined by the evidence he presents. The PPO-with-value-model-init versus GRPO difference is a confound he waves past, and 4K context is arguably sufficient to exhibit short backtracking if the capability were latent. The honest version is that he has three candidate causes and no ablation. Still, the anecdote is valuable precisely because it is a negative result from someone with the receipts.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
KellyBench is real, published (arXiv 2604.27865, "KellyBench: A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Sequential Decision Making"), and the results are more specific than the talk gives. Eight frontier models including Claude, Grok, Gemini, and GPT-5.4 were placed in a simulated market for the 2023-24 Premier League season with historical data, advanced stats, lineups, and public odds, tasked with building ML models, finding edge, sizing bets, and managing risk across the season. All eight lost money. Claude Opus 4.6 performed least badly at an average 11% loss. Grok 4.20 went bankrupt on all three attempts. The paper's framing is a "knowledge-action gap": every model could recite the Kelly criterion, none could apply it. That is a sharper and more damning result than "models are bad at long horizons," and it is the detail worth carrying forward. It got mainstream coverage (Financial Times, TechSpot, Benzinga) in April 2026.

The pipeline RL discussion aligns with current systems research. Synchronous RL for long-horizon agents wastes an estimated 60-80% of potential GPU throughput because the batch stalls on the longest trajectory. PipelineRL's specific contribution is in-flight weight updates, so inference workers receive fresh weights without pausing generation, keeping data near on-policy while maintaining utilization. Related 2026 work includes WAR (workload-aware rollouts for synchronous agentic RL, arXiv 2607.17299) and JigsawRL (arXiv 2604.23838). The 8-step tolerance Chengxi cites is an empirical finding from their setup, not a published constant, so treat it as a starting point rather than a rule.

Sources: [KellyBench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27865v1), [Introducing KellyBench](https://www.gr.inc/releases/introducing-kellybench), [TechSpot coverage](https://www.techspot.com/news/112043-new-study-shows-even-best-ai-models-lost.html), [WAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17299), [JigsawRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23838v1)

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Add a knowledge-action gap probe to router evaluation.** KellyBench's central finding is that reciting a method and applying it are separable capabilities. Standard benchmarks measure the first. A router calibrated on knowledge-style benchmarks will overestimate a cheap model's competence on execution-style tasks. Build a small paired eval: for each of five methods, one question that asks the model to state it and one that requires applying it to data. Measure the gap per model. If the gap is model-dependent (likely), it belongs in the routing features.
2. **Reconsider GRPO-by-default for any long-horizon internal RL.** If trajectories are long or variable-length, budget for a value model up front. The Meta 2023 recipe (outcome reward model initializing the value model) is a concrete, unpublished-but-now-public trick worth reusing, and it sidesteps the cold-start problem that makes critics painful.
3. **Measure GPU idle fraction before optimizing anything else in an RL loop.** If synchronous long-horizon RL wastes 60-80% of throughput on straggler waits, that is a larger multiplier than most kernel-level work. Instrument the stall time attributable to the longest trajectory in each batch, then decide between pipeline RL, workload-aware batching, or bootstrapping.
4. **Note the multi-epoch and data-efficiency lineage for compression work.** Galactica's 105B-token result is the historical ancestor of the Datology thesis in the adjacent talk. Both say the same thing: token count is a poor proxy for information. For distillation set construction, this is now two independent confirmations from different decades of the field.
5. **Take the "capability prerequisites determine visible ideas" point seriously for tooling.** If having the better model earlier let OpenAI see further, then the internal equivalent is ensuring research work runs against the strongest available checkpoint, not the convenient one. Cheap-model-only experimentation systematically hides the ideas that only appear at higher capability.
