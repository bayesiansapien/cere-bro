# SimulationMaxxing: How Nubank ships agents 20x faster with simulations

**Channel:** AI Engineer
**Published:** 2026-07-29
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KMR_RBoCa4M
**Speakers:** Shreya Rajpal (CEO, Snowglobe), Aman Gupta (Principal ML Engineer, Nubank)

## TL;DR
One claim with production evidence behind it: generate your eval data in simulation instead of waiting on production data and you ship agents roughly 20x faster. The argument is that evals decompose into metrics and data, that metrics are a solved playbook (LLM-as-judge aligned to human labels, then auto-optimized), and that **data is the unsolved bottleneck** because a single agent data point is now a multi-turn trajectory with internal tool calls and consistent state, which is expensive to author by hand and dangerous to harvest from production. Nubank (135 million customers across Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, quarterly revenue past $5 billion in Q1 2026) reports transaction NPS on five production support agents rising from poor to approaching and in several cases exceeding human quality, one agent's TNPS doubling, self-service rate up 4% on one agent, and open-source model bake-offs that previously took multiple weeks now running in hours. The release cycle compresses from weeks to under a day.

## Key Takeaways
- **Evals are only two things: metrics and data.** Metrics have a playbook: LLM-as-judge classifiers aligned against human judgment, then iteratively improved with automated prompt optimization. Data does not.
- **The data cost exploded specifically with agents, and the reason is structural.** Classical ML data was structured rows. Early chatbot and single-turn QA data was still tractable rows. A multi-turn agent data point is a trajectory containing internal tool calls that all need consistent state. Even one data point is expensive to generate and annotate, and scaling to a sample set is much harder.
- **Both existing data sources are broken, for opposite reasons.** Manual authoring requires hand-planning state updates and trajectories, keeping synthetic state consistent across every tool call. Production traces are almost free but the cost is that **you are testing on real live users every time**, which makes running many parallel experiments (the normal ML workflow) effectively impossible in prod.
- **The timeline they are compressing, stated concretely.** Changing an agent harness, prompt, or tools takes a few hours at best. Offline evals on hand-curated data take days. An A/B test in production monitoring for regressions takes forever, because customer feedback arrives through sparse noisy feedback forms. Simulation leaves the harness change at a few hours and collapses the other two.
- **What a simulation mechanically is.** Point the Snowglobe SDK at your agent with no code changes, identify which tools to mock, then supply the steering inputs: personas, use cases, and the data points to simulate. Output is thousands of multi-turn conversations against the real agent with tools mocked, plus per-turn judge scores, piping into the existing eval pipeline.
- **The grounding-consistency detail is the technically hard part.** Their example persona (Maria Souza, 34, designer, first-time credit card customer) comes with generated grounding data: a fake address, a fake credit card, account data, all of which must stay consistent across the whole agent execution so the agent's tool calls return coherent results. Persona also encodes tone and interaction style, and the resulting conversation reflects it (curt one-line messages because that is how the persona was specified).
- **Sim-to-real validation is the load-bearing measurement, and they did it.** Correlation between eval scores computed on simulated data and on real production data is high, and **80% of domain expert human labels confirmed simulations give usable data**, for greenfield agents as well as mature ones. Without this number the whole approach is unfalsifiable.
- **The de-risking result is separate from the improvement result.** Simulation caught a regression that would have reached production, and caught an issue in another agent that would have lowered self-service rate. These are avoided losses, not gains, and they are the easier ROI case to make internally.
- **The A/B test funnel effect is the actual mechanism behind 20x.** If you would run 10 A/B tests a quarter, you now run the candidates through simulation and launch one, short-circuiting the first five or six. The speedup is not that any single step got faster, it is that most steps get eliminated before they touch users.
- **The TNPS versus self-service rate tradeoff did not materialize.** Customer service orthodoxy says raising satisfaction costs you containment. Nubank reports improving both, with SSR up 4% in one case, which they attribute to principled iteration rather than throwing things at the wall.
- **The open-source model bake-off is the highest-leverage application.** With simulation they can throw a batch of open-source models at their agent harness and eval setup and see which versions work. Saved multiple weeks, with live A/B tests running at the time of the talk to determine whether open-source models match frontier models.
- **The stated three takeaways.** Generate eval data in simulation rather than solely from production. Close the sim-to-real gap with offline, online, and human-review metrics so you can trust the results. And in an enterprise setting, all the self-improving-agent and RSI excitement reduces to two things, data and metrics: aligned metrics that catch the signals you care about, plus a reliable way of generating data those metrics can score, and the self-improvement loop assembles itself.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
The mechanically important observation is that **this and the AlphaLab talk are the same argument arriving from opposite ends**. AlphaLab concluded that all enterprise value lives in environments and evals, and that the harness is disposable. Snowglobe and Nubank say that in an enterprise setting the entire self-improving-agent story reduces to data plus metrics. Same conclusion, and Snowglobe is supplying the missing half: AlphaLab tells you the environment is the asset, Snowglobe tells you how to populate it when the task is not a Kaggle-shaped prediction problem but an interactive multi-turn system.

For optimization work, three mechanics transfer.

**First, simulation as the substrate for model substitution decisions.** Their open-source bake-off is exactly the LLM routing problem in disguise. The question "which of these models is adequate for this workload" is unanswerable without a data-generation mechanism, because you cannot afford to A/B test six candidates on real traffic. A simulation harness converts a routing decision from a production experiment into an offline sweep. That is the single most directly applicable idea here: **a routing policy is only as good as the offline evaluation that lets you fit it**, and simulated trajectories are how you get enough labelled interaction data to fit a policy rather than hand-setting a confidence threshold.

**Second, mocked tools as a variance-reduction device.** Mocking tool responses with consistent synthetic grounding data removes external-service variance from the measurement. For quality-versus-cost comparisons across models this is not just convenient, it is necessary, because otherwise upstream API variance swamps the model-quality delta you are trying to measure. The same principle applies when benchmarking a quantized model against its full-precision parent inside an agent loop: without deterministic tool responses, the comparison is noise-dominated.

**Third, the persona-as-steering-input structure is a coverage mechanism.** Personas with specified tone, interaction style, and grounding state are a way of deliberately sampling the input distribution rather than hoping production traffic covers it. In compression evaluation the analogous gap is that calibration and evaluation sets rarely cover the tail behaviours that break after quantization: terse inputs, code-switched inputs, adversarial phrasing. Persona-driven generation is a systematic way to construct that tail rather than discovering it in production.

The caution worth carrying: their own honest framing is that closing the sim-to-real gap is a prerequisite, not a detail. An 80% human-label confirmation rate is good and it is also a 20% disagreement rate. A quantization or pruning decision made purely on simulated interaction data inherits that gap, so the sim result should gate which candidates reach a real A/B test, exactly as Nubank uses it, rather than replacing the test.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The claims are verifiable and the provenance is worth knowing, because it changes how to read the talk.

**Snowglobe is a Guardrails AI product, which is not mentioned.** Shreya Rajpal is presented as CEO of Snowglobe, and Snowglobe was introduced by Guardrails AI in August 2025 as a simulation engine for testing AI agents and chatbots at scale before production. Its published description matches the talk closely: it constructs nuanced user personas for diversity, creates realistic multi-turn dialogues, and automatically labels every generated scenario. Rajpal is the founder of Guardrails AI, so the lineage is guardrails-and-validation infrastructure repositioned as simulation infrastructure, which is a coherent evolution but worth knowing when weighing the vendor's framing of the bottleneck.

**The KDD 2026 paper exists and the venue is right.** Nubank's AI agent framework has been accepted to KDD 2026, held 9 to 13 August 2026 in Jeju, Korea. Notably, there is a **KDD Workshop on Evaluation and Trustworthiness of Agentic AI** at that conference, focused on evaluation methodology across the full deployment lifecycle, which is the natural home for this work and suggests the topic has reached a critical mass of academic attention rather than being a single vendor's framing.

**The Nubank scale figures are consistent with public reporting.** 135 million customers across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia with a US launch pending, and quarterly revenue crossing $5 billion in Q1 2026.

**One thing to weigh.** The 20x figure is a release-cycle compression claim, not a quality claim, and the two are presented adjacently in a way that invites conflation. The quality result (TNPS rising to and past human parity on five agents) is attributed to several quarters of work involving robust evals, automated prompt optimization, and simulation together, not to simulation alone. Aman is careful about this in the talk. Summaries of the talk will not be. The defensible claim is that simulation removed the data bottleneck, which allowed far more experiments per quarter, which is what produced the quality gains. Simulation is the enabling condition, not the cause.

**The related literature is moving fast in the same direction.** Adjacent 2026 work includes evaluation of LLM simulators as differentially private data generators and persona-grounded transaction simulators in fintech, which suggests the simulated-data-for-evaluation pattern is generalizing beyond conversational agents into structured domains.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Use simulation as the pre-filter for model substitution decisions, which is the direct transfer to routing and compression work.** Their open-source bake-off is the template: fix the harness and the eval, sweep candidate models offline, and only A/B test the survivor. Applied to routing, this means building a simulated trajectory set once and using it to fit the routing policy, instead of hand-tuning a confidence threshold against production feedback that arrives sparse and noisy.
2. **Mock the tools before comparing models.** For any quality-versus-cost comparison inside an agent loop, deterministic mocked tool responses are a prerequisite, not a nicety. Without them, external service variance dominates the model-quality delta and the comparison is uninterpretable. This is the cheapest measurement improvement available and it applies immediately to any quantized-versus-full-precision agent comparison.
3. **Measure your own sim-to-real correlation before trusting any simulation result, and hold their number as the bar.** Nubank validated with offline metrics, online metrics, and human review, reporting high eval correlation and 80% domain-expert label agreement. Any simulation setup without that validation step produces unfalsifiable results. The specific practice: sample simulated trajectories and matched production trajectories, score both with the same judge, and check correlation before acting on the simulated scores.
4. **Build personas to cover the tail your calibration set misses.** The persona mechanism is a systematic way to sample terse inputs, code-switched inputs, and unusual interaction styles, which are exactly the behaviours that degrade first after aggressive quantization and are exactly what a held-in calibration corpus does not contain. Construct the tail deliberately rather than discovering it after deployment.
5. **Treat the 20x as an experiment-throughput number and set the internal expectation accordingly.** The mechanism is eliminating five or six A/B tests per quarter, not making any single step faster. Framing it internally as a quality claim will not survive scrutiny. Framing it as "we can now afford ten times as many experiments" is both defensible and the actual reason the quality improved.
6. **Note the convergence with AlphaLab and act on it once, not twice.** Two independent enterprise teams, one in quant research and one in customer support, concluded in the same conference that the durable asset is the environment plus the metric and that the harness is replaceable. If both are right, the correct investment is a reusable environment-and-metric substrate for compression and routing work, and simulation is how you populate it for anything interactive.
