# Introducing gpt-transcribe and gpt-live-transcribe

**Channel:** OpenAI
**Published:** 2026-07-28
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeP9VUf1OoE

## TL;DR
Two transcription models split by workload shape rather than by capability tier. **gpt-transcribe** takes a completed file and returns a full transcript, optimized for accuracy and throughput on call archives, podcasts, and batch jobs. **gpt-live-transcribe** holds an open connection and returns text as audio arrives, for captions, dictation, and voice interfaces where latency affects the experience. Both support 57 languages, and the improvements target the specific places transcription historically breaks: accents, multilingual speech, very short answers, names, and numbers. Developers can supply a vocabulary list of proper nouns or domain terms (the demo uses phishing, ARR, and A1C from cybersecurity, sales, and healthcare). Both handle background noise better, so side conversations and ambient music are less likely to be transcribed as speech. Language hints are optional because multilingual switching is followed automatically, demonstrated live by switching English to Spanish and back within one session. A half-hour recording processes in a little under a minute.

## Key Takeaways
- **The split is by latency requirement, not by quality tier, and that is the correct product decomposition.** A batch model optimized for throughput and a streaming model optimized for time-to-first-token are genuinely different serving problems. Most providers ship one model and let you choose how to call it, which leaves both cases suboptimal.
- **The failure modes they targeted are the ones that actually matter in production.** Accents, multilingual speech, very short answers, names, and numbers. Notice what these have in common: they are all low-redundancy, high-consequence tokens. A misheard filler word costs nothing. A misheard drug dose, account number, or person's name breaks the downstream use entirely.
- **The vocabulary hint mechanism is the most immediately useful developer feature.** Supplying proper nouns and domain terms addresses the single largest source of unusable transcripts in technical domains. Anyone transcribing ML research discussion has a long list of terms (GPTQ, AWQ, vLLM, PagedAttention, GQA) that a general model will mangle.
- **Noise robustness is framed as suppression, not just accuracy.** Ambient music and side conversations are less likely to appear as speech at all. That is a different property from getting them right, and it is the more useful one, because spurious transcribed speech from a neighbouring conversation corrupts downstream analysis in ways that a slightly wrong word does not.
- **Automatic multilingual switching within a session, without language hints, demonstrated live.** English to Spanish and back with the transcript following in both directions. For Indian usage this is the headline feature, because code-switching between English and Hindi mid-sentence is the normal register rather than an edge case.
- **The throughput figure is impressive and understated in the video.** A 30-minute file in under a minute.

## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
This release is a clean illustration of a principle that generalizes well beyond speech, and it is worth extracting explicitly: **the same underlying capability requires two different serving configurations depending on whether a human is waiting.**

The batch model can accumulate the entire audio input before producing output, which means it can use bidirectional context over the whole utterance, run large batch sizes, and optimize purely for tokens per dollar. The streaming model must emit tokens with bounded delay from the audio arriving, which forbids full bidirectional context, forces small effective batch sizes, and makes time-to-first-token the governing metric. These are not the same model with a flag. They are different architectural commitments, and the pricing reflects it: roughly a 3.8x price difference between the two.

That ratio is the useful number. It quantifies what latency costs, and it is a reasonable prior for other modalities. When designing a system, the question "does a human need this within a second" has roughly a 4x cost consequence in this domain, which is a strong argument for pushing work into the async path wherever the product allows.

The second mechanic worth noting is that **the vocabulary hint is a cheap conditioning mechanism that substitutes for fine-tuning.** Supplying a term list biases decoding toward domain vocabulary without touching weights. For anyone who would otherwise consider fine-tuning a speech model on domain audio, this is the cheaper first move by a large margin, and it should be exhausted before any training is contemplated. The general pattern (constrain the output distribution with cheap conditioning before reaching for parameter updates) applies broadly.

The third is that the accuracy improvements on names and numbers are the improvements that make **downstream automation** viable rather than just making transcripts nicer to read. A transcript is rarely the product. It is an input to extraction, search, or analysis, and errors on proper nouns and numerals are exactly the errors that propagate into wrong extracted fields. Improvement on high-redundancy words barely changes downstream accuracy because context recovers them. Improvement on low-redundancy tokens changes it a lot.

## Grounded Context (Web Enrichment)
The video omits the two numbers that determine whether to adopt this, and both are favourable.

**Pricing.** gpt-transcribe is priced at **$0.0045 per minute of audio**, a 25% reduction on its predecessor. gpt-live-transcribe is **$0.017 per minute**, matching gpt-realtime-whisper. The batch-versus-streaming ratio is therefore roughly 3.8x, which is the concrete cost of latency in this domain.

**Accuracy, with an important caveat.** gpt-transcribe cuts word error rate from **40.37% to 19.27% versus whisper-1** on Common Voice across 22 languages, roughly a 52% relative error reduction. That is a large improvement and it is why this is described as replacing Whisper rather than supplementing it. The caveat matters: independent benchmarking reports that gpt-transcribe **improves on its predecessor but does not catch ElevenLabs, Google, or Mistral on error rates**. So this is a substantial improvement within the OpenAI family and not a category-leading model. Anyone selecting a transcription provider on accuracy alone should benchmark alternatives rather than assuming the newest release from the largest lab wins.

**Throughput.** gpt-transcribe processes pre-recorded audio at roughly **34 times real time**, which is consistent with the demo's half-hour-in-under-a-minute claim and slightly better than it.

**The 57-language claim is not independently corroborated.** The published benchmark comparison covers 22 languages on Common Voice. The 57-language support figure comes from OpenAI's own announcement, and per-language quality is not broken out. Treat 57 as the supported set and 22 as the measured set, and verify quality for any specific language before depending on it. For Hindi and Indian English specifically, no published per-language numbers were found.

**The release timing is not coincidental.** These models shipped on 28 to 29 July 2026, the same day as OpenAI's four ChatGPT Work sales demos, one of which (revenue intelligence) depends on analyzing an entire sales organization's customer conversations. Cheap high-accuracy batch transcription is the enabling infrastructure for that product, and the two announcements are two halves of one story.

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
1. **Build a vocabulary list for ML and inference terminology before transcribing any technical audio.** This is the immediate, concrete action. Terms like GPTQ, AWQ, vLLM, PagedAttention, GQA, MoE, KV-cache, and speculative decoding will be mangled by a general model and are exactly the tokens that matter in a research discussion. The hint mechanism costs nothing and addresses the primary failure mode. Do this before evaluating whether the model is good enough, because an evaluation without it measures the wrong thing.
2. **Use the 3.8x streaming premium as a design forcing function.** At $0.0045 versus $0.017 per minute, ask explicitly for every transcription use case whether a human is waiting. Conference talks, meeting archives, and podcast processing are all batch, and paying the streaming rate for them is a straight 3.8x overpayment. This ratio is also a reasonable prior for what latency costs in other modalities.
3. **Benchmark against ElevenLabs, Google, and Mistral rather than adopting on the strength of the error reduction claim.** The 52% relative improvement over whisper-1 is real and the model still trails those three on published error rates. If transcription accuracy is load-bearing for a downstream pipeline, the incumbent-lab default is not the accuracy-optimal choice, and the benchmark is cheap to run.
4. **Verify Hindi and Hindi-English code-switching quality directly, because it is unmeasured.** The automatic language-switching capability is the most valuable feature for Indian usage and it is demonstrated only on English-Spanish. The published error figures cover 22 languages without per-language breakdown. Test on real code-switched audio before building on it.
5. **Recompute the viability of any shelved audio-analysis pipeline.** At $0.0045 per minute, a thousand hours of audio transcribes for roughly $270. Projects previously blocked on capture cost are now blocked only on analysis cost, which moves the optimization problem entirely into the LLM layer. That is a regime change worth acting on rather than noting.
6. **Evaluate on low-redundancy tokens specifically, not on aggregate word error rate.** Aggregate WER is dominated by common words that context recovers anyway. The errors that break downstream extraction are on names, numbers, and domain terms. Build a small evaluation set consisting only of utterances containing proper nouns and numerals, and rank candidate models on that. It will produce a different ranking than aggregate WER and it will be the ranking that predicts downstream performance.
