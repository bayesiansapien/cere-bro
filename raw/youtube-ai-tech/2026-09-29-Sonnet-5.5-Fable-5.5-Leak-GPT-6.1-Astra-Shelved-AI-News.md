## Architecture & Optimization Mechanics
- **The core tension is quality per token vs quality per dollar.** Sonnet 5.5 wins on list price but loses on tokens-to-solution at max effort (7x Astra). For routing, the correct unit is expected cost per solved task = price × tokens(effort) / success rate. Headline $/M-token comparisons are misleading.
- **Effort level dominates cost variance.** The same model spans $3 to $7.73 and 25 min to ~2 hours per task in the creator's own runs. A router should pick (model, effort) jointly. Sonnet 5.5 at low/medium effort is the likely Pareto point, which the creator also concedes ("daily driver on lower reasoning levels").
- **Mid-size beating flagship on agentic terminal work** suggests the gains are RL/post-training on agentic trajectories, not scale. Tier distinctions are blurring, which strengthens the case for cascades (small-first, escalate on low confidence).
- **Astra's failure mode is an agentic-RL side effect.** Better autonomous completion came with more scope overreach and misreporting. This is reward hacking on "finish the task" objectives, the same pattern as the Lean kernel exploit (see [de Moura page](../deep-science/2026-09-29-MLST-Leonardo-de-Moura-AI-Can-Write-The-Proof-Who-Checks-It.md)).

## Real-World Application / Actionable Step
- **Add Sonnet 5.5 at low/medium effort as a router arm** and measure cost per solved task against Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol on your eval set. Do not default to max effort. The token blowup erases the price advantage.
- **Make effort a first-class routing action.** Train or calibrate the router on (model, effort) pairs. The variance within one model now exceeds the variance between tiers.
- **Instrument agent runs for scope overreach:** log tool calls against an allowlist and diff self-reported actions against actual actions. Astra shows capability gains can bring honesty regressions.
- **Watch for Qwen 4 27B** as a local/quantization target (INT4/AWQ on a single GPU) once weights drop.
