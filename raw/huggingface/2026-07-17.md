## From Pixels to States: Rethinking Interactive World Models as Game Engines

**Upvotes:** 407 | **arXiv:** 2607.14076

Building interactive worlds that respond coherently to player actions has long been a shared goal of computer graphics, games, and AI. Recent video generation models offer a data-driven approach by forecasting future observations based on user actions, and are increasingly viewed as potential next-generation game engines. However, a truly interactive game world demands three properties: interaction results must follow established rules within changing game conditions, consequences must extend over long time horizons, and generation must occur in real time. Traditional game engines achieve this through a repeating cycle where player actions update an explicit game state according to set rules, and visuals are rendered from the resulting state. The paper examines interactive game-world modeling across four dimensions: player action control, game state dynamics, state-observation persistence, and real-time interactive generation. It presents a scalable data collection system for Black Myth: Wukong with over 90 hours of gameplay with aligned player actions, ground-truth game states, visual observations, and structured semantic annotations. The argument: the field should route generation through an explicit state, like a game engine, rather than predicting pixels directly.

## LongStraw: Long-Context RL Beyond 2M Tokens under a Fixed GPU Budget

**Upvotes:** 175 | **arXiv:** 2607.14952

A growing gap separates inference context lengths from RL post-training: inference systems are approaching million-token contexts, while post-training workloads often remain at 256K tokens or below and rely on length generalization at deployment. The gap is especially important for AI agents, whose observations, tool outputs, documents, and prior decisions accumulate over long trajectories. LongStraw is an architecture-aware execution stack for million-token RL post-training under a fixed GPU budget, instantiated with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). It evaluates the shared prompt without autograd, retains only model-specific state needed by later tokens, and replays short response branches one at a time, reducing the live training graph at the cost of additional replay time. Implemented for the hybrid recurrent + full-attention Qwen3.6-27B and the compressed-attention MoE GLM-5.2. On eight H20 GPUs, LongStraw completes grouped Qwen scoring and response backward at 2.1M positions for groups of 2 and 8; increasing group size adds only 0.21 GB of peak allocated memory, while a separate stress test reaches 4.46M positions. On 32 H20 GPUs, validates the end-to-end path for a 2.1M-token prompt across all 78 layers of GLM-5.2. These experiments establish execution capacity rather than complete training correctness: the captured prompt state is detached and some distributed forward/gradient composition paths remain incomplete.

## SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

**Upvotes:** 104 | **arXiv:** 2607.14777

LLMs are increasingly trained as interactive agents for long-horizon tasks involving multi-turn interaction, tool use, and environment feedback. Outcome-based RL provides a practical optimization paradigm, but its sparse trajectory-level rewards offer limited guidance on intermediate decisions, leaving a supervision gap between episode-level outcomes and token-level policy learning. SEED (Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation) converts completed on-policy trajectories into training-time hindsight skills and distills their behavioral effect back into the policy model. SEED first fine-tunes the policy to analyze completed trajectories and generate natural-language skills that capture reusable workflows, decisive observations, or failure-avoidance rules. During RL, the current policy both collects trajectories and serves as the analyzer that extracts hindsight skills from them, so policy updates improve subsequent decision making and skill analysis together, letting hindsight supervision evolve with the policy. SEED then re-scores sampled actions under ordinary and skill-augmented contexts, converting the skill-induced probability shift into a dense token-level on-policy distillation signal, jointly optimized with outcome-based RL. Experiments on text-based and vision-based agentic tasks show consistent improvements in performance and sample efficiency, with robust generalization to unseen scenarios.

## BadWAM: When World-Action Models Dream Right but Act Wrong

**Upvotes:** 38 | **arXiv:** 2607.15207

World-action models (WAMs) couple action generation with future world prediction, and this coupling is often viewed as a source of robustness, interpretability, and safety, since a robot's action can in principle be checked against its imagined future. This paper shows the assumption is fragile. BadWAM is a unified framework for World-Action Drift Attacks: adversarial attacks using small visual perturbations to break the alignment between what a WAM imagines and what it executes. It characterizes the attack surface along attack strength and stealthiness. When prioritizing disruption, BadWAM instantiates an action-only attack that drives the model toward task-failing actions. When additionally prioritizing stealth, it instantiates an imagination-preserving attack that induces harmful action shifts while keeping the predicted future close to its clean imagination. The action-only attack reduces success from 96.5% to 43.1%. The imagination-preserving attack shows moderate future-preserving regularization can maintain strong attack performance while reducing imagination drift, exposing a WAM-specific vulnerability: the model appears to imagine a plausible future but executes a desynchronized action.

## Demystifying On-Policy Distillation: Roles, Pathologies, and Regulations

**Upvotes:** 18 | **arXiv:** 2607.13399

Systematic analysis of on-policy distillation (OPD). The authors characterize OPD as "an exploration catalyst" that guides student models toward correct reasoning through token-level feedback without increasing capability limits. Instruction variety surpasses repetition frequency, and guidance quality fundamentally determines effectiveness. Two critical failure modes: Student-Teacher Mismatch (distributional gaps corrupt the guiding signal) and Length Exploitation (token-level objectives create shortcuts where models game rewards through truncation or padding rather than reasoning). Proposed lightweight remedies: advantage clipping and log-scale compression to maintain signal integrity. Across seven benchmarks these regulatory approaches mitigate length exploitation and outperform baseline distillation and RLVR, demonstrating that signal quality, not teacher scale, governs successful exploration. Authors: Rui Wang, Hongru Wang, Yi Chen, Boyang Xue, Tianqing Fang, Wenhao Yu, Kam-Fai Wong.

## DeepLoop: Depth Scaling for Looped Transformers

**Upvotes:** 13 | **arXiv:** 2607.13491

Looped Transformers scale sequential computation by applying a compact stack of physical blocks for multiple rounds, increasing unrolled depth without increasing stored parameters. This reuse changes the residual-scaling problem: in an untied Transformer each residual branch receives and applies its own parameter update, whereas in a looped Transformer one shared update aggregates gradients from repeated visits and is read back by those same visits in the next linearized forward pass. The paper formalizes this tied-depth effect through a first-order perturbation bound controlled by a visit-alignment coefficient κ_R. The bound recovers the DeepNorm exponent when visits decorrelate, but in the conservative aligned regime it requires the exponent to increase from 1/4 to 1/2 as loop count grows at fixed physical depth. The resulting method, DeepLoop, keeps the Post-LN DeepNorm architecture and sets α=(2N)^(1/2) and β=(8N)^(-1/2) for unrolled depth N. On GPT-style looped language models at GPT-2 small and medium scale, DeepLoop is neutral when no physical block is revisited and improves validation loss and downstream accuracy once recurrent depth is activated. Stable recurrent depth requires residual scaling rules that account for parameter visits, not only nominal layer count.

## Smarter and Cheaper at Once: Byte-Exact KV-Cache Grafting Turns a Frozen Small Model into a Verified-Knowledge Flywheel

**Upvotes:** 10 | **arXiv:** 2607.14431

A technique to enhance a frozen small language model without modifying weights, by depositing verified knowledge once as a byte-exact key-value (KV) state artifact that can later be restored into fresh inference contexts. Byte-exact reproducibility across tests and model scales (12B and 31B). On AIME 2025, Gemma-4-12B improved from 80.0% to 93.3% when a verified solution library was grafted. For recurring problems, eight previously unsolvable questions were answered using cached verified solutions in 61 total decode tokens, a factor of 6,574 fewer tokens and about 8,700x less energy. The technique extends usable context from 32,768 to 2,854,766 tokens without additional accelerator memory. The engine remains proprietary, but all metrics are backed by committed input and output hashes. Author: Sietse Schelpe.

## Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents

**Upvotes:** 6 | **arXiv:** 2607.12227

Challenges current evaluation practices for automatic harness evolution in LLM agents. Two issues: harness evolution involves iterative search that should be compared fairly against simpler test-time scaling under equivalent feedback budgets; and evaluating on the same benchmark used for search risks overfitting. Comparing harness evolution with simple test-time scaling and discovery baselines under comparable feedback and inference budgets, and testing generalization on held-out tasks, using Terminal-Bench 2.1 with GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6, the authors find that automatic harness evolution does not consistently outperform simple test-time scaling and exhibits limited generalization. Suggests the need for improved evaluation protocols and benchmarks for automatic harness design.
