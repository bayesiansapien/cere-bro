# Who Prices Cognitive Labor in the Age of Agents? Compute-Anchored Wages

This paper challenges the intuition that AI agents, due to their low marginal replication cost, will suppress cognitive labor wages. Instead, the author argues that agents are not labor; they are a production technology that converts compute capital into effective units of cognitive labor. The key contribution introduces a Compute-Anchored Wage (CAW) framework establishing that competitive human wages on substitutable tasks are bounded by compute capital rental rates, compute intensity, and relative productivity metrics. The conclusion is that the price-setter for cognitive labor is no longer the labor market but rather the compute capital market, fundamentally reshaping how we understand wage dynamics in an AI-driven economy.
