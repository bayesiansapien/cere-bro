## When is Routing Meaningful? Diversity and Robustness in Language Model Societies

**arXiv:** 2607.09197 | **Authors:** Fantine Huot, Michael Kaisers, Mirella Lapata (DeepMind-affiliated)

Routing policies for multi-model systems are evaluated almost exclusively on task accuracy and inference cost. The authors argue that two properties, orthogonal to performance, determine whether routing is meaningful. First, the society of actors must be behaviourally differentiated: if all actors respond identically, routing is vacuous. Second, the routing policy must be stable: surface-form variants of a query should be assigned to the same actor. High task accuracy is compatible with violating both properties, since a router can operate over a redundant society or assign queries inconsistently, preventing specialisation regardless of performance. They adapt Hierarchic Social Entropy (HSE) to language-model societies and introduce a perturbation-based robustness metric to diagnose these failure modes. Applied to EmbedLLM and RouterBench, HSE exhibits strong diminishing returns, suggesting a curated subset of fewer than ten agents recovers most available diversity in a large pool (a practical coreset heuristic for society design). KNN routers gain accuracy from specialist societies but collapse in robustness under perturbation, while prompted routing remains stable across all perturbation types, illustrating that accuracy and meaningfulness can sharply diverge.

## Tracing the Decisive Step: Agentic Failure Attribution (OAT)

**Source:** DAIR.AI weekly #5 (Microsoft + UW-Madison)

Finding which step in a failed agent run caused the failure usually needs failure labels or expensive per-step prompting. OAT skips both: one-class learning with neural controlled differential equations models the latent dynamics of successful trajectories, then scores each step of a failed run by how far it strays from that learned flow. With only 100 successful trajectories and no failure labels, it turns failure attribution into anomaly detection, delivering a 200-5000x speedup over prompting-based attribution while improving F1 by 20% in-domain and 7% out-of-distribution.

## Failure as a Process: Annotating 63,000 Coding-Agent Steps

**Source:** DAIR.AI weekly #6

Treats coding-agent failure as a timeline rather than a pass/fail label. Annotates 63,000+ execution steps across 3,843 runs from seven frontier models on three scaffolds (Terminal-Bench, filtered to 1,794 valid trajectories). Each trajectory gets three timestamps: the decisive error, the point where the error becomes irreversible, and the first observable failure, exposing a "fix window" and an "observability lag" that pass/fail labels erase.

## Self-Improving Agents Survey

**Source:** DAIR.AI weekly #1

Frames a modern agent as a foundation model plus an operational scaffold (prompts, memory, tools, control logic), and self-improvement as a self-induced update committing changes to either the weights or the scaffold. Organizes methods by learning-signal source: intrinsic generative demonstrations, intrinsic evaluative feedback, extrinsic exploratory experience.

## Metacognition in LLMs Survey

**Source:** DAIR.AI weekly #2 (Yale + UC Irvine)

Argues confidence calibration, self-verification, knowing when to stop, and knowing what you don't know are facets of one capability, metacognition, organized as a monitor-and-control loop around the model. Catalogs measurement (signal detection theory, calibration/AUROC/ECE, activation neurofeedback, interpretability probes) and shows gains in hallucination reduction, knowledge-boundary detection, and persuasion resistance.
