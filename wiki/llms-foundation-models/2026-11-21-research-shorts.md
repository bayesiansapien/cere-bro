# Research shorts, 2026-11-21: steering vectors, distillation for diffusion, circuits in weights

**Sources:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-11-20 · X feed · raw: `raw/huggingface/2026-11-20-*.md`

- **RL post-training in diffusion transformers is activation steering (2611.26627).** A fixed set of steering vectors added to block outputs of the frozen base model (0.29M parameters, 65x fewer than the RL LoRA on SD3.5-M) recovers 90.0-96.3% of the reward gains of six RL-tuned image models and 89.8-111.9% on two video models. Trained directly with online RL, the vectors beat weight-based RL on OCR and PickScore. RL mostly steers toward what the model can already do. Compression angle: most of an RL fine-tune fits in a constant shift.
- **Do probing and steering favor the same directions? (2611.19499).** No. For truthfulness and sentiment in three model families, the best probe direction is not the best steering direction; steering prefers directions near the mean difference (59 of 60 layer-model-concept settings). Probes transfer across models (81-91% retained); steering vectors transfer worse. Steering toward "unknown" raises abstention by 18-44 points on uncertain errors.
- **Posterior Matching Distillation (2611.26629).** Few-step diffusion distillation usually matches distributions with reverse KL, which is mode-seeking and loses diversity. PMD matches exponentially tilted posterior means, which under Gaussian noise is one teacher call at a shifted input, and proves this pins down the full posterior. Best 4-step FID on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, with any-step sampling and no auxiliary score network.
- **A CPU inside an LLM (2611.26450).** Closed-form "circuit grafting" compiles exact 4-8 digit add/subtract/multiply circuits into pretrained weights with zero training examples, adding at most 0.036-0.057% non-zeros. Granite 4.2 3B and Qwen 3.5 4B reach 99.17% and 100% on 6-digit arithmetic, against 42.2% and 61.7% for a 12.8K-sample LoRA. Joins the weight-space programming thread (11-19).
- **Training vision models for weight-space programming (2611.26408).** Edit-Ready Training rehearses the closed-form edits LLMs make, so networks become easier for GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 to edit by hand (2,600+ edit runs).
- **CSPD, composable soft-prompt distillation (ByteDance, via X).** Each RL expert (math, code) is distilled into its own 256-token soft prompt (about 0.01% of model parameters) on a frozen pre-RL base; experts compose by concatenating prompts in input space instead of merging weights. Reported to recover 90%+ and sometimes over 100% of each expert's gain. ([@alacheng](https://x.com/alacheng/status/2123746209584316659))
- **In situ training of optical neural networks by coding agents (2611.23875).** A coding agent trains real optical hardware from camera images alone, forming interpretable optical parameterizations, and approaches backprop-through-a-calibrated-model at a fraction of the cost.
- **Twice-told critiques (2611.26286).** Critique overlap between reviews of the same ICLR paper rose from 12.0% (2026) to 21.3% (2027); LLM-flagged reviews overlap more with each other. A third review now adds 5.46 new problems, down from 6.12.
- **Cross-source confirmed (HF + Kurate): ITPD (2611.26232).** A sound and complete local-to-global causal discovery method for nonstationary time series; 2.3x, 9.1x and 40.9x faster than iterative IAMB, DF-PC and PCMCI on 2,000-node graphs. Kurate cs.LG #1 this week (unscored tournament). Outside the core reading list.
