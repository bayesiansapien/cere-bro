# Nvidia reinstates Huang, Megaspeed blacklisted, Marlowe raises

## What happened

- **Reinstatement.** Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director on 11-23, 11 days after he resigned on 11-12. The board said it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident his return poses no national security risk. The independent investigation into whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort continues. Harvey Jones stays chair; Colette Kress returns to CFO. Nvidia is creating a National Security and Export Control Committee and hiring a new chief compliance officer ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director), [AI Breakfast](https://aibreakfast.beehiiv.com/)).
- **How.** Per The Information, the Attorney General told Trump the Justice Department had no evidence of wrongdoing, and at Trump's direction JD Vance relayed that to the board ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/trump-put-jensen-huang-back-charge-nvidia)). Huang testifies before the House on 12-01 ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/us-spies-tracked-nvidia-scandal-figures-ties-to-chinese-intelligence-for-over-a-year/)).
- **Market.** NVDA had fallen 48% from its early-October high to about $125 on 11-20, about $2.4T of value. It closed 11-23 up 31% at $164.92; the Nasdaq 100 rose 6.8% ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/tech-stocks-rally-across-board)).
- **Megaspeed.** Commerce added Singapore-based Megaspeed, once one of Nvidia's biggest customers and led by Alice Huang, to the Entity List. Nvidia had already stopped selling to it in June 2025, so the listing mainly closes indirect routes through other vendors' servers and affiliates ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/trump-administration-will-add-nvidia-linked-megaspeed-trade-blacklist), [@rohanpaul_ai via Semafor](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2124983432854036813)).
- **Marlowe.** A six-month-old startup from an ex-Microsoft AI-infrastructure VP and ex-Convoy CEO raised a **$277M seed at $1.15B**, led by Accel, to build inference systems on AMD and Etched chips instead of Nvidia ([AI Weekly Espresso via GeekWire](https://aiweekly.co/)).
- **AMD.** Up 98% this year; The Information argues its valuation already prices in share gains from Nvidia's turmoil plus the 6GW OpenAI and Meta deals ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/amds-valuation-getting-ahead-fundamentals)).
- **Amazon and Decart.** Amazon is acquiring Decart's chip-software technology and top staff to run AI compute, including Trainium, more efficiently. Earlier reporting (11-22) framed it as a roughly $7.5B purchase ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/amazon-acquires-chip-software-business-ai-startup-decart)).

## The policy debate underneath

Understanding AI and Derek Thompson's "five debates" essay puts the strongest case each way on selling chips to China. Against: no Chinese frontier model has been trained on Chinese chips, and civil-military fusion makes any sale a military sale. For: export bans speed China's indigenisation, and the durable advantage is infrastructure and adoption, not a temporary model lead ([Understanding AI](https://www.understandingai.org/p/the-five-most-important-debates-in)).
