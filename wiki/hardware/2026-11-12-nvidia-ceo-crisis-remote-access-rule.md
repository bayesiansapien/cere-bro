# Nvidia's CEO crisis, the remote-access rule, and chips changing hands

## TL;DR

Nvidia's board met on 11-11 to consider removing Jensen Huang, per WSJ and other outlets citing Trump administration officials, three days after saying it had confidence in him. A New York Times story said he had an affair with a company executive as well as with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive US officials suspect of working for China's Ministry of State Security; Megaspeed rented access to Nvidia GPUs. Nvidia is down roughly 26% over a month and shareholders filed a securities class action. Gary Marcus's question is the compute-relevant one: Nvidia is one of seven strategic buyers of $26B of Anthropic stock at the IPO, so an acting CEO could revisit that and other circular financing commitments. Separately, Commerce is finalizing a rule requiring export licenses for Blackwell-class chips in seven more countries (including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway and South Africa), aimed at Chinese firms renting chips remotely through foreign data centers. Evidence the rule targets a real leak: a PLA-linked university on the Entity List bought two H200s for about $123K through a reseller in October and rents more through five contractors (Reuters via AI Weekly). On the supply side, AMD's 10-Q shows it paid $2.9B for Taalas, which hardwires LLM weights into silicon, and The Information reports that inference resellers that never owned GPUs are now buying their own because they cannot lease enough capacity.

## Key numbers

- Anthropic IPO: 825M shares at $70-78, up to $64.4B public plus $26B private placement from Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD and Micron; implied up to $1.404T; Nasdaq ticker ANT around 11-19. FT reports the book is oversubscribed.
- TSMC's board approved buying Anthropic stock at the IPO price (WSJ via @rohanpaul_ai).
- Ramp: US corporate AI spend +1.65% in October; 1% of companies drive 80% of OpenAI and Anthropic revenue.
- AWS CEO Matt Garman: inference "not even 1%" of its eventual size; Amazon installs one GPU every 14 seconds; orbital data centers 10-20 years away.
- Meta's head of global infrastructure, Santosh Janardhan, is leaving; AWS veteran Dave Brown takes over.
