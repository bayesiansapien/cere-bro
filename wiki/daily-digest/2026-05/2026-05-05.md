### MotionCache. Motion-Aware Caching for Autoregressive Video Generation

> Pixels with low inter-frame motion need fewer denoising steps. Two-phase schedule: warm-up first, then motion-weighted cache reuse. 6.28x on SkyReels-V2 with 1% VBench drop. The video-AR analogue of selective KV-cache reuse.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01725) · [Wiki](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-05-motion-aware-caching-video.md)
**Tier:** 1, inference efficiency, KV-cache analogue

```
ITERATION UNIT AS OPTIMIZATION UNIT — FOUR PAPERS, FOUR MODALITIES
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
KV Packet (04-17)    text     reuse cached KV across contexts
TurboQuant (04-22)   text     compress KV to 2.5–3.5 bits per channel
PrfaaS (04-22)       text     transport KV across datacenters via hybrid attention
MotionCache (05-05)  video    reuse denoising iterations where motion is low

Shared principle: the iteration unit (token, KV row, KV transfer, denoising step)
has heterogeneous information density and should be allocated proportionally.
```

The mechanism is structurally simple. An autoregressive video diffusion model normally runs a fixed number of denoising steps per frame. MotionCache notes that pixels in regions with low inter-frame motion are very close to their previous denoised value, so re-running the full denoising schedule there is wasted compute. The two-phase schedule first runs a warm-up that establishes semantic consistency, then switches to motion-weighted cache reuse: regions with high motion get full updates, regions with low motion skip steps. The implementation is training-free and the code is on GitHub. The 6.28x acceleration on SkyReels-V2 is the largest video-AR speedup the wiki has seen at this quality preservation level (1% VBench drop).

For the cere-bro reading list, this is the fourth instance of the same architectural pattern in seven weeks. The pattern is that the iteration unit (whatever it is, in whatever modality) has heterogeneous information density, and most production systems treat it uniformly. Once you identify the right delta signal (attention saliency for KV, motion delta for video), you can recover most of the quality at a fraction of the compute. The conceptually interesting move is that "what counts as the iteration unit" varies by modality. KV Packet operates on cached KV blocks, MotionCache on per-pixel denoising steps. The substrate-level claim is that *iteration heterogeneity is a universal property of generative systems and should be the default optimization target.*

The natural composition is with SDVG (04-22), which extended speculative decoding to video by accepting whole blocks at the inter-block scale. SDVG and MotionCache work at orthogonal scales: SDVG saves time across blocks, MotionCache saves time within them. Both are training-free. Both preserve quality in the operating range. A joint deployment that runs SDVG block-level acceptance and MotionCache pixel-level skipping has not been measured, but the speedups should compound. On long videos at minute scale, where motion accumulates and warm-up no longer dominates, the joint system would be the right test of whether the cache strategy degrades gracefully.

For the Tier 1 KV cache thread the implication is more subtle. The same logic that picks "which pixels can skip denoising" generalizes to "which KV rows can be evicted." Most KV eviction policies today are LRU or magnitude-based; a temporal-similarity-based eviction policy that mirrors MotionCache's motion-delta logic has not been tried. The cross-domain transfer is the open paper.

**Why it matters:** Production video AR has been considered impractical because of the iteration cost. A 6.28x training-free speedup at 1% quality drop changes the deployment calculus. The deeper point is that the wiki's KV cache thread now has a clear cross-modal companion, and the unifying principle is bigger than text inference.

**Research angle:** (1) MotionCache + SDVG joint speedup at minute-scale video. (2) Motion-style temporal similarity as a KV eviction signal in long-context language inference. (3) Tail behavior at long video horizons where motion accumulates beyond the warm-up phase's effective range. The paper does not report on long-tail degradation; whoever does sets the production deployment standard.

### T^2PO. Token- and Turn-Level Policy Optimization for Stable Multi-Turn Agentic RL

> Multi-turn RL collapses because policies emit actions that neither reduce uncertainty nor advance the task. T^2PO monitors marginal uncertainty change at the token level and exploration progress at the turn level, and intervenes at both granularities. Stable training and gains across WebShop, ALFWorld, Search QA.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02178) · [Wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-05-t2po-uncertainty-multi-turn-rl.md)
**Tier:** 2 with Tier 1 routing intersection

```
SAME CONTROL SURFACE, INFERENCE VS TRAINING
───────────────────────────────────────────
Inference (05-02 Step-Level Optimization):
  Stuck Monitor      → trajectory not advancing → escalate to frontier model
  Milestone Monitor  → semantic drift detected  → escalate to frontier model

Training (05-05 T^2PO):
  Token-level        → marginal uncertainty change below threshold → thinking intervention
  Turn-level         → exploration progress negligible              → resample turn

Both papers identify the same trajectory-information-stalls signal.
Inference uses it to escalate. Training uses it to intervene and resample.
```

T^2PO's diagnosis of multi-turn RL collapse is the right kind of mechanistic argument. Policies in long-horizon environments do not collapse from high-loss states; they collapse from chains of low-information actions that do not push the trajectory anywhere. The fix has the same shape at two granularities. At the token level, when marginal uncertainty change drops below a threshold, T^2PO injects a thinking intervention to break the action stream and force the policy to re-evaluate. At the turn level, when a turn yields no exploration progress, T^2PO resamples the turn rather than wasting rollout budget on it. WebShop, ALFWorld, and Search QA all show stability and capability gains.

The wiki has been tracking the inference-side version of this signal for three weeks. Step-Level Optimization (05-02) put the same machinery at deployment: a Stuck Monitor on trajectory advancement and a Milestone Monitor on semantic drift, both triggering escalation to a frontier model. The two papers describe the same control surface from opposite sides of the training-deployment boundary. T^2PO catches the trajectory-information-stalls signal at training time and resamples; Step-Level Optimization catches it at inference time and escalates. The natural compositional question is whether a policy trained under T^2PO is more sensitive to the same signal at deployment, making escalation cheaper. No paper measures this.

The other composition is with AgenticQwen (05-04). AgenticQwen's two RL flywheels (self-failure mining + adversarial simulated users) hardened a 30B-A3B MoE on its own errors and on misleading-instruction trajectories, but the flywheels themselves depend on stable training that does not collapse. T^2PO is exactly the stability primitive the AgenticQwen pipeline needs as a control loop. AgenticQwen plus T^2PO is the natural recipe: AgenticQwen for distribution hardening, T^2PO for per-turn control.

For the wiki's joint-budget composition open problem, T^2PO adds a fifth dimension. The 05-04 digest noted four interacting budgets (Ch 14 fallback chain, Ch 15 structured-output retry budget, agent tool budget, Step-Level Optimization escalation budget). T^2PO's per-turn resample budget makes five. The operations research problem of jointly allocating these budgets has been flagged twice now and remains unsolved. With Clark's automated AI R&D timeline (today, Tier 1) running, this problem is one that automated R&D itself will have to solve.

**Why it matters:** T^2PO is the missing training-time pair to Step-Level Optimization. The same uncertainty signal that lets a deployed agent know when to escalate is the signal that lets a trainer know when to resample. This is the cleanest example so far of an idea that transfers across the training-inference boundary without modification.

**Research angle:** (1) T^2PO + Step-Level Optimization composition: a T^2PO-trained policy should escalate less under Step-Level Optimization at the same threshold. (2) Token-level uncertainty derivative as a KV eviction signal: the same metric T^2PO uses to trigger thinking is computable from KV attention weights. (3) Joint-budget formalization with five interacting budgets, building on the Ch 14/Ch 15 + Step-Level Optimization stack.

### The Distillation Panic. Nathan Lambert on Why the Term Matters

> Anthropic's blog post called Chinese-lab API jailbreaking "distillation attacks." Congress is now drafting bills, the White House issued an executive order, and committees are probing U.S. companies that build on Chinese models. Lambert argues this conflates legitimate distillation, the technique every lab depends on, with abuse, and the long-tail Western contributors are the ones who lose.

**Source:** Interconnects AI (Nathan Lambert)
**Links:** [Post](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/the-distillation-panic) · [Wiki](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-04-distillation-panic-lambert.md)
**Tier:** 1, knowledge distillation, policy

The argument has two halves. First, the technical half: distillation is an industry-standard technique that every lab uses. Lambert's working definition includes both the data-engine pattern (using a stronger model's outputs to build instruction, preference, or RL verification data) and the skill-transfer pattern (training a smaller model on a stronger one's outputs for specific capabilities like math or code). Modern pipelines often chain multiple distillation steps in ways that make any single "this model was distilled from that model" claim incomplete. NVIDIA's Nemotron is partly distilled from Chinese open-weight models. Ai2's Olmo is distilled from a mix of open and closed models. xAI's Musk testified at the OpenAI trial: "Generally AI companies distill other AI companies." The technique is not the line; the line is whether the access used to obtain teacher outputs respects the API's terms of service.

Second, the policy half. The Anthropic blog post Lambert is responding to is rhetorically careful: it normalizes distillation generally and pins illicit use on three Chinese labs that used jailbreaking and identity spoofing to extract reasoning traces from APIs. But the term "distillation attack" has propagated to a House bill, an executive order, and committee oversight aimed at U.S. companies building on Chinese models. The most likely outcome of a hasty multi-pronged regulatory push is not a clean ban on jailbreaking but a grey area that exposes Western academics and small labs to compliance risk that closed-platform providers and large companies can absorb. The Chinese labs the legislation targets will keep doing it. The long-tail open-weight ecosystem in the U.S. and Europe will not.

The wiki has been tracking distillation as a technical thread for three weeks: TIP (04-16), TESSY (04-18), Switch-KD (04-18), BLD (04-17), Tide (04-30), CoPD (05-01). Five of those papers built neutral exchange representations between mismatched teacher and student. The sixth, CoPD, made experts mutually distill in real time during RLVR training. The technical thrust of the field is to make distillation more, not less, of the standard substrate. Lambert's piece adds the political shadow: the technique has become so essential that misnaming it could legally entangle the work itself.

The connection to last week's Defense Trilemma + NP-hard reward hacking results is structural. Both arguments say the regulatory and procurement frames being pushed through institutional channels will misclassify load-bearing technical practice as a threat surface. The trilemma argues complete defenses are mathematically impossible; Lambert argues that distillation cannot be cleanly partitioned into legitimate vs abusive. Both arguments point at the same failure mode: institutional response to AI risk is converging on demands that are structurally impossible to meet, and the impossibility falls on the actors who play by the rules.

The Kevin Xu reframe quoted at the end is worth keeping. If Chinese labs are addicted to distillation as a capability shortcut, the long-run effect of a U.S. crackdown is that they finally have to learn frontier techniques themselves. The short-term lead becomes a long-term liability. This is the same logic the U.S. semiconductor playbook is supposed to have demonstrated. Whether it transfers to AI is the open question.

**Why it matters:** Distillation is now the foundation of the wiki's tracked post-training literature. Policy that misnames it as an attack surface threatens the entire research practice. The technical field has a six-week, six-paper convergence on neutral-channel distillation. The policy debate is six weeks behind.

**Research angle (Tier 1):** (1) Distillation provenance audit: of the public open-weight models in production, what fraction has been distilled from which sources? Olmo and Nemotron are the only two with disclosed pipelines; the rest are reverse-engineering targets. (2) API design for distillation-resilience: rate limits, refusal-density patterns, reasoning-trace gating are not formally characterized as anti-distillation primitives. (3) Quantified open-weight transition cost: Lambert claims the lead time on a domestic open-weight ecosystem replacement is 6+ months. A defended number with capex and talent inputs would be a useful policy-debate input.

### Import AI 455. AI Systems Are About to Start Building Themselves — Jack Clark

> 60% probability of fully autonomous AI R&D by end of 2028, 30% for end of 2027. The argument is built from public benchmark trajectories: SWE-Bench from 2% to 93.9% in 2.5 years; METR time horizons from 30 seconds to 12 hours in four years; CORE-Bench declared solved in 15 months. Engineering-perspiration tasks are now in the trajectory; creativity is the open question.

**Source:** Import AI 455 (Jack Clark)
**Links:** [Post](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-04-import-ai-455-automated-ai-rd.md)
**Tier:** 1, research direction, AI R&D automation

Clark's argument moves through six benchmark trajectories that all share the same shape. SWE-Bench: launched in late 2023 with Claude 2 at 2%, now 93.9% with Claude Mythos Preview, effectively saturated. METR time horizons: 30 seconds with GPT-3.5 in 2022, 4 minutes with GPT-4 in 2023, 40 minutes with o1 in 2024, 6 hours with GPT-5.2 in 2025, 12 hours with Opus 4.6 today. METR's Ajeya Cotra thinks 100 hours by end of 2026 is reasonable. CORE-Bench: 21.5% in September 2024, declared solved at 95.5% by December 2025. MLE-Bench: 16.9% in October 2024, 64.4% by February 2026 with Gemini3 in an agent harness with search. Anthropic's LLM-training-optimization speedup: 2.9x in May 2025, 16.5x in November 2025, 30x in February 2026, 52x in April 2026 with Claude Mythos Preview. Humans take 4-8 hours for a 4x speedup. PostTrainBench: AI systems now achieve about half of expert human uplift on fine-tuning a base model.

The pattern is consistent. A new benchmark of "AI doing AI development work" launches; a frontier model scores 15-30%; within 12-18 months, the benchmark is saturated. Clark's argument is not "AI will be smarter than humans by 2028." It is "AI is now good enough at the engineering schlep of AI development that the engineering can be automated, and engineering accounts for ~99% of the actual work in the field." The Edison "1% inspiration, 99% perspiration" frame is doing serious work in the argument: even without creative breakthroughs, the field can advance by chained-engineering scaling.

The wiki has been tracking the substrate (MIT superposition 05-03), the operational targets (TIP, LongAct, CvS, Safety Drift), the architecture map (Ken Huang World Models 05-03), and the routing/efficiency stack (MiMo 05-03, AgenticQwen 05-04, Step-Level Optimization 05-02, T^2PO today). Clark's piece argues these pieces compose into automated R&D, and the composition is now within reach. That makes the trilemma's open question from yesterday — formalize routing-as-uncorrelated-defense, measure defense-layer correlation on a deployed stack — not an abstract research problem but a clock running against a deadline.

The compounding-error footnote is the load-bearing one for alignment. If an alignment technique is 99.9% accurate per generation, after 50 generations the residual is 95.12%, and after 500 generations it is 60.5%. Recursive self-improvement compounds whatever rate of failure the alignment approach has. The trilemma's persistent-unsafe-region result and Wang/Huang's reward-hacking-grows-with-tools result are exactly the failure modes that would compound here. A formal bound on alignment preservation under N generations of self-improvement does not exist; under Clark's timeline it now needs to.

The other piece worth keeping is Clark's caveat. He explicitly notes that math and CS may be unusual domains for AI-driven discovery. Move 37 has not been replaced in ten years. If creativity scales as fast as engineering, the timeline collapses; if it stalls, the 60% number drops. The caveat is doing real work because it identifies the load-bearing assumption.

**Why it matters:** This is the most concrete public timeline argument from a frontier insider on automated AI R&D. The wiki's Tier 1 reading list is now better understood as "these are the pieces that compose into the takeoff." Routing, KV cache, distillation, harness architecture, agent benchmarks, interpretability — none are isolated topics; they are the load-bearing components of the trajectory Clark describes.

**Research angle (Tier 1):** (1) Trajectory-aware multi-agent routing under uncorrelated failure modes is the natural alignment-defense composition for recursive self-improvement. (2) Compounding-error bound: given a per-generation alignment-preservation rate, what is the formal bound on N generations? Trilemma + Sun-Smith-Nayebi give ingredients. (3) Predictive interpretability via superposition geometry (the 05-04 digest open prediction) is now load-bearing under Clark's timeline.

### Why Your Agentic AI Pentester Is Probably Just a Fancy Scanner — Ken Huang

> Same Gemini 3 Flash model, three architectures, three different worlds. RidgeGen produced 55 evidence-backed findings, 0 hallucinations, with a cascade from a single JWT bypass into 12 IDOR findings, mass assignment, and admin enumeration. Shannon produced 27 findings, 17 unconfirmed, 63% hallucination rate. Strix produced 6. Architecture, not weights, is the variable.

**Source:** Agentic AI / Ken Huang Substack
**Links:** [Post](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/why-your-agentic-ai-pentester-is) · [Wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-04-agentic-pentester-architecture.md)
**Tier:** 1, agent architecture, harness design

```
SAME MODEL, THREE ARCHITECTURES, THREE OUTCOMES
───────────────────────────────────────────────
                  Findings   Evidence   Hallucination   Tokens/finding
RidgeGen             55         55          0%            846K
Shannon              27         10         63%           2138K
Strix                 6          6          0%               (low coverage)

Common backbone: Gemini 3 Flash held constant.
Variable under test: system architecture.

Three failure modes named:
  1. Belief state amnesia   — each tool call independent, no propagation
  2. Best-effort evidence   — hallucinated finding can reach the report
  3. Pattern matching only  — can find SQL injection, cannot find logic flaws
```

This is the cleanest empirical instantiation the wiki has of GTA-2's "execution harness dominates model capability" claim. The methodology is the right experimental design: same model across all three platforms, controlled environment, network-isolated runs, anti-cheat for memorized walkthroughs, LLM-as-judge for whether each solved challenge reflected real exploitation or template knowledge. With the model held constant, the variable under test is architecture, and the architectures diverge by a factor of more than 5x in evidence-backed findings.

The three failure modes Ken Huang names are each the negative space of an architectural primitive that has shown up elsewhere in the wiki. **Belief state amnesia** is the absence of the trajectory state that Step-Level Optimization (05-02) requires: without it, the Stuck and Milestone monitors have nothing to evaluate. RidgeGen's cascade from JWT alg:none into 12 cascading IDOR findings, mass assignment, and admin enumeration is exactly trajectory-aware reasoning at the offense side. **Best-effort evidence** is the absence of AHE's (05-04) contract structure: AHE makes harness decisions falsifiable by gating them on benchmark contracts; RidgeGen makes findings falsifiable by gating them on execution evidence. Same architectural primitive, two domains. **Semantic reasoning vs syntactic pattern matching** is the same distinction that Themis (05-04) drew at the reward-modeling level: pattern matching finds known vulnerability classes, semantic reasoning finds gaps between application intent and behavior.

The token-efficiency numbers are the load-bearing operational result. Shannon spent 2.5x more tokens per finding than RidgeGen and required 63% manual validation. Including analyst time, the cost gap is substantially larger. For organizations integrating automated security testing into CI/CD, this gap compounds at scale. The wiki's MiMo / AgenticQwen tokens-per-task thread (05-03/04) is about model-level efficiency. This is harness-level efficiency. Both compound.

The connection to Clark's automated AI R&D timeline (today, Tier 1) is direct. If autonomous AI R&D is ~30 months out, the harness-level architecture decisions described here are the substrate that automated R&D itself will have to build on. Belief state representation is not a feature; it is a precondition for any agent that reasons about its own state long enough to do meaningful research. The pentester benchmark is a measurable, falsifiable demonstration that this primitive matters. Three years from now, the same primitive will be the difference between an automated researcher and a fancy scanner.

The disclosure caveat: Ridge Security sponsored the post. Disclosure is upfront, methodology is rigorous, and the architectural claims are general (they generalize beyond the specific RidgeGen/Shannon/Strix comparison). Replication by an independent evaluator on a different target is the canonical next step.

**Why it matters:** "Harness > weights" is the wiki's most-cited cross-paper claim. This is the cleanest empirical demonstration of it to date. For Tier 1 routing and agent design, the implication is that belief-state representation, evidence-as-architectural-invariant, and trust propagation are the three primitives any production agent system needs to be measurable, auditable, and capable of compound reasoning.

**Research angle (Tier 1):** (1) Belief state representation as a measurable harness property: a public standard for what counts as belief state is missing. (2) Trust propagation in non-security domains: the cascade pattern works in pentesting because vulnerabilities compose; whether it transfers to debug-then-test, read-then-edit, plan-then-act in production engineering is unmeasured. (3) Architecture-vs-model decomposition methodology: same-model-different-architecture is the right experimental design for harness research and should become the standard.

### PhysicianBench + AcademiClaw. Two New Realistic Agent Benchmarks

> Eighth and ninth benchmarks in the wiki cluster, both confirming the same pattern with new dimensions. PhysicianBench: 100 EHR-mediated clinical workflows, 27 tool calls per task, best closed-source model at 46%. AcademiClaw: 80 academic-level student tasks across 25+ domains, best at 55%, with the surprising finding that compute consumption does not predict output quality.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [PhysicianBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02240) · [AcademiClaw](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02661) · [Wiki PhysicianBench](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-05-physicianbench-ehr-agents.md) · [Wiki AcademiClaw](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-05-academiclaw-student-tasks.md)
**Tier:** 2, agent benchmarks, professional domains

The agent-benchmarks cluster is now eight strong (OccuBench, GTA-2, DR3-Eval, PRL-Bench, Claw-Eval-Live, InteractWeb-Bench, AcademiClaw, PhysicianBench). Each finds the same headline result with a different dimension highlighted. Today's two add two specific findings worth pulling out.

PhysicianBench's 27-tool-call average is the highest tool-call horizon in the cluster. The open-source ceiling at 19% versus the closed-source ceiling at 46% is the largest gap in the cluster, and the gap is explicitly at long-horizon multi-tool workflow rather than at knowledge recall. This is consistent with Google DeepMind's blind-physician-test win (05-01) and Marcus's "very little evidence for LLMs benefiting patients" piece (05-03): LLMs match clinicians on knowledge, but EHR-mediated workflow is a different game. The gap is plausibly a tool-use trace data gap, not a raw capability gap, since vendor EHR APIs have specific schemas that closed labs likely have more training data on.

AcademiClaw's compute-quality decoupling is the more surprising finding. The default deployment heuristic ("more thinking tokens equal better results") is empirically broken across 80 academic-level tasks and 25+ domains. This connects to Marcus's "code that compiles is not correct code" (05-01), the ARC-AGI-3 systematic-errors paper (05-03), and the Pi/Pragmatic Engineer "vibe slop" theme (04-29). All four observations cluster around the same complaint: compute-as-quality-proxy fails. AcademiClaw turns the qualitative complaint into a measurable cross-domain finding.

For the routing thread, both papers add the same kind of input. PhysicianBench's 21 specialties make specialty-conditional routing an immediately measurable experiment. AcademiClaw's "significant capability variations across task domains" makes domain-conditional routing measurable across 25+ domains. A router that dispatches dermatology to one model and oncology to another, or olympiad math to one and full-stack debugging to another, should beat any single best model. Neither benchmark has been combined with Step-Level Optimization yet, but the pieces are in place.

**Why it matters:** The eight-benchmark convergence on 30-55% frontier-agent failure rates is no longer a coincidence. With AcademiClaw's compute-decoupling finding added, the cluster now also says that the standard production heuristic for getting better results (more thinking tokens) does not work. The implication is that the routing layer needs more than per-query cost optimization; it needs per-domain capability profiling.

**Iteration-as-optimization-unit is now four-papers-deep across modalities.** KV Packet (text reuse), TurboQuant (text compression), PrfaaS (text transport), MotionCache (video denoising). Four substrates, one principle: the iteration unit has heterogeneous information density and should be allocated proportionally. The next paper in this thread should generalize the principle explicitly. Audio diffusion has the same iteration profile (silence regions need fewer denoising steps); 3D scene generation has it (low-detail background regions); structured output generation has it (template tokens vs novel tokens). The unifying paper that generalizes the principle and predicts the speedup ratio from a modality-level information-density profile is the open Tier 1 problem.

**Same control surface, training and inference.** T^2PO (today) and Step-Level Optimization (05-02) identify the same trajectory-information-stalls signal and use it on opposite sides of the training-deployment boundary. T^2PO catches it at training and resamples; Step-Level Optimization catches it at inference and escalates. The natural composition is a policy trained under T^2PO running under Step-Level Optimization at the same threshold; the prediction is that the trained policy escalates less, because it has been trained to recognize the signal. No paper has measured this. The deeper claim: trajectory state is a primitive that transfers across the training-deployment boundary without modification, and the wiki should treat any paper that uses it on one side as a hint about what's coming on the other.

**Distillation policy + Defense Trilemma + automated R&D timeline = the same wall, three directions.** Lambert (today) argues that "distillation attacks" terminology is being used to push regulation that will hit Western academics rather than the Chinese labs it targets. The Defense Trilemma (05-04) argues that complete defenses are mathematically impossible, so any policy that demands "guaranteed safe" wraps is demanding the structurally impossible. Clark (today) argues that automated AI R&D is ~30 months out at 60% probability. The three arguments compose into a single observation: institutional response to AI is converging on demands that are structurally impossible to meet, just as the underlying trajectory accelerates. Whoever writes the formal compounding-error bound on alignment under recursive self-improvement (the trilemma + Sun-Smith-Nayebi machinery applied to Clark's timeline) defines the policy debate for the next two years.

**Harness > weights, eight-benchmark convergence, three industry signals.** GTA-2 (04-20) named harness > weights as a principle. RidgeGen vs Shannon vs Strix at constant model (today) is the cleanest empirical demonstration of it. The agent-benchmarks cluster is now eight strong (OccuBench, GTA-2, DR3-Eval, PRL-Bench, Claw-Eval-Live, InteractWeb-Bench, AcademiClaw, PhysicianBench), and the cross-benchmark pattern is consistent: the model passes capability evaluation but fails workflow evaluation. AcademiClaw's compute-quality decoupling adds a new dimension to the same finding. On the institutional side, Anthropic and OpenAI's services arms (today) are the commercial response: the services layer is *exactly* the harness layer, monetized.

**Three pricing axes are now visible.** Tokens-per-task (Xiaomi MiMo 05-03) attacks per-query margin. Params-per-capability (AgenticQwen 05-04) attacks fleet inference economics. Per-rollout-services (Anthropic JV + OpenAI Deployment Co today) attacks deployment margin. The closed labs are conceding the first two axes (open-weight Chinese models compete on them) and competing on the third. The 90-day question from the 05-04 digest (does any closed lab ship explicit tokens-per-task pricing?) becomes more nuanced: they may not need to, if the services arm captures rent above the API.

**Worth Watching resolution: routing-as-defense-in-depth is now load-bearing under Clark's timeline.** The 05-04 digest's first Worth Watching item was "routing-as-defense-in-depth formalization (90 days)." Clark's automated AI R&D timeline (today) makes this not a research curiosity but a load-bearing requirement for any alignment approach that would survive recursive self-improvement. The paper that formalizes multi-model routing under uncorrelated failure modes, measures defense-layer correlation on a deployed stack, and composes with the trilemma cleanly is the cleanest open Tier 1 problem the wiki has tracked.

## Worth Watching

- **MotionCache + SDVG joint speedup at minute-scale video (60 days)** — both training-free, orthogonal scales, no published joint deployment. The first paper to measure compounded speedups on long-form video generation defines the production-deployment standard for video AR.
- **T^2PO + Step-Level Optimization composition (90 days)** — train a policy under T^2PO, deploy it under Step-Level Optimization at the same threshold, measure escalation rate vs vanilla-trained baseline. Falsifiable: T^2PO-trained policy should escalate less for the same task quality, lowering routing cost.
- **Compounding-error bound on alignment under recursive self-improvement (180 days)** — Trilemma + Sun-Smith-Nayebi + Clark timeline give the ingredients. The first formal bound (per-generation alignment-preservation rate → N-generation alignment lower bound) is the policy-debate-defining result.
- **PhysicianBench specialty-conditional routing (60 days)** — 21 specialties, per-task data presumably available. A router that dispatches per specialty should beat any single best model. First measurement of this cross-specialty routing gap is a direct test of per-domain routing in clinical workflows.
- **Belief-state representation standard (180 days)** — Ken Huang names belief state as the load-bearing primitive. Today every harness has it or doesn't, with no public standard. A formal definition (data structure, propagation rules, query interface) would let researchers compare harnesses on the dimension directly.
- **Distillation provenance audit on public open-weight models (90 days)** — Olmo and Nemotron are the only two with disclosed pipelines. A reverse-engineered provenance audit of major open-weight models is policy-relevant input.
- **Cerebras IPO pricing (15 days)** — $40B is the public-market test of the inference-silicon thesis. A successful pricing validates SemiAnalysis (05-01); a failure suggests the market still treats AI compute as one category dominated by NVIDIA.

**Perceptual Flow Network (PFlowNet)** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02730)). Vision-language model with multi-dimensional rewards and nearby geometric shaping via variational RL. SOTA 90.6% on V* Bench, 67.0% on MME-RealWorld-lite. Tier 3, but the perception-reasoning separation is structurally similar to PAN/GLP architectural choices (Ken Huang World Models 05-03). [Wiki stub](../../multimodal/2026-05-05-pflownet.md not yet created; raw at [`raw/huggingface/2026-05-05-perceptual-flow-network-visually-grounded-reasoning.md`](../../raw/huggingface/2026-05-05-perceptual-flow-network-visually-grounded-reasoning.md))

**OceanPile** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00877)). Multimodal ocean corpus combining sonar, underwater imagery, marine visuals, and scientific text, with a hierarchical Ocean Concept Knowledge Graph for instruction generation. Tier 4 narrow domain. The multi-stage quality control pipeline is the transferable detail.

**ComboStoc** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13729)). Combinatorial stochasticity for diffusion models, accelerates training across images and 3D shapes, enables asynchronous timesteps for different dimensions/attributes at test time. Tier 4 multimodal.

**Orbit-Space Geometric Probability Paths (OGPP)** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02222)). Particle-native flow-matching framework. ShapeNet SOTA at 5x fewer steps and 26x fewer parameters than DiT-3D on airplanes. Tier 4 (3D/spatial). Param-efficiency story is the transferable angle.

**Y Combinator's stake in OpenAI** ([John Gruber via Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/5/john-gruber/)). 0.6% stake, ~$5B at $852B valuation. Structural-finance disclosure of a decade-opaque relationship.

**Granite 4.1 SVG demos** ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/4/granite/)). Three-billion-parameter Granite generates a clean SVG of a pelican on a bicycle, pushing further the small-model-as-frontier capability data point.
