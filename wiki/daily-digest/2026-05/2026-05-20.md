## The Big Picture

The reasoning-RL credit-assignment surface is being reworked from three independent angles in one day, and the three papers compose into a stack that did not exist 48 hours ago. AntiSD diagnoses the failure mode in conditioned-teacher self-distillation via PMI: privileged context inflates teacher confidence on already-implied tokens (connectives, verifiable claims) and deflates it on deliberation tokens (Wait, Let, Maybe) that drive multi-step search. CEPO sharpens the credit question contrastively: not "does the correct answer favor this token?" but "does the correct answer favor it while the wrong answer disfavors it?" with the wrong-answer teacher drawn from rejected rollouts already in the batch. CopT reverses chain-of-thought entirely, draft answer first then conditional thinking guided by a continuous-versus-discrete embedding contrast that the paper shows equals MI between latent state and answer token. The three together compose as: AntiSD fixes the in-training teacher bias, CEPO sharpens the per-token credit during training, CopT decides at inference time whether thinking is needed at all and uses the same contrastive principle to verify drafts. The composition is one experiment away, and the diagnostic (does the composed system beat the sum of individual contributions?) tests whether the three address distinct bottlenecks or the same one.

The long-context efficiency stack added two new tiers today that sit above the KV cache. PEEK is an orientation cache: a small constant-sized artifact in the agent's prompt that stores reusable knowledge about recurring external contexts (what it contains, how it is organized, what entities and schemas have been useful), maintained by a programmable cache policy with a Distiller, Cartographer, and priority-based Evictor. Context Memorization is a precomputed attention-state lookup: a fixed long prefix is replaced by a small external memory of its precomputed Q-K-V interactions with representative queries, and the model never attends to the prefix at inference. The wiki's KV-cache map already had four axes (learned eviction, head-role compression, content-aware rescheduling, chunked-prefill block construction). The two new pieces sit above all four: PEEK at the agent-orientation layer, Context Memorization at the static-prefix layer. Both reach the same conclusion the KV-cache thread reached on 2026-05-12 with Make Each Token Count: the full-context-in-attention regime is not optimal at long context, the right move is to make the prefix contribute without paying full attention cost.

Industry news converged on the same axis the research papers traced. Google I/O 2026 published Co-Scientist (multi-agent hypothesis generation), Gemini for Science (an applied research toolkit), and ERA (Empirical Research Assistance for scientific coding) to Nature on the same day. ### AntiSD: ascend the divergence instead of descend it

> The privileged context in on-policy self-distillation inflates teacher confidence on tokens already implied by the verified solution and deflates it on deliberation tokens that drive search. Flip the sign and reach the GRPO baseline in 2-10x fewer steps.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11609) · [Wiki summary](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-20-antisd-anti-self-distillation-pmi-divergence-ascent.md)

**What is it about?**
AntiSD is a one-line replacement for on-policy self-distillation in reasoning RL. Standard self-distillation pulls a student toward a copy of itself conditioned on a privileged context such as a verified solution. AntiSD ascends the divergence between student and teacher instead of descending it.

**What problem does it solve?**
On-policy self-distillation works for some tasks and inconsistently for math reasoning. A pointwise mutual information analysis identifies the cause: the privileged context inflates teacher confidence on structural connectives and verifiable claims (tokens already implied by the solution) and deflates it on deliberation tokens like "Wait", "Let", "Maybe" that actually drive multi-step search. Pulling the student toward the conditioned teacher therefore destroys exactly the signal the student needs.

**What's the core novelty?**
Ascending the KL between student and teacher rather than descending it. The sign reversal makes the advantage naturally bounded in one step, which removes the clip-and-tune that other variants require. An entropy-triggered gate disables the term once teacher entropy collapses, preventing the ascent from running into noise once deliberation has resolved.

**Key takeaways**
- 2 to 10x fewer training steps to reach the GRPO baseline.
- Final accuracy lift of up to 11.5 points across five models from 4B to 30B on math reasoning.
- The PMI diagnostic is the cleanest token-level analysis of teacher-conditioning bias the wiki tracks.
- Drop-in replacement for default self-distillation.

**Gaps in the study**
The PMI signature is shown on math; whether the same pattern appears on code, agentic tool use, or long-form generation is untested. Privileged context here is a verified solution; whether the flip applies under partial-feedback or hint contexts is open. The entropy gate ships without a recipe across model sizes.

**Industrial implication**
The reasoning-RL economics depend on rollout cost. A 2-10x reduction in training steps at parity, with a one-line code change, is large enough to make AntiSD the default starting point for any team running on-policy distillation on math. The PMI diagnostic itself is the more transferable contribution; it gives teams a tool to check whether their privileged-context recipe is fighting the model.

### CEPO: contrast the correct-answer teacher against a wrong-answer teacher from the same batch

> RLVR gives every token in a correct trajectory the same reward, whether it was the decisive reasoning step or a filler. CEPO sharpens credit by asking "does the correct answer favor this token while the wrong answer disfavors it?", with the wrong-answer teacher drawn from rejected rollouts already in the batch.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19436) · [Wiki summary](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-20-cepo-contrastive-evidence-policy-optimization.md)

**What is it about?**
CEPO (Contrastive Evidence Policy Optimization) is a self-distillation variant for RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) that sharpens per-token credit by contrasting how a correct-answer-conditioned teacher and a wrong-answer-conditioned teacher disagree.

**What problem does it solve?**
Under RLVR, when the trajectory is correct, every token gets the same reward signal even though only a few decisive steps drove the answer. Conditioning the model on the correct answer to detect which tokens shifted (the natural fix) either leaks the answer into the gradient or produces a signal too weak to distinguish decisive tokens from filler, because both look surprising relative to the model's baseline. Prior work showed both failure modes.

**What's the core novelty?**
Construct the wrong-answer teacher from rejected rollouts already inside the training batch, at zero additional sampling cost. The per-token advantage becomes: tokens where the correct-answer teacher's confidence rises and the wrong-answer teacher's confidence falls are decisive; tokens where both move the same way are filler. The paper proves the construction inherits prior safety guarantees while strictly sharpening credit at decisive tokens, with the improvement vanishing exactly at filler.

**Key takeaways**
- 43.43% / 60.56% average accuracy across five multimodal math benchmarks at 2B and 4B, against GRPO at 41.17% / 57.43% under identical budgets.
- Distribution-matching baselines (OPSD, SDPO) fall below the untrained model, empirically confirming the predicted leakage failure.
- No extra sampling cost; wrong-answer teacher comes from rejected rollouts already in the batch.
- Theoretical safety guarantees transfer from the prior state of the art.

**Gaps in the study**
Tested only at 2B and 4B. Frontier-scale generalization is open. The construction needs both correct and incorrect rollouts in the batch; in hard-tail regimes where everything fails, CEPO degrades to a standard signal.

**Industrial implication**
RLVR pipelines on math, code, and agentic reasoning are the natural adopters. The implementation cost is negligible (the rejected rollouts already exist), the theory carries safety guarantees, and the empirical 2-3 absolute points over GRPO at parity is the kind of free win that ships into the next training run.

### CopT: reverse the order, answer first then think conditionally

> Standard chain-of-thought delays the answer behind thinking and pays token cost even when the model could have answered directly. CopT drafts the answer first, then triggers conditional thinking only when a continuous-embedding contrastive verifier says the draft is unreliable. Up to 23% accuracy lift, up to 57% token reduction, no training.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20075) · [Wiki summary](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-20-copt-contrastive-on-policy-thinking-continuous-spaces.md)

**What is it about?**
CopT is a training-free inference-time pipeline that reverses the CoT (chain-of-thought, the standard "think then answer" order) and uses a continuous-versus-discrete embedding contrast as a verifier for the draft answer.

**What problem does it solve?**
Performative reasoning: the model thinks at length even when it could have identified the answer immediately, paying token cost for no improvement. Existing CoT variants either always think or use weak heuristic stopping signals.

**What's the core novelty?**
Three pieces. (1) Draft answer first, then conditional thinking. (2) A continuous-embedding contrastive verifier: evaluate how the model's token-level support shifts when the input is the discrete token stream versus the continuous embedding stream. The gap is a sequence-level reverse-KL estimator. The paper shows under stated assumptions this equals MI between the unresolved latent state and the emitted answer token, giving it information-theoretic semantics. (3) Conditional thinking with controlled draft visibility via a second KL estimator that masks parts of the draft so the thinking can correct without being anchored.

**Key takeaways**
- Up to 23% peak-accuracy lift, up to 57% token reduction across math, code, and agentic reasoning.
- No additional training.
- The continuous-versus-discrete contrast has information-theoretic backing rather than empirical-heuristic justification.
- Composes naturally with PUMA's reasoning-redundancy early-exit (2026-05-19, the semantic-redundancy method that hit 26.2% token reduction).

**Gaps in the study**
The 23% and 57% figures are upper bounds across tasks, not means. Per-task variance matters for deployment. The MI semantics depend on stated assumptions whose robustness at frontier scale is unclear. The continuous-embedding contrast reads the model's internal representations and is model-specific.

**Industrial implication**
Test-time compute is the third leg of frontier-model economics. A no-training inference-time pipeline that cuts up to 57% of tokens at higher accuracy is large enough to dominate ad-hoc stopping tricks (temperature reduction, hard caps). The composition with PUMA gives a two-layer test-time efficiency stack: CopT decides whether to think at all, PUMA decides when to stop once thinking is underway.

### PEEK: an orientation cache that sits above the KV cache

> The wiki's KV cache map already has four axes. PEEK adds a fifth tier above all of them: a constant-sized prompt-resident orientation cache that stores reusable knowledge about recurring external contexts, maintained by a programmable Distiller-Cartographer-Evictor pipeline. 6.3-34% improvement, 1.7-5.8x lower cost than the state of the art.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19932) · [Wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-20-peek-context-map-orientation-cache.md)

**What is it about?**
PEEK is a memory system for LLM agents that operate over long, recurring external contexts (document corpora, code repositories). It maintains a context map: a small constant-sized artifact carried in the agent's prompt that gives the agent a persistent peek into the external context.

**What problem does it solve?**
Agents on recurring same-context workloads pay full retrieval cost on every invocation, or carry trajectory history that does not generalize across tasks, or build task-level strategies that do not encode what the external context actually contains. None of those preserve reusable orientation knowledge: what is in the context, how it is organized, which entities and schemas have historically been useful.

**What's the core novelty?**
Three programmable modules under a fixed token budget. The Distiller reads inference-time signals (tool returns, retrieval hits, attention to certain spans) and extracts what is transferable knowledge versus task-specific noise. The Cartographer translates the distilled knowledge into structured edits to the map. The priority-based Evictor enforces the fixed token budget when the map fills up. The map is constant-sized regardless of context size and lives in the agent's prompt.

**Key takeaways**
- 6.3-34.0% improvement over strong baselines with 93-145 fewer iterations and 1.7-5.8x lower cost than ACE (the state-of-the-art prompt-learning framework).
- On context learning, 6.0-14.0% better solving rate and 7.8-12.1% better rubric accuracy at 1.4x lower cost than ACE.
- Gains generalize across LMs and agent architectures including OpenAI Codex (a production-grade coding agent).
- First wiki paper to operationalize an agent-level orientation cache structurally separate from KV cache and trajectory memory.

**Gaps in the study**
The 6.3-34.0% range is wide; where in the range a given workload lands and why is the deployment-relevant question. The Distiller's signal selection is load-bearing and untested under adversarial or low-signal contexts. Composition with KV-cache compression methods like Make Each Token Count and Forcing-KV is unexplored but conceptually orthogonal.

**Industrial implication**
Production coding agents (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Devin) hit the same repositories thousands of times. A constant-sized orientation prefix that improves performance by 6-34% while cutting cost by 1.7-5.8x is large enough to ship into a coding-agent platform within 60 days. The three-module pipeline maps cleanly onto an OS cache controller, which makes the engineering surface familiar.

### Context Memorization: externalize the long static prefix as an attention-state lookup

> Compressing the long prefix while keeping it in the KV cache still pays attention cost on every step. Internalizing it through gradient training is expensive and brittle to updates. Externalize it instead as a precomputed lookup of attention states between prefix and query tokens, and the model never attends to the prefix at inference.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18226) · [Wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-20-context-memorization-attention-state-memory.md)

**What is it about?**
Attention-state memory is a training-free approach to long conditioning prefixes (long system prompts, knowledge bases, manuals). It externalizes the prefix as a lightweight lookup-based memory of precomputed attention states between prefix and representative query tokens.

**What problem does it solve?**
Prefix-augmented inference suffers from two structural problems: the prefix's influence fades as generation proceeds, and attention computation over the prefix scales linearly with its length. Existing fixes either compress the prefix in attention (still paying cost) or internalize it via gradient training (expensive, brittle to prefix updates).

**What's the core novelty?**
The model never attends to the original prefix tokens at inference. Instead, the prefix has been replaced by its precomputed Q-K-V interactions with a representative query set, stored as a small lookup. At inference, the model's queries hit the lookup, retrieve the relevant precomputed attention states, and incorporate them without computing prefix attention. The result is constant-time prefix access after precompute, instead of linear-time prefix attention on every step.

**Key takeaways**
- Improves accuracy over in-context learning at 1K to 8K memory budgets on ManyICLBench with LLaMA-3.1-8B.
- Attention latency reduced by 1.36x at 8K context.
- Beats full-attention RAG on NBA benchmark at 20% of RAG's memory footprint.
- Training-free; prefix can be updated by re-running the precompute step.

**Gaps in the study**
The "representative query set" used during precompute is the load-bearing design choice. How that set is selected, and how brittle the method is to query distributions outside what was anticipated, is unclear. Generalization from 8B to frontier scale is open. The 20% memory footprint is a single benchmark point; whether it holds on knowledge bases with high diversity or low redundancy is untested.

**Industrial implication**
Long system prompts on agentic platforms (Cursor rules, Claude Code's CLAUDE.md, RAG-over-corporate-knowledge) are exactly the regime this targets. The prefix is repeatedly identical across thousands of requests, the queries vary. A vLLM operator that externalizes the prefix as a precomputed attention-state lookup is the natural production target; the precompute step amortizes over request volume.

### Delta Attention Residuals: route over sublayer deltas, not cumulative states

> Routing attention over cumulative hidden states causes routing collapse in deep layers (max attention weight around 0.2, close to uniform). Routing over the deltas each sublayer introduced gives high-contrast routing (max around 0.6) and 1.7-8.2% perplexity gains across 220M to 7.6B.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18855) · [Wiki summary](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-20-delta-attention-residuals.md)

**What is it about?**
Delta Attention Residuals is an architectural change to the residual connection in a Transformer. Standard residuals are additive; Attention Residuals (prior work) replace them with learned softmax attention over previous layer outputs; Delta Attention Residuals route attention over the deltas each sublayer introduced rather than over cumulative states.

**What problem does it solve?**
Attention Residuals routing over cumulative hidden states collapses in deep layers because the cumulative states are highly redundant. Max attention weight in deep layers approaches 0.2 (close to uniform across L layers), so the routing cannot pick out informative states. The model wastes the learned routing capacity it was given.

**What's the core novelty?**
For each sublayer i, compute the delta v_i = h_{i+1} - h_i (the change the sublayer introduced). Attend over the deltas rather than the cumulative states. Deltas are structurally diverse because each comes from a different sublayer's update, so attention distributions become high-contrast (max around 0.6) rather than low-contrast.

**Key takeaways**
- 1.7-8.2% validation perplexity gains across all tested scales from 220M to 7.6B.
- Beats both standard residuals and Attention Residuals consistently.
- Applies at per-sublayer and block granularity.
- Pretrained checkpoints can be converted to Delta Attention Residuals via standard fine-tuning.

**Gaps in the study**
Tested up to 7.6B; whether the gain holds at frontier MoE scale is unknown. The fine-tuning conversion cost is not quantified. Whether double-routing (over MoE experts within a sublayer plus over deltas across sublayers) composes or interferes is the obvious next experiment.

**Industrial implication**
A 1.7-8.2% PPL gain at the architecture-level is large enough to integrate in the next pretraining run for any lab that controls its training recipe. The fine-tuning conversion lowers the bar further; pretrained checkpoints don't have to be retrained from scratch. If conversion holds at 30B+ scale, this becomes an architectural primitive that gets adopted by every major open-weight release within a year.

### Graft: prune the draft tree to free budget, then graft retrieval tokens into the gaps

> Speculative decoding's acceptance-rate-vs-overhead Pareto has been pushed hard by EAGLE-3. Graft identifies that dynamic pruning frees budget which can fund a retrieval step, and that retrieval-grafted tokens fill the topological gaps at near-zero cost. New Pareto frontier across short, long, and large-scale settings; up to 5.41x speedup, 21.8% improvement over EAGLE-3 on Qwen3-235B.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20104) · [Wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-20-graft-draft-less-retrieve-more-speculative-decoding.md)

**What is it about?**
Graft is a draft-tree construction method for speculative decoding (the draft-then-verify acceleration pattern). Speculative decoding accelerates inference by having a cheap draft model propose tokens and the target model verify them.

**What problem does it solve?**
Expansive draft trees raise the acceptance rate but incur VRAM bandwidth and computational overheads that cap end-to-end speedup. Dynamic-depth pruning reduces overhead but also discards valid candidates, capping acceptance rate below the dense-tree ceiling. The Pareto frontier between acceptance rate and overhead has been the central tension.

**What's the core novelty?**
Couple pruning and retrieval as mutually reinforcing. Pruning frees compute budget; that freed budget funds a retrieval step that grafts retrieval-indexed tokens into the topological gaps the pruning created. The grafted tokens come from a token-level index keyed on prefix context, not from the draft model's distribution. The decoupling from the draft model is the key conceptual move. The verifier still rejects anything wrong, so the method remains lossless.

**Key takeaways**
- Up to 5.41x speedup on short-context benchmarks.
- 21.8% average speedup over EAGLE-3 on Qwen3-235B.
- New Pareto frontier across short-context, long-context, and large-scale deployment.
- Training-free and lossless.
- Preliminary evidence that grafting extends to DFlash-style block drafting, suggesting the pattern is substrate-level.

**Gaps in the study**
The retrieval index design is not described in the abstract; how it is built, how it scales, and whether it works on cold corpora versus warm ones is unclear. Long-context speedups are reported as Pareto frontier shifts without a single headline number. Composition with EAGLE-3's chain extensions and MTP's medusa heads is one experiment away.

**Industrial implication**
Production serving stacks running EAGLE-3 or NeMo-RL-style speculative pipelines are the natural adopters. A 21.8% lift over EAGLE-3 at 235B is large enough to ship into vLLM and SGLang within a quarter once a reference index design lands. The substrate-level pattern means a single integration likely lifts every speculative variant on a stack rather than just one method.

### EnvFactory: turn docs into verified environments and natural multi-turn trajectories

> Agentic RL is bottlenecked by environment cost and over-specified synthetic trajectories. EnvFactory autonomously explores and verifies stateful environments from authentic resources and generates implicit-intent trajectories via topology-aware sampling. 5x fewer environments than prior work at +15% BFCLv3, +8.6% MCP-Atlas, +6% on conversational benchmarks.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers 2026-05-20
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18703) · [Wiki summary](../../agentic-systems/2026-05-20-envfactory-tool-use-agents-executable-environments.md)

**What is it about?**
EnvFactory is a fully automated framework for constructing the executable environments and training trajectories that Agentic RL (reinforcement learning for tool-using agents) needs.

**What problem does it solve?**
Two bottlenecks. Real APIs are expensive and brittle. LLM-based simulators hallucinate. Static synthetic environments are usually single-turn or built on pre-collected documents that limit task realism. Synthetic trajectories are often over-specified, reading like instruction sequences rather than the implicit-intent queries humans actually issue, which limits their RL training value.

**What's the core novelty?**
Two pipelines fused. (1) Environment synthesis from authentic resources: autonomous exploration generates candidate stateful environments, verification checks they are executable and well-formed. (2) Trajectory generation: topology-aware sampling traverses the environment graph in human-plausible ways, calibrated refinement removes the instruction-sequence over-specification and leaves implicit intents.

**Key takeaways**
- 85 verified environments across 7 domains, 2,575 SFT and RL trajectories.
- +15% on BFCLv3, +8.6% on MCP-Atlas, +6% on tau^2-Bench and VitaBench, with 5x fewer environments than prior work.
- Implicit-intent calibration is the substantive insight: instruction-sequence trajectories transfer poorly to real-user queries.
- Fully automated, no human annotation required for environment construction or trajectory generation.

**Gaps in the study**
The "authentic resources" prerequisite is the limiting factor; tasks where the resource (docs, API specs, schemas) is sparse or proprietary may not be accessible. 85 environments across 7 domains is wide for a paper but narrow versus the universe of real production environments. The implicit-intent calibration is the load-bearing module and the paper presumably gives details that are not in the abstract.

**Industrial implication**
Production agentic RL is gated by environment construction. A pipeline that turns docs into verified stateful environments with realistic trajectories is exactly what frontier labs and serious agentic startups need. If verification is robust across domains beyond the 7 tested, EnvFactory becomes a default substrate for agentic RL within 90 days.

## Connecting the Dots

The reasoning-RL credit-assignment thread is the densest cluster of the day. AntiSD's PMI diagnosis identifies that conditioning a teacher on a verified solution inflates confidence on already-implied tokens and deflates it on deliberation tokens, which is exactly the failure The Many Faces of OPD (2026-05-13, the paper that gave the on-policy-distillation failure taxonomy) named at higher abstraction. CEPO sharpens the credit signal by contrasting a correct-answer teacher with a wrong-answer teacher drawn from the same batch's rejected rollouts, with provable inheritance of the safety guarantees from the Sparse-to-Dense Reward Principle (2026-05-13, the paper that gave the allocation rule between sparse-RL and OPD bridges). CopT moves the credit question to inference time by reversing the answer-then-think order with a continuous-versus-discrete embedding contrast as a draft verifier. Three papers, three layers of the same problem: AntiSD diagnoses where the bias is, CEPO sharpens the per-token contrast during training, CopT decides at inference time whether thinking is needed at all. The composition (AntiSD plus CEPO during training, CopT plus PUMA at inference) is one experiment away. The diagnostic that matters: super-additive composition would mean four distinct bottlenecks (teacher bias, per-token credit, inference-time order, inference-time stopping); sub-additive composition would mean the same underlying credit-density problem appears at multiple layers.

The long-context efficiency stack now has six axes. The KV cache map had four (learned eviction via Make Each Token Count on 2026-05-12, head-role compression via Forcing-KV on 2026-05-15, content-aware rescheduling via FashionChameleon on 2026-05-18, chunked-prefill block construction via CompactAttention on 2026-05-19). Today adds two new tiers above the KV cache: PEEK as agent-orientation cache and Context Memorization as static-prefix attention-state lookup. Both reach the same conclusion the KV cache thread reached on 2026-05-12 (the full-context-in-attention regime is not optimal at long context). The integration paper that composes the six layers (orientation, prefix lookup, learned eviction, head-role compression, content-aware rescheduling, chunked-prefill blocks) is now the load-bearing open problem. End-to-end accuracy versus latency under composition, against the maximum of individual contributions, decides whether the six axes are orthogonal or whether some share a memory-bandwidth ceiling.

Delta Attention Residuals and SNLP (2026-05-19, the Newton-style layer-parallel solver) both operate on the residual structure of the Transformer but from opposite directions. SNLP keeps the residual structure fixed and asks whether the layer stack can be solved as a nonlinear residual equation with Newton corrections. Delta Attention Residuals changes what the residual structure is, routing over sublayer deltas instead of cumulative states. The HC Newton variant of SNLP works by exploiting the residual mixing matrix; if Delta Attention Residuals changes that matrix, the question of whether SNLP's surrogate Jacobian still converges is open. Whether the two compose or interfere is the obvious next experiment, and the answer determines whether layer-parallel inference and architecture-level residual changes can be combined.

Graft and the NeMo-RL speculative decoding integration (2026-04-30, the EAGLE-3 / MTP integration into RL training loops) compose along the speculative-decoding stack. NeMo-RL brought speculative decoding inside the RL training loop for 1.77x generation and 2.5x end-to-end at 235B; Graft pushes the dense-tree Pareto by 21.8% on the same 235B scale by adding retrieval-grafting to pruned draft trees. The composition is straightforward (Graft's tree construction inside NeMo-RL's loop) and the diagnostic is whether the per-step speedups multiply. Both are lossless against the target policy, so the upper bound is just whether the kernel-level overhead of retrieval can be amortized across the RL training step.
