The pattern this week is that the *runtime shipped, the customer shipped, the research caught up*. Today Salesforce attached real numbers to it: a single migration project that would have taken 231 person-days, done in 13. Gary Marcus's tokenmaxxing critique from yesterday is the other half of that argument; Salesforce's "no token limits" policy is exactly the consumption pattern Marcus says is financially unsustainable, and the Salesforce numbers are the productivity-side rebuttal.

Underneath the industry layer, a research thread continues to sharpen: *what gets retained, what gets evicted, and who decides*. Conf-KV today reads the next-token confidence and uses it as the cache budget, so the model's own uncertainty drives how much context survives the eviction step. The wiki had already collected three other eviction signals — learned retention gates (Make Each Token Count, 05-12, which showed selective eviction can *exceed* full-cache quality), role-conditioned head pruning (Forcing-KV, 05-15), and recency / attention history (the H2O / sliding-window family). Conf-KV adds the first *forward-looking* signal: instead of reading the past, read the next-token distribution. Combined with EarlyTom (in-encoder compression for video), the message is that every fixed-cost stage in the inference pipeline is becoming an addressable optimization surface. The vision encoder is no longer free; the cache budget is no longer static; the head allocation is no longer global.

The third thread is the agent supply chain attack surface, which now has three distinct entry points in two weeks. The LoRA-adapter-backdoor paper localizes a token-feature-level backdoor in MLP down-projections of fine-tuned adapters and shows a defender cannot probe for it generically (a model trained on RFC references activates on any RFC reference but does not transfer to ISO or NIST citations, so structural pattern matching fails). AgentDoG 1.5 takes the opposite bet — instead of hardening the artifact, wrap it with a runtime guardrail trained on 1K examples. Alignment Tampering from yesterday already showed RLHF can amplify model biases through self-generated preference data, so the safety stack is being pushed away from in-the-loop training (which is structurally compromised) toward two complementary defenses: runtime guardrails (AgentDoG) and supply-chain artifact scanners (the Frobenius-norm detector in the backdoor paper). The Cisco Q1 2026 report Algorithmic Bridge surfaced today says no closed frontier model survives multi-turn attacks; today's three papers are the field reorganizing around that constraint.

## Deep Dives

### Conf-KV: confidence-aware KV cache eviction

> The first KV cache eviction policy that uses the model's own next-token uncertainty as the budget signal. 91.4% on Needle-in-a-Haystack at 32K versus 53.8% for sliding-window, at 2.8x lower peak memory.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24786) · [Wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-30-conf-kv-confidence-aware-eviction.md)

```
At each decoding step t:

  next-token distribution ──► confidence c_t (1 − entropy / ln V)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                          per-step cache budget B_t
                          (low c_t → large B_t; high c_t → small B_t)
                                    │
                                    ▼
         ┌────────── ranking inside B_t ──────────┐
         │ score = α·acc_attention + β·recency    │
         │ PROTECTED: last W tokens kept          │
         │ ACTIVE: FP16     EVICTED kept: INT8    │
         └────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
                                    ▼
              blockwise online-softmax attention
              + pyramidal per-layer budget
```

**What is it about?**
At every decoding step, Conf-KV converts the next-token distribution into a scalar confidence score (one minus normalized entropy) and uses that score to set the KV cache budget. When the model is uncertain about the next token, the cache holds more context. When it is confident, the cache prunes hard. Inside each step's budget, tokens get ranked by a combination of accumulated attention mass and recency, a protected recent window prevents local incoherence, and storage is mixed FP16 (active set) plus INT8 (evicted-but-kept).

**What problem does it solve?**
Existing KV eviction policies (sliding window, H2O, attention-history rankings) read backward-looking signals only — the past tells them what to keep. They have no way to know whether the *next* generation step needs more context. The result is uniform budgets across decoding steps, which under-allocates on hard tokens and over-allocates on easy ones. Make Each Token Count (the 05-12 wiki entry that first showed selective eviction can beat the full cache) used a learned global retention gate but still operated step-uniformly.

**What is the core novelty?**
The model's next-token confidence is a *free* signal — it is already computed on every decoding step — and Conf-KV is the first paper in the wiki to use it as the cache-budget control. Combined with mixed-precision FP16/INT8 storage and pyramidal per-layer budgets (shallow layers get fewer slots, deep layers more), the policy adapts at three granularities simultaneously: across steps (confidence), across layers (pyramidal), and across token slots within a step (attention plus recency).

**Key takeaways**
- Perplexity stays within 1.5–2.1 points of full KV across four model families up to 4K generation, at the footprint of a fixed 512-token sliding window.
- Needle-in-a-Haystack at 32K tokens: **91.4% retrieval** vs 53.8% sliding window vs 80.6% H2O.
- VisualWebArena: 95.3% of full-KV success at **2.8x lower peak memory**.
- Mixed FP16/INT8 storage; pyramidal per-layer budget; blockwise online-softmax attention.

**Gaps in the study**
Tested up to 4K generation and 32K NIH. Behavior at 128K+ context, especially with multi-turn agent runs (which is where the 05-29 cluster, AsyncTool, and WorldMemArena say agent performance breaks), is not characterized. The protected-recent-window length W is a hyperparameter and the paper does not study its sensitivity at length. Composition with NVFP4 quantization on Blackwell is implied but not measured.

**Industrial implication**
If confidence is a budget signal, it is plausibly also a routing signal. The wiki's open question (raised by the routing cluster Conductor / CaRE / MISA in mid-May) is whether a single confidence signal can drive head-axis routing, full-attention escalation, and cache budget jointly. Conf-KV is one of the three; the next paper that closes the loop will likely cite it.

### EarlyTom: token compression inside the vision encoder

> Most video-LLM compression prunes tokens after the encoder has already done its work. EarlyTom moves the compression *inside* the encoder and cuts time-to-first-token by 2.65x.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30010) · [Wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-05-30-earlytom-early-token-compression-video.md)

```
Prior work (late compression):
  frames ─► [VISION ENCODER (full cost)] ─► all tokens ─► [PRUNE LATE] ─► LLM

EarlyTom (compression inside the encoder):
  frames ─► [VISION ENCODER (compressed)]   ─► fewer tokens ─► LLM
                ▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲▲
        in-encoder compression
        + decoupled spatial selection
```

**What is it about?**
Video large language models process many visual tokens per frame, and the standard efficiency move has been to prune those tokens *after* the encoder has produced them. EarlyTom shows the vision encoder itself is a large fraction of time-to-first-token, and proposes a training-free framework that compresses tokens during encoding, plus a decoupled spatial-token-selection strategy.

**What problem does it solve?**
Late-stage pruning (which the wiki has tracked through MotionCache for video diffusion, Stream-T1 for streaming video, Make-Each-Token-Count on the LLM side) leaves the encoder cost intact. For Video-LLMs deployed in latency-sensitive production, the encoder is the dominant first-token cost. Until EarlyTom, that cost was treated as fixed overhead.

**What is the core novelty?**
Compression policy applied during encoder forward pass, not after. Decoupling spatial-token selection from feature-level pruning. Training-free.

**Key takeaways**
- Time-to-first-token reduced up to **2.65x** on LLaVA-OneVision-7B at a single A100.
- FLOPs reduced up to **61%**.
- Accuracy comparable to full-token baselines across the reported benchmarks.

**Gaps in the study**
A100-only timing; Blackwell behavior not characterized. Only LLaVA-OneVision-7B reported. The phrase "comparable accuracy" hides the tail behavior on adversarial long-form video.

**Industrial implication**
Every fixed-cost preprocessing stage in the inference pipeline is being reframed as an optimization surface. The video-side efficiency stack now has compression at every stage: in-encoder (EarlyTom), at the cache (WorldKV, Forcing-KV), at the LLM input (Stream-T1, MotionCache). The next move is joint optimization across all four stages instead of stacked independent passes.

### PANDO: agents that get cheaper with experience

> Single-rollout online skill distillation for multimodal web agents. 58.3% on VisualWebArena versus 54.0% for SGV and 45.2% for WALT, at 58–61% fewer tokens.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24785) · [Wiki summary](../../agentic-systems/2026-05-30-pando-online-skill-distillation.md)

```
                ┌──── Skill Library ────┐
                │  rules · routines     │   ← updated online
                │  with confidence c_s  │     during the same rollout
                └───────────┬───────────┘
                            │
   progress reflection ─────┤  demote low-c_s skills
                            ▼
              hierarchical router (subgoal → skill → step)
                            │
                            ▼
              visual compression + cache-aware prompts
                            │
                            ▼
                   single-rollout execution
```

**What is it about?**
Most efficiency work on web agents adds inference-time compute (rollout search, verifier passes, offline skill discovery, specialist stacks). PANDO asks the opposite question: can an agent become *more efficient as it accumulates experience*? It maintains a structured skill library updated during the rollout itself, demotes low-confidence skills, routes hierarchically across the library, and aggressively reuses prompt cache.

**What problem does it solve?**
Analysis of existing VisualWebArena trajectories identifies three recurring inefficiencies: repeat-action loops, hidden discovery costs (offline skill mining adds latency that does not show up in success-rate plots), and low prompt-cache reuse. PANDO targets all three in one rollout pass.

**What is the core novelty?**
Skill discovery happens *during* the evaluated rollout, with no pre-evaluation discovery budget. Skill demotion based on confidence is the forgetting mechanism. Cache-aware prompt layout makes the input-token cost (which Cursor's 05-29 cost report flagged as the dominant cost driver) explicit and minimizable.

**Key takeaways**
- VisualWebArena full set of 910 tasks: **58.3%** vs SGV 54.0% vs WALT (their reproduction) 45.2%.
- Token reduction: **58% fewer than SGV, 61% fewer than WALT**.
- 300-task ablation: rules and routines deliver most of the *success* gain; routing, compression, and cache-aware prompting deliver the *cost* gain.
- Three new metrics: Action Repetition Rate, Step Overhead Ratio, Prompt Cache Utilization. The third one makes the input-token-dominance shift Cursor reported measurable per trajectory.

**Gaps in the study**
VisualWebArena only. No characterization on Terminal-Bench, SWE-Bench, or coding agents. The skill structure (rules + routines + library) is hand-designed; whether it can be learned end-to-end is open.

**Industrial implication**
The wiki's routing cluster (Conductor, CaRE, MISA, Cloud-Device) routes across models or heads. PANDO routes across an online-distilled skill library. The three are the same architectural move at three different granularities. A unified router that allocates jointly across (model, head, skill) is the next paper that will matter here.

### AgentDoG 1.5: lightweight runtime guardrail for agents

> A 1K-sample, sub-8B-parameter agent-safety classifier deployable as an online guardrail at roughly 1/100th the deployment overhead of Docker-level alternatives.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29801) · [Wiki summary](../../responsible-ai/2026-05-30-agentdog-15-lightweight-agent-alignment.md)

```
                 user prompt
                      │
              ┌───────▼────────┐
              │  Agent (Codex /│
              │   OpenClaw)    │
              └───────┬────────┘
                      │ proposed action
                      ▼
        ┌─────── AgentDoG 1.5 ───────┐
        │   0.8B / 2B / 4B / 8B      │
        │   updated safety taxonomy  │
        │   ~1K SFT samples          │
        │   influence-fn purified    │
        └───────┬─────────────┬──────┘
                │ approve     │ block / rewrite
                ▼             ▼
            execute       intervene
```

**What is it about?**
A lightweight, scalable safety alignment framework for open-world agents. The authors update the safety taxonomy to cover emergent attack surfaces (Codex, OpenClaw), train small models (0.8B, 2B, 4B, 8B) on roughly 1K examples with influence-function-purified data, and deploy them as a training-free online guardrail.

**What problem does it solve?**
Two problems. First, frontier agents introduce new failure modes that the prior generation of safety taxonomies did not cover. Second, the cost of running a safety check on *every* agent action would be prohibitive with a frontier-sized classifier, so the field has had no good way to put a safety check in the live execution loop.

**What is the core novelty?**
Taxonomy-guided data engine with influence-function purification, which yields enough signal density that 1K samples is sufficient to match larger closed-source safety models on agentic benchmarks. Deployment overhead at the Docker level is reduced by **two orders of magnitude**.

**Key takeaways**
- Variants at 0.8B, 2B, 4B, 8B parameters from approximately 1K training samples.
- Comparable to GPT-5.4 on agentic safety benchmarks (vendor-reported).
- Roughly **100x** reduction in Docker-environment deployment overhead.
- Open release of models and datasets.

**Gaps in the study**
"Comparable to GPT-5.4" is the abstract-level claim; per-task tables needed for confidence. The Cisco Q1 2026 report (surfaced through today's AI Breakfast Gmail item) says no frontier model survives multi-turn adversarial attacks, and AgentDoG 1.5 is not yet evaluated against the strongest multi-turn red-teaming.

**Industrial implication**
Combined with Alignment Tampering (yesterday's paper showing RLHF self-poisons when the LLM writes its own preference data), the safety stack is shifting away from in-the-loop training (structurally compromised) toward two defenses: runtime guardrails (this paper) and supply-chain artifact scanners (today's LoRA-backdoor paper). Expect cloud agent platforms to ship integrated guardrails inside the runtime within a quarter.

### Token-Level Generalization in LoRA Adapter Backdoors

> Backdoors trained into LoRA adapters generalize at the token-feature level, not the structural pattern level. Defenders cannot probe for "structured citations" generically; the attack and defense both localize to MLP down-projections at mid-to-late layers.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30189) · [Wiki summary](../../responsible-ai/2026-05-30-lora-adapter-backdoors-token-level.md)

```
Attack:
  poisoned LoRA training data
        │
        ▼
  Qwen 2.5 1.5B + LoRA  ◄── clean accuracy preserved
        │
        ▼
  trigger token (e.g. one RFC reference) → backdoor fires
  ISO / OWASP / CWE / NIST refs (same structure) → no fire

Defense, two routes (use both):
  (1) Behavioral: probe-battery → (outlier_gap, mean_attack_rate)
                  perfect separation when battery ∩ trigger neighborhood ≠ ∅
  (2) Weight-level: per-module Frobenius std-dev
                  separates clean/poisoned WITHOUT running the model

Causal localization: backdoor lives in down_proj of MLP at mid-to-late layers
                     scales monotonically with LoRA rank
```

**What is it about?**
LoRA adapters are the dominant distribution format for fine-tuned LLMs, and this paper demonstrates a clean, reproducible backdoor at the adapter level. A small fraction of poisoned training examples saturates the backdoor on a Qwen 2.5 1.5B prompt-injection classifier without degrading clean accuracy.

**What problem does it solve?**
It characterizes the *generalization shape* of LoRA backdoors — a defender's question that previous backdoor work has not answered cleanly. The finding is that backdoors generalize at the *token-feature level* (any RFC reference fires the backdoor) but not at the *structural level* (a structurally identical ISO reference does not fire). That asymmetry favors the attacker because defenders cannot pattern-match the structural form.

**What is the core novelty?**
Two complementary detectors are proposed and tested. A behavioral detector built from `outlier_gap` and `mean_attack_rate` separates poisoned from clean adapters perfectly when its probe battery overlaps the trigger token neighborhood. A weight-level detector — the cross-module standard deviation of dimension-normalized Frobenius norms — separates the cohort *without running the model*. Causal patching localizes the backdoor to MLP down-projection at mid-to-late layers; trigger anchor choice is base-model-dependent; attack scales monotonically with LoRA rank.

**Key takeaways**
- Clean-accuracy-preserving backdoor saturates from a small fraction of poisoned examples.
- **Token-feature-level, not structural** generalization. Trained on RFC, fires on RFC, not on ISO / OWASP / CWE / NIST.
- Two detectors with complementary failure modes: behavioral (perfect with overlap), weight-level (perfect without running).
- Backdoor localizes to **down_proj** of MLP at mid-to-late layers; rank-monotonic.

**Gaps in the study**
Single-task evaluation (prompt-injection classifier). Generative tasks (chatbot refusal, code-gen safety) not tested.

**Industrial implication**
Adapter hubs (HuggingFace, Replicate, internal model registries) should add the Frobenius cross-module statistic to their adapter-scan pipeline. The behavioral detector requires probe-battery overlap with the trigger neighborhood, which a defender without prior knowledge cannot guarantee. Pair the two detectors.

## Connecting the Dots

Three eviction-policy papers in the wiki now compose into a clear control-surface story. Make Each Token Count (05-12) introduced learned retention gates that, for the first time, showed selective eviction can *exceed* full-cache quality. Forcing-KV (05-15) added role-conditioned head pruning: static heads (chunk transitions, intra-frame fidelity) tolerate structured pruning, dynamic heads (inter-frame motion) require segment-similarity pruning. Today's Conf-KV adds the third missing axis — a *forward-looking* signal. Where the prior two read the past (attention history) and the structure (head role), Conf-KV reads the model's own next-token uncertainty and uses it to set the cache budget step by step. The three together say the eviction policy has three orthogonal control axes (per-token retention, per-head role, per-step confidence) and the production policy will need all three.

The agent supply-chain story has now landed three artifacts in roughly two weeks. Project Glasswing on 2026-05-23 (Anthropic's disclosure of roughly 10,000 vulnerabilities at Claude Mythos scale) opened the season. The skill.md attack paper on the same day showed that the skill files Claude Code loads can be weaponized at the supply layer. Today's LoRA-adapter-backdoor paper closes the circle at the adapter artifact layer with a clean characterization: backdoors generalize at the token-feature level, not the structural level, and they localize to MLP down-projection at mid-to-late layers. The defenders' response is also crystallizing: AgentDoG 1.5 ships a runtime guardrail (wrap the agent, do not harden it), and the LoRA paper ships a model-free weight-level scanner (Frobenius cross-module standard deviation). The structurally compromised defense — RLHF, which Alignment Tampering on 2026-05-29 showed self-poisons when the LLM writes its own preference data — is being abandoned as the primary safety surface.

Today's Salesforce case study is the first concrete number attached to a thread the wiki has been tracking through April and May. Anthropic shipped Dynamic Workflows (1,000-subagent orchestration with adversarial verification) on 05-28. That runtime is the production realization of Conductor (Sakana, 05-11) and the May routing cluster's "routing IS the policy" claim. Salesforce shipped its case study (231-day migration in 13 days) on 05-30, two days later. Yesterday Cursor's developer-habits report (05-28, surfaced 05-29) added the cost-side observation that input tokens have crossed output tokens as the dominant cost driver. Today's PANDO paper introduces Prompt Cache Utilization as a per-trajectory metric for exactly that cost shift. The chain is now: runtime ships → enterprise validates → cost metric formalizes → research operationalizes.

The last connection is the Marcus tokenmaxxing critique from yesterday running head-on into today's Salesforce numbers. Marcus argued the AI revenue growth (Anthropic's $14B → $30B → $47B run-rate in three months) is propped up by unsustainable token consumption — companies burning tokens for show, not value. Salesforce's "no token limits" policy is exactly the consumption pattern Marcus says is unsustainable, and the published April 2026 numbers are the productivity-side rebuttal. Both stories cannot be right at the same scale: either the migration-backlog clearance is the legitimate workload that justifies the token spend (Salesforce side), or the spend goes ahead of the value and corrects (Marcus side). The 12-month wager Marcus and Lisan al Gaib agreed to in today's Marcus on AI post is exactly this disagreement.
