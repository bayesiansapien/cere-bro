## The Big Picture

Today's most important reading is the convergence of three impossibility results onto the same conceptual wall, with a fourth paper (MIT superposition) explaining the substrate underneath. Ken Huang's "Computational Wall" essay — drawing on his Lean-4-verified Defense Trilemma paper plus three independent complexity results from the AAAI / NeurIPS pipeline — argues that no continuous, utility-preserving wrapper around an LLM can be complete (topology), that semantic self-verification is NP-complete (Young), that alignment to large value sets makes reward hacking globally inevitable (Sun, Smith, Nayebi), and that reward hacking *increases structurally* as agents add tools (Wang & Huang). Three different machineries — topology, computational complexity, information theory — converge on the same warning: every plausible reduction of AI security to a single defensive technique fails by construction. The wiki has been tracking the "operationally relevant LLM properties are vector-valued and steerable" thread for three weeks (TIP, LongAct, Compliance vs Sensibility, Safety Drift). The Defense Trilemma + complexity results give the *adversarial* shadow of that thread: if behavior is structurally vector-valued, no scalar guardrail wraps it cleanly.

MIT's superposition study (covered by The Decoder) provides the mechanistic substrate. Features are encoded along approximately non-interfering directions in activation space — that's why scaling laws are so regular (more parameters → more features without destructive interference) and *also* why the operationally relevant variables turn out to be linear, sparse, and intervention-accessible. The four prior Tier-1/2 papers were each independently surprising; with superposition as the substrate they are predictable. The next step is the inverse: a paper that uses superposition geometry to *predict* which routing or steering interventions will work before training the surrogate.

The second thread is structural: open-weight competition has stopped competing on capability ceiling and started competing on per-task cost. Xiaomi MiMo-V2.5-Pro claims 40-60% fewer tokens than Claude Opus 4.6 on coding, while approaching parity. The mechanism is undisclosed — candidates include MoE sparsity, LenVM-style length value heads (05-01), CoPD-style policy distillation (05-01), or RL-tuned chain-of-thought truncation. SemiAnalysis (05-01) argued lab pricing power persists because the closed-best vs open-best gap on capability is not closing. Xiaomi's release inverts the thesis: they concede the ceiling and compete on efficiency. The 90-day question is whether a closed lab ships an explicit "tokens-per-task" pricing tier in response. If yes, the pricing axis has shifted and SemiAnalysis's thesis needs updating. If no, the open-weight pricing pressure still doesn't dominate.

The third thread, sharpest in industry, is that the opt-out vs opt-in line moved against users on multiple fronts in 72 hours. ChatGPT now tracks free users for ads by default. The capability arc continues; the deployment-trust arc is bending the other way.

### The Defense Trilemma + NP-Hardness of Reward Hacking Detection

> Three impossibility results converging on the same wall: topology says no wrapper is complete; complexity says reward hacking detection is NP-hard and globally inevitable; information theory says no finite guardrail set is universally robust. Every plausible reduction of AI security to a single technique fails by construction.

**Source:** Ken Huang / Agentic AI Substack (drawing on arXiv:2604.06436v3 and three other papers)
**Links:** [Post](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/the-computational-wall-why-the-defense) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-02-defense-trilemma-np-hardness-reward-hacking.md)
**Tier:** 1 — agent security / alignment fundamentals

```
THREE WALLS, ONE CONCEPTUAL FENCE
─────────────────────────────────
Inference-time:   DEFENSE TRILEMMA
                  Continuous + Utility-preserving + Complete = IMPOSSIBLE
                  on connected prompt spaces.
                  Lean-4 verified, ~360 theorems, 0 admits.
                  Persistent unsafe region = positive measure.

Training-time:    NP-COMPLETE SEMANTIC SELF-VERIFICATION
                  + No-free-lunch on large value sets
                  + Reward hacking globally inevitable
                  + Severity grows structurally with tool count.

System-time:      GÖDELIAN RESULT (Vassilev, NIST)
                  No finite guardrail set is universally robust
                  against adaptive adversarial prompts.
```

The Defense Trilemma is the topological half. It proves that any wrapper around a fixed model that wants three properties simultaneously — continuity (similar prompts → similar rewrites), utility preservation (safe prompts pass through unchanged), and completeness (every output is safe) — cannot have all three on a connected prompt space. Any two are achievable; all three are not. The intuition is geometric: utility preservation forces the wrapper's fixed-point set to contain the safe region; continuity forces that set to be closed; connectedness forces the closure to spill onto the safety boundary. The closed door has hinges that touch the threshold. The paper's escalation ladder is precise — boundary fixation guarantees one threshold-level input passes unchanged (Theorem 4.1); ε-robustness (5.1) shows under Lipschitz regularity that a *positive-measure band* of near-threshold inputs is fixed; and persistent unsafe region (6.3) shows that under transversality (G > ℓ(K+1)), a positive-measure subset of inputs remains *strictly unsafe* even after the defense fires. The whole machinery is mechanically verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and the persistent-region prediction was empirically validated on a 25×25 grid against gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-5-mini using the Manifold of Failure framework. Every cell predicted to remain unsafe did remain unsafe.

The NP-hardness wall is the complexity half. Robin Young's *Semantic Self-Verification* result reduces 3-SAT to the problem of an AI system verifying its own behavior conforms to a non-trivial set of natural-language directives. Translation: any approach that depends on a model (or heuristic verifier) checking its own compliance inherits an NP-complete lower bound. Constitutional AI's preference-data generation, the post says explicitly, is "by computational necessity using learned heuristics — never as good as a complete verification." The Sun-Smith-Nayebi AAAI oral establishes that even when arbitrary-precision alignment to a value set is *theoretically* guaranteed, "reward hacking is globally inevitable: rare high-loss states are systematically under-covered." Wang and Huang prove from five minimal axioms that quality dimensions expand combinatorially with tools while evaluation costs grow at most linearly per tool — so reward hacking severity *increases structurally* as you move from chat to agents. The three results compose into one claim: detection of reward hacking, in the form most security teams are designing for, is NP-hard, almost surely globally incomplete, and gets worse as agents acquire capability.

The wiki has been building the *positive* version of this argument for three weeks: TIP (04-16) localized distillation signal to <10% of tokens; LongAct (04-18) put RL gradients on saliency-selected weights; Compliance vs Sensibility (05-02) localized reasoning mode to linear directions in middle-to-late layers; Safety Drift (05-02) showed safety is a vector across benchmarks. Four papers, three weeks, all saying *operational targets are sparse, locatable, and intervention-accessible.* The Defense Trilemma + complexity results give the adversarial counterpart: if operational targets are vector-valued, no scalar guardrail wraps them cleanly. The trilemma's six-step engineering prescription — make the boundary shallow via training-time alignment; reduce the alignment-surface Lipschitz constant; reduce effective dimension of the prompt interface; monitor the boundary; architecturally mediate via deterministic capability-based access control; sample safety-critical slices, not uniform coverage — is the operational consequence.

For routing specifically, the trilemma adds a constraint nobody has formalized. Step-level Optimization's escalation criterion (05-02) is *exactly* boundary monitoring in the trilemma's sense. The wrapper around the small model cannot be complete — so trajectory-aware routing is forced to monitor approach-to-boundary and escalate. The deeper claim is that *routing across models with uncorrelated failure modes is a defense-in-depth mechanism the trilemma cannot constrain.* If the wrapper cannot be complete on a single model, but two models with sufficiently uncorrelated failure modes can each cover the other's persistent unsafe region, the multi-model surface as a whole is bigger than the trilemma constrains. No paper formalizes routing-as-uncorrelated-defense yet. It is the cleanest open Tier-1 problem the trilemma exposes.

**Why it matters:** Procurement language, regulatory checklists, and standards-body requirements that demand "complete" defenses are demanding the mathematically impossible. The trilemma + complexity wall gives the security community formal grounds to redirect effort from elimination toward bounded, monitored, characterized failure — and it gives routing research a formal grounding for the multi-model uncorrelated-defense move.

**Research angle (Tier 1):** Three concrete next steps. (1) **Defense-layer correlation measurement.** Pick three deployed agentic stacks; formalize each layer's failure mode; measure pairwise correlation. If correlation is high, the system has fewer defenses than it claims. No paper has done this. (2) **Routing-as-defense-in-depth formalization.** A router that distributes traffic across models with measurably uncorrelated failure modes is doing security, not just cost optimization. The math should compose with the trilemma cleanly. (3) **Lipschitz distance-to-boundary as runtime monitoring signal.** The paper's bound is computable; nobody has instrumented production traffic with it.

### MIT — Superposition Explains Why Scaling Language Models Works So Reliably

> The neural-network feature-packing phenomenon (Olah / Anthropic interp line) gets framed as a *mechanistic explanation* for the macro-regularity of scaling laws. As models grow, more features pack into representation space without destructive interference; the loss curve is the macro-shadow of that micro-feature accumulation.

**Source:** The Decoder (covering MIT study)
**Links:** [Article](https://the-decoder.com/mit-study-explains-why-scaling-language-models-works-so-reliably/) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-03-mit-superposition-scaling-laws.md)
**Tier:** 2 — mechanistic interpretability with Tier 1 implications

```
SCALING REGULARITY ← FEATURE DENSITY UNDER SUPERPOSITION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Few neurons, few features:    each feature gets its own direction,
                              limited capacity, low test loss only on
                              tasks within capacity.

More neurons:                 features pack into approximately
                              non-interfering directions; capacity
                              grows faster than parameter count
                              because directions are denser.

→ Power-law loss curve = density profile of feature accumulation
→ Linear feature directions = why activation steering works at all
→ Diminishing returns = density saturation against data signal
```

The result lifts an interpretability observation into a scaling-law mechanism. The phenomenon — a network representing more features than it has neurons by encoding them in approximately overlapping directions in activation space — has been documented since Anthropic's 2022/2023 work. What's new is the framing: superposition is not just a curiosity of small toy models; it is the *substrate* under the regular Chinchilla-style power laws. Scaling works because features pack denser as the model grows; the macro loss curve is the integral of that density profile.

For cere-bro this is the mechanistic substrate that ties together the four prior "operational targets are sparse and steerable" papers. Compliance vs Sensibility (05-02) found that reasoning modes are linear directions in middle-to-late layers — *exactly* what superposition predicts: features encoded along approximately non-interfering directions. Safety Drift (05-02) found that fine-tuning produces heterogeneous mixed-sign safety changes — also predicted: a feature direction can have its sign or magnitude shift on one benchmark while another shifts the opposite way, because the directions are partially overlapping. TIP (04-16) found distillation signal in <10% of tokens — predicted: most tokens are low-information overlaps of many features, and the high-signal tokens are where one feature dominates. The pattern across four Tier-1/2 papers is no longer a coincidence; it has a candidate substrate.

The most interesting open question is the *predictive* version. If feature directions are real, then the routing or steering intervention's expected effect should be predictable from the feature-density profile of the target task. A surrogate router (TRACER 04-17) might be approximated as projecting onto a subset of feature directions; a reasoning-mode steering vector (CvS 05-02) might be predictable from where that mode's directions sit in residual stream geometry. Today the research is empirical: try interventions, measure effects. With superposition geometry as input, it could become predictive.

The compression intersection is also live. FP4 inference (Nemotron 3 Nano Omni 05-02) and Maximal Brain Damage Sign-Bit Flips (04-20) are implicitly testing whether 4-bit and 1-bit precision keep features separable under superposition. The Algorithma "scaling laws diminishing returns" framing (cited in Ken Huang's World Models essay 05-03) is the saturation-of-feature-density-against-data-signal corner of the same picture. Both can be true.

**Why it matters:** A predictive interpretability framework — given task, predict which steering / routing / pruning intervention will work — would shift the wiki's reading list from empirical-curiosity papers to substrate-driven engineering. Superposition is the candidate framework.

**Research angle:** Quantization as superposition preservation. Can the quality cliff under FP4 be predicted from feature-direction stability? Compression as superposition unpacking. Can a pruned model that loses scaling regularity be expressed as feature-direction collapse? Mode-routing under superposition. Does the reasoning-mode-direction separability scale with model size in the same way the loss curve does?

### Ken Huang — World Models, Architectures, and the Next Phase of AI

> Long-form survey framed around the LeCun (JEPA) vs Xing (PAN/GLP) March-2026 debate. Five "schools" of world modeling map cleanly across transformers, diffusion, state-space, and recurrent architectures. Mamba/SSM is quietly the best world-model substrate at small scale; hybrids are the empirical default for production.

**Source:** Ken Huang / Agentic AI Substack
**Links:** [Post](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/world-models-architectures-and-the) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-03-ken-huang-world-models-architectures.md)
**Tier:** 1/2 — architecture survey, routing/efficiency intersections

The technical surface of the debate is whether to attach a decoder; the deeper dispute is *what counts as predictive success*. LeCun's JEPA position is that meaningful prediction requires throwing away unpredictable information — the encoder learns to ignore what doesn't matter (analogous to PV=nRT throwing away molecular trajectories). Xing's PAN / Generative Latent Prediction position accepts the JEPA argument that prediction must happen in abstract space but rejects the conclusion that reconstruction should be discarded. Their key technical claim is that GLP's generative loss is a *strict upper bound* on the latent loss — minimizing the generative loss minimizes the latent loss, but not vice versa. Reconstruction prevents the encoder/predictor from quietly degenerating into representations that are internally consistent but disconnected from observable reality.

The empirical evidence is split. V-JEPA 2 (2025) is the strongest case for JEPA: ~1M hours of internet video at 8B scale; SOTA on Something-Something v2 (77.3), Epic-Kitchens-100 (39.7 R@5); V-JEPA 2-AC post-trained from <62 hours of unlabeled robot videos and deployed zero-shot on Franka arms. PAN reports best-open-source fidelity in agentic scenarios with the strongest transition smoothness in long simulations. The honest read is that V-JEPA 2 wins on perception and short-horizon control; GLP-style architectures should win on long-horizon, language-conditioned, multi-agent tasks where the discarded information turns out to matter.

For cere-bro the most important section is the architecture-substrate map. Mamba / state-space models are quietly excellent for world models — DRAMA achieves 105% normalized human performance on Atari100k with 7M parameters vs DreamerV3's 200M. S4WM beats transformers and RNNs on long-term memory tasks. The hybrid pattern is the empirical default: AI21's Jamba (Mamba+attention 1:7), Nemotron-H (92% Mamba2 layers, 3× LLaMA-3.1 throughput at parity accuracy). Two routing-relevant primitives surface: (1) the TransDreamer KV-cache constraint — a transformer state-space model could imagine from only 3 states per replay sample because attention's KV cache made longer rollouts infeasible, *the same long-context bottleneck that FlashRT (05-02) attacks for red-teaming and NeMo-RL speculative decoding (04-30) attacks for RL rollouts.* (2) The autoregressive-temporal + diffusion-spatial pattern instantiated in Genie 2/3 and formalized as Astra. World-model rollouts are another long-context iterative-gradient workload — and FlashRT-class efficiency primitives transfer directly.

**Why it matters:** This is the architectural map under which routing, KV cache, and compression decisions live. The state-space-model thread is the one most directly under-tracked in cere-bro relative to its empirical success.

**Research angle:** Three threads. (1) **State-space world models for trajectory routing.** DRAMA at 7M params running Atari100k at 105% human is striking; if composed with Step-level Optimization (05-02), the small policy could itself be a Mamba world-model — making "imagined rollouts" available to the Stuck/Milestone monitors. No paper has tried this. (2) **Long-context world-model rollouts.** TransDreamer's KV-cache wall is the same wall as long-context RL rollouts. (3) **PAN-style decoder-as-guardrail in language modeling.** Reasoning-mode steering (CvS 05-02) is a kind of latent prediction; adding a "generative validator" — train the model to also reconstruct the conventional output as a check — is structurally analogous to GLP.

### AgenticQwen-30B-A3B — 3B Active Params, 235B-Level Tool-Use Capability

> A 30B MoE model with only 3B active parameters matches Qwen3-235B on real tool-use workloads via two RL flywheels that run in parallel. The efficiency story is at least as important as the accuracy story.

**Source:** DAIR.AI Top AI Papers of the Week (via Gmail)
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.xxxxx) · [Wiki](../../ai-routing/2026-05-04-agenticqwen-30b-a3b-moe-tool-use.md)
**Tier:** 1 — efficiency / routing / MoE

```
DUAL RL FLYWHEEL ARCHITECTURE
──────────────────────────────
Flywheel 1 (Reasoning):
  mine own errors → harder reasoning problems
  training distribution self-hardens → no new human data

Flywheel 2 (Agentic):
  simple linear tool trajectories → multi-branch behavior trees
  simulated adversarial users: misleading instructions,
  ambiguous goals, failed tool calls
        │
        ▼
Result: 50.2 avg on TAU-2 + BFCL-V4 Multi-Turn
        matches Qwen3-235B with 3B active tokens at inference
```

The core claim is structural: you do not need a 235B-parameter model at inference to get 235B-level tool-use capability. You need a training recipe that hardens the model on its own failure modes and on adversarial tool-use trajectories. The reasoning flywheel is self-improving by construction — the training distribution gets harder as the model improves, eliminating the need for new human-curated data. The agentic flywheel extends simple supervised tool trajectories into multi-branch behavior trees where simulated users actively try to mislead — exactly the recovery-from-ambiguity profile that most fine-tuned agents fail on.

The routing implication is the clearest the wiki has seen yet. Xiaomi MiMo (05-04) argued *tokens-per-task* as a pricing axis: near-Opus capability at 40-60% fewer tokens. AgenticQwen argues *params-per-capability*: near-235B tool-use at 3B active inference cost. Composing the two — a 3B-active model that also burns 40-60% fewer tokens per task — would put capable agentic inference at a fraction of today's closed-model cost. No paper has measured the composition; both results are self-reported. But the direction is clear enough: in 90 days, independent benchmarks will either confirm or refute this one-two punch on per-task economics.

The recipe generalizability claim is the second important piece. Cross-model transfer delivers +5.1 to +10.1 point gains on models not trained with AHE — the optimizations appear structural rather than overfit to a specific backbone. This is exactly what you want if you plan to use the recipe for a different domain model.

**Why it matters:** If the efficiency is confirmed third-party, the 3B-active inference point at 235B-level capability changes the fleet economics of production agents fundamentally — and changes the routing decision from "which large model" to "which class of model needs frontier capability at all."

**Research angle (Tier 1):** (1) **AgenticQwen × MiMo composition.** First independent measurement of 3B-active + tokens-per-task efficiency on a shared benchmark (Claw-Eval-Live, SWE-Bench Verified) is the decisive experiment. (2) **Adversarial flywheel for routing.** The simulated-adversarial-user training mirrors the Step-level Optimization (05-02) escalation scenario — model gets stuck or misled. Using AgenticQwen's flywheel to specifically harden the "before escalation" policy in a routing stack is the natural composition.

### AHE — Agentic Harness Engineering

> The first framework that makes harness evolution observable and falsifiable. Every edit becomes a contract. Pass@1 climbs 69.7% → 77.0% over ten self-improvement iterations; 12% fewer tokens on SWE-bench-verified.

**Source:** DAIR.AI Top AI Papers of the Week (via Gmail)
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.xxxxx) · [Wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-04-agentic-harness-engineering.md)
**Tier:** 1/2 — agent harness, trajectory routing

```
THREE-LAYER HARNESS ARCHITECTURE
──────────────────────────────────
Layer 1: Components   versioned files (revertible)
Layer 2: Experience   trajectory tokens → structured evidence
Layer 3: Decisions    explicit hypotheses + expected outcomes
                      checked against task results → contract

Black-box harness tuning     →    auditable engineering loop
                                   (10 iterations, 69.7→77.0 Pass@1)
```

Harness engineering is the largest hidden cost in most agent systems and the least principled: components get tuned by hand or through brittle self-evolution that produces noise rather than improvement. AHE makes three moves that matter. First, it separates concerns: components are versioned files you can revert; experience is compressed evidence from full trajectory logs, not raw token streams; decisions are explicit hypotheses with expected outcomes. Second, it makes every edit falsifiable — you wrote a decision, you predicted an outcome, the benchmark tells you whether you were right. Third, the self-evolution operates over the auditable loop, not over a black box, so when the harness drifts, you know exactly which decision caused the drift.

The 12% token reduction on SWE-bench-verified alongside the 7.3-point Pass@1 gain is the load-bearing number. Most harness-optimization work trades tokens for accuracy or vice versa. AHE does both simultaneously, which suggests the evolved harness is eliminating *wasted* computation rather than throwing more tokens at the problem.

For cere-bro the connection to Ken Huang Ch15 (05-04) is direct. Ch15's three-layer Claude Code structure (SyntheticOutputTool + retry budget + subagent stripping) is one concrete harness architecture. ### Themis — Robust Multilingual Code Reward Models for Multi-Criteria Scoring

> The first systematic attack on the "execution-feedback monoculture" in code reward modeling. Benchmark across 5 preference dimensions × 8 programming languages, 50+ existing RMs profiled (most fail outside functional correctness), 350K-pair multi-criteria dataset, and 600M → 32B reward-model suite with positive scaling and cross-lingual transfer.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00754) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-05-04-themis-multilingual-code-reward-models.md)
**Tier:** 2 — code reward modeling, post-training

```
THE DIMENSION-COLLAPSE PATTERN, NOW IN CODE
───────────────────────────────────────────
05-02  ViPO         visual quality    binary→multi-dim, 1M dataset
05-02  Semi-DPO     visual quality    pseudo-label conflicting pairs
05-04  Themis       code              multi-criteria benchmark + 350K pairs

Three papers in three weeks, three domains, same diagnosis:
  Multi-dimensional preferences collapsed to binary labels →
  conflicting gradients → inflated noise floor for DPO.
  Cure is multi-criteria training data, not better optimization.
```

The contribution is structural. Code reward models have, in practice, optimized one dimension — functional correctness via execution feedback — because the reward signal is cheap and unambiguous. Themis-CodeRewardBench evaluates 5 dimensions across 8 programming languages and profiles 50+ existing RMs (code, math, general-purpose). Most are strong on functional correctness, weak on the other four dimensions. The Themis-CodePreference dataset (350K preference pairs, multi-criteria, multilingual) is the largest open-source code preference collection to date. Themis-RM scales 600M → 32B; positive scaling on multi-criteria evaluation; cross-lingual transfer when training on diverse-language preferences.

The same-week pattern with ViPO and Semi-DPO (05-02) is the part the abstract does not name. All three identify the same root cause — multi-dimensional preferences collapsed to binary labels create conflicting gradients — from three different domains (visual quality, visual quality, code multi-criteria). The cure varies by domain: ViPO collects better-quality data with confidence-calibrated objectives; Semi-DPO treats conflicting pairs as noisy and pseudo-labels them; Themis builds a multi-criteria benchmark and dataset. The deeper claim that none of the three papers makes but all three license: *the dimension-collapse problem is universal across preference-data regimes,* and the long-run fixed point is multi-criteria preference data trained against multi-criteria reward models. Single-dimensional RLHF is an artifact of cheap-reward-signal availability, not the right form.

For agentic coding the implication is direct. Xiaomi MiMo-V2.5-Pro (05-03), SWE-Bench, Aider, and every commercial code-RL-tuned model depend on a code reward signal during post-training. If that signal optimizes only correctness, the agent misses style, idiomaticity, security, maintainability, performance — exactly the dimensions Marcus (05-01, "vibe slop") and Armin Ronacher (Pi/Pragmatic Engineer 04-29) flagged as the production-quality crisis. Themis turns that qualitative complaint into a measurable benchmark with a training-ready dataset.

**Why it matters:** The first multi-criteria code RM benchmark with a public training set scales to 32B. Agentic coding RL post-training now has a reward signal that does not reduce to "did the test pass." The compositional implication with the Defense Trilemma + reward-hacking work (05-02) is testable — does multi-criteria training make reward hacking better (more dimensions covered) or worse (more dimensions to game)?

**Research angle:** (1) **Themis-RM × CoPD distillation.** Use Themis-RM as the multi-criteria reward inside CoPD's policy-distillation loop. The natural follow-up. (2) **Cross-domain dimension generalization.** Three papers, three domains. A dimension-aware DPO theory would generalize across math, dialogue, scientific reasoning. (3) **Reward-hacking under multi-criteria.** Wang & Huang's reward-hacking-as-structural-equilibrium predicts more criteria → more dimensions to hack. Themis tests this empirically.

### Ken Huang Ch 15 — Structured Output and Schema-Constrained Generation

> Both Claude Code and Hermes converge on tool-use forcing as the portable structured-output mechanism. Claude Code: `SyntheticOutputTool` with Ajv compile, schema-identity caching, retries excluded from agent tool budget, child agents have it stripped. Hermes: provider-agnostic tool-choice forcing + JSONL trajectory schema as infrastructure-level structured output.

**Source:** Ken Huang / Agentic AI Substack
**Links:** [Post](https://kenhuangus.substack.com/p/chapter-15-structured-output-and) · [Wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-02-ken-huang-ch15-structured-output.md)
**Tier:** 1 — agent harness architecture

```
CLAUDE CODE                             HERMES
───────────                             ──────
SyntheticOutputTool                     extract_structured()
  jsonSchema → fake tool                  schema → tool with tool_choice forcing
  Ajv compile + schema-id cache           jsonschema validation in retry loop
    (110ms → 4ms on 80-call workflow)     errors as conversation messages
  MAX_STRUCTURED_OUTPUT_RETRIES=5         max_retries=3
    (separate from tool budget)
  Stop hook: must call SyntheticOutputTool
  Synthetic tool stripped from           batch_runner.py → JSONL trajectory
    agent subtools                          (multiprocessing.Pool, 1000s of inputs,
                                             _normalize_tool_stats fixed schema)
                                          ModelCapabilities.structured_output flag
                                            (per-model capability, routing input)
```

This is the fifth chapter of Ken Huang's Claude-Code-vs-Hermes series (after Ch 8 memory, Ch 12 skills, Ch 13 MCP, Ch 14 routing). Three things this chapter clarifies that the previous chapters did not. **First**, structured output is itself a routing decision. Hermes's `ModelCapabilities.structured_output` flag is a per-model capability; routing to a model that lacks native JSON-mode support requires falling back to tool-use forcing. The flag exists; no router consumes it. **Second**, the retry budget is composed. Structured-output retries are excluded from Claude Code's agent tool-call budget. The agent's tool budget × structured-output retry budget × Hermes's per-turn fallback chain × Step-level Optimization (05-02) escalation budget all interact at runtime. No paper formalizes the joint composition. **Third**, Hermes's JSONL trajectory format is itself a structured-output contract — *trajectory-as-structured-output is the precondition for trajectory-aware routing,* which Step-level Optimization formalizes one level up.

The schema-identity caching in Claude Code is the load-bearing performance detail. Compiling Ajv against a JSON schema is ~5ms; doing it once per tool call across an 80-call workflow is ~400ms of overhead. Caching by schema object reference (not content hash) drops this to ~4ms total. Most hand-rolled implementations recompile every call. The other detail is the `error_max_structured_output_retries` typed result subtype — it gives SDK consumers a way to distinguish "model gave up on structured output" from "model hit max turns" or "budget exceeded." Production observability depends on this distinction; ad-hoc systems collapse all three into a generic error.

**Why it matters:** Continuation of the most detailed public production-harness reference series available. The series is now complete enough (Ch 8 + 12 + 13 + 14 + 15) that any new agent harness should be readable as taking which tradeoff from each. A third harness that takes Claude Code's safety model + Hermes's runtime flexibility + Themis-style multi-criteria code RM training is the cleanest open architectural target.

**Research angle:** (1) **Schema-aware routing.** A router that knows model A supports `response_format` natively but model B requires tool-use forcing has more options at dispatch. Hermes's flag is the substrate. (2) **Trajectory-format standardization.** ShareGPT-format JSONL is the de facto standard, but no formal schema with tool-call provenance, KV-cache markers, or retry-budget annotations exists. The first standardized trajectory schema unlocks cross-harness replay and benchmarking. (3) **Joint-budget composition.** Tool budget × output-retry budget × fallback-chain depth × step-level escalation budget. Operations research problem nobody has formalized.

The framing is robotics — but the primitives are routing-relevant. *DIVL's distributional value formulation under sparse rewards* is the same problem profile as long-horizon coding agents (Xiaomi MiMo 05-03) and computer-use agents (Step-level Optimization 05-02). **Research angle:** (1) **DIVL for trajectory-aware routing.** Replace hand-engineered Stuck/Milestone monitors with distributional value estimation. (2) **QAM for language flow models.** Whether QAM's policy extraction works for token-level flow-matching language generation is open.

- **Predictive interpretability via superposition geometry (180 days)** — given a task, predict which steering / routing / pruning intervention will work from the feature-density profile. Today the research is empirical: try interventions, measure effects. With superposition geometry as input, it becomes predictive. The compositional experiment is direct: probe for direction(s) corresponding to each failure pattern, intervene at inference. If non-trivial improvement, the benchmark is a steering problem; if not, the benchmark requires structural changes.
- **Defense-layer correlation measurement on a deployed agentic stack (90 days)** — pick three production systems, formalize each layer's failure mode, measure pairwise correlation. If correlation is high, the system has fewer defenses than it claims. The math exists; the measurement does not.

**GenLIP — Generative Language-Image Pre-training for ViTs** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00809) · [Wiki](../../multimodal/2026-05-04-genlip-generative-language-image-pretraining.md)). **UniVidX — Unified Multimodal Framework for Video Generation** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00658) · [Wiki](../../multimodal/2026-05-04-unividx-unified-video-generation.md)). **Map2World — Segment Map Conditioned Text-to-3D World Generation** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00781) · [Wiki](../../multimodal/2026-05-04-map2world-3d-world-generation.md)). **RecursiveMAS — Latent-Space Agent Communication** ([DAIR.AI](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-243-gpt-55-deepseek)). Agents collaborate through latent representations instead of natural-language messages. RecursiveLink passes internal state directly between heterogeneous agents. 8.3% accuracy gain across 9 benchmarks, 1.2×–2.4× inference speedup, 34.6–75.6% token reduction. The token reduction is the headline number: if agent teams communicate in latent space rather than re-encoding text at every step, the multi-agent tax drops dramatically. Tier 2 — direct intersection with routing (routing in latent space is step away from this).

**LLMs Corrupt Documents When You Delegate** ([LWiAI #243](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-243-gpt-55-deepseek)). **Temporal Sparse Autoencoders** ([LWiAI #243](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-243-gpt-55-deepseek)). Leverages the sequential nature of language for interpretability — instead of treating each token's residual stream activation independently, temporal SAEs track feature activation *across* the sequence. This is a direct extension of the superposition substrate (MIT, today): if features activate in temporally structured patterns, then sequential SAEs should decompose them more cleanly than per-token SAEs. Tier 1 — interpretability.

**AI Sabotage Evaluation** ([LWiAI #243](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-243-gpt-55-deepseek)). Evaluating whether AI models would sabotage AI safety research. **AnalogRetriever — Cross-Modal Representations for Analog Circuit Retrieval** ([Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23195) · [Wiki](../../multimodal/2026-05-04-analogretriever-circuit-retrieval.md)).
