A day about paying the expensive model only where it changes the answer. Arena's Max router and Cohere's agent teams put the strong model at the bottleneck and cheap models everywhere else, for 37-70% lower cost. LFM2.5 and Ling-3.1 keep three quarters or more of their layers off the KV cache. Gemini 4 Argon's 95% cached-input discount and IBM's 98% prefix-cache hit rate say the same thing about agent loops: rereading should be nearly free. Even the training papers agree: BranchOPD, PR-OPD and a 0.22%-budget SFT method all supervise only the few decisions where the student is unsure.

In this episode - Gemini 4 Argon: Google's new frontier model. First on the Vals Index, behind Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench. $2/$10 per million tokens, cached input 95% off. - Session routing: Arena's Max router and Cohere's agent teams both spend the frontier model only at the bottleneck. 37-70% lower cost. - LFM2.5-24B-A2B and Ling-3.1-flash: two open MoE hybrids where most layers keep a fixed-size state. Cheap decode on a laptop or at 1M context. - Fused MoE on Hopper: swap the matrix operands so decode fills the tensor-core tile. Plus a vLLM fix for bit-identical decodes across processes. - HyperThink: replace the thinking trace with a query-specific weight update. Short answers, no trace, better accuracy at low latency. - Selective distillation: BranchOPD, PR-OPD and progressive contraction supervise only uncertain decisions. One trains on 0.22% of the usual budget.
