Four separate papers today take something the field had been treating as a single fixed method and show it was one setting of a knob nobody knew was there. The knob is usually an allocation: how many parameters go to memory, how many heads read the depth history, how much teacher gets mixed into the target, how much of the target distribution you are willing to rewrite.

In this episode - Memory got its own day: eight papers, and they split three ways on where it belongs. A 6.9B memory on a frozen 410M base beats a 12B model. - Filesystem memory audited for the first time: keeping your agent's markdown folder tidy halves retrieval cost and improves zero answers. - Deep Research agents swallow planted falsehoods, and the verifier that catches those falsehoods in isolation misses them inside the run. - Lossy speculative decoding: all published relaxations reduce to two families, and one of them can lose to the baseline it approximates. - Kilo Code: Kimi K3 plus Grok 4.5 scored 93 to Opus 5's 98 on the same build, at $1.27 against $31.71. - BM25 beats agentic search above 10 million corpus tokens, by nearly 20 points at full scale, with no LLM indexing cost.
