## The Big Picture

Two things happened simultaneously on April 24 that reframe the competitive landscape: OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 and SemiAnalysis changed their daily driver. For the past six months, Opus 4.7 was SemiAnalysis's go-to for coding. GPT-5.5 ended that. The shift matters not because of benchmark numbers — it's because SemiAnalysis is one of the few shops that actually measures "cost per task" rather than "cost per token," and they're saying GPT-5.5 is better on that metric.

At the same time, Anthropic acknowledged that Claude Code's widely-noticed quality degradation (dubbed "AI shrinkflation" by users) was caused by three product-layer bugs — not model regression. Reasoning effort was quietly reduced to cut latency. A caching bug repeatedly cleared short-term context. Strict system prompts limited response depth. All fixed in v2.1.116. The timing is uncomfortable: OpenAI's coding frontier shift happened the same week Anthropic disclosed they'd been shipping a degraded experience.

On the research side, both Expert Upcycling (MoE) and NPO (RLVR) point at the same underlying shift: compute efficiency per training dollar is becoming the core metric, not raw parameter count or benchmark score. Expert Upcycling shows you can grow a MoE without paying to retrain from scratch. NPO shows you can improve RLVR without external teachers. Both are responses to the same pressure.

### GPT-5.5 and the Token Efficiency Shift

> SemiAnalysis declared GPT-5.5 the new frontier for coding — the first time they've said that about an OpenAI model since Opus took the lead in November.

**Source:** SemiAnalysis  
**Links:** [Post](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-coding-assistant-breakdown-more) · [Wiki](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-04-24-gpt-55-launch-system-card.md)  
**Tier:** 2 — LLM architecture / industry

GPT-5.5 is OpenAI's first new pre-train since GPT-4.5. The post-training (RL) ran on a 100k GB200 NVL72 cluster; the pre-train is still on Hopper. Priced at $5/$30 per million input/output tokens — 2x GPT-5.4, slightly above Opus 4.7. Five reasoning levels (xhigh through non-reasoning) let users trade cost for capability explicitly.

If you're measuring wall-clock cost per completed coding task (their actual metric), the 2x price per token becomes less relevant. This is the "token efficiency" thesis that SemiAnalysis has been building toward for months — and GPT-5.5 is the first model they're citing as its clearest expression.

One model releases per week for three months (GLM-5.1, Qwen3.6-Plus, Kimi K2.6, Composer 2, Gemini 3.1 Pro) means benchmarks are nearly meaningless as durable signals. SemiAnalysis's shift is credible precisely because they use production usage data, not benchmark tables.

**Why it matters:** The first credible third-party endorsement of GPT-5.5 over Anthropic at the frontier of coding changes the commercial narrative heading into the next few weeks.

### Expert Upcycling: Growing MoEs Without Retraining

> You don't have to train a 13B-expert MoE from scratch — you can clone a 7B-expert model, let the duplicates specialize, and save 32% of GPU hours.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers (Apr 23)  
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19835) · [Raw](../../../raw/huggingface/2026-04-23-expert-upcycling-moe-compute-efficient-frontier.md)  
**Tier:** 1 — MoE / compression / training efficiency

MoE models decouple total parameters from per-token inference cost, but training them is expensive: memory and inter-device communication scale with total parameters, not active ones. Expert Upcycling sidesteps this by starting from a trained E-expert checkpoint and expanding to mE experts via duplication. Per-token inference cost is unchanged (top-K routing is fixed). The duplicated experts start from the same weights, then diverge through continued pre-training — breaking symmetry via gradient-driven specialization.

The clever part is utility-based expert selection: rather than cloning experts uniformly, gradient-based importance scores identify which experts carry the most signal. Cloning those first triples the gap-closure rate when CPT budget is limited. The theoretical framework decomposes the quality gap into a capacity term (how much more the larger model can hold) and an initialization term (how far below optimum the warm-start lands). Both are trackable.

**Why it matters:** If upcycling generalizes, the cost of scaling MoEs drops dramatically — you pay for pre-training once and pay for capacity expansion incrementally.

**Research angle:** The duplication + CPT approach assumes expert specialization happens organically via gradient descent. What's the minimal CPT duration needed for genuine specialization vs. superficial divergence? And does the utility-based selection hold at 100B+ total parameter scales, or does importance scoring break down when expert count grows large?

### Near-Future Policy Optimization (NPO): Your Future Self as Teacher

> RLVR needs off-policy trajectories that are both better than you and not too far from you. NPO's answer: borrow from a later checkpoint of your own training run.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers (Apr 23)  
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20733) · [Raw](../../../raw/huggingface/2026-04-23-near-future-policy-optimization-npo-rlvr.md)  
**Tier:** 1 — RL training / post-training efficiency

RLVR's core tension: you want auxiliary trajectories that are "strong enough" (higher Q — more new knowledge to learn) and "close enough" (lower V — variance cost stays manageable). External teachers are strong but distributionally far; replayed past checkpoints are close but quality-capped. NPO formulates this as maximizing S = Q/V, and identifies the near-future checkpoint (a later point in the same training run) as the natural solution: stronger than the current policy and closer than any external source.

The practical result: a simple bootstrapping procedure (use checkpoint at step t+n when training step t) that improves Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct from 57.88 → 62.84 average performance. AutoNPO automates the checkpoint selection and timing, pushing to 63.15 without manual tuning.

**Prior context:** This connects to PreRL (04-16) and TIP (04-16), which also addressed where to concentrate training signal. PreRL worked at the pre-train distribution level; TIP worked at the token level. NPO works at the checkpoint level — orthogonal to both. Three different papers, same underlying question.

**Why it matters:** Off-policy trajectory mixing is becoming a standard RLVR technique. NPO's "self-improvement via near-future borrowing" is the most elegant framing yet — no external teacher dependency.

### LLaDA2.0-Uni: Diffusion Meets Multimodal

> A diffusion-based LLM that unifies image understanding and generation — not bolted together, but architecturally integrated through masked diffusion.

**Source:** HuggingFace Daily Papers (Apr 23)  
**Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20796) · [Raw](../../../raw/huggingface/2026-04-23-llada20-uni-unifying-multimodal-understanding-generation.md)  
**Tier:** 2 — Architecture / multimodal

LLaDA2.0-Uni is a discrete diffusion LLM (dLLM) that handles both multimodal understanding and generation in a single forward pass. The architecture: SigLIP-VQ discretizes visual inputs into tokens; the MoE backbone runs masked diffusion over both text and vision tokens jointly; a diffusion decoder reconstructs image tokens into pixels. The unified nature is genuine — the same backbone processes understanding and generation tasks, not separate heads.

Key efficiency move: parallel decoding with prefix-aware optimizations + few-step distillation in the decoder. Matches specialized VLMs on understanding benchmarks while delivering strong generation quality. The MoE backbone makes this Tier 1-adjacent: this is a large MoE-based architecture that replaces attention with diffusion as the generative mechanism.

**Tier:** 2 with Tier 1 intersection (MoE backbone). The diffusion + MoE combination is architecturally novel.

## Connecting the Dots

```
PreRL (04-16) — concentrate training at pre-train distribution level
TIP (04-16)   — concentrate training at the token level (10% carry signal)
NPO (04-24)   — concentrate training via near-future checkpoint mixing
  └── All three: same question, three orthogonal levels of the training stack
```

Expert Upcycling and the token efficiency framing from SemiAnalysis are pointing at the same thing from different directions. SemiAnalysis says the competitive metric is "cost per task." Expert Upcycling says you can achieve a given capability level at lower GPU-hours by starting warm. These two ideas compose: cheaper MoE training (Expert Upcycling) + more token-efficient inference (token efficiency) = lower total cost per task at the frontier.

GPT-5.5 and DeepSeek V4 dropped on the same day — same week, same frontier tier, opposite strategies. OpenAI: closed, GB200-trained, premium pricing. DeepSeek: open-weight, Huawei hardware, near-zero marginal inference cost. The field has split into two distinct competitive strategies. Both appear viable.
