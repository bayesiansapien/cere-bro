## Curated retweets

### @deedydas on ProgramBench, retweeted 15:23 UTC

The original tweet: *"The creators of SWE-Bench just dropped a really simple new benchmark every LLM gets 0% on. ProgramBench asks: can models recreate real executable programs (ffmpeg, SQLite, ripgrep) from scratch with no internet? We are far from saturated on model quality."*

Why this retweet matters: ProgramBench is the third capability-ceiling result this week, after PhysicianBench (46% on EHR-driven medical tasks, [wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-05-physicianbench-evaluating-llm-agents-real-world-ehr-environments.md)) and AcademiClaw (55% on academic-style problem framing, [wiki](../../agents-tool-use/2026-05-05-academiclaw-when-students-set-challenges-for-ai-agents.md)). What makes ProgramBench different from the other two is the difficulty profile. PhysicianBench is hard because the domain is unfamiliar. AcademiClaw is hard because the task framing is adversarial. ProgramBench is hard because the *artifact itself* is large, internally consistent, and unforgiving. ffmpeg is roughly 1.4M lines of C. SQLite is around 250K lines plus its full SQL grammar. ripgrep is rust with regex internals. The 0% score is not "models miss edge cases." It is "models cannot construct any working version at all." Daniel Das (@deedydas) is the right person to flag this; he was on the original SWE-Bench team and his bar for what counts as a meaningful benchmark is high.

The retweet matters even more because it closes a loop in today's digest. Today's Big Picture argued there is a tension between Clark's 2028 automated-AI-R&D timeline (Import AI) and the capability-ceiling cluster. ProgramBench is the cleanest data point in that cluster so far: a binary 0% on tasks that are *exactly the kind of engineering work* Clark argues will be automated first. Either Clark is right that the 80% of repetitive engineering automates without solving ProgramBench, or ProgramBench is the kind of capability that has to be solved before the 2028 window closes. Worth Watching: which top-3 lab posts the first non-zero ProgramBench score, and on which sub-task. Sub-tasks matter here. A model that can recreate a working basic regex engine but not ffmpeg's full codec pipeline tells a different story than one that fails uniformly.
