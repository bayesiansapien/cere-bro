- **PyTorch moves off CUDA by config (cluster of 6)** ([@PyTorch](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2112958324325199945), [vLLM-TorchTPU](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2112991914920456391), [@PyTorch FlyDSL](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2112967243956945293), [@dylan522p](https://x.com/dylan522p/status/2112660770458927578), [@DimitrisPapail](https://x.com/DimitrisPapail/status/2112977842770203036), [@vincentweisser](https://x.com/vincentweisser/status/2113001829592080779); [TorchTPU blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/introducing-torchtpu/)). TorchTPU, built by Google with Meta's PyTorch team, replaces PyTorch/XLA's lazy tensors with a native backend: real eager mode plus torch.compile, so a model moves to TPU with a device swap. vLLM and SGLang now run natively on it, and Google's launch post says SGLang-TorchTPU already serves production traffic at Meta. FlyDSL, AMD's Python kernel language that lowers through MLIR, now plugs into PyTorch on MI350 GPUs with up to 1.68x geomean GEMM speedups and up to 3.01x end-to-end vLLM serving. Around it ran a megakernel thread: Dylan Patel's "FlashAttention was a megakernel", a claim of trillion-parameter-scale pretraining inside one megakernel, and Papailiopoulos's quip that the final megakernel is a transformer in an endless loop. The thread's point: hardware lock-in is moving from the framework to whoever writes the kernels. See [DeepInfer and the Huawei stack](../../hardware/2026-10-22-deepinfer-and-huawei-deepseek-symbiosis.md).

- **Cache Engineering for Agents** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2112968165680640143); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28577)). When an agent trims or summarizes its history, the edit invalidates the KV cache (the stored attention state that saves recomputation) for everything after it, so the kept context must be processed again. This Tsinghua paper gives the agent two operations on its own cache instead: `drop` hides chosen entries from future attention and `repos` shifts the positions of what remains, with the transcript untouched. A rolling drop of old tool outputs scores 1.7 points higher at 27.7% lower cost than summarization, and the AKV serving engine adds up to 1.2x throughput. Self-improving cache policies cut cost another 7.1%. See [the summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-10-22-cache-engineering-for-agents-akv.md).

- **Harness research, evolved and audited (cluster of 4)** ([@rohanpaul_ai OO-Harness](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2113118167627448470), [@snwiki238337](https://x.com/snwiki238337/status/2113089225080049905), [@dair_ai Agent S4](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2113017003178889290), [@omarsar0 continual learners](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2112728082042015769); papers [2610.28380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28380), [2610.28601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28601), [2610.25858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858)). Object-Oriented Harness (Google Cloud AI Research) evolves a family of harnesses as subclasses of a base harness, then a router picks one per task. It improves up to 15% over the base and falls back to the base on unfamiliar apps. Agent S4 (Simular) has a meta-agent build new computer-use interfaces from unlabeled runs: an open 27B model reaches 21.3% on OSWorld V2, above Opus 4.8, at about 1.5% of its cost. The counterweight: Google DeepMind and Mila find self-evolving agents (ReasoningBank, GEPA, Meta-Harness) trade stability for plasticity, because global prompt edits interfere with other tasks and memory retrieval collides across domains. See [the harness-evolution wave](../../agentic-systems/2026-10-22-harness-evolution-wave.md).

- **Épi, a persistent research agent** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2112618107038835011); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24944)). Épi works on the same research problem across many model sessions and keeps its methods and arguments inspectable. It reports 120 records, including computer-assisted results on the Cohn-Elkies bound in dimensions 10 and 11 and a case of Tuza's conjecture. A lighter configuration, Épi-turbo, builds solvers for routing and scheduling and scores 5.4% above Codex on 30 FrontierOR tasks at 64% of the model cost.

- **Jev as an eval judge** ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2112632133357346889); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)). Widely shared. A decision model that returns label probabilities instead of text lands within 3 points of GPT-6 as a judge, at 0.36% of the fee and 0.15 s latency, where the verdict can be read off the text. With a threshold fixed in advance, accepting confident verdicts and escalating the rest beats GPT-6 by 0.9 points at 41% of the fee. It contrasts with a same-day paper where an uncalibrated Jev made an agent 19% more expensive. See [decision models under audit](../../ai-routing/2026-10-22-decision-models-good-judgment-bad-advice.md).

- **Speculative decoding slows down under load** ([@theomlondhe](https://x.com/theomlondhe/status/2113162304900747772)). Quietly high-signal from a small account. EAGLE-4's results table on Qwen3.8-27B with 4 B300 GPUs: DSpark and DFlash 2 are 2.6-2.8x faster than plain decoding at one request but fall below it at 512 concurrent requests (14.2K and 16.6K vs 21.2K tokens per second). EAGLE-4 shrinks its draft tree as load rises: 3.59x at low load, 1.34x at high load. See [drafters under load](../../inference-efficiency/2026-10-22-drafters-under-load-eagle4-dspark-ubtree.md).
