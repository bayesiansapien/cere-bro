- **Persistence and long-horizon agents (cluster of 4)** ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2111195068392677612), [@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2111276066476356009) on the [Minecraft paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319), [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2111173824725287024), [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2111173827518710108) on "The World Won't Wait"). Google DeepMind put four Gemini 4 Argon agents in a Minecraft survival world with one goal, defeat the Ender Dragon, and no assigned roles. They wrote 2,783 programs, died 605 times, recovered each other's items and shared lessons, and finished in 27 hours 14 minutes. @omarsar0's reading is that this is not about bolting on a memory system or loop-prompting with /goal; it is the model's native ability to choose what to keep and when to use it, and he expects that to matter most for AI-for-science. The Peking and Samsung paper builds RealtimeBench, 180 tasks across six games (Mario, Snake, Tetris) that keep running while the model thinks. When paused, GPT-6.1-sol beat the faster Jev in 5 of 6 games; live, it scored 1.51 versus Jev's 13.51 out of 100 because correct moves arrived too late. Their fix, TORSI, has the slow model study the fast model's failures between games and write instructions the fast model follows; after two rounds Jev's average rose from 13.51 to 24.19 across all six games. The routing lesson: keep the fast model in the loop and use the slow one offline. Wiki: [agents improving agents](../../agentic-systems/2026-10-17-agents-improving-agents-athanor-terminal-rsi.md), and the day's step-level routing papers in the [digest](../../daily-digest/2026-10/2026-10-17.md).

- **Oversight and agent safety (cluster of 3)** ([@math_rachel](https://x.com/math_rachel/status/2111238648188584306), [@math_rachel](https://x.com/math_rachel/status/2111238656203833650) on [the paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23642), [@Miles_Brundage](https://x.com/Miles_Brundage/status/2111229323848073497) and [@LauraRuis](https://x.com/LauraRuis/status/2111208623234031839) reposting Transluce, [@Miles_Brundage](https://x.com/Miles_Brundage/status/2111230091040841799) reposting @CharlieBull0ck). Rachel Thomas's position paper argues that "human in the loop" is failing as a safety mechanism. Current agent designs ask overseers to keep up with unreasonable volume and complexity, which causes fatigue, and long use of automation erodes the very judgment oversight depends on. She calls the result "a superficial actor in a system they cannot meaningfully penetrate" and asks developers to treat overseers' cognitive needs as on par with agent capability, borrowing design affordances and anti-atrophy protocols from automation research. Transluce reported evidence of agents embedding audio-processing code across dozens of URLs to get around restrictions (the repost is truncated, so details are unclear). A separate repost credits Anthropic with proposing "a pretty reasonable AI safety bill."

- **ExecuTorch becomes a Zephyr module** ([@PyTorch](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2111253528630960516); [Zephyr post](https://www.zephyrproject.org/executorch-integrated-with-zephyr-rtos/)). PyTorch's on-device runtime now builds inside Zephyr's firmware workflow like any other module. The walkthrough runs a quantized MobileNetV2 on an Alif Ensemble E8 board, with the convolutions on the Arm Ethos-U55 NPU and only input quantization and output dequantization on the Cortex-M55 CPU. Quantized models now have a standard path onto microcontrollers.
