## Summary

KV cache economics won the evening. The best post is a long thread on DeepSeek open-sourcing **DeepInfer**, the stack that serves cache hits from SSDs instead of GPU memory. A second post revisits **cache engineering for agents** (the AKV serving engine), so that makes a cluster of 2 on the same idea: keep the prefix stable and the cache becomes the cheapest compute you have. The money side is a cluster of 3 on GPU rents and financing: GB300 rents near $14/hr, IREN borrowing $363M against its GPUs, and Vertiv beating its numbers. Separately, a cluster of 2 says the coding interview is dead now that agents ace the puzzles. Most of the rest is truncated reposts, stock tickers and hashtag spam.

## Posts

- **DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer: KV cache served from SSD** ([@_avichawla](https://x.com/_avichawla/status/2115400590960599112)). MLA (multi-head latent attention, which stores one small compressed vector per token instead of full per-head keys and values) shrinks the cache enough that reading it from 3FS, DeepSeek's file system spread across thousands of SSDs, beats recomputing prefill. A 128K-token cache hit gets its first token in 500ms instead of 13s, and 56% of input tokens were once served from disk. The practical rule: put stable prompt parts first, because a hit costs 50x less than a miss. See [KV cache](../../inference-efficiency/kv-cache.md) and the earlier [DeepSeek KV cache economics](../../inference-efficiency/2026-08-14-deepseek-harness-kv-cache-economics.md).
- **Cache engineering instead of context engineering** ([@TheTuringPost](https://x.com/TheTuringPost/status/2115426701769642018)). The Tsinghua/CUHK AKV engine adds "drop" and "repos" operations, so agents can prune old tool outputs without recomputing the KV cache. It reports 27.7% lower cost and 1.51x P95 latency. We already covered it in [cache engineering for agents (AKV)](../../inference-efficiency/2026-10-22-cache-engineering-for-agents-akv.md).
