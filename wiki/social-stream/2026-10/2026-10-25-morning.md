- **Harness engineering (cluster of 6)** ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2114050042705379461), [@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2114050546189709424), [@omarsar0 on subagents](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2114057736363352127), [@IntuitMachine](https://x.com/IntuitMachine/status/2114140884296175790), [@beamnxw](https://x.com/beamnxw/status/2114058366347554833), [@TheTuringPost](https://x.com/TheTuringPost/status/2114105782526493133); papers [2610.30499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30499), [2610.31461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31461)). In the ARC-AGI-3 study, each frontier model writes a complete harness from a fixed bootstrap, plays five development games, and rewrites its whole codebase twice. All versions are scored on 20 held-out games. Across 27 lineages, first harnesses range from zero to near-ceiling, but two rewrites add only 2.80 points of RHAE (Relative Human Action Efficiency) with a 95% interval of -1.44 to 7.35, and the model's own cost cuts often lower the score ([summary](../../agentic-systems/2026-10-25-llms-improve-own-harness-arc-agi-3.md)). The skill-granularity paper lets a controller pick primitive tools, a macro skill, or a macro that falls back to primitive steps; a learned router hits 100% on a 12-task study where a heuristic fails ([summary](../../ai-routing/2026-10-25-adaptive-skill-granularity-and-context-cliffs.md)). Omar Sarabia and IntuitMachine both endorse Anthropic's advice to fold most subagents back into the main loop with skills and tool search, keeping subagents for parallel research; IntuitMachine calls most multi-agent setups "Conway's Law for AI." Beamnxw turns Anthropic's Claude Duet harness read into a checklist: brief, context, tools, observations, verification, repair, delivery, with missing evidence marked UNVERIFIED. The Turing Post reports strong opinions on harnesses from AI Engineer NYC.

- **Inference cost (cluster of 4)** ([@deedydas](https://x.com/deedydas/status/2114195317860266432), [@Sprytixl](https://x.com/Sprytixl/status/2114094189977723325), [@arena](https://x.com/arena/status/2114008313054527901), [@VaibhavSisinty](https://x.com/VaibhavSisinty/status/2114038898477408373)). Margin depends on utilization and stack quality, Chinese labs now ask large resellers for revenue share, and tens of providers launch weekly, which he expects to push compute prices up and token prices down ([summary](../../ai-industry/2026-10-25-token-arbitrage-neocloud-squeeze.md)). A hype-styled thread claims a cheap Claude model's per-token price jumps 5x past a context threshold, so an Opus orchestrator should fork Haiku workers before they cross it ($6.15 for one agent versus $0.85 for nine). The linked docs page does not show that tier, so treat the numbers as unverified. Arena puts Kimi K3.1 Pro (Max) at #11 in its agent leaderboard, the top open model, at a $1.03 median cost per task. Jassy's "barbell" (labs and cost-cutting enterprises at the ends, ordinary apps in a thin middle, with a 2027 crunch in chips, memory and power) was covered yesterday.
