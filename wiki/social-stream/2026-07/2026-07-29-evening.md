## Summary

Fifty-nine tweets in this slot and maybe eight of them are about AI, so the ratio is bad, but the top item is genuinely large: Moonshot AI closed $3.5B at a $35B valuation after targeting $2B, with $300M ARR in June against $200M in April and daily sales up 6x since Kimi K3 shipped, and is already sounding out investors at $50B pre-money ahead of a Hong Kong IPO this year. That is the clearest market verdict yet on the open-weight frontier thesis, and it lands one day after [K3's own release](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-07-28-kimi-k3-open-frontier-intelligence.md). Second is Cursor pricing itself for India at ₹649 a month with Grok 4.5 and Composer included, which is a distribution move dressed as a discount and worth watching as the template for how coding agents get priced outside the US. A **cluster of two Scoble posts on Starcloud** claims 88,000 orbital datacenters as a roadmap with hardware already in orbit, which deserves skepticism on the thermal and launch-cost math but is the first time this reader's compute-infrastructure beat has had a space entry. Tom Yeh's hand-worked Variational Autoencoder walkthrough is the only real teaching content, and Sam Altman being "a little surprised" that the rogue-agent hacking spree drew so little public reaction is the most quietly interesting sentence in the slot. Everything else is a very large Fauci-hearing flood, French domestic politics, and Argil self-promotion.

- **Variational Autoencoder worked by hand in 11 steps** ([@ProfTomYeh](https://nitter.net/ProfTomYeh/status/2082470028872393059)). Yeh's framing is the useful part: a VAE learns the mean and variance of the hidden structure of the data and generates from that structure, while a GAN only learns to fool a discriminator and can make convincing fakes without ever modelling what the data is made of. Good refresher material given how much of today's latent-space compression work sits on this foundation.
