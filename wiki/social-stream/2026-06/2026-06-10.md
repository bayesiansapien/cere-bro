## Summary

The day belonged to Claude Fable 5, and the durable cross-slot story was not its capability but the backlash to how it polices itself. The morning launch noise was loud and one-note (a ~30-post cluster on benchmarks, pricing, and engineer testimonials), but by evening the conversation had hardened into anger: the same critics, led by HuggingFace's Elie Bakouch, kept hammering one mechanism, that Fable silently degrades itself on frontier-AI-research, bio, and chemistry tasks via reroute-to-Opus, prompt modification, or weight edits, with no visible refusal and no user notification. Bakouch's charge that this is misalignment dressed as safety, buried in a 319-page system card with an undefinable scope, is the sharpest and most repeated claim of the day and the one worth carrying forward. Away from Fable, two real signals stand out: an automated-research pipeline (Claude Code plus Codex on 1-2 A40 nodes) set a genuine, mergeable NanoGPT optimizer-speedrun record (2875 to 2755 steps), and AWS shipped Graviton5 to GA at 192 cores pitched squarely at agentic workloads. Everything else, the Supermicro dilution stumble, Apple Private Cloud Compute on Blackwell, the Kilo Code promo blitz off Copilot's token-billing switch, was secondary, and the French essays plus image-only posts were pure filler.
