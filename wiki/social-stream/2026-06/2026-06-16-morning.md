## Posts

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro and the cheap-and-smart quadrant** ([@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2066707805890077128#m), quoting Artificial Analysis). Bakouch's point: DeepSeek V4 Pro, a 1.6T-total / 49B-active model, is *cheaper per task* than MiniMax M3 (428B total, 23B active) despite being far larger, because cost per task is driven by tokens emitted and routing, not raw parameter count. The attached Artificial Analysis chart (Intelligence Index vs Cost per Task, log scale) makes it vivid: DeepSeek V4 Pro (Max), MiMo-V2.5-Pro, and MiniMax-M3 sit in the green "most attractive quadrant" at Intelligence ~44 for roughly $0.04-$0.2 per task, NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra and Grok 4.3 cluster just to their right, and Claude Fable 5, Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.5 Flash are stranded on the expensive right edge at $1-$3 per task. DeepSeek V4 Pro runs a single Intelligence-Index task over 20x cheaper than GPT-5.5 and 40x cheaper than Claude Opus 4.8. This is the open-weight price war that today's [daily digest](../../daily-digest/2026-06/2026-06-16.md) reads as the demand pull behind the day's efficiency papers.

- **Terse is trained, not smart** ([@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2066714360895156543#m)). A short corrective: you might think a model "outputs less token because it's smarter," but "it's the opposite." Models that emit fewer tokens have usually been trained for token efficiency, not blessed with deeper insight. The remark previews the token-efficiency post-training (shortest-correct-response distillation, capability-per-output-token) in Ant Group's Ling-2.6/Ring-2.6 report that Bakouch covers in the afternoon slot, and it is a useful skeptic's note for anyone reading "fewer tokens" as a capability win.

- **Anthropic-Washington standoff and vendor defection** (cluster of 2). The Fable 5 / Mythos export-control freeze keeps producing fallout. The Department of War CTO ([@DoWCTO](https://nitter.net/DoWCTO/status/2066592032810770756#m)) announced that "over two-thirds of the @DeptofWar has officially transitioned off Anthropic models in daily workflows in favor of alternative AI vendors," framing it as a deliberate move so the DoW is "no longer single-threaded to one AI provider." Separately, @ns123abc ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/15/trump-officials-meet-with-anthropic-to-discuss-a-truce-00962698)) relayed that Anthropic flew its senior security team to Washington to meet Commerce and the National Cyber Director, and quoted a White House official warning that the longer it drags on, "the more likely it becomes a de facto licensing regime" where "every model going forward needs to ask the government's permission for whether it can be released." Together they show the freeze is no longer a single-product story: it is reshaping procurement and edging toward release-gating policy.

- **Google ends the free Gemini CLI tier June 18** ([@kilocode](https://nitter.net/kilocode/status/2066667318608826525#m)). kilocode's pointed take: Google open-sourced the Gemini CLI, built a community around it over a year, and on June 18 ends the free tier and steers developers toward the closed-source Antigravity CLI. The framing ("the license said open, the roadmap said otherwise") is a pitch for harnesses that cannot be pulled out from under you, but the underlying fact, a major lab retiring a free open coding harness for a closed one, is a real shift in the agent-tooling landscape.
