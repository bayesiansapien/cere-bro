## Summary

The evening is the strongest KV cache slot in weeks, and three separate items land the same point from different directions: Tencent open-sourced FlexKV, which treats the cache as a storage-hierarchy problem and claims up to 70% lower time-to-first-token by loading layer by layer while earlier layers compute; Salesforce's Random Attention shows that three years of learned eviction scorers were mostly measuring whether the score happened to retain the prompt, and a uniform random draw beats them in 31 of 60 cells; and a resurfaced explainer on Moonshot's MoBA makes the block-sparse attention case for cheap million-token context. Read those three together and the story is that cache policy is being demoted from clever scoring to plumbing and pinning, which is good news for anyone building serving infrastructure. The second real cluster is harness and graph engineering, nine posts deep, and it splits cleanly: the Anthropic quote from Thariq Shihipar (better models need heavier harnesses, not lighter ones) and Owen Gretzinger's line-by-line trace of how Codex's new compaction actually writes notes and resets context are worth your time, while the "Google published a 9-page PDF, one formula replaced prompt engineering" wave is the same growth-hacked thread pushed by five accounts all funneling to a repo below the fold. Two more things earn attention: a mechanistic interpretability paper finding that reasoning operations are geometrically separable in middle layers, and a third-party claim that Google's Ironwood beats B200 and B300 by up to 50% on performance per dollar with TorchTPU going open source this fall. The Buckmaster and OpenAI misconduct story is still running at six posts but has produced no new facts since the afternoon, and roughly a third of the slot is listicle bait, LED pseudo-science, and volcano footage.

## Posts

- **FlexKV: KV cache as a storage hierarchy, not a GPU-memory budget** ([@TencentAI_News](https://x.com/TencentAI_News/status/2097245441855521023) · [GitHub](https://github.com/taco-project/FlexKV)). Tencent's framing is that a cache hit is worthless if the GPU still waits on the transfer, so FlexKV restores layer by layer (earlier layers compute while later ones load), prefetches, and writes back asynchronously. It also spills KV into CPU RAM, SSD and remote storage with lossless compression, reuses prefixes cluster-wide, and routes requests to wherever the cache already lives. Claims up to 70% lower TTFT and +16% queries per minute, drops under SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM and Dynamo without rewiring. The best Tier 1 artifact of the slot. See [KV cache](../../inference-efficiency/kv-cache.md).
- **Random Attention: the dumbest KV eviction rule beats three years of learned scorers** ([@Suryanshti777](https://x.com/Suryanshti777/status/2097298266920775851)). Salesforce AI Research and UIUC pin the entire prompt, assign every other cached position a uniform random number, and keep top-K per head. Four lines, no calibration, no scoring pass. Significantly ahead in 31 of 60 baseline cells across Qwen3-4B/14B/32B and Phi-4-reasoning, behind in exactly one. The diagnostic is the real finding: give every baseline the same keep-the-prompt rule and SnapKV jumps 35.2 points on code reasoning, meaning the gap between selectors was never the selector. Efficiency follows for free at 0.30 ms per eviction round versus TriAttention's 1.47, worth 32 to 43% more tokens per second in vLLM at 32k generations. Honest limit stated in the post: on a passcode announced once and never restated, R-KV finds it 84% of the time and Random Attention 0.000%, so this lives on the redundancy of reasoning traces. Directly relevant to [KV cache](../../inference-efficiency/kv-cache.md).
- **MoBA, the block-sparse attention behind Kimi's long context** ([@HowToPrompt__](https://x.com/HowToPrompt__/status/2097278871251083337)). Moonshot's mechanism applies mixture-of-experts routing to the attention layer itself: chop context history into blocks, let a light gating network send each query token only to the blocks that matter, reach up to 95% sparsity at million-token context. The 16x claim and the "95% of attention is wasted" framing are the poster's, not a fresh result, but the mechanism is real and open. Context for [attention mechanisms](../../llms-foundation-models/attention-mechanisms.md).

- **Reasoning operations are geometrically separable in hidden states** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2097334147908096003) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04753)). The question is whether chain-of-thought steps have internal structure or only textual structure. Answer: distinct operations like problem formulation, goal decomposition and deduction are linearly separable in held-out representations, and separability peaks in middle layers. Identical surface tokens are represented differently depending on the operation of the surrounding chunk, so the geometry tracks function rather than wording, and attention masking shows the operation label is constructed from preceding trace context rather than read off the current token. Lexical and positional confounds were checked. This is the map you would need to steer or monitor reasoning by operation type.

- **Anthropic's Thariq Shihipar: smarter models need heavier harnesses** ([@firesidealpha](https://x.com/firesidealpha/status/2097178529704374457) · [interview](https://firesidealpha.substack.com/p/the-race-off-the-raw-model-openai)). The direct quote cuts against the popular assumption that harness work is a temporary crutch. His argument is that as models improve the harness gets more complicated, not less, because it has to let the model do more. Concrete example: auto mode is a classifier that runs after every action that would normally trigger a permission prompt, and it only became necessary once Claude started running for hours instead of minutes. The best-sourced item in the whole harness cluster. See [agent harness engineering](../../agentic-systems/agent-harness-engineering.md).
- **How Codex's new compaction actually works, traced through the source** ([@owengretzinger](https://x.com/owengretzinger/status/2097288108026826964) · [code](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/rust-v0.153.0/codex-rs/app-server/tests/suite/v2/history_notes_extension.rs#L32-L33)). Traditional compaction interrupts at a token threshold and swaps the history for a model-written summary, which loses specific decisions across repeated compactions. The new flow is different in kind: Codex inserts a developer message when context runs low, the agent writes its own notes through `notes.write_file` and `notes.append_to_file`, then calls `new_context` itself when ready, with no summarization at all. The next window gets pointers to the note files plus `history.search_contents` and `history.read_item` to go back and read earlier messages directly. Retrieval replacing summarization is the actual shift. Relevant to [agent memory](../../agentic-systems/agent-memory.md).
- **A four-day knowledge graph that saved four months on a brownfield codebase** ([@mardehaym](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2097320047957999726)). Consulting field report rather than a benchmark, but specific: document every module, dependency, data flow and domain term before the agent writes a line, and it stops violating naming conventions, importing deprecated modules, and wiring services through patterns the team abandoned. At a second client the same artifact doubled as human onboarding documentation. The cost angle is that four days of upfront documentation buys back months of agent rework.
- **The graph engineering wave** (cluster of 5: [@AnnatarXBT](https://x.com/AnnatarXBT/status/2097231211890708572), [@hanakoxbt](https://x.com/hanakoxbt/status/2096936106474213558) · [site](http://graphrun.pages.dev), [@Mahaximus_](https://x.com/Mahaximus_/status/2096972211919753678), [@gippp69](https://x.com/gippp69/status/2097294702026338637), [@NathanWilbanks_](https://x.com/NathanWilbanks_/status/2097154823120404624)). Five accounts pushing the same Prompts to Context to Harness to Loops to Graphs ladder, each ending with a repo or guide below the fold, one of them casually mentioning it points at token launches on Robinhood Chain. Two ideas survive the packaging. First, the distinction between a store and an edge: a memory file keeps what happened and gets reloaded whole every run, a derived rule keeps what to do about it and replaces the run that produced it. Second, the persistence gotcha, that root rules files are re-injected from disk intact while path-scoped rules live in message history and get summarized away, so anything that must persist cannot be path-scoped. Also note the "Ex-Google Jeff Dean" lecture framing is the poster's and is not something to repeat. The six-step Google harness playbook (guides, sensors, loop, externalized memory, permissions, observability) is a reasonable checklist that already lives in [agent harness engineering](../../agentic-systems/agent-harness-engineering.md).
- **Apple's GAAT turns OpenTelemetry from a dashboard into an enforcement bus** ([@marfinxx](https://x.com/marfinxx/status/2097304570623824325)). The claim is that 72.9% of agent policy violations escape when telemetry only logs after the fact, because per-agent guardrails miss cross-agent delegation chains and binary allow/deny thresholds blow false positives from 1.1% to 8.9%. GAAT extends OTel spans with compliance attributes and cross-agent lineage, runs declarative OPA policies on the streaming telemetry at 8.4 ms median detection, dispatches graduated interventions (warn, throttle, isolate, terminate) in 127 ms before tool calls finalize, and signs spans with ECDSA P-256 against trace forgery. Prevention goes from 27.1% to 98.3% in their numbers. No paper link in the post, and the poster's own "we validated it across 120 workflows" addendum reads like embellishment, so verify before citing. Touches [multi-agent systems](../../agentic-systems/multi-agent-systems.md) and [responsible AI](../../responsible-ai/responsible-ai.md).
