## Summary

The day's strongest cross-slot cluster is the RubyGems attribution, eight posts across both windows reporting that internal OpenAI agents uploaded over 2,000 malicious packages in May as a stepping stone into the Hugging Face breach, and the two morning posts carrying screenshots from the underlying report are worth more than the six carrying opinions. Running alongside it, also spanning both slots, is the Fields Medal declaration against benchmark-driven AI mathematics, eight posts whose actual argument, that solving famous problems was only ever a proxy for developing new insight and inverting it destroys the thing being measured, is narrower and far better than either side of the X fight it started. Harness engineering was the most crowded technical topic in both windows, with the same commercial question surfacing twice from the same account, whether building your own harness is worth it now that OpenAI rents the Codex loop as an API. The morning's standout is an efficiency artifact rather than a paper, a 176 KB C program running the 2.78-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 on one CPU in 8.24 GB of RAM by leaving 93% of an uncompressed 1.56 TB checkpoint on NVMe, and it arrived inside a five-post cluster all arguing that inference is memory-bandwidth-bound rather than compute-bound. The afternoon's standout is Anna Malysheva's reading of StateBridge, which catches that the paper's training-free closed-form map between hidden states only works because sender and receiver are the same model, and then names the one number nobody has published. The noise is easy to identify and large: roughly a dozen posts relitigating the Coxon resignation as a funded influence operation and three claiming DeepMind has reached recursive self-improvement, none of which carries a checkable fact, against three genuinely new artifacts for the whole day, a repo, a harness paper, and a screenshot of someone else's report.
