The morning slot belonged to tiny corp, which posted seven times and turned a hardware launch into the clearest local-inference datapoint of the month: a $160,000 tinybox pro v2 black running GLM-5.2 at 119 tokens per second single-stream and 917 aggregate, with the machine's own bringup done by a different GLM-5.2 instance in about an hour. The curated retweet feed was empty for a third consecutive slot, so the AI handle feed carried everything, and a large fraction of that feed was European migration politics from @brivael and @dhh with no AI content at all. The genuine research signal was one item: Google Research released the Science One Framework, an autonomous research prototype whose pitch is that maintaining verifiable chains of evidence natively eliminates hallucinated citations, which is a direct structural response to the research-agent reliability failures this wiki logged yesterday. On the industry side Mira Murati posted Inkling-Small, 276B total and 12B active parameters matching a model four times its size with open weights and Tinker fine-tuning available same day, and AWS CEO Matt Garman posted Q2 numbers that are the strongest single quarter of AI-attributable cloud growth so far, 37% year-over-year with Bedrock spend exceeding all prior quarters combined. George Hotz also posted a brokerage screenshot showing tiny corp still holds its entire $5.1 million seed round three years on, which reads as a deliberate counter-position to the capital-burn norm rather than a flex. Everything else in the slot was politics, Tesla product clips, or personal posting.

## Posts

- **tiny corp ships the tinybox pro v2 black and posts GLM-5.2 numbers on it (cluster of 7)** ([@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082868479334723838#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082929188668153915#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082932397709086783#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082930235964874779#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082931754160283828#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082935804029186229#m), [@__tinygrad__](https://nitter.net/__tinygrad__/status/2082948256792003052#m), [product page](https://tinycorp.myshopify.com/products/tinybox-pro-v2)).
  The machine is a rackable workstation at **$160,000**: eight RTX 5090s or eight RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell cards, every GPU on 16x PCIe5, two 128-core AMD Genoa CPUs, 384 GB of RAM, four 2000W supplies requiring 208 to 240 volts. The benchmark claim is the part that matters. GLM-5.2, which Hotz characterises as an Opus-tier model, runs at **119 tokens per second single-stream and 917 tokens per second aggregate**, and he frames it as "an Opus tier model running in your house faster than Opus ever did." Two details do more work than the headline number. First, the bringup was performed by a different GLM-5.2 instance in roughly an hour, and Hotz says there is still performance left on the table, so the optimisation ceiling is unknown and the optimiser was the model itself. Second, he demonstrates the throughput by having it write a browser-based Minecraft clone in 32,000 tokens, which is a workload-shaped demo rather than a synthetic one. The framing throughout is sovereignty rather than economics: running locally means "nobody can take it away," and you can run an abliterated model if you want to. Read against the wiki's [local coding model cache economics](../../inference-efficiency/2026-07-30-local-model-kv-cache-economics.md) note from yesterday, which found a 20x spread in KV cache footprint across comparable models and argued architecture beats cache management by an order of magnitude, this is the same argument at the opposite end of the budget: at $160,000 the constraint stops being cache footprint and becomes wall power. A separate post in the cluster advertises a hiring need for the "exabox" project, wanting HVAC, mechanical engineering and datacenter experience for a container in the tiny corp backyard in San Diego, which suggests the next product is a scale-up rather than another workstation.

- **Google Research releases the Science One Framework, an autonomous research prototype built around chains of evidence** ([@GoogleResearch](https://nitter.net/GoogleResearch/status/2082929539248951548#m), [blog](https://goo.gle/4wzlGWU)).
  The claim is that an autonomous research system should maintain verifiable evidence chains **natively**, as part of how it works rather than as a post-hoc citation check, and that doing so eliminates hallucinated citations and makes AI-produced science reproducible. The fetched page confirms the framing, "a verifiable autonomous research framework via Chain-of-Evidence," though the farmer captured navigation chrome rather than the body so the mechanism is not readable from the capture. The timing is what makes this worth flagging rather than filing as a product post. Yesterday's digest covered [shadow evaluations](../../agentic-systems/2026-07-30-shadow-evaluations-ai-research-agents.md), which handed frontier agents the central open question of two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 papers and had the papers' own authors grade the results: all the engineering was completed unassisted and both outputs were unambiguously rejected, with four of the five reproduced failure modes being judgment rather than knowledge. Provenance is not one of those five failure modes. So Science One is solving a real problem (fabricated citations) that is adjacent to, but not the same as, the problem the strongest available evaluation says is actually blocking research agents. Worth watching whether the framework reports anything about research judgment or only about traceability.

- **Mira Murati posts Inkling-Small: 276B total, 12B active, open weights, comparable to a model four times its size** ([@miramurati](https://nitter.net/miramurati/status/2082886925988598138#m), [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-small/)).
  The linked announcement fills in what the tweet does not. Inkling-Small is a mixture-of-experts transformer, meaning each token is routed through a small subset of specialised sub-networks rather than the whole model, so only 12B of the 276B parameters activate per token. It was trained on **NVIDIA GB300 NVL72** systems and keeps the features of the larger Inkling: native reasoning over audio and images, a variable thinking-effort control, and a context window up to **1 million tokens**. Full weights are available, and it is fine-tunable on Thinking Machines' Tinker platform the same day. Murati's own framing is deliberately understated, "comparable to Inkling at a quarter the size," which is the efficiency claim stated as a size claim. Two things make this more interesting than a standard open-weights drop. The GB300 NVL72 detail is the first public confirmation of what Thinking Machines is training on. And releasing a model that makes your own flagship look overpriced, four days after DeepSeek did the same thing to OpenAI, is a pattern rather than a coincidence.

- **AWS CEO Matt Garman posts Q2 numbers, and the AI-attributable lines are the story** ([@mattsgarman](https://nitter.net/mattsgarman/status/2082959479436345353#m), [Amazon IR](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)).
  Four numbers, three of them AI-specific. AWS grew **37% year over year on a very large base**, described as its fastest growth in eighteen quarters. **Bedrock customer spend exceeded all prior quarters combined**, which is a cumulative comparison and therefore a much stronger statement than a growth rate. **AgentCore revenue grew more than 4x quarter over quarter.** And usage of Kiro, Amazon's coding agent, **tripled quarter over quarter**, with a claim that it is up to 50% more cost-effective than alternatives. The Information's coverage adds the parts a CEO post omits: operating margin rose to 39.4%, the highest since early last year, the stock jumped 9%, and Amazon raised 2026 capital expenditure guidance by $20 billion to **$220 billion** while burning $7.6 billion of cash in the quarter. Lifting margin while depreciation on new chips and servers is climbing is the genuinely difficult thing here, and it is the number that says the AI cloud business is not being subsidised into existence.

- **NVIDIA runs two posts, one on physical AI with Japanese manufacturers, one on the Open Secure AI Alliance** ([@nvidia](https://nitter.net/nvidia/status/2082934294180221043#m), [@nvidia](https://nitter.net/nvidia/status/2082911218734617015#m), [Cosmos announcement](https://nvda.ws/4xeuXUk), [alliance blog](https://nvda.ws/4pD8Fc5)).
  The physical-AI post is Jensen Huang laying out a full stack framed as the foundation of the next industrial revolution: Cosmos and Omniverse to develop physical AI in virtual worlds, Isaac and Newton as physics-simulated gyms where robots learn skills, and Jetson as the computer the resulting intelligence runs on, with Japan's robotics and manufacturing leaders named as the deployment partners. The second post welcomes new members to the **Open Secure AI Alliance**, whose blog frames the pitch around open source already underpinning cloud, finance, manufacturing, telecoms and government, with cybersecurity among its top three beneficiaries. That alliance sits alongside the separate 230-organization "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" letter that NVIDIA also backed this week, so NVIDIA is now running two parallel open-ecosystem coalitions, one on security tooling and one on policy. Both serve the same commercial interest, which is that open weights run on more of NVIDIA's hardware than a handful of closed frontier APIs do.
