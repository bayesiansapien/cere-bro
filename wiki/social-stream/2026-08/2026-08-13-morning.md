- **Grok 4.6 optimized its own harness** ([@aksheyd](https://x.com/aksheyd/status/2087622695718662338), [x.ai/build](https://x.ai/build)). The claim is one sentence, "Grok 4.6 optimized the Grok Build harness for itself. We are not done," and it is the most load-bearing thing in the slot. A harness is the scaffolding around a model at inference time: the context constructor, the tool routing, the orchestration loop. Today's research board contains two papers making exactly this the object of study, one showing a strong model can write a harness that nearly doubles a weaker model's accuracy with no weight changes, and one benchmark showing frontier coding agents improve an external artifact under an unspecified objective in 63 of 64 sessions. xAI is reporting the same capability as a product feature. What is missing on the vendor side is the cost-per-success number that made harness quality measurable in the first place. Feeds [Agent Harness Engineering](../../agentic-systems/agent-harness-engineering.md).

- **tinygrad and comma launch the "chestnut" eGPU dock (cluster of 5)** ([@__tinygrad__](https://x.com/__tinygrad__/status/2087667429543645510), [comma.ai/shop/chestnut](https://comma.ai/shop/chestnut)). - **Google Research: recall, not storage, is the factuality bottleneck** ([@GoogleResearch](https://x.com/GoogleResearch/status/2087586341660069989), [post](https://goo.gle/4wXia92)). A behavioral framework called knowledge profiling that measures **encoding and recall separately**, then uses the split to locate where parametric factuality actually fails in frontier models. The framing in the post title is the summary: empty shelves or lost keys, and the answer is lost keys. The distinction matters because the two failures have opposite fixes. If a model never encoded a fact, more retrieval at inference is the answer. If it encoded the fact and cannot retrieve it, more retrieval is treating a symptom and the fix belongs in how the model addresses its own parameters. This is the only substantive non-Grok research post in the slot, and it connects to the memory thread the Media Zone has been tracking, where several saved pieces argue memory should become a native model capability rather than a bolted-on vector database.

- **Bakouch on European sovereign AI, sharper than the announcement** ([@eliebakouch](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2087584657328022006), [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/)). Mistral announced in-region inference and new European infrastructure, serving open models rather than only its own. Bakouch's read goes past the press release to the actual risk: it is bad for Europe that Mistral's models trail the frontier, but the thing that worries him more is critical actors, governments and large companies, being **forced** to use them for work like cybersecurity where capability gaps translate directly into exposure. He extends it beyond cyber, arguing entire R&D-driven industries could fall behind purely from being mandated onto less capable models. His conclusion is that Mistral hosting *better* models on sovereign infrastructure is the encouraging part of the announcement, because it decouples the sovereignty requirement from the model-quality requirement. That is a more useful frame than the sovereignty debate usually gets.

He also demonstrated installing OpenAI's new ChatGPT Linux desktop app from Omarchy's own Cloudflare-backed package repository, and noted all Omarchy agents are managed through mise on an out-of-band upgrade path from system packages.
