## Posts

- **DeepSeek's open-sourced harness is engineered around never touching the KV cache** ([@eliebakouch](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2087908415775408346)). Bakouch read the release and describes it as a web UI containing multiple harnesses, able to spawn Claude Code and Codex agents through their SDKs. It supports several modes, which are themselves harnesses: a code mode with programmatic tool calling in TypeScript, a bash-plus-edit mode typically used in evaluations, and a standard read/write/edit tool mode. The line that matters most for cost: there is first-class KV-cache-aware design, meaning the system makes sure the KV cache (the store of already-computed attention keys and values that lets a model skip recomputing tokens it has already seen) is never altered. Editing anything in the middle of a conversation invalidates every cached token after the edit point and forces a full recompute, so an append-only discipline is what keeps a cache hit a cache hit. He adds that DeepSeek's harness was itself heavily developed using Codex, with roughly 20% of commits and PRs coming from Codex worktrees. Read alongside the six-fold cache-hit price increase in the same release, the three pieces describe one position: the cache is the cost center, the harness must be designed around it, and the harness is worth giving away while the cache is worth charging much more for. → [wiki summary](../../inference-efficiency/2026-08-14-deepseek-harness-kv-cache-economics.md)

- **Gemini 3.7 Flash lands in Devin at Sonnet 5 performance for under half the price** ([@cognition](https://x.com/cognition/status/2087994792819237357), [blog](https://devin.ai/blog/gemini-37-flash)). Cognition's FrontierCode 1.1 is their proprietary benchmark grading models on real engineering tasks by quality and mergeability, with solutions that fail blocking criteria scoring zero. Their published numbers put Gemini 3.7 Flash at 56.3 against Claude Sonnet 5 at 56.2 and GPT-5.6 Terra at 56.2, with Claude Opus 5 at 63.6 and Claude Fable 5 at 64.9 above them. The specific behavioral note is more useful than the headline: within Devin, Gemini 3.7 Flash performs particularly well on tightly scoped refactors, where it produces minimal diffs that match existing repo conventions. It is available at an additional 50% discount through August 27. The score-versus-cost chart they publish is the same argument today's digest makes from the research side, that the interesting axis is quality per dollar rather than quality alone. → [digest](../../daily-digest/2026-08/2026-08-14.md)

- **Devin now works directly against live MongoDB Atlas data** ([@cognition](https://x.com/cognition/status/2087892577185829004)). Their framing is about removing three specific failure modes: stale schemas, context copied in by hand, and waiting on a human to set up a database. This is a small post but it is a concrete instance of the harness expanding to own more of the environment rather than the model getting better, which is the thread running through today's research as well.
