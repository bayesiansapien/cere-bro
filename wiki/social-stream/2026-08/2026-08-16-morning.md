- **Prime Intellect measures autonomous AI research at unprecedented scale** (cluster of 4) ([@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2088736971250090393), [@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2088736593800524178), [@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2088737025960673379), [@eliebakouch](https://nitter.net/eliebakouch/status/2088745056564109682) · [blog](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/measuring-autonomous-research) · [results](https://www.primeintellect.ai/research/nanogpt-speedrun) · [prior run](https://www.primeintellect.ai/auto-nanogpt)).
  The four tweets are the blog post, the results explorer, a framing tweet, and a credits tweet, and the article content attached to each is the substance. The experiment: give an agent the nanoGPT training script and ask it to reduce the number of steps needed to reach a target validation loss, changing only the optimizer, schedules, initialization, and hyperparameters. 153 runs, 18 frontier models, multiple seeds per model, up to eight days per run on 8xH200s. Bakouch's own framing tweet says this is, to his knowledge, the largest open experiment on autonomous agents iterating on a research environment, and he gives the comparison that makes the scale legible: the closest published equivalents are Anthropic's internal evaluation, which optimizes a model on a CPU node, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol system card, which reports nanoGPT Track 1 on a single H100 for less than a day. The leaderboard reports **share of the human-record gap closed** rather than a raw score, so runs of different lengths are comparable: Fable 5 at 2,726 steps and 81.7% closed over 8.7 agent-days, Opus 5 at 2,920 and 53.6% over 2.9 days, Kimi K3 at 2,930 and 52.2% over 3.6 days, Opus 4.8 at 39.4%, GPT-5.6 Sol at 35.9%. The human baseline was 2,990 steps. Two things Bakouch says are more useful than the ranking. First, the noise: *"one run in the same setting has a ~50 step spread after 24h,"* which means any two adjacent rows on that leaderboard are not distinguishable. Second, from the earlier two-week experiment where Codex and Claude Code did about 10,000 runs burning roughly 14,000 H200-hours, the durable qualitative finding: **agents are very good at optimizer search, hyperparameter sweeps, and stacking methods together, and they struggle to come up with new ideas on their own, needing upstream human records to keep improving.** That earlier post also documented opposite behavioural failure modes, with Opus repeatedly stopping and refusing to stay in the autonomous loop while Codex never stopped but got stuck grinding the same direction. Every scratchpad, run log, script, and config is public. In the follow-up he credits METR and Recursive_SI for related work, Keller Jordan for modded-nanoGPT, and Karpathy for nanoGPT and Autoresearch. See [Measuring Autonomous AI Research](../../agentic-systems/2026-08-16-measuring-autonomous-ai-research.md).

- **The tokenizer is a 30% billing difference nobody prices** ([@ns123abc](https://nitter.net/ns123abc/status/2088869903369420992), quoting @thsottiaux).
  Tibo Sottiaux's claim, quoted here, is that an OpenAI token and an Anthropic token are not the same unit: the two use different tokenizers, OpenAI's is significantly more efficient, and the difference is around 30% in the number of tokens for the same text. Because API and usage plans bill per token, that is a straight 30% cost delta that never appears in a per-million-token price comparison. The quoting commentary is about positioning rather than economics, arguing that the moment a lab's messaging becomes competitor-referential rather than category-defining it has ceded narrative control. The economics is the part worth keeping: every cost-per-task comparison published this month, including the disagreement between the AlphaSense study and Artificial Analysis over whether US frontier models or Chinese open models are cheaper per finished task, is denominated in a unit that differs by roughly a third across providers.

The third is a bundle-economics post itemising what the $300/month SuperGrok Heavy tier includes, from an agentic harness with Grok 4.6 at xhigh effort to 720 queries a day on X. Treat all three as marketing-adjacent, since none of them is a controlled comparison, but the direction is consistent with the day's other cost signal.

- **San Mateo County moves to regulate humanoid robots, and the mandate targets teleoperation** ([@Scobleizer](https://nitter.net/Scobleizer/status/2088897375322673653)).
  Scoble relays a Humanoids Daily report that San Mateo County voted unanimously to draft humanoid-robot regulations. The proposed terms as listed: robots must be supervised at all times by a trained on-site human; businesses must pay a County Automation Impact Fee funding worker retraining; and annual fees will fund specialized hazmat equipment for high-heat lithium-ion battery fires. The on-site-human requirement is the one with teeth, because it directly breaks the remote-teleoperation business model that the current wave of $30/hour robotic cleaning services depends on. Scoble's framing is that startups launching those services one county north in San Francisco just got a boundary drawn for them.

- **Robot-hand economics from the ground** (cluster of 2) ([@Scobleizer](https://nitter.net/Scobleizer/status/2088864071429075134), [@Scobleizer](https://nitter.net/Scobleizer/status/2088831291349500092)).
  From a robot-fights event in San Francisco, Scoble reports a conversation with a founder starting a stealth robot-hand company who argues Tesla will eventually move from tendon-driven hands to electric motors with tiny gears, and whose commercial argument is that current hands are absurdly expensive.
