## Summary

The scheduled morning slot captured no curated reposts, no tracked-handle tweets and no new saved posts, so this synthesis reads the late-morning Following-feed capture, which covers the US Wednesday afternoon and evening conversation. The strongest signal is a cluster of 6 posts on Google's Gemini 4 Neon: GitHub made it generally available in Copilot, Arena ranked it #2 on its new Agent Arena Index, and early testers said it feels close to Gemini 4 Carbon at about 2.5x the speed. The most consequential single post is OpenAI's research-acceleration data, amplified by Kevin Roose and Rohan Paul: the median OpenAI researcher uses about $2,400 of tokens a day, yet experiments rose only 68%, and agents succeed just 25% of the time on inference and serving code. A second cluster of 4 posts covered open-weight inference at scale: opencode said 44% of its tokens (16T in a day) ran on its own GPUs, vLLM v0.34 shipped with an extensible KV cache on by default, and Red Hat promoted a session on what moves speculative-decoding acceptance rates. A third cluster of 3 continued the self-improving-agent thread from the MIT/Tübingen paper on agents that design their own learning loops, plus Google's gated harness edits. Standout items: the Japanese-language recap of models rewriting their own weights, the Agent Arena Index launch, and a pointed reply arguing chip designs mean little without fab, HBM and packaging capacity.

- **Gemini 4 Neon lands everywhere at once (cluster of 6)** ([@github](https://x.com/github/status/2123181574116233646), [@arena](https://x.com/arena/status/2123169189397709174), [@GoogleAI](https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2123168242797809944), [@VaibhavSisinty](https://x.com/VaibhavSisinty/status/2123171262881231332), [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123170168201486582), [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-11-18-gemini-4-neon-in-github-copilot/)). Arena put Neon (High) at #2 on its new Agent Arena Index with 1336 points from 4,200+ real agent sessions, ahead of Carbon (1303), Hydrogen (1271) and Argon (1229), which makes it Google's best agent model. Rohan Paul's summary: 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, third behind larger and slower models, at $3/$15 per million tokens. - **OpenAI's research-acceleration data** ([@OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2123191365983248653), [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123250234457530538), [@kevinroose via @jeffclune](https://x.com/jeffclune/status/2123279276179087421), [post](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/)). OpenAI says researchers' AI use keeps rising exponentially and agents complete longer tasks, but humans stay central. The numbers people quoted: the typical researcher burns about $2,400 a day in tokens at API prices, and the heaviest 10% pass $23,000. Agents run as many hours per researcher as 5.7 people on 8-hour shifts, up 11x, but experiments rose only 68% over 2025 and capability gains sit just above trend. For GPT-6.2 Astra, pretraining researchers said Codex helped find key insights, which OpenAI calls AI's first reported foothold in research taste. Agents succeed 79% of the time monitoring training runs but only 25% writing inference and serving code, which could cap the overall speedup. Gary Marcus [called](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2123193201964314870) handing research to AI reckless. See the [industry summary](../../ai-industry/2026-11-19-anthropic-ipo-and-ai-research-acceleration.md).

- **Agents that design their own self-improvement (cluster of 3)** ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2123129152887046557), [@omarsar0 repost](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2123260155504869585), [@ai_database](https://x.com/ai_database/status/2123256612928323813), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23219)). The MIT CSAIL and Tübingen paper gives a frozen model a compute budget and simple tools: it can write practice games, watch another copy of itself play, and edit that copy's prompts and skill files, with no real data and no imposed loop. Capable models do self-improve and invent strategies resembling self-regulated learning and self-generated curricula, and the text artifacts transfer to other games and 3D navigation. Elvis Saravia reposted his Google gated-harness thread alongside it. A Japanese account summarized the separate "models rewrite their own weights" paper: an open Qwen3.5 model inspected its wrong answers and internal states, then rewrote its own weights and got better. See [gated self-improving harnesses](../../agentic-systems/2026-11-19-gated-self-improving-harnesses.md) and the [weight-space programming summary](../../llms-foundation-models/2026-11-19-weight-space-programming-field.md).
