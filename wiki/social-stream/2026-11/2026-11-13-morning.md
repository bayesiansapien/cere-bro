- **Gemini 4 Hydrogen: the fast, cheap tier (cluster of 4)** ([@demishassabis](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2120940338869055788), [@GoogleDeepMind](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2120940338869055788), [@arena](https://x.com/arena/status/2120940869889175840)). - **Recursive self-improvement on a whole pre-training run** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2120898418801525191), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10719)). "No Second Run" (GAIR at SII with Xinxu AI) asks whether RSI (recursive self-improvement, AI improving the process that builds AI) can reach pre-training, where a run costs hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, happens once, and is judged by how much headroom it leaves for post-training. Their answer is to split it into small loops that can close: agents debug infrastructure and data, check headroom with tiny SFT (supervised fine-tuning) runs, and turn expert judgment into data-pipeline metrics, with a shared context store that keeps negative results. The output, 801-30B-A3B (an open 30B MoE with 3B active parameters), beats Qwen3-30B-A3B and Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B as a base model. With only 6B tokens of SFT and no RL it posted the highest average on four agentic benchmarks against fully post-trained models.

- **Agent memory that rewrites itself (cluster of 2)** ([@omarsar0 on SoMem](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2120888604402815485), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11929), [@rohanpaul_ai on ReHarness](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2121040336361533502)). SoMem (City University of Hong Kong and Tencent) drops the fixed memory schema: the agent stores memory as ordinary linked documents, decides what to summarize, which topics to create and when to revise them, and every summary links back to the original interaction as evidence. It beat the strongest baseline by 2.66 points on LoCoMo, 3.10 on BEAM-100K and 2.00 on LongMemEval-S, and memory written by one model worked when read by another. ReHarness (MIT and Meta) attacks stale notes: after each task, a revision agent narrows or corrects the notes that were retrieved, using only facts from each note's original task. It averaged 0.240 normalized gain versus 0.168 for the best raw-trajectory baseline, and replaying full raw history cost 3.4x the tokens. Concept page: [agent memory](../../agentic-systems/agent-memory.md).

- **Training the harness, and testing harness changes (cluster of 2)** ([@omarsar0 on JoMoHa](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2121035321211318565), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11499), [@dair_ai on Trustworthy RSI](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2120898418801525191), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10787)). JoMoHa (Salesforce AI Research and HKU) trains two policies with RL from the same task outcomes: one writes the executable harness (the code around the model that manages tools, context and control flow), one solves tasks inside it. Naive turn-taking made them drift apart and performance rose then fell; separate histories and value estimates per policy fixed it. It averaged 70.3% across ALFWorld, WebShop and DBBench, with 97.0% on ALFWorld, and a trained harness writer beat a frozen one. Trustworthy RSI targets the opposite failure: self-improving agents usually keep any harness change that raises the validation score, so noise gets accepted and costs performance later; the paper measures how often that happens and adds statistical acceptance tests. Concept page: [agent harness engineering](../../agentic-systems/agent-harness-engineering.md).

- **Helion kernels that skip autotuning** ([@PyTorch](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2120924747378651565), [blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/)). Helion is PyTorch's DSL (a small language) for portable ML kernels, and autotuning was its bottleneck: one team spent 10 hours tuning a single paged-attention kernel across 72 shapes. The new heuristics read the kernel's IR and dataflow at compile time, classify it as matmul, reduction or pointwise, and pick block sizes, warps and pipeline stages directly, at a cost of tens of milliseconds. On H100, across nine linear-attention variants the computed config beats Flash Linear Attention's handwritten Triton by 1.18x forward (1.12x with backward), and across six production vLLM reduction kernels it is 1.27x faster than vLLM's handwritten CUDA/C++.

- **A 1T-parameter MoE on Europe's shared supercomputer** ([@PyTorch](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2120851611580785017)). The EUROPA consortium (Domyn, Fraunhofer IAIS and IIS) scaled pre-training of a 951.9B-parameter MoE (32.8B active, 384 routed experts, top-8, 64K sequence length, Muon plus AdamW) on JUPITER, the EuroHPC exascale machine shared by hundreds of projects. Per-GPU throughput rose from 90 to about 1,300 tokens per second across 384 GH200 GPUs per data-parallel replica, a 14x gain from fine-grained TorchTitan tuning on hardware that was not designed for AI.

- **SGLang on IBM Spyre in under a week** ([@PyTorch](https://x.com/PyTorch/status/2120894526650831034)). Two IBM engineers with AI coding agents wrote a roughly 1,500-line plugin plus four small SGLang patches. It delivered about 3.6x the throughput of the reference torch-spyre implementation, matched vLLM on Granite 3.3 8B and Gemma 4 26B A4B, and RadixCache prefix sharing worked unchanged. Same theme as yesterday's Mythos-on-AMD claim: agents shrink the cost of bringing up new silicon.

- **Agents as meta-optimizers (cluster of 2)** ([@DimitrisPapail rant](https://x.com/DimitrisPapail/status/2120982787851161950), [@DimitrisPapail on quants](https://x.com/DimitrisPapail/status/2120880397500194973)). - **Six serving techniques that remove six kinds of waste** ([@techNmak](https://x.com/techNmak/status/2120765962366357609)). A clear explainer: prefix caching reuses shared-prefix KV states, continuous batching swaps finished requests out between iterations, PagedAttention stores the KV cache in blocks to stop fragmentation, chunked prefill interleaves long prompts with decoding, speculative decoding drafts tokens cheaply and verifies them, and prefill/decode disaggregation runs the two phases on separate hardware.

- **CoT monitorability gets measured** ([@rasbt](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2120895037064020406)). Raschka summarizes a thread on what makes chain-of-thought harder to monitor. Better pre-training (lower perplexity) raises how much a model can reason in one forward pass without writing anything down, and architecture mostly does not matter. RL alone raises CoT controllability (how well a model can reshape its written reasoning on request), and that controllability passes through distillation. Both make monitoring harder; process supervision was not the main culprit.
