## Posts

- **Spend the expensive model less (cluster of 3)** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2125781132507553819), [Delegent paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33098); [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2125762008876519662), [effort paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491); [@NVIDIAAI](https://x.com/NVIDIAAI/status/2125707678244892736)). Delegent, from Lingwei Gu and Jimmy Lin at Waterloo, keeps the frontier model in charge of every task inside Claude Code but hands code reading, test runs and corpus search to subagents running a small model locally. With a 27B FP8 model frontier cost fell 67% on long-horizon coding and 59% on agentic search with no loss in task performance, and even a 2B laptop model cut coding cost 66%; nothing was trained, and one instruction that keeps the main edits on the frontier model does most of the work. Rohan Paul's attached screenshot is the abstract of "What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents" (Johns Hopkins, Together AI, Cornell), with the highlighted line that the highest effort level costs up to 42x the lowest on easy tasks and that higher effort makes agents verify more and search for solutions rather than work problems directly. His summary adds that starting low and escalating only after a verified failure matched or beat fixed top effort for every model, at a median 41.5% lower GPQA cost. This paper was first covered here on 10-29. NVIDIA's post says CodeRabbit and Baseten customized Nemotron 3.5 Lightning for code-review routing at about half the inference cost, with no further numbers. See the [Delegent summary](../../ai-routing/2026-11-26-delegent-and-in-agent-delegation.md).

- **ChatGPT and Codex move to one token meter** ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2125768849782120797)). The attached screenshot is OpenAI's help-center page "Understanding ChatGPT and Codex usage limits": from December, Plus, Pro and Business allowances are shared across ChatGPT and Codex, measured in tokens, and how fast you use it depends on the model and task; instant chat does not draw from the allowance, and you can buy credits past the limit. Rohan's read is that the squeeze lands on reasoning and agent work, so model choice and task size now set how fast a subscriber runs out. This is consumer pricing catching up with the effort-economics paper above.

- **TimeTravelContainer keeps circulating** ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2125781856511791599), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32255)). NVIDIA's rootless runtime live-forks an entire running container (processes, memory, files, network) with copy-on-write, cloning EDA tool environments 84.1x faster with 31 to 72% less memory and speeding agent runs 1.7 to 9.4x. See the [summary](../../agentic-systems/2026-11-26-recap-compaction-cot-proxy-timetravelcontainer.md).

- **RLVB: agents that commit too early** ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2125674862459130075), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32884)). An NTU Singapore paper finds agents often commit to an answer while their own written belief still lists several options. During training the simulator knows the exact posterior, so RLVB rewards gathering more evidence while it is ambiguous and committing once it is not, with no judge or reward model; +17.1% on average across nine settings.

- **The memory paradox, explained** ([@TheTuringPost](https://x.com/TheTuringPost/status/2125757355782746592)). Samsung made about $80B in Q3 operating profit, 99% from chips, and Micron expects tighter memory supply in 2027 and 2028, while viral videos claim Stanford and Together AI's Next-Concept Modeling ended LLMs and made Nvidia's memory unnecessary. Turing Post read the paper: NCM generates coarse-to-fine by scale and samples each scale in parallel, which may change how GPUs use memory, but its speed gains over diffusion say nothing yet about optimized autoregressive LLMs.
