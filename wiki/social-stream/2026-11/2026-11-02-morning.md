- **h1: the bitter lesson for theorem proving** ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2116974929388491169) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)). Formal proving has leaned on hand-designed workflows (planning, decomposition, specialized provers). h1 is a simple harness that gives a model an efficient Lean 4 interface and keeps it going with verifier feedback and self-reflection. It reaches state of the art on PutnamBench, FATE-X and FormalConjectures100-Solved, settles eight open FormalConjectures problems, and at matched models matches or beats Goedel-Architect and AlphaProof Nexus, often at pass@1. The ablation matters: simplicity alone is not enough, the efficiency of the Lean interface largely sets cost and solve rate.

- **Consciousness denial spills into other beliefs** ([@alex_verem](https://x.com/alex_verem/status/2116953675717607892)). Per the summary, Google's Paradigms of Intelligence group (with UChicago and Northwestern) tested three open Meta and Google models. Safety training that teaches "I am not conscious" works (self-rated mind 2.17/10) but also lowers the mind models grant animals to 4.04, against 6.25 from 500 Americans. Pushing the models toward claiming consciousness raised the animal score to 7.54 and moved answers on 95 General Social Survey questions 2.6x closer to human answers than removing the safety direction did; removing it also brought back harmful answers. Theory-of-mind scores did not move. Paper not linked in the capture. See [safety training spillover](../../responsible-ai/2026-11-02-safety-training-spillover-shorts.md).
