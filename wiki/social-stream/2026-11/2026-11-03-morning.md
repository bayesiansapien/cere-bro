## Posts

- **Weight-space programming: an LLM edits another model's weights** (cluster of 4) ([@arpit_bhayani](https://x.com/arpit_bhayani/status/2117462093851156734), [@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2117389038579531983), [@NeelNanda5](https://x.com/NeelNanda5/status/2117375052618539485), [@SchmidhuberAI](https://x.com/SchmidhuberAI/status/2117447416580575628)). MIT researchers gave GPT-6 Astra the weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B in a Python notebook and a budget of 50 test runs on practice math problems. Astra noticed the model kept repeating itself until it hit the length limit, traced the habit to one internal signal written by two layers, and subtracted that signal out of the weights. Changing 0.016% of weights took MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%, because the model could now finish answers it already knew how to solve. Elvis Saravia called it big for recursive self-improvement. Neel Nanda's read is more careful: Astra is doing old-school mechanistic interpretability (finding a direction and removing it) better than expected, not something spooky. Schmidhuber says self-modifying weights are the 1990s fast-weight programmers. Ben Goertzel asked whether an open model this capable could improve itself this way; nobody has shown that yet.

- **RoboArchitect: harness evolution for robots** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2117410434890535310) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42141)). Stanford gives a coding model the rollouts of a frozen robot policy. The coding model diagnoses failures and rewrites the control code around the policy: when to call it, when to use vision, when to retry. Unseen-scene success rises from 9.7% to 43.3% with no weight change. It is the robotics version of the harness-over-model result the wiki has tracked in [agent harness engineering](../../agentic-systems/agent-harness-engineering.md).
