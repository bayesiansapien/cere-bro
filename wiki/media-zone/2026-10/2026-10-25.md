<span class="mz-card-badge paper">Paper · DAIR pick</span>
<h4>Can LLMs improve their own harness? (ARC-AGI-3)</h4>
<p>Each frontier model writes a complete harness from a fixed bootstrap, plays five development games, and rewrites its whole codebase twice. Every version is scored on 20 held-out games that never feed back. First harnesses range from useless to near-perfect across 27 lineages, but two rewrites add only 2.80 points of RHAE (levels completed, weighted by action efficiency against humans), with a 95% interval of -1.44 to 7.35. Gains on visible games overstate held-out gains, and revisions that cut dollar cost tend to lower the score. It is the second clean result, after the 10-22 HDA audit, that self-revision mostly overfits.</p>

<h4>Adaptive Skill Granularity for coding agents</h4>
<p>A coding agent can take many small tool calls or run one packaged macro skill. This paper makes that a per-task routing decision: a controller reads task structure, execution state and earlier macro failures, and a finite-state executor falls back from macro to primitive steps as an explicit transition. It pairs with Anthropic's advice to fold subagents into one loop with skills: skills become recoverable policies, not just retrievable files.</p>

### Evals are the agent's regression suite

- **Arize:** a one-line prompt change made a support agent 17 points safer and 17 points worse at finishing tasks. The fix is a loop: build datasets from production traces, score them, compare against a baseline, and block merges in CI.
- **Axim:** two frontier models disagreed sharply on whether iHeartMedia goes bankrupt or restructures. Splitting the analysis into checked layers (extraction, derivation, judgment, prediction) made them agree. That leaves open whether agreement means they are right.
- **Contour ($6B fund):** live A/B tests of analysts with and without AI tools, tracked as "cost per useful insight." That is a token-spend metric worth borrowing for routing evals.
