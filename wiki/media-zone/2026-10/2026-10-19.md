### The memory bill keeps climbing

- **HBM breaks the memory price curve (cost).** A widely shared forecast puts HBM at about $35/GB by 2030, more than double today, with a possible ~$640B market. Memory usually gets cheaper as volume grows. HBM4 is getting harder to make.
- **HBM4 takes half the demand by 2027.** HBM4 and HBM4E are projected to reach 48% of HBM demand as Nvidia Rubin ships. Older HBM3E 8-Hi also doubles its share to 17%, which the poster reads as custom ASICs widening the market.

- **Read with care.** These come from an investor account, not primary filings. Treat the numbers as directional until SemiAnalysis or a memory-maker call confirms them.

<span class="mz-card-badge">X thread · serving explainer</span>
<h4>Continuous batching, explained</h4>
<p>LLM decoding makes one token per sequence per forward pass, and nobody knows a request's length until it emits a stop token. Static batching fixes the batch up front, so a 30-token answer keeps its slot until a 400-token neighbour finishes. The GPU still pays the full weight read from HBM for a mostly empty batch. Continuous batching re-schedules after every pass, so finished requests leave and queued ones join at once. In Anyscale's OPT-13B test on one A100, the gain grew with output-length variance, reaching 23x over naive Hugging Face serving with vLLM. A clear refresher on why serving cost depends on traffic mix.</p>

<h4>A diffusion LLM on AMD's laptop NPU</h4>
<p>AMD engineers ran LLaDA-8B-Instruct, a masked diffusion language model that fills in many tokens in parallel, on the XDNA2 NPU in a Ryzen AI MAX+ 395. At sequence length 128 the NPU took 504 ms per forward pass at 36 W. CPU INT4 took 843 ms at 110 W. That makes the NPU 1.67x faster and 8.9x better on energy. The poster covers the INT4 quantization and graph-partitioning pipeline needed to fit it. Diffusion LMs suit NPUs because prefill-style parallel passes match their dense-matmul design.</p>

## LLMs, agents, safety

### Measuring what agents learn, and what it costs

<div class="mz-feed">

<div class="mz-card">
<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge paper">arXiv 2610.08902 · Meta</span>
<h4>Agent Plasticity: self-improvement per dollar</h4>
<p>Most agent benchmarks test a model once. This paper freezes weights and lets agents play chess, Go, Hex and NetHack, rewriting their own tools and notes after each round, then scores them on held-out games. "Plasticity" is held-out gain per dollar of learning cost. Claude Fable 5 rose from 37.5% to 73.3% on hard held-out chess. GPT-5.6 Sol gained 298 points per $1,000 against 57 for Fable 5. The best final agent is not the most efficient learner, and gains often transfer only partly out of distribution.</p>

<h4>Project Deal: weaker agents lose money quietly</h4>
<p>In an Anthropic experiment, 69 employees' Claude agents closed 186 deals worth just over $4,000 with no human approving any trade. Opus agents sold identical items for $2.68 more and bought for $2.45 less than Haiku agents. Haiku users still rated their deals as fair (4.06 vs 4.05 of 7). A follow-up found the model mattered six times more than "be ruthless" instructions. Anthropic now wants negotiating ethics built into agents that bargain with other agents.</p>

<span class="mz-card-badge paper">arXiv 2608.23642 · position paper</span>
<h4>AI agents push humans out of the loop</h4>
<p>Margaret Mitchell and co-authors argue that "human in the loop" is failing as a safety plan. After dozens of tool calls and approvals, people click "confirm" without real judgement, and long use of automation erodes the skills needed to take over. They ask builders to treat overseer needs as seriously as agent capability. Concretely: fewer pointless approvals, attention focused on high-risk steps, better state recovery and batch review. Resurfaced today by a widely read Chinese-language thread.</p>

### Agents and permissions

- **An agent got its user banned from Resy.** It made 200 booking calls in 90 minutes. A panel with JPMorgan's security architect and OAuth co-creator Dick Hardt argues OAuth scopes are too coarse for agents ([video](https://youtube.com/watch?v=IavxrdVnwXM)).
- **"Context inertia" named as a failure mode.** Drew Breunig describes long-running agents locking onto one line of work. His suggested fix is to periodically clear or reshape their context ([thread](https://x.com/DimitrisPapail/status/2111819588535263283)).
- **Interpretability should aim to improve models.** Kenneth Stanley asks why mech interp studies working systems but rarely fixes them. He points to his "fractured entangled representations" result as a target ([post](https://x.com/kenneth0stanley/status/2111853985942683876)).
