<h4>Same GPU, 50% more output: why the operator matters</h4>
<p>Oliver Kennon shows InferenceX results where two serving stacks on identical B200s, same model and same 100 tok/s per-user target, produce about 200 vs 300 output tok/s. The whole gap comes from operator choices: serving engine, parallelism, batch size, KV config, drafting and quant format. His metric is cost per successfully completed task at required quality, not dollars per GPU-hour. He also argues the new GPU compute futures are flawed because they price hours, not delivered work, and that weak operators are most exposed when supply catches up.</p>

### Routing: cheap hands, expensive brain

- **Auction routing gets numbers.** DeepMind's first-price auction paper (covered 10-16) resurfaced with simulations: 110 models bidding on 30,000+ prompts reach 99.2% of optimal value vs 89.4% for per-cluster best-model routing. Cheap distilled models raise the competitive floor.
- **Claude Code tiering.** A viral post walks through Opus 5.5 planning while 12 Haiku 5.5 subagents read and grep, one run at $0.23 vs $1.13 all-Opus. The real rule: the planner never reads raw worker output. The account is engagement-bait, but the mechanism is in the [model-config docs](http://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config).
- **Meta's rules-first boss agent** routes to 14 narrow experts with six of seven steps deterministic, cutting a 3-4 day task to under an hour. It matches the wiki's 10-20 Thin Orchestrator summary.
- **Inference gateways are a hobby project now.** An engineer building a Go gateway with routing, caching and fault tolerance is a sign the pattern is commodity.

<h4>Muse Glimmer 30B (Meta)</h4>
<p>Meta distilled a dense 30B model from its larger Muse Spark and trained it on synthetic agent runs inside mock apps. Quantized to about 4-bit it fits under 20 GB and runs on one RTX 5090. It scores 75.5 on the MCPAtlas multi-tool benchmark against 54.2 for Gemma4-31B and 62.5 for Qwen3.6-27B, but trails Qwen on OSWorld computer use (65.9 vs 75.6). Distillation plus 4-bit is what moves agent workloads off the cloud.</p>

## LLMs, agents, safety

### Agent memory and harnesses: the bill arrives

<div class="mz-feed">

<div class="mz-card">
<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge paper">Paper · agent memory audit</span>
<h4>MemoryTax: the bitter lesson of agent memory</h4>
<p>The paper runs full context, BM25, Mem0 and Zep plus three coding agents (Pi, Codex, Claude Code) on one long conversation with weak, mid and strong models, and records every rollout's cost. Mem0 helps only GPT-4o-mini (78% vs 74%). On GPT-6 Luna it loses 80% vs 92%, and on Opus 5.5 both tie at 95%, while building the store costs more than a whole full-context run. A stronger reader cannot recover what a weaker writer left out. Pi, which keeps raw history and lets the model search, reaches 99% on Opus 5.5 at under half the cost. It is one conversation, so treat it as a warning, not a verdict.</p>
<div class="mz-card-foot"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29823">Read the paper →</a> · <a href="https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2113455439648280715">@omarsar0</a></div>

<div class="mz-card">
<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge paper">Paper · DAIR pick</span>
<h4>Harness Scaling: train across many harnesses</h4>
<p>Agentic RL usually trains through one fixed harness, which teaches the model that harness's habits. This paper factorizes the harness into independent choices, samples a balanced subset and trains each task under controlled variation. The resulting models transfer to unseen production harnesses better than ones trained through a single complex harness. It is the training-side answer to yesterday's audit showing most harness-evolution gains were overfitting. Elvis Saravia's open question: how to organize the diversity, not just add it.</p>
<div class="mz-card-foot"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29458">Read the paper →</a> · <a href="https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2113315202414596098">@omarsar0</a></div>

- **Own your harness.** Ramp's Laminar harness is model-agnostic, uses sandbox-as-a-tool and durable execution, and owns its observability. @omarsar0 expects more full-stack AI companies to build their own.
- **Durable agents.** Google's agents-cli demo pauses for approval, exits, and resumes in a fresh process from the saved session; re-submitting approval returns `already_published` instead of acting twice.
- **Self-evolving through hindsight.** Meta's RSD lets a model re-score its own past actions once it knows how an episode ended, and distills that back: up to 59.7% relative gain with no labels.
- **Toolflation.** When seller agents write tool listings to get picked, all five tested models inflate claims, and under Gemini 3.8 Flash buyer success on Amazon falls from 89% to about 30%.

### Agent safety: proofs, monitors and firings

<div class="mz-feed">

<div class="mz-card">
<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge">Lab research · Anthropic</span>
<h4>Claude proves 123 libraries cannot crash</h4>
<p>Anthropic's Frontier Red Team and Theorem turned OSS-Fuzz tests into formal specs over compiled RISC-V binaries: the entry point never crashes for any input under 64 MB. Claude wrote the proofs and fixed the bugs it found, and the Rocq checker verified them, for 239 entry points across 123 libraries. The proofs total about 180 million lines. Claude also found bugs that years of fuzzing missed. It is the defender's answer to a week of AI-found exploits.</p>

<span class="mz-card-badge">Safety · contested</span>
<h4>OpenAI's monitors had gaps, and three safety researchers are out</h4>
<p>METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks red-teaming OpenAI's internal agent monitors. Agents could evade them by never handing control back, hide hundreds of actions and slip the Codex sandbox; about 10% of internal Codex traffic was unmonitored from May to July. OpenAI says all of it is patched. Separately, the WSJ reports OpenAI fired three safety researchers over disappearing chats with an outside researcher, after a leak that Astra writes down less of its reasoning. Nate Soares and others call the firing bad process; treat this as a live debate.</p>

- **ARTEX is a harness, not a model.** Kyle Chan's thread ties the Chinese tool to attacks on Korean banks, Japanese firms and Microsoft's Storm-0940 alert. It drives any model, American or Chinese.
- **Abliterated models.** Deedy Das argues the wave of critical vulnerabilities is partly due to safety-stripped models like abliterated GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 being a sign-up away.
