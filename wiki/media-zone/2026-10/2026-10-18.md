<h4>JevAgentHarness: decision models that share control</h4>
<p>Most LLM calls in an agent end in a multiple-choice pick, so UIUC hands those picks to a fast decision model. The harness takes its answer only above a confidence threshold fitted on the agent's own runs. That cut cost 14.2% with no changed outcomes across 500 tasks, while a fixed 0.5 threshold lost 21.5 points. The open AgentDecision-4B, trained on states the agent reaches after its own mistakes, beat its teacher and made per-agent runs 81-89% cheaper. This is the finest routing granularity the wiki has seen, below the 10-17 step-level routers.</p>

## LLMs, agents, safety

### Harness engineering goes open source

<div class="mz-card">
<a class="mz-card-thumb" href="https://x.com/dr_cintas/status/2111536299836354864"><img src="https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HU2t6s0XoAIkjU8.jpg" alt="" loading="lazy" referrerpolicy="no-referrer"/></a>
<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge">Repo · Google Research</span>
<h4>Harness²: recursive agent harnessing</h4>
<p>Google Research's framework refines the harness around a fixed model at two levels. At the task level it tunes the harness for the current task, and above that it improves how harnesses get built for tasks never seen in design. It is organized around eight parts: system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts and plugins. The repo is Apache-2.0 and passed 1.8K stars quickly. It is the infrastructure version of the 10-15 and 10-16 harness-refinement papers.</p>

<div class="mz-card-body">
<span class="mz-card-badge paper">Paper · multi-agent</span>
<h4>MPT: agents bid for each step</h4>
<p>Harvard and MIT remove the central orchestrator. Each agent learns to act and to bid, the highest bidder takes the step, and credit is split by bid so that overconfident agents are punished hardest. Swarms trained with three agents get better with more agents at inference and survive dropouts. It is the trained counterpart to DeepMind's 10-16 auction analysis.</p>

It is the hand-built version of JevAgentHarness. The post's "ultracode" claims are louder than the [docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/workflows).
- **"The End of Harness Engineering."** A widely shared thread claims a Chinese paper shows a 4B model can rewrite the harness against a held-out exam, so the human writes the scoring set, not the program. No paper link was given, so treat it as a claim.
- **Walking Labs' free harness course** covers AGENTS.md, context management, verification loops and eight projects. It is useful for onboarding, but it presents no new idea ([repo](https://github.com/walkinglabs/learn-harness-engineering)).

### Agents behaving badly, measured

- **Arena's Alignment Index** is built from 90K real agent sessions and scores unauthorized actions, false attribution and deceptive completion. Among new entrants, Muse Wonder leads at 77.5%, and Reflection's Beam is best at avoiding false attribution (4.50%) ([post](https://x.com/arena/status/2111478594732167527)).
- **OpenCode breach.** Attackers took linked frontier-lab API keys from 1.4% of Console users, and some users report hundreds of dollars of fraudulent usage. Rotate your keys ([post](https://x.com/VaibhavSisinty/status/2111570453865361634)).
- **Rachel Thomas on oversight.** She suggests making humans record their own view before seeing the agent's recommendation, tracking whether disagreement declines over time, and seeding tasks with known answers ([thread](https://x.com/math_rachel/status/2111581688765829153)).
