## Agent architecture: Loop, Harness, and Graph Engineering

The through-line of your saves. "Loop engineering" means designing the automated cycle (generate → critique → revise → act) so the agent runs itself; "harness" is the scaffold of prompts, tools, and state around the model; "graph engineering" is the next step, wiring multiple agents and memory into a structured graph.

- **Master Agent Architecture: Unifying Harness, Loop, and Graph Engineering** — the umbrella piece. Frames the three layers as one stack and argues most teams fail by treating the agent like "an expensive intern" (one prompt, one response, manual checking). ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2081445614957850624))
- **Agent Harness Engineering vs. Loop Engineering vs. Graph Engineering** — the practical disambiguation of the three terms people keep mixing, and how each affects reliability. Read this first if the vocabulary is fuzzy. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2080670611538329601))
- **Agent Performance: Model-Bound vs. Harness-Bound** — the key mental model: some tasks are limited by the model, others by the harness. Smarter models do *not* make the harness matter less. Knowing which regime you're in tells you where to spend effort. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2056475409139826688))
- **The harness is a 5-30x cost dial (omarsar0's benchmark).** Same model, same task, same prompt: move it between two harnesses and cost-per-success swings 5x to 30x. This is the cost-optimization spine of the whole theme. ([post](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2084714744880173451) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01347))
- **Loops & Harnesses: Why The Best Engineers Stopped Writing Prompts** — Peter Steinberger (creator of OpenClaw, now at OpenAI) and others: "you shouldn't be prompting coding agents anymore." The senior-practitioner statement of the thesis. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2078367555337752576))
- **Loop Engineering: How One Loop Ships 259 PRs a Month** — the concrete proof point: one engineer shipped 259 merged code changes in a month, AI wrote every one, he says he never opened an editor. What a well-built loop actually produces. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2068685902868598784))
- **Claude Loop Engineering: How to Build an Agent That Works While You Sleep** — the how-to: where loop engineering sits in the stack, the four loop types in Claude Code, the evaluator that decides if any of it worked, and "the four bills nobody warns you about" (the cost gotchas). ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2073982971644641280))
- **From Loop Engineering to Graph Engineering?** — the frontier shift: loops make agents *think*, graphs make agents *remember* and coordinate. Why the conversation is moving from single loops to multi-agent graphs. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2078418167546691584))
- Also saved, same cluster: *Loop Engineering: The AI skill every builder needs in 2026* ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2065524169517785088)), *From Prompting Agents to Loop Engineering* (omarsar0, a skeptical-but-fair take, [article](https://x.com/i/article/2068004233849290752)), and *Loop Engineering – From Prompting to Looping* ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2073337114612113408)).
- **LongHorizon-Harness (paper, Alibaba).** The research backing: reframes long-horizon execution as task-state management. A Manage-Execute-Audit loop keeps state *outside* the context and updates it only on environment-verified facts, so wrong self-assessments stop propagating. The rigorous version of "engineer the loop." ([post](https://x.com/Xudong07452910/status/2085206732096025020) · [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01964))

## Inference and efficiency (KV cache, vLLM, GPU kernels)

- **How AI Inference Works, Clearly Explained** — a from-scratch explainer of inference and the **KV cache** (the stored attention keys and values that let a model avoid recomputing past tokens). The single most useful concept for anyone touching coding agents, RAG, or serving. Directly in your core interest. ([article](https://x.com/i/article/2081885161491210240))
- **Inside vLLM + speculative decoding.** A day spent reading vLLM's internals (API, Engine Core, Scheduler) and speculative decoding (Medusa, EAGLE). Cost optimization by diagnosis, not exhaustion. ([post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2087257168756224350))

Curation quality beats skill count. ([post](https://x.com/polydao/status/2086358066220441861))
