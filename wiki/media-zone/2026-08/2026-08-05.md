# Media Zone | 2026-08-05

> A kernel release out-signalled every product launch, and the loudest infrastructure story of the day was about putting GPUs in orbit.

## Today's signal

- Dominant story: Cursor open-sourced its MoE training megakernel. Practitioners amplified it, not marketers.
- Pattern: two independent teams took GPU scheduling away from the layer that normally owns it.
- Cross-source: NVIDIA hardware appeared in three unrelated stories today. Orbit, robotaxis, and Cursor's kernel.
- Counter-signal: nobody posted a throughput number for anything shipped today.
- Quiet area: no curated reposts at all this slot, and Reddit farmed empty for a second day.
- Noise floor: roughly 40 of 71 tweets were political commentary from three accounts.

## Routing, KV cache, compression, GPU

### Mixture-of-Kittens: the kernel release that practitioners actually read

- Cursor open-sourced a fused MoE training kernel for NVL72 racks, Apache-licensed.
- Claimed 2.37x over the fastest public baseline, 1.41x in their own production.
- HuggingFace's @eliebakouch called it "insanely good," then flagged the gap: mxfp8, no nvfp4.
- That critique is the highest-value reply of the day. Blackwell supports FP4 and this ships without it.
- Amplification came from kernel people at HuggingFace and xAI, not from launch accounts.

### Taking scheduling away from the vendor stack

- tinygrad ran its first kernel on custom MEC firmware on an AMD 7900XTX.
- Stated next phase: "tinygrad will be operating system." They mean displacing the driver.
- Same direction as Cursor's megakernel, arrived at independently on rival hardware.
- Both moves are about owning scheduling rather than sitting on top of it.
- Separately, DeepSeek V4 Flash now runs on two RTX Pro 6000 Blackwells in a $53k desktop.

## LLMs, agents, safety

### Agent crews ship while the research says the parts do not work

- AWS open-sourced Kiro Crew: several scheduled agents at once, wired into existing tools.
- Matt Garman announced it personally, though the blog frames it as three engineers' side project.
- The pitch is that you are currently the integration layer, and everything stalls when you step away.
- Video side has been circling the same problem: MCP Tasks on async durable agents, Theta on long-horizon environments.
- Nobody in the launch discussion mentioned today's benchmarks finding agent skill libraries mostly fail to abstract.

### Evals are the moat, and the eval discourse is ahead of the product discourse

- Waymo's Dolgov: "build your eval before you build your technology." Model is table stakes.
- His framing number: demo in 18 months, product in 15 years. Each extra nine costs 10x.
- Benchmaxxing video argues the surge in AI benchmark gaming is now the field's central measurement problem.
- Gradient Flow made the industry version today: passing your evals does not mean you are safe.
- Its evidence is legal, not technical. A ChatGPT memory suit, a Workday hiring case, a German chatbot ruling.

- Anaconda acquired EnkryptAI, folding AI risk detection into the platform that owns Kilo Code.
- Linux Foundation opened an RFC on SAFE incident-disclosure guidelines, timed to Black Hat.

## Multimodal / vision / audio

### Qwen-Image-3.0 targets documents, not art

- Ranked first among Chinese models, second overall, in Arena's text-to-image leaderboard.
- Spec reads like a productivity tool: 4.5k-token prompts, newspapers and exam papers in one pass.
- Text legible down to 10px, 12 languages, 20+ fonts, realistic UI and web-page simulation.
- Their own line: not pursuing good looks, pursuing usefulness.
- Scoble's framing was competitive rather than technical: "The Chinese are shipping. Will Grok keep up?"

### Alpamayo 2 Super and the inspectability pitch

- NVIDIA's open autonomous-driving reasoning model went commercially licensed, weights on HuggingFace.
- Sold on long-tail rare events plus inspectable decisions, not aggregate accuracy.
- Google DeepMind's @zhu_hanqing666 congratulated the team, calling physical-world reasoning "a major mountain."
- A competitor endorsing your release is a stronger signal than your own benchmark chart.
- Pairs with the Waymo talk above: both argue explainability is what a safety-critical stack actually needs.
