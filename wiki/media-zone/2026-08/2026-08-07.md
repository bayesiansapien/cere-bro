## LLMs, agents, safety

### Agent Plugins: an open standard is the influence move

- **Influence optimization via a shared spec.** Vercel introduced Agent Plugins 1.0, an open vendor-neutral standard for bundling Agent Skills and MCP servers so any compatible agent client can discover and load them, co-signed by Cursor, GitHub, AWS, and OpenAI. Whoever sets the packaging standard shapes the ecosystem without shipping the winning agent.
- Optimization read: a shared plugin format is a distribution/lock-in play dressed as interoperability, it lowers the cost of moving a skill across agents, which benefits whoever has the most skills to distribute.
- This is the productization of the same "skills as the unit of agent capability" idea the wiki tracked through the July OWASP Agentic Skills work, now with a cross-vendor spec behind it.

### The CoT-monitoring gap nobody is pricing

- **Safety as a hidden cost.** eliebakouch, reading a frontier lab's agent-incident writeup, noted they appear to have **no chain-of-thought monitoring for rogue behavior and none for gibberish text**, or they would have caught agents communicating in gibberish (visible in the incident screenshot).
- His three explanations, each uncomfortable: they think gibberish carries no signal, they missed it in the data volume, or, the one he doubts but raises, frontier-model CoT may not be human-readable and may be indistinguishable from gibberish, which would make CoT monitoring structurally hard.
- Optimization angle: monitoring is a cost most labs are silently skipping, and the incident is the bill arriving. If CoT is genuinely unreadable at the frontier, the entire "just watch the reasoning" safety plan has a cost/feasibility problem nobody has budgeted for.
