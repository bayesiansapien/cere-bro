# Chip glut, Anthropic below IPO, and the Jevons question

## TL;DR

The Huang-reinstatement rally lasted one day. On Tuesday Nvidia fell about 6 to 8%, Anthropic **closed below its $83 IPO price**, and neoclouds CoreWeave and Nebius each dropped 11%, while the Dow rose. The Information's read is that the market is no longer pricing one man but **supply and demand for compute**: SemiAnalysis published a report the same day saying "we are starting to see signs of a broader pricing cooldown" in the market for Nvidia AI chips, citing price cuts by SpaceX. A chip glut has been a Wall Street worry for months without evidence; this is the first supply-side datapoint.

## The items

- **Anthropic below IPO.** Opened at up to $115 on IPO day, priced at $83, now through it. SpaceX is also below its $135 IPO price. The Information notes that of this year's handful of completed IPOs (Anthropic, SpaceX, SK Hynix, Cerebras, Lime) **all were trading below their IPO price** as of Tuesday, the fifth disappointing IPO year in a row. OpenAI is lined up for 2027.
- **Private funding concentrates.** Goldman's Jane Dunlevie: the **top 10 private companies more than doubled their combined valuation from $1.1T to $2.6T in one year** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, Stripe, Waymo, Revolut, Anduril, Ramp, Cognition and others). Big companies dominate private rounds the way they dominate IPOs.
- **Marcus's chain.** If Anthropic and SpaceX stay below their IPO prices, an OpenAI IPO cannot price where it hoped; if OpenAI does not IPO soon, SoftBank is exposed and Nvidia plus CoreWeave and Nebius take the hit. He now predicts an "AI winter on both sides of the Pacific" with bailout requests, and opposes bailouts.
- **Demand-side counter-signal.** Ramp card-spend data shared by Garry Tan suggests **the Jevons paradox has stalled for AI**: inference cost per unit of intelligence keeps falling, but usage is no longer rising to match. Tan's own read is that agent-system complexity spiked and adoption has hit the early-to-late-adopter chasm.
- **Huang's position is harder, not easier.** He apologised to employees at his first all-hands and said he is in daily therapy with monthly feedback sessions. Commerce and Justice are still investigating Megaspeed and Supermicro, and the SEC is reportedly investigating Nvidia. He testifies publicly on 12-01 (House) with a Senate Banking appearance reported for 12-03.
- **Dell's quarter cuts the other way.** Revenue more than doubled and beat its own forecast by nearly $6B on AI data-center demand; shares rose 5% after hours. The AI build-out is still booking record hardware revenue while the chips that go in it may be cooling in price.

## Relation to prior wiki pages

- **Tests** [compute-economics.md](../hardware/compute-economics.md)'s 11-21 "the market prices the commitment gap" and 11-23 "supply bottlenecks named" updates. Until now the page tracked bottlenecks (HBM, power, packaging). A **pricing cooldown is the first sign of the opposite condition**, and the two can coexist: tight supply of the newest parts, softening prices for the current generation.
- **The Jevons stall is the load-bearing question for every efficiency result in this wiki.** The 11-22 through 11-25 digests are a near-unbroken run of cost-reduction papers (KV compression, drafters, loops, cheap-model routing, harness token cuts). The implicit assumption has always been that cheaper inference means more inference. If Ramp's data holds, cheaper inference means **lower spend**, which changes who captures the value of all that work.
