# Nvidia CreditWatch, China curbs, Week 47 chips

## Nvidia governance

- **S&P Global placed Nvidia on CreditWatch Negative** on 11-25, citing management and governance risk exposed by the Huang ouster and reinstatement; a downgrade decision within 90 days ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/s-p-global-puts-nvidia-creditwatch-negative-due-governance-risks)). Martin Peers notes Nvidia's board had no chair until two weeks ago ([column](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/s-p-discovers-nvidias-governance-lapses)).
- Huang testifies before the Senate Banking Committee on 12-03 in an export-controls oversight hearing (AI Breakfast), after a House appearance on 12-01 noted yesterday.

## China: chips and tokens

- The Commerce Department's new rule throttles Chinese access to advanced Nvidia chips in data centers **outside** China, plugging a long-standing loophole; it stays in force despite Huang's return ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/chinese-ai-firms-confront-dire-future-u-s-tightens-access-chips-tokens)). The Semiconductor Newsletter lists the same item as "export controls extended to remote computing infrastructure".
- Combined with US labs' anti-distillation measures, The Decoder reports DeepSeek researchers internally estimate they are **up to nine months behind** US models and the gap is widening ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/us-chip-access-curbs-and-anti-distillation-measures-are-reportedly-already-slowing-chinese-ai-labs/)).
- Huawei was convicted on 10 of 12 counts (trade-secret conspiracy, bank fraud) in Brooklyn, acquitted of racketeering. Huawei and Phlexing reported the first integrated domestic Chinese EDA toolchain on a live chip project (AI Weekly Espresso).

## Week 47 in semiconductors (The Semiconductor Newsletter)

- NVIDIA record **$115.8B** quarterly revenue as Vera Rubin enters commercial deployment.
- Intel secured an **external 18A-P foundry customer** and expanded advanced packaging work with Microsoft.
- Soitec photonics SOI revenue +50%; IQE raised growth guidance above 40% on AI optical demand; Infineon SiC for 800 VDC AI power.
- Lam Research broke ground on a Taiwan technology center under a $3B R&D expansion; Siemens is acquiring Breker Verification Systems (EDA).
- EU Chips Act: over EUR 33B across 21 state-aid decisions. US proposes a five-year extension of the 35% semiconductor manufacturing investment tax credit.
- Trump to lead the TSMC Arizona expansion groundbreaking on 12-04 ([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/trump-lead-tsmc-arizona-groundbreaking-ceremony-bringing-huang-back-nvidia)).

## Compute economics signals

- **CoreWeave 2031 bonds yield near 18%** despite contracts with Meta, Microsoft and OpenAI, per a widely shared post pairing it with falling GPU rental prices ([post](https://x.com/StockSavvyShay/status/2125619576834843006)). Gary Marcus's bear list (tapering usage, falling spot GPU prices, rising credit spreads) circulated alongside.
- **Memory paradox** (Turing Post): Samsung made about $80B Q3 operating profit, 99% from chips, and Micron expects tighter memory supply in 2027-28, while viral videos claim Stanford and Together AI's Next-Concept Modeling (coarse-to-fine, parallel-per-scale generation) makes memory chips unnecessary. Turing Post's read: it may change how GPUs use memory, but speed gains over diffusion say nothing about optimized autoregressive LLMs ([post](https://x.com/TheTuringPost/status/2125757355782746592)).
- Dell: Q3 revenue doubled to **$54.9B**, AI server backlog **$132.3B** after $60.2B new orders, FY27 outlook raised to $202B (AI Weekly Espresso).
